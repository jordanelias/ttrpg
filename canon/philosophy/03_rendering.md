# §3 — Rendering

**Status:** PROPOSED
**Owns:** rendering's nature and dual consequence; the passive stratum; the stabilizing tendency; the one/many question
**Sources:** F §3.1, §3.2, §4.2 (first half), §4.3 (the primitive system)

---

## §3.1 Rendering as constitutive process

Rendering is always already being performed. It is the way the world is given — the process by which
the thread-substrate becomes experienceable reality. It is not an objective mechanism external to
minds, nor a cosmic process operating independently of consciousness.

Because threads are constitutive ground and condition of possibility simultaneously (§2.1), rendering
is **not a subjective overlay on an independent objective world**. What rendering produces is the
world *as world*. The rendered world is not an illusion concealing a real thread-substrate; it is
the thread-substrate as it is given. The relation between threads and rendering is not
appearance/reality but **constitution/experience**.

**On "performed by minds."** The source suite said rendering is "performed by our minds as conscious
beings," and simultaneously borrowed *being-in-the-world* — a phrase whose entire purpose in its
home tradition is to refuse an inner subject that constitutes an outer world. The framework cannot
have both formulations as stated.

The framework's own resources resolve this, and the resolution is better than either horn. Layer-two
self-rendering (§4.1) is *unconscious and automatic*. Constitution of that kind is not a mental
performance. Rendering is accordingly restated as **a constitutive process of embodied being, of
which conscious apprehension is the reflective face** — not an act consciousness undertakes, but the
process in which a conscious being already stands. This keeps everything §3.1 needs (rendering is
not external, not independent, not an overlay) and stops the framework asserting a subject-side
constitution it elsewhere denies.

## §3.2 The dual consequence of thread operations

Thread operations do two things at once, because they change both the constitutive ground and the
conditions of possibility.

First, **the facticity of the world is changed**: the threads constituting reality are rearranged,
and with them the objective facts.

Second, **how the world comes to be rendered is changed**, because rendering is always the rendering
of the threads, and the threads have moved.

So the distinction between *changing the world* and *changing how the world is experienced*
collapses for thread operations. A Weaving that heals a wound does not merely change how the wound
is experienced; it changes the threads constituting the wound, which changes both the world's
facticity and its rendering. Non-sensitive observers see the healing because the threads have
changed and their rendering of those threads changes accordingly. The change is real at every level
because it occurs at the level from which all levels are constituted.

*(Terminological note: "facticity" is used here in the ordinary sense of factual state. It does not
carry the technical sense of the factical character of a being's own situated existence.)*

## §3.3 The passive stratum

**Rendering has an active and a passive stratum, and the framework has been using the passive one
under four different names without naming it once.**

The four uses in the source suite: a "primitive system" that registers valence and intensity "with
lower epistemological demands than the rendering proper"; the somatic and perceptual labour by which
others register a drifting practitioner's failure before they can articulate it; a "passive
seismographic awareness" of remote disturbance requiring no deliberate act; and the substrate that
reflexive self-rendering screens out "as noise below the threshold of intelligible experience."

These are one thing. The phrase "the rendering *proper*" already concedes it: an improper stratum is
being invoked.

**The passive stratum is pre-predicative.** It registers affect, intensity, salience and trace
without composing them into a determinate presentation of an object. It is not outside rendering —
if it were, the claim that rendering is the way the world is given would be false. It is rendering's
lower register.

**Two things follow immediately, and both matter elsewhere.**

- **The residue of a confrontation is not unconstituted.** §5.3's central formulation — "the
  remainder was not lost; it was not constituted" — is too strong by its own account, since the same
  passage insists the emotional residue, the somatic trace and the fragments are *real*. A trace is
  something constituted. The correct statement is: **not actively constituted; passively registered
  as trace.** This does not weaken the finitude argument; it makes it precise, and it explains why
  anything survives an encounter that exceeded active constitution.
- **The bootstrap of thread sensitivity is closed.** §5.5 depends on this.

## §3.4 The stabilizing tendency, and where it lives

The rendering privileges stable actualization: configurations with determinate structure, temporal
depth and epistemic accessibility. This is not a value built into reality. It is a consequence of
the rendering being a finite process operating on an infinite ground. It can process what is finite,
determinate and temporally accumulated; it cannot process what is infinite, indeterminate, or
radically present without becoming.

**The tendency is a fact about rendering, not about the ground.** This is stated flatly because the
source suite located it in both places, and the operation-type taxonomy — the whole account of why
some threadwork costs the practitioner and some does not — was built on the version that puts it in
the ground.

That version cannot stand. A spooling that "would resume," was "already moving toward," or has a
"tendency toward coherence" is responsive to disruption, and §1.5 forbids that. Disclaiming active
resistance is not sufficient; the objection is to directedness, not to force.

And the ground-located version fails on the framework's own facts at the one place it matters most.
§8.5 states that **new threads cannot spool through regions where the fabric's structural integrity
is broken.** So in a Locked Zone the spooling would *never* resume the prior configuration, no
matter how much time passed. Mending torn substrate is therefore not "what the constitutive spooling
would do, granted sufficient time" — and by the ground-located definition, Mending a torn region
would not count as restorative at all. The setting's central benign practice would be ungrounded
exactly where it is most needed.

**The tendency, restated.** It has two faces, and both are rendered-side:

- **For beings:** the target's own layer-two self-maintenance (§4.1) — the continuous unconscious
  threadwork holding a configuration in its shape.
- **For places and configurations without layer two:** the equilibrium of the shared rendering that
  holds them.

A configuration fully held by the rendering is drawn toward equilibrium; the rendering performs
continuous, unconscious work to maintain it. This is what drift decouples from (§7.2), and what
operation type is measured against (§6.6).

## §3.5 One rendering or many

**This is the framework's largest unanswered question, and several other answers depend on it.**

The suite speaks of "the rendering" in the singular when it maintains configurations, and of "their
renderings" in the plural when observers fail to integrate a drifting practitioner. It invokes
shared reality, co-worlding and collective rendering without defining any of them. And §3.2 explains
inter-observer agreement by appeal to a substrate each mind independently tracks — which is the
appearance/reality picture §3.1 rejects, with threads occupying the reality slot.

Two answers are available and they build materially different worlds. The supplement sets out both
(ruling R-4). The framework's own commitments lean toward the first:

**Co-constitution.** The shared world is constituted *through* the community of renderings; each
subject apprehends others as themselves rendering, and objectivity is what holds across that
apprehension. On this reading "collective rendering" has a definite meaning, Coherence's outward
facing is genuinely constitutive rather than merely evidential, and §4.3's claim that Coherence is
*structurally intersubjective* is literally true: a practitioner losing their outward facing is the
community failing to constitute them as human. It also explains, without appeal to a hidden
substrate, why non-sensitive observers see the healing.

**Realism about threads.** Threads exist determinately regardless of any rendering; each mind
apprehends them more or less well. This is simpler and it costs §3.1: the denial that rendering is
an overlay on an independent world has to go, and constitution/experience reverts to
reality/perception.

Until this is ruled, three things remain undefined: what a hermit's outward-facing Coherence is,
what "shared reality" names, and whether drift propagates to people who are not present.

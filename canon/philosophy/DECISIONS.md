# D-0. The load-bearing assumption

**Not a choice between options — a check on whether one sentence means what the rewrite assumed.**

## What your text says

From the Inseparability Principle:

> "the three dimensions are not three separate properties that happen to be correlated, but three
> aspects of a single unified structure. **To move one is, necessarily and inescapably, to move
> all.**"

And, two sentences earlier, that this is *"a metaphysical fact about the nature of threads"* — not a
rule about practitioners.

## The question

Does "to move one is to move all" hold for **any** movement of a thread by any process — or only for
**practitioner manipulations**?

The rewrite reads it generally, on the strength of "a metaphysical fact about the nature of threads."
That reading is what licenses the central derivation at §4.2: rendering positions a thread on its
intelligibility axis, therefore rendering moves that dimension, therefore it moves all three,
therefore **rendering is a thread operation**, therefore self-rendering is threadwork — which is
layer 2.

## What breaks if you meant it narrowly

The chain breaks at step two, and with it:

- the derivation of layer 2 (§4.2) reverts to the source's mis-cited version;
- the stabilizing tendency loses its derivation and D-2 below becomes a bare choice;
- the operation-type taxonomy loses its grounding;
- the outward facing stops being a *force* and reverts to mere appearance, which unmakes D-1's
  recommendation;
- roughly a third of what the suite now claims is entailed reverts to stipulated.

## What it costs to read it generally

One commitment: **rendering actualizes what it renders.** That is your own "what rendering produces
is the world as world" taken at its word — but it is a real commitment and you should make it
knowingly, not inherit it from a rewrite.

**Recommendation:** read it generally. Confidence: high, because your own text calls it a fact about
threads rather than a rule about practice, and because the general reading is what makes the
framework's best structures derivable rather than asserted.

**Your ruling:** ☐ general ☐ narrow ☐ needs thought

## D-1. One rendering, or many?

**The largest open question. Several other answers depend on it.**

### What your text says

Denying an independent world:

> "rendering is not a subjective overlay on an independent objective world. What rendering produces
> is the world as world."

Explaining agreement between observers:

> "Non-sensitive observers see the healing because the threads have changed, and **their rendering**
> of those threads changes accordingly."

And elsewhere: *"the shared reality upon which all co-worlding occurs"*; *"collective rendering"*;
*"Coherence is, in this sense, structurally intersubjective."*

### The collision

The first passage denies an independent objective world. The second explains inter-observer agreement
by an independent substrate that each mind separately tracks — which is exactly the
appearance/reality picture the first passage rejects, with threads occupying the reality slot.

The definite singular ("*the* rendering" maintains configurations) and the plural ("*their*
renderings" fail to integrate a drifter) are used interchangeably. Shared reality, co-worlding and
collective rendering are invoked and never defined.

### What depends on it

- Whether "collective rendering" means anything at all.
- Whether a hermit has an outward-facing Coherence.
- Whether drift propagates to people not present.
- What a thread *is* when nobody renders it — which the framework never says.

### The options

**(A) Co-constitution.** The shared world is constituted through the community of renderings. Each
subject apprehends others as themselves rendering; objectivity is what holds across that
apprehension.

- Coherence's outward facing becomes genuinely constitutive rather than evidential — others rendering
  you as human is part of *what holds you* as human.
- "Collective rendering" acquires a definition: the aggregate thread-force of all renderings of a
  configuration.
- A person unrendered by others is at structural risk. Isolation becomes dangerous, not just lonely.
- Explains inter-observer agreement without a hidden substrate.
- **Cost:** strange, and it makes community metaphysically load-bearing in a way you cannot later
  walk back quietly.

**(B) Realism about threads.** Threads exist determinately regardless of rendering; minds apprehend
them more or less well.

- Simpler, and it matches how the operational rules already read.
- **Cost:** the denial of an independent world has to go. Constitution/experience reverts to
  reality/perception, and the framework becomes realist about threads and phenomenological only about
  access. That is a smaller philosophical claim than the one you currently make.

### Argument for one option

**(A)**, with high confidence — and note that under D-0's general reading it is not merely
preferable but **entailed**: if rendering moves threads, then others' rendering of you moves your
threads, and the outward facing is a holding force whether or not you decide it is.

**If you take (A):** §3.5 commits; §4.3's outward facing keeps both components; §7.3's two channels
stand; §4.7's "residual maintenance" is defined as others' continuing rendering.
**If you take (B):** §3.1 loses its second paragraph; §4.3's outward facing becomes evidential only;
§4.6's account of third-mode origin fails and D-8 reopens.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B

## D-2. Where does the stabilizing tendency live?

**One clause in your text decides the entire operational layer.**

### What your text says

In the rendering:

> "The rendering privileges stable actualisation … a consequence of the rendering being a finite
> process operating on an infinite ground."

> "the tendency **the rendering** exerts on what it is holding … the rendering performs continuous,
> unconscious work to maintain the configuration in its stable shape."

In the substrate — and note the slide happens **mid-sentence**:

> "Operations aligned with **the rendering's** stabilising tendency — those that accelerate what **the
> substrate's own spooling** would resume given sufficient time, restoring configurations **the
> substrate** was already moving toward — leave the practitioner's configuration aligned with the
> direction **the substrate** is going in its own tendency."

And in the Leap amendment:

> "Restorative operations are aligned with **the substrate's** tendency toward coherence: they do what
> the constitutive spooling would do, granted sufficient time."

### Why this is the seam

Everything upstream of that mid-clause slide is derivable. Everything downstream that routes through
"what spooling would do" — the operation-type taxonomy, the Coherence cost channel, Mending's
immunity, reality-strain, the Locked Zone account — is stipulated or contradicted.

### Two things kill the substrate version

**Your own constraint.** Spooling is defined as *"Passive, from the ground."* A spooling that "would
resume," was "already moving toward," or has a "tendency toward coherence" is **responsive to
disruption**. Your P-07 test forbids attributing "agency, intention, or responsiveness" to the
ground. Disclaiming *active resistance* does not help — the objection is to directedness, not to
force.

**Your own Locked Zones.** You write:

> "New threads cannot spool through regions where the fabric's structural integrity is broken."

So in a Locked Zone spooling would **never** resume the prior configuration, however much time
passed. On the substrate-located definition, **Mending torn substrate is not restorative** — and the
Edeyja's zero-cost lifetime Mending is ungrounded exactly where the setting needs it most.

### The options

**(A) In the ground's spooling.** Gives the taxonomy a cosmic backing: alignment means alignment with
how being itself proceeds.
**Cost:** the ground acquires a direction, which your own constraint forbids; and the Locked Zone
case fails outright.

**(B) In the rendering.** Two faces: for beings, the target's own unconscious self-maintenance; for
places, the equilibrium of the shared rendering.
**Cost:** "restorative" then depends on what a *rendering* would hold, which ties this to D-1. And
Mending must be re-described — not as "what would have happened anyway," but as **labour that
re-enables the conditions under which spooling can resume.**

### Argument for one option

**(B)**, with high confidence. The audit finds the taxonomy **underivable** on (A) and **entailed** on
(B), by two independent routes. §3.4 and §6.6 already take it.

**Note the gain:** on (B), Mending stops being "accelerating the inevitable" and becomes *completing
what the breach interrupted*. That is a better thing for your Wardens to be doing, and it is what
makes their work meaningful rather than merely faster.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B

## D-3. What was the Calamity?

**The only genuine contradiction left. It cannot be edited into agreement.**

### What your text says

The tensile account:

> "The Einhir's sustained precision Weaving over generations … pulling their own substrate tighter and
> tighter in pursuit of ever-greater coherence and civilizational refinement. Eventually, the
> structural limits of the substrate were exceeded. The fabric tore at the anchor points."

And, in the rule set: *"an over-drawing of the substrate — **not a spell gone wrong** but a
fundamental structural event."*

The inversion account:

> "It was caused by a foundational-scale manipulative operation that **inverted during execution** …
> The directional vector of the working flipped, converting the entire output toward maximum potential
> rather than actualisation. Every thread the network had accumulated released simultaneously."

### Why they cannot be merged

They put the cause on **different sides of the boundary**. The first is a substrate failure with no
event and no operator error. The second is an operator event — precisely "a spell gone wrong," which
your own rule excludes — and it locates the failure practitioner-side, in Coherence cost returning
through the network's knots.

They also use different names throughout: *Calamity* in one document, *Catastrophe* in the other,
neither acknowledging the other exists.

### A complication you should know about before choosing

The tensile account survives **only** on a specific reading of what "drawing taut" means.

If strain is caused by *use* of the substrate — drawing on it at density — then Mending at scale
strains too, and the claim that Mending could never cause a Catastrophe-equivalent event is false.

If strain is caused by **holding against** the rendering — the summed difference between what
practitioners hold and what the collective rendering holds — then restorative work cannot strain, and
the claim holds.

The second reading is the only one on which your taxonomy and your Calamity account cohere. **But it
requires editing one phrase:** "in pursuit of ever-greater coherence" must become something like *"a
refinement the rendering of their time could not hold."* A working aligned with what rendering held
could not have strained anything.

### The options

**(A) Tensile.** The Calamity is a consequence of scale. The forward warning — that a high-reach
Coherence-0 being reproduces it locally — holds literally and identically.
**Cost:** the inversion account goes; and the Lurianic breaking-of-vessels parallel weakens, since
that break comes at maximum intake rather than from fatigue.

**(B) Inversion.** The Calamity is an event with an operator error.
**Cost:** the "not a spell gone wrong" rule is struck; "identical in mechanism" for Coherence-0
beings weakens; and you owe a definition of "potential" as opposed to actualization, which the
foundations never supply.

**(C) Phased.** Strain as the precondition, inversion as the trigger. Genuinely available — but it
must be *stated* as phased, and "identical in mechanism" becomes "identical in precondition."

### Argument for one option

**(A) on the holding-against reading**, with medium confidence — medium because (C) is dramatically
better material and costs only a sentence of honesty. §8.1 provisionally states (A) and names the
event *the Calamity*, and flags the whole question inline.

**Either way: pick one name.** Two names for one event is the exact failure your own vocabulary
discipline warns about.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B ☐ C · **Name:** ☐ Calamity ☐ Catastrophe

## D-4. Own the reification, or re-scope "ontological"?

### What your text says

The claim:

> "The framework adopts Heideggerian terminology **in its correct orientation** … The
> thread-constitution beneath the rendered world — Being as such, the condition of possibility for
> beings — is ontological."

And, recorded in your own findings table: *"Heidegger inversion … Corrected."*

But the same thread-constitution is something you can *"actively touch"*; that is knotted, wrapped
and frayed; that has *"structural limits"* which are *"exceeded,"* tearing at anchor points in what
you insist is *"not a metaphor"*; that is perceived at scales; in which permanent knots *"form at
substrate depth"*; and of which you write *"places where being-as-such has failed."*

### The problem

A substrate with magnitudes, locations, tensile limits, that can fail *in a place*, is an entity — a
very fundamental one, but an entity. Treating the condition of possibility as the most fundamental
thing there is happens to be the exact move the borrowed distinction was formulated to diagnose.

Your text felt it — *"threads are both a noun and a not-noun"* — and then asserted correctness
without addressing it.

### What this costs you concretely

You currently have **two different accounts of Thread Sensitivity** and no statement of which it is:

- perception of a hidden layer — *"how much of the below-waterline Thread-substrate the practitioner
  can perceive"*; *"the world's actual constitution beneath the rendering's simplified presentation"*;
- a change in one's relation to one's own constituting — *"incrementally loosens the practitioner's
  commitment to the rendering as given"*; *"to feel the rendering as a process rather than inhabiting
  it as a given."*

These are not the same faculty.

### The options

**(A) Own the reification.** Keep the labels. State outright that the framework deliberately renders
the condition of possibility as a manipulable substrate — that this is its founding departure and the
premise of threadwork itself.
**Cost:** only the fidelity claim. But you keep using a distinction whose point you have discarded.

**(B) Re-scope.** Three tiers instead of two: **ontical** (the rendered world), **substrate**
(thread-constitution — deep, normally inapparent, and *ontic*), **ontological** (the register of
givenness: that there is rendering at all, that a configuration is held rather than given).
Sensitivity then has two components — ontic access to the substrate, and the ontological loosening —
and §5.5 already needs that split for independent reasons.
**Cost:** one section rewritten and the vocabulary table regenerated.

### Argument for one option

**(B)**, with medium-high confidence. §9.1 takes it and states (A)'s cost fairly. Both are honest;
the present arrangement — asserting correct orientation while operating the substrate as a layer of
entities — is not.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B

## D-5. Is the Leap dangerous in itself?

**This one may be dissolved rather than decided. Confirm you want the dissolution.**

### What your text says

Yes, inherently:

> "The retention roll at the end of a Leap … is a test of whether the always-already self-rendering
> successfully reconstitutes after suspension."

> "The Leap cannot occur without briefly suspending layer 2 — **this is why Leaps have a vulnerability
> window.**"

No, only type matters:

> "The practitioner's Coherence is not inherently reduced by restorative operations. Mending's
> Coherence cost is zero … The Edeyja's capacity to **Mend across lifetimes without cumulative
> Coherence degradation** rests on this alignment."

### The collision

If a Mending Leap still carries a retention roll, Mending is not zero-cost. If it carries none, the
universal vulnerability window is false.

### The dissolution

Deriving the failure mode gives a third answer that makes **all four source claims true at once**.

During suspension, the reflexive facing is not holding the configuration — and that facing is
precisely what holds it against environmental thread-forces. So for the Leap's duration those forces
act **unresisted**, and the configuration drifts in proportion to **environment × duration**, less
whatever others' rendering continues to hold. Then, at re-engagement, **knot feedback arrives**,
weighted by operation type.

Reconstitution fails when self-rendering cannot re-hold against the sum.

| Your claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Every Leap has a vulnerability window | **True** — the window is the unresisted interval |
| Mending's Coherence cost is zero | **True of the knot term** |
| Warden erosion is environmental, not operational | **Derived** — they Mend where environmental force is high, so that term is large while their operational term is zero |
| The retention roll tests reconstitution | **True** — it is where both terms land |

### What this gives you

**A Mending in a quiet village is genuinely free. The same Mending at a Gap margin is not**, and
nothing about the operation changed — only where it was performed. Danger becomes a property of
*place and duration*, not of the act.

### The options

**(A) Yes, inherently risky.** Every suspension can fail. Mending is low-cost, not zero.
**(B) No.** Only operation type costs. The universal window is struck.
**(C) The dissolution.** Risk is environment × duration, plus type × scale.

### Argument for one option

**(C)**, with high confidence. §6.8 takes it. It is the highest-value reconciliation in the audit and
it turns a contradiction into a formula.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B ☐ C

## D-6. Does ordinary self-rendering count as an "operation"?

### What your text says

> "the always-already rendering of one's own being is **a form of threadwork**: unconscious,
> automatic, constitutive."

> "**Every** operation produces Temporal Disjunction." / "all thread ops produce some TD, not just
> Past-Pulls."

### The consequence, by transitivity

If self-rendering is threadwork, and all threadwork produces Temporal Disjunction, then **every human
is continuously disjoined from their own history**, all the time, whether or not they ever touch a
thread deliberately.

### Is that absurd, or is it a thesis?

Consider what disjunction is: a gap between what is retained and what now factually was. For ordinary
self-rendering, the retained past is retentional, and the thread-past is what one's own rendering
continuously moves. So the two never coincide exactly — **because retention is itself a rendering
that moves what it retains.**

That is not absurd. It yields three things you already want:

- **Memory is reconstructive**, which gives "one does not cleanly delete a memory" a much deeper
  reason: no memory was ever a clean record.
- **Disjunction was already a magnitude** in your text ("the degree of disjunction varies"), so a
  non-zero floor is compatible; operational disjunction is the baseline magnified.
- **The Leap's disjunction becomes continuous with ordinary experience** rather than a foreign
  intrusion — which is what "the world becomes more real" needs.

### The options

**(A) Exempt it.** "Operation" means a deliberate layer-three act; "threadwork" is the wider term.
Clean, and §6.1 adopts this vocabulary regardless.
**(B) Include it.** Human being is constitutively disjoined from its own history.
**Cost:** disjunction mechanics must measure **excess over baseline**, or every person's disjunction
rises unboundedly across a life. Though "past-present mismatch grows with age" is not an absurd thing
for your world to hold.

### Argument for one option

**(B)** as a thesis worth adopting, with medium confidence — medium because it is a real addition to
what your world claims, not just a tidy-up. (A) is safe and available. The suite defines the
vocabulary either way and leaves the substantive question to you.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B

## D-7. What is Thread Sensitivity?

### What your text says

Capacity built:

> "They have exactly the perceptual architecture **they built** through confrontation."
> "understand Thread-level structures that **no human rendering can access**."

Constitutive finitude:

> "Its finitude is **not a limitation upon a potentially-unlimited capacity** but a constitutive
> feature of what the rendering is … an encounter the rendering's specification **was never going to
> include**."

### The collision

If sensitivity is capacity built, the cup grows and the finitude argument fails. If the cup does not
grow, "architecture built" and "no human rendering can access" need re-wording.

There is also a circularity: confrontation is defined as the mechanism producing sensitivity, while
sensitivity is said to provide the felt encounter confrontation requires. The first confrontation
cannot be registered by a faculty it is supposed to create.

### The resolution, from your own text

Your text already says what develops:

> "Each confrontation … incrementally **loosens the practitioner's commitment to the rendering as
> given.**"

That is not an enlargement of capacity. It is a change in one's relation to one's own constituting.
**The cup does not grow; the drinker stops insisting on the cup.**

And the circularity dissolves via the passive stratum: the first confrontation is registered
passively — as valence, somatic trace, fragments — before any sensitivity exists.

### The options

**(A) Capacity built.** Cost: the finitude pivot — the best passage you have written — weakens.
**(B) Loosening plus sub-threshold access.** Cost: re-word "architecture built" and "no human
rendering can access."

### Argument for one option

**(B)**, with high confidence. §5.5 and §9.1 take it, and the Leap's derivation independently supports
it.

**Two consequences to accept knowingly:** the trauma signature does **not** diminish with sensitivity
— what grows is equanimity, not immunity. And capacity for the surfeit is fixed for everyone,
forever, at every level of skill.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B

## D-8. Threadcut persistence, and what happened to Solmund

**Three linked questions with one underlying premise.**

### What your text says

> "Mode 3: continuous Thread work, **indefinite persistence**." / "no aging, no organic change."

> "**Deteriorates** because it lacks the continuous renewal that grounded beings receive."

> "Solmund is active for a generation or less before **his dissolution**."

### Question 1: do threadcut beings deteriorate absent a failure of maintenance?

Both source answers are underdetermined until you rule one premise:

> **Does rendering yield temporal depth, or does only spooling?**

If rendering yields depth, then a threadcut being's own continuous self-rendering accumulates depth,
and **it stabilizes with age** — needing less effort over time, not more. If only spooling yields
depth, rendering yields displacement without accumulation, and the being must work at undiminished
cost forever.

These give you very different immortals.

### Question 2: what dissolved Solmund?

A mechanism is derivable but is not an account. Others' rendering **holds** a third-mode being
(D-1(A)); so a change in how the community rendered him would weaken what held him. And the
essentialist reinterpretation you describe — the community reading his teaching through a theology it
could bear — is exactly such a change.

**That is suggestive and it is quite beautiful:** the Church's misunderstanding of him is what
dissolved him. But the framework does not establish it, and you should decide whether you want it.

### Question 3: did his presence strain reality?

Your own text says every layer-3-sustained being does:

> "A practitioner whose Coherence has failed and who has the perceptual reach to maintain themselves
> through deliberate layer-three threadwork is, **by structural necessity**, drawing on the substrate
> the way the Einhir site-network drew on it."

A threadcut being is the same profile minus spooling; the derivation is untouched by the difference.
**So Solmund strained the substrate for the whole generation of his presence — and the record notes
nothing of it.**

This needs an answer either way. If he did, the historical record should show it and does not. If he
did not, the reality-strain principle needs an exemption it currently lacks.

**Your rulings:**
Depth from rendering? ☐ yes (stabilizes with age) ☐ no (undiminished cost forever)
Solmund's dissolution: ☐ ceased work ☐ insufficient maintenance ☐ the community's re-rendering ☐ his own strain ☐ leave open
Did he strain reality? ☐ yes, record it ☐ no, exempt threadcut beings ☐ needs thought

## D-9. Who performs the contraction?

### What your text says

> "in Lurianic cosmology, the tsimtsum (contraction) **creates** the finite space in which the world
> can exist. The rendered world is analogous to this contracted space."

> "The rendering is the consciousness-performed apprehension of **a contracted region of Being**."

The verb has no subject. In the tradition it does: the Ein Sof performs its own withdrawal — and your
own constraint forbids that here.

### The options

**(A) Originary and uncaused.** The contraction simply is; asking what performed it is asking a
malformed question, like asking what preceded time.
**Cost:** nothing, and it is fully consistent with your epistemic charter. But it must be **said** —
at present the passive voice hides the question rather than answering it.

**(B) The rendering is the contraction**, performed from the finite side. Consciousness's finitude is
what contracts the infinite into a bounded region.
**Cost:** none against your constraint — the contraction becomes a fact about finitude, not an act of
the ground. And it pays a dividend: **threads become the line of continuity between the contracted
region and what lies beyond**, which is exactly what your own text already calls the anchors —
*"the points at which threads are continuous between the intelligible and the unintelligible."*

**(C) The ground withdraws.** Traditional fidelity.
**Cost:** violates your no-agency constraint, unless that constraint is scoped to the
post-contraction ground.

### Argument for one option

**(B)**, with medium-high confidence. §1.4 names it as strongest without committing. It is already
one word away in your text, it costs nothing, and it makes the anchors structurally meaningful rather
than a metaphor that happens to be load-bearing.

**Your ruling:** ☐ A ☐ B ☐ C

**(A)**, with medium-high confidence. It is what your §9 prophylaxis argument needs and never says,

## D-11. Name Derrida, or scrub the vocabulary?

### The situation

Derrida appears in none of the five documents' lineage. His machinery is throughout:

- **"Always-already"** — some twenty uses as a term of art.
- **The trace** — *"somatic echo retaining traces the mind cannot retrieve"*; *"the trace of exactly
  what it was not"*; the pulled memory's *"perceptible wound."*
- **The supplement, with its reversal** — layer 3 substitutes for a failed layer 2, and then the
  "original" turns out to be the same kind of act as the substitute. Your own text draws the
  conclusion — *"not a categorical difference in kind"* — and retracts it three lines later.
- **The aporia of the origin** — your regress of grounds.

And the central figure is his. *Thread, weave, texture, textile* and *text* share a root; the woven
text is his image for a structure with no outside. **Your cosmology arrived independently at the
figure he used for this exact problem.**

### The options

**(A) Name him.** Costs one paragraph owning the constituting/constituted doublet — that once the
condition of possibility is also the stuff, and the stuff is manipulable, the transcendental has
become empirical. Which is a real philosophical position, and stating it turns your Inseparability
Principle from an assertion into an acknowledgement: foregrounding is impossible because the thing to
be foregrounded *from* is the same thing.

## D-12. Which "Providence" survives — and should the word?

## 2. The calibration case, in full: Arc 1 — The Unworked Clause

**Premise.** King Almud will not touch the Einhir caste question because every component of the cost
exceeds its component of the gain, and because he has not decided whether acting is right. Confessor
Himlensendt accumulates Church Influence by pastoral competence nobody can object to. Neither man is
wrong. Their parallel restraint fills a vacuum neither intended, in the south, where nobody is acting
at all.

**The arc's own mechanics, verbatim:**

> *Primary mechanics: Almud Belief 2 (Sovereign Constraint), Restoration Movement ambient track
> (per-territory, Piety/Restoration unified track), Church Influence (CI) seasonal accumulation,
> Mandate thresholds, Public Instability (PI)*

### 2.1 What the design refuses, and where it says so

All five, by name, in four separate places:

| the arc's object | refused at | the refusal's stated reason |
|---|---|---|
| the RM **ambient track**, drifting "−0.5 toward the Restoration pole per season, passive" | `01` §6 | *"an unrest, loyalty or morale gauge on a settlement… derived from the grievance stances of the persons in it, or it is dead state that reads as mechanism"* |
| **Church Influence**, a faction-wide number rising +1/season | `01` §6, `07` §8 | *"a faction-wide reputation scalar… a single number destroys the case where the Church is loved in Himmelstift and hated three valleys south"* |
| the **seasonal accumulation** itself | `09` §8.3 | *"the only clock-driven quantities in the game are matter, bodies, and the confidence of a memory. Every social quantity… moves only when an act causes an event."* Enforced by P1's phase membership, not by discipline |
| **Mandate thresholds** and **PI** | `14` §10, `01` §6 | a mandate meter is refused; grievance-to-revolt thresholds are refused because *"a threshold would let the world revolt without anyone having decided to"* |
| the arc's **resolution trigger** — "one of the three systems crosses a threshold that forces the restraint to become a position" | `05` §5.3 | thresholds are preconditions; the design permits only postconditions |

Five for five. A naive reading returns *unreachable* and learns nothing.

### 2.2 The route to the same story, named person by named person

**Almud's restraint is not a Belief. It is the output of his own act menu, and it is dominated.** `05`
§1.1 gives one function for every person at every rung: `shortfall(p, prop) = urgency − reach`, where
`reach` is taken over the person's **view**. The King runs the identical computation as a hamlet
fisher; his menu is larger only because office adds entries to `own_acts`. The play-space exercise ran
this and verdicted him **THIN**: *"`issue` structurally dominated, `confer` moves `sovereign_fraction`
by zero, `convene`/`determine` self-referential, `revoke` near-empty — and his one non-dominated act is
BLOCKED at a venue convened by his rival."*

The arc says Almud is correct about the costs. The design computes something stronger: `14` §6 derives
a Crown that is *structurally* weak — *"a Crown that conferred few offices can revoke few offices,"* the
patronage cut unavailable against exactly the persons who matter, with levies, consecration, Hafenmark
and soldiers all things he **must ask for**. Almud's inaction is not a character trait dressed as a
mechanic. It is what his option set returns.

**Himlensendt's accumulation is likewise a dominance result.** The play-space finding on him:
*"`confer` dominates by shape: compounding gain and zero compounding cost, because with no
establishment he has no capacity an appointee's shadow standing can empty."* And `07` §7 supplies the
second half with no rule naming the Church: catechesis is an ordinary `tell` depositing an early,
general, high-confidence explanation into a child's ledger, foreclosing an unbounded family of later
inferences. *"Pastoral compassion and ethnic suppression are the same act because they are the same call
to `tell`."*

**The southern drift, which is the arc's hardest half, has an exact producer.** `05` §4 is a named man
with one seat and four candidate items, dropping a grain bundle **silent** because the judging set he
answers to is the Row's burghers and not the hamlet's forty-four, and because *"his ledger holds no
claim that the Southern Einhir hamlet can hurt him."* §4.1 then deposits grievance conditionally —
*when a backer learns*, through a telling that may be distorted or may never happen — and the telling's
grammar decides whether the grudge lands on Aldwin or on the praefecture. §8.1 flags suppressed rows
**dormant at full magnitude**, inheritable through the hearth's succession pointer, re-arming on the
next refusal so that *"each collision fires lower than the last. The accumulator does not reset, because
nothing resets it."* And §5.1: *"a hamlet with no cell, holding Yrsa Vossen's proposition at confidence
0.4 plus a fresh grievance whose referent that proposition addresses, produces commits. No recruiter
was present."*

That is the ambient track, produced with no track: grievance accumulating in named persons' ledgers
because no petition is ever carried, and commitments migrating as a result. **The arc's own sentence —
*"Not from any faction's action. From the absence of one"* — is the design's `drop`.**

### 2.3 What is lost, and it is the ending

The arc's last paragraph is its thesis: *"When the arc resolves, it will not be because someone decided
to end it. It will be because one of the three systems… crosses a threshold that forces the restraint
to become a position."*

**The design cannot do that, and it says so on purpose.** It has exactly one position-forcing object,
the **standing date** (`01` §5.3): a scheduled moment at which a prize is allocated, which makes a
proposition contestable *because both sides know when the argument ends*. `08` §5 makes both outcomes
bind and §11 resolves a sitting nobody attends. But a standing date requires **a convener who wants it**, and `05` §3.1 makes the agenda itself an act:
*"a man who can keep an item off the list for four sittings running has more power over Grauwald than
most of the men who vote on it."* In Arc 1, by construction, **nobody wants the position forced.** Both
principals are refusing to raise it and the vacuum is in a place with no carrier. The arc's own engine
guarantees the absence of the person its ending requires.

So Arc 1 in the new design runs forever, or ends when some third party — Baralta putting the Einhir
question on the Hafenmark agenda to embarrass the Crown, a Dicastery visitation, a broken granary door
in Goldenfurt — convenes it for reasons of their own. Arguably a better ending, because the grievance
then arrives with a name attached: `05` §10's *"Duke Magnus hears — for the first time, through a broken
door rather than through a petition — the proposition he would have granted in season 3."*

**VERDICT: REPRODUCED-BETTER, with its closure transformed.** Three authored objects removed (ambient
drift, a faction scalar, a threshold), the story arrives by composition, and both principals' restraint
is *computed* rather than asserted.

### 2.4 The calibration finding, stated so it binds the other seventeen

**The two acts that reproduce Arc 1 are the two acts the play-space exercise reports as design
defects.** Its D-8 names *"`confer` dominates for the Confessor… the realm-scope decree is dominated
for the King"* as dominance defects to fix.

They are Arc 1's engine. A King whose decree is dominated is a King who does not act; a Confessor whose
conferral compounds at zero cost is a Confessor who accumulates unimpeachably. **Fix both and Arc 1
stops being producible.**

This does not make D-8 wrong — dominance is genuinely bad for a *player* in that seat, which is what
the play-space exercise measured. It means the two exercises measure different things and disagree
here, and the disagreement is worth carrying: **a season that is thin to play may be exactly the
season that makes the history.** Where a dominated act belongs to a named NPC whose whole function is
restraint, dominance is the mechanism, not the defect. I record this rather than resolve it; it is a
design call, not a lane's.

## 3. Arcs 2–18

Each entry: premise · the arc's own mechanics line · what the design refuses and where · the route or

### Arc 2 — The Unseeing Eye · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Cardinal Klapp becomes Thread-sensitive because the Church put its best scholar in its most dangerous
archive and had no institutional reason to look at him.*

> *Primary mechanics: Klapp Thread Sensitivity growth check (Spirit TN 7, Ob 1), Inquisitor Exposure
> track, Church institutional continuity bias, Church Influence secondary effect*

**Refused.** The Exposure track: `07` §1.3 makes exposure *derived, never stored* — `Σ confidence ×
hostility` over persons holding a claim — and it *"rises only when an investigation spends acts… It
cannot rise on a clock."* Also TS growth as a per-season roll on a contact-hour counter, and CI.

**Route.** `07` §7 supplies the growth function:
`ts_gain(p, season) = κ(p) · Σ over unresolved anomalous claims c of confidence(c) · seasons_unresolved(c)`,
a claim being unresolved while the person holds no higher-confidence explanation entailing it. **Klapp is
the worst-placed man in Valoria on that formula:** his profession is accumulating anomalous claims he is
professionally forbidden to close, and his Conviction-primary is inquiry rather than faith, so the
catechetic general explanation resolves fewer of them for him (`03` §9's `admitting_share`). "Contact
hours" becomes *unresolved questions you are paid to keep open*. Nobody watches him because nobody holds
a claim prompting an investigation, and no clock will produce one.

**Cost: none.** Both gaps the arc flagged vanish — GAP-ARC-01 because the growth function takes no
attribute roll, GAP-ARC-02 because the Church's response is `Force(restrain, office)` at a Dicastery
convened on summons.

**Cross-exercise note.** Klapp is **THIN with a blocked core** in the play-space report — *"one dominant
act (`research`); everything consequential is gated on a `confer` he has no act to pursue."* Arc 2 asks
him to do exactly one thing: research. **A character who is thin to play can be a perfectly good arc
engine.**

### Arc 3 — The Hammer's Reserve · REPRODUCED

*Baralta holds the Sovereign Authority Doctrine for the perfect target and discovers the optimal window
closed two seasons before she chose it.*

> *Primary mechanics: Baralta Sovereign Authority Doctrine (once-per-arc Domain action, pool Mandate 7 +
> Reach 5 vs. Ob 4), Church Influence seasonal advance, Ehrenwall Coup Counter (0–3), Mandate threshold*

**Refused.** The Doctrine as a once-per-arc faction power (`07` §8 refuses faction verbs and action-point
pools; a faction's only channel to an act is `requisition`, an ask between two persons); CI; the Mandate
threshold; and the **Coup Counter**, the corpus's purest specimen of the banned object — it increments
when *"Crown Domain action = 0"*, and at 3 *"the coup fires at next accounting."*

**Route.** `07` §4.2 is the arc's thesis restated as an R-check: waiting *gains* and *costs*, and *"Power
is never invulnerable — only expensive to unwind, and the bill is paid by whoever waited too long."* Her
wait for *"a Church overreach she can name"* is §4's ideological basis, whose only cut is **a witnessed
hypocrisy** — *"hardest to obtain, cheapest to fire, irreversible."* She is waiting for the one cut her
target's basis admits. Her instrument survives without being a power: `14` §5 makes her **convener of the
Hafenmark Court Parliament and its veto-holder**, and `08` §10 names the veto's outcome,
`CARRIED-WITHOUT-FORCE`.

**Cost, and it is real.** Ehrenwall's count is gone and nothing replaces the *legibility* of a counter
ticking 0→1→2→3. What replaces the coup is better and less watchable: `12` §6.3, authority collapsing
through the claim ledger — *"One public refusal in front of sixty people lowers sixty people's
willingness on the next order… Authority collapses the way a bank run does."* The play-space report has
Ehrenwall as the roster's most contagious refuser and notes *"the King's only cut against her arms her."*
The Löwenritter still end the Crown, by declining, at a speed nobody can read off a track. **Not
REPRODUCED-BETTER, because a watchable countdown was a real thing the arc had.**

### Arc 4 — Vaynard's Wager · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Vaynard refuses to sanction or suppress Maret Uln, and three clocks that do not wait for him convert
his sophistication into abandonment.*

> *Primary mechanics: Vaynard Thread Investigation Track (TK, 0–5), Maret Uln starting loyalty (4),
> Inquisitor investigation procedure (Stage 1 Ob 3; Stage 2 Ob 4; Stage 3 Grand Debate), Varfell Intel
> stat, Vaynard Belief 3, Church Influence secondary effect at TK 3+*

**Refused.** The Varfell Intel stat (`07` §8: no faction stats, no faction verbs); the TK track; the
loyalty scalar. The arc is candid that its engine is a *missing* mechanic — *"Maret's loyalty began at 4
and has no mechanic to increase it"* — which the corpus's own analysis grades *"a design gap that the arc
relies on rather than a designed interaction."*

**Route, and it removes the gap.** Commitment degree (`07` §1.2) moves both ways on one operation.
Vaynard's inaction is not an ask, so it costs nothing on that edge — but it is available to Maret as **a
petition he can drop**. She holds standing at a duchy's court, supplicates for protection, he drops
silent, and `05` §4.1 deposits grievance **on him by name** because the telling names an actor. §5.1 then
makes her commitment to a rival proposition cheap in proportion to that grievance; the People's
Revolution contacts need no contact mechanic, because a proposition travels as a claim independently of
any member. The Inquisitor's three stages become `14` §5's **Dicastery of the Defense of the Faith** —
ENTER *"the accused, if summoned"*, sitting *"on summons, unscheduled"* — and the Grand Debate becomes
`08`'s stasis ladder with defeat by named fault. **Closure preserved:** a summons is a standing date and
it forces Vaynard's public choice.

**Cost / caveat.** The play-space verdict on canon's Maret Uln is RICH with a blocked core: *"her dual
loyalty is permanently stable, because the burden term protects it."* My reading is that the
petition-and-drop route sidesteps this — grievance is a stance row, not an edge — but Arc 17 hits the
same term from the other side and the two should be read together.

### Arc 5 — The Quiet Fracture · **LOST**

*The Church's Weaving is partially succeeding, consistently, at scale, which is worse — and the world is
quietly running down while everyone reports competence.*

> *Primary mechanics: Mending Stability (MS) baseline decay (−1/year, PP-255), Thread operations
> (cumulative MS cost), Church Influence passive advance (+1/season, PP-402), Weaving scale escalation*

**What blocks it, precisely.** The new design has **no Thread operations system and no world-state Thread
quantity.** Thread appears in exactly four places across the suite: `Thread-Read` as an investigation act
(`03` §6.1), Coherence as a cost on the operator (`02` §5), the TS ≥ 30 Knot gate (`02` §4), and
rendering-side facets in `witness` (`03` §9). There is no Weaving, no scale escalation, no Gap, no
Rupture clock, and nothing anywhere that an act degrades on behalf of the world.

**This is an omission, not a refusal, and the distinction is the whole finding.** `01` §6 refuses gauges
on containers because they are *social* quantities that should derive from persons' stances. A Thread
substrate's condition is not social; it is the same class as larders, harvests, bodies and prices — all
of which the design holds as world state and updates on a clock in **P1 SETTLE** (`09` §1.2), *"the only
phase that changes the world with no act behind it, restricted to metabolism."* A world Thread condition
would sit on that shelf without violating a single stated rule. Nobody wrote it.

**Consequence.** Arc 5's payload is unreachable end to end: *"the winters feel longer than they used to"*
has no producer, and the Truthseekers' ten-season trajectory has no trajectory. The design produces the
*epistemic* half of the theme — `07` §7, `03` §9 — but that is suppression of perception, not decay of the
world. **The world is not dying in this design. It is only being misunderstood.**

The corpus's analysis calls Arc 5 *"the most thematically essential arc in the game. If this arc doesn't
fire, something is wrong with the campaign."* Taking that at face value, this is **the most important
single result in the lane.**

### Arc 6 — The Debate That Won the Wrong Thing · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Hafenmark wins the debate, the Church concedes the principle and keeps the practice, and the Crown's
implementation fails in public.*

> *Primary mechanics: Debate system (Piety Track, Genre weights, Composure), Mandate stat, Stability cost
> on Domain action Failure (PP-403), CI Suppress (PP-402)*

**Refused.** The Piety Track (a 0–10 persuasion meter); genre weights (a faction-wide ethical-framework
multiplier — `07` §8's refusal of *"a leader as a flat bonus on a roll"* kills it by the same argument);
Mandate and Stability as faction scalars; Domain action as a faction verb.

**Route, and the fit is unusually clean.** The arc's subject is *the gap between a won debate and an
implemented one*, and `08` §10 gives that gap a name: **`CARRIED-WITHOUT-FORCE`**. The Church's narrow
concession is `08` §2 rung 3, *Quality* — it happened, it is that, and it was right — which concedes the
act and its label and **not** its justification, with the record row stating exactly what was conceded.
The arc had to hand-wave this (*"[EDITORIAL: ED-322 — exact language is a design decision]"*, i.e. a GM
wrote the loophole); the design computes it from where the Church chose to stand. The failed
implementation stops being a 35% roll and becomes `06` §3's **compliance contest** — `enforcer_presence`
*"zero if the issuer has no one to send"*, the local judging set's stance, distance — plus `06` §8's reach
cap. And "the implementation mechanism was now poisoned" is `12` §6.3's `obeyed_claims`.

**Cost: none identified.** One GM fiat removed, one faction multiplier removed, one meter removed.

### Arc 7 — The Army That Stayed · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Two Löwenritter units fail a 9% Discipline check; the Battle Leader cannot stand them down without
admitting the problem; a three-season political crisis follows from a dice accident.*

> *Primary mechanics: Mass combat (Discipline, Morale, Phase 6 Cascade), Institutional Pressure (IP),
> Faction Stability, Domain actions post-battle*

**Refused.** Discipline and Morale as unit stats; the persistent Morale condition; the Rally procedure;
IP; Faction Stability. `09` §5.1 refuses the layer at the signature: *"There is no army object and no
battle object… NEVER reads: a faction id · a faction military stat · a national strength number."*

**Route, and every link exists.** The rarity survives — `09` §2.4's variance-matched roll exists so that
*"the underdog never wins, the rout never happens"* is not the auto-resolve signature, and T-S requires
`cohort routed` to be producible at every fidelity. The persistent condition survives **as claims in the
men's ledgers rather than state on a unit**: `12` §6.3's `obeyed_claims` runs −3..+3 on the balance of
obeyed-versus-refused orders, and *"refusal is contagious through the claim ledger and nowhere else."* A
commander whose men broke in public risks his next order being refused in front of witnesses, and §6.3's
remedies — *"give small orders certain to be obeyed, to rebuild the claim balance"* — do not include
standing down publicly. The grain-buying and the camp expanding south are §6.4 steps 3–4: *"Their larder
is the wage."*

**Cost.** The arc's version was diagnosable off a unit sheet; the design's is diagnosed by counting whose
ledgers hold what, which is an investigation. I score it BETTER because the arc's own trap — *"he is
trapped by his own units' state"* — required a mechanical object no person in the world could see, which
is the shape `01` §6 rejects as *"dead state that reads as mechanism."* **Frame note:** the arc's Altonian
probe prices its gain in IP, and off-board pressure is the suite's declared open item (`00_INDEX` §3.3);
the aftermath, which is the arc, does not need it.

### Arc 8 — What the Guilds Know · TRANSFORMED

*Trade networks produce information as a byproduct; the Church wants the Guilds' ledger; the arc is what
they will trade it for.*

> *Primary mechanics: Faction stats (Wealth 6, Influence 4), Domain actions (Intel), nine political axes,
> CI Suppress eligibility, Stability costs*

**Two things break at once, and only one is the design's doing.** First, **"the Guilds" is not an
agent** — Jordan ruled 2026-08-30 that they are *"just a loose collective of economic factions"*, and `07`
§6.1 makes the same point structurally: an office cluster has no owning node, so *"the Dicastery decided"
is never expressible*, and *"the fiction must never render an institution as a speaker."* Arc 8's
protagonist and its decision-maker do not exist. Second, to the arc's credit, its best line anticipates
the design: *"The Guilds do not have an Intel stat… they do not need one."* `03` §6.1 generalises it —
*"Investigation's currency is the SAID row… There is no clue counter, no case object, no investigation
skill, and no threshold anyone sets."* The ledger the Church wants **is a person's claim ledger**,
literally, and getting it is `interview` (which leaks — it *"deposits `INTENDS(you, investigate X)`"*),
`research` (gated by an admission act held by persons with stances), a Knot's `counsel`, or theft.

**What happens instead, and the cost.** Five or six named economic actors — Feldhaus, Kessler, Grindvold,
Tallow, Bergvall — each hold their own proposition, and the Church negotiates with whichever it can
reach. One dramatic choice becomes a race in which one factor sells and the others find out. Better, and
different: the arc's question *"what will the Guilds trade it for"* is unanswerable because there is no
*they* to answer.

### Arc 9 — The Practitioner Who Stopped · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*A Truthseeker at Coherence 3 stops operating, tells nobody, and rations herself trying to finish
something before the transition takes the part of her that chooses.*

> *Primary mechanics: Thread Sensitivity accumulation, Coherence track (10→0), Mending Stability shared
> cost, PP-261 (Coherence 0 → Non-Player Character transition), Faction Stability*

**This is the one arc whose core mechanic the new design re-derives independently and keeps.** `02` §5.2:
*"0 **Severed** — You stop individuating. You return to cohort fidelity, cannot originate petitions,
cannot hold office. A person has become an object."* That is PP-261, reached from a different direction
(`09` §8.2's de-individuation is the same primitive that bounds the world's population) and given a
mechanism rather than a GM instruction.

**And the descent improves.** The arc has one cliff at 0 and nothing between. `02` §5.2 gives four bands,
each removing a *social capability* rather than applying a penalty: Dissonant (presented marks read at −1
confidence, so passing gets harder); **Fragmented (4–3), her actual state — at most two primary
Convictions, the third decaying, and one stance row per season losing its provenance and becoming
undefendable in argument**; Fractured (*"your tellings land at halved confidence… you may not `carry` a
petition"*). Her silence acquires a computed reason: `07` §4's standing is a support set, and a patron's
clients re-evaluate on the news. `08` F11 strikes all the grounds of a Fractured speaker, so she loses the
argument before she loses herself.

**Cost:** the MS-shared-cost leg, per Arc 5.

### Arc 10 — The Penitent Duke · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Baralta's motion forces the Church to excommunicate her or concede; Haelgrund's thoroughness buys her a
season of public penance; the reversal comes at Parliament.*

> *Parliamentary Manoeuvre Ob = Church Influence ÷ 2; Excommunication; Sovereign Authority Doctrine; PI
> adjustments; Almstedt (#8 — CONSERVATIVE), Haelgrund (#4 — PROCEDURALIST)*

**Refused.** CI÷2 as an obstacle; PI; the Doctrine; and **NPC flaws as mechanical modifiers** — `01` §6
refuses a trait vector, keeping only credulity and obstinacy, because *"a trait that duplicates a stance
is a second copy that can disagree with the first."* "PROCEDURALIST adds +1 season" has nowhere to live.

**Route.** `06` §1 carries `ExcommunicationTerm(person_or_faction)` as a first-class dispensation term —
*"strips Church-conferred marks and standing at every node in scope"* — with the Dicastery of Doctrinal
Adjudication as its venue and the Confessor holding the veto (`14` §5). Baralta's motion is a motion, put
by her as convener; the Church's forced answer is `08` §5's rule that both outcomes bind plus F3 (silence
when pressed) closing the sitting against a party that will not answer. Public penance is witnessed acts
moving stance rows in named judging sets, which is what "sympathy" should have been. **And Haelgrund's
flaw becomes an act:** `14` §5's Defense of the Faith sits *"on summons, unscheduled"*, its convener is
its Cardinal, and *"the convener holds the cheapest real power in the game."* A proceduralist who takes an
extra season is a man choosing when to sit, witnessably and attributably.

**A consequence the arc only gestured at.** `08` §10.1's Crown Succession Contest has five separately
proved articles, one being *"no prior conceded record."* Baralta's excommunication and any concession she
makes to reverse it become a **permanent citable row against her own Crown Claim**, and F2 makes it a live
hazard at every later sitting. Arc 10 becomes a computed cost against Arc 3.

### Arc 11 — Three Crowns in a Season · REPRODUCED

*The Crown falls; Strand holds the administration together for two seasons and then breaks; Brandt takes
the army to the border and leaves the interior ungarrisoned; the Church offers to mediate.*

> *Crown Stability → 0, elimination per PP-500, Political Vacuum (1 season), Löwenritter Coup Counter → 4,
> Strand OVERPERFORMER buffer, Laskaris flip at Elske Loyalty ≤ 2, IP*

**Refused.** Faction Stability as a hit-point track; **elimination**; the Political Vacuum token; the Coup
Counter; IP; Loyalty scalars; NPC flaws as timers.

**Route — `14` §2 is Arc 11 at higher resolution.** `vacate(office, cause)` writes **nothing** to the
world; four things become true by computation, and the suite corrected itself on the critical one:
*"compliance drops for each person as and when a claim of the death reaches them… The interregnum is not
abolished; it is **smeared along the channels**… and the map of where obedience has lapsed is exactly the
map of where the news has gone."* That is the Political Vacuum with the token deleted, and it mints an act
the arc never imagined: *"the household that keeps the Duke's body quiet for a season keeps his
dispensations enforced for a season."* Strand's buffer is `07` §5.2's **shadow standing** plus `14` §2.2's

`de_facto` returns nobody. Brandt's march is an order whose `will` computes fine and whose consequence
nobody ordered. And the arc never had this: *"the forged succession edict is not a mechanic. It is one lie
plus one compliance contest."* **Closure preserved by the calendar** — §2.1's point 4 opens a conferral
standing date at the container's horizon.

**Cost, and it is why this is not BETTER.** The Altonian half has no producer. Laskaris's sandbagging is
reproduced beautifully as a **non-act**, and the play-space report names the consequence: *"Sandbagging is
the absence of a spend, so it deposits nothing and cannot be investigated,"* and *"zero of eleven acts has
a Valorian container in scope."* The arc's payoff needs the off-board polity to act, which is `00_INDEX`
§3.3's declared open question.

### Arc 13 — Wait Until It Sings · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Almstedt does nothing extraordinary for eight seasons. Everyone who attacked has weakened themselves. His
Ministry, which never attacked, is the only intact governance mechanism.*

> *Almstedt CONSERVATIVE (+1 Ob on any Parliamentary structure alteration), PI recovery +1/season,
> Stability −1 on failed Domain action (PP-403), Ministry Mandate*

**Refused.** PI recovery — `09` §8.3 is categorical and enforced by phase membership: *"There is no phase
in which a restoring timer could run."* Also Stability, Ministry Mandate, and the flat +1 Ob flaw.

**Route — this arc is the design's own headline discovery.** `05` §3.1 makes `compose_agenda` an act by a
named convener over *"the petitions v HOLDS A CLAIM OF — not the petitions that exist"*, and `14` §5
concludes: *"The convener holds the cheapest real power in the game… Influence measured in volume of
things filtered, held by a person with no binding power at all."* The play-space exercise verdicted Peder
Almstedt RICH with the line **"`compose_agenda` reaches more outcomes per act than any of the King's six
remit acts. The design serves the clerk better than the King."** Arc 13 is that sentence, written in
2026-04, and the design supplies the counterweight the arc lacked: `05` §9 fork (d) — *"A perpetual
dropper ends as a man with a seat and no constituency."*

**Two costs.** PI recovery is refused, so his value can no longer be that his inaction *heals* the polity;
it must be that his seat is the one nobody contests, which is smaller and truer. And **closure lost**: the
Kingmaker's Moment fires on a compound state trigger (*2+ factions at Stability ≤ 2 AND PI ≥ 5 AND
Ministry Mandate ≥ 2*) with nobody deciding. Here somebody has to come and co-opt him — a `commit`, or a
`requisition` priced by `07` §1.2 — and if nobody does he goes on composing agendas forever, which is
arguably the more frightening ending.

### Arc 14 — The Purple Shroud · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Public Instability reaches crisis, the Church seizes territory, the streets erupt, and the Church offers
to restore order at a price that determines the world that follows.*

> *PI ≤ 2 crisis threshold, Church CI 75 Territorial Seizure, Church offer at CI +5 plus a permanent

**Refused.** The PI threshold — which the arc never had (GAP-ARC-14: *"PI crisis threshold… not

**Route.** `12` §4 is the arc's central event with the threshold deleted: *"no riot object exists. A riot

outsiders,"* worth 0.44 of every man's willingness. §1.3's `Press > Hold` settles it at resolution, never
as a precondition on anyone's decision to rise. The Church's offer becomes a `Dispensation` plus
conferral, and Himlensendt's `confer` dominance means his price is **always offices** — CI +5 restated as
a move on `14` §6's `sovereign_fraction`, except that you can name every office in it.

**One authored object refused outright.** The arc's `THE SOUTHERN VOICE` card supplies *"a forced Einhir
consequence"* per resolution option — a table of CV deltas. The design refuses to author consequence: `12`
§4's `warrant` *"changes nothing physical. It changes what witnesses deposit."* What follows a suppression
is `05` §8.1's dormant rows at full magnitude with the suppressor's name in the re-arm predicate.

### Arc 15 — The Banker's War · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Feldhaus funds both sides of a conflict he profits from; Strand detects it and is fed curated data; Almud

**Cost:** GAP-ARC-15's Solberg track was never built and nothing replaces it; the play-space report has

### Arc 16 — The Empty Fort · REPRODUCED-BETTER

*Vossen spreads the Restoration thin — five territories at one Presence each — because every marker the

> *RM Presence markers, AP accumulation, martyrdom CV −1 on successful Presence removal (ED-NEW-1, flagged

## 4. Which arcs are blocked by a character the design cannot motivate

The play-space exercise found **19 of 55 characters with a blocked core**. Eleven of my eighteen arcs
are driven by one of them. The result is not what I expected:

| arc | driver with a blocked core | does the blocked core block the arc? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Almud** — *his one non-dominated act is BLOCKED at a venue convened by his rival* | **No — it IS the arc.** His inability to reach the Einhir question is Arc 1's premise |
| 2 | **Klapp** — *gated on a `confer` he has no act to pursue* | No. Arc 2 needs him to research, his one dominant act |
| 3, 10 | **Baralta** — *zero of eleven acts touches article 3 of her own victory condition* | No for Arc 10; **yes, downstream, for Arc 3**, whose stakes are the Crown Claim article 3 blocks |
| 4, 17 | **Maret Uln** — *dual loyalty permanently stable* | **Partly.** The petition route reaches Arc 4; Arc 17's standoff may never tighten |
| 11 | **Laskaris** — *zero of eleven acts has a Valorian container in scope; sandbagging cannot be investigated* | **Yes, for the Altonian half.** The cascade survives; the pressure does not |
| 12 | **Feldhaus** — *her canon revelation arc cannot be delivered by any deposit path* | **Yes, and precisely.** Found independently by both exercises |
| 16 | **Vossen** — *`tell` dominates; she cannot carry, remonstrate, hold a Knot, or start a clock* | No. Arc 16 is a `tell`-and-spread arc |

**The finding.** *A blocked core blocks an arc only when the arc's payload is the blocked want itself.*
Where the arc asks the character to do the thing that dominates their season, a THIN or blocked
character is a perfectly good engine — Klapp and Vossen both. Where the arc's dramatic question **is**
the unreachable want, it dies: Feldhaus's revelation, Laskaris's report, Baralta's article 3.

**Three arcs take real damage** (3 downstream, 11 half, 12 at its centre) and one is at risk (17). The
other seven cited above are unharmed. So the two exercises' findings compose rather than multiply, and
the play-space report's instruction — *"for each of the nineteen, either supply an act whose object is
the character's stated want, or accept explicitly that the want is unreachable"* — should be read with
this filter: **the wants that matter most are the ones some arc's ending depends on.**

## 5. Does the threshold refusal cost stories?

**Yes, three times in eighteen, and the losses have one shape.**

The design has exactly one position-forcing object: the **standing date** (`01` §5.3, `05` §3.1, `08`
§10, `14` §5). It is a *calendar* trigger held by a *named convener*, not a *state* trigger. `05` §5.3
defends the distinction with three falsifiers, of which the strongest is that no signature in the engine
has return type "revolt" and `force`/`hold` never appear in a precondition — they are computed by
`resolve`, which already takes the world, to settle a fight that is already happening.

Sorting the eighteen arcs by what their ending was:

**Closure survives — 13 arcs — because their ending was already a sitting.** The examination (2, 16),
the summons (4, 10, 12), the court's implementation (6), the Year-End review (15, 18), the conferral
date after a vacancy (11), the reckoning (14), the confrontation Vaynard schedules (17), the Rally the
commander does or does not order (7), Coherence reaching 0 (9). The corpus, it turns out, mostly closed
its arcs at institutional dates. Those all survive intact and several improve, because a date has a
convener and a convener can be bought, delayed, or killed.

**Closure is lost — 3 — and every one of them ended at a counter with nobody deciding.** Arc 1's
"one of the three systems crosses a threshold that forces the restraint to become a position." Arc 3's
Ehrenwall Coup Counter reaching 3, at which *"the coup fires at next accounting."* Arc 13's Kingmaker's
Moment firing on a three-clause state predicate. In all three, the design's answer is: **somebody must
convene it, and if nobody wants to, it does not end.**

**So the honest cost is not "arcs do not resolve." It is narrower and worse:**

> **The design cannot resolve an arc whose premise is that nobody wants it resolved.**

That is exactly Arc 1, and it is not a coincidence that Arc 1 is also the corpus's best arc. The
threshold's real job in these documents was not to be a gauge; it was to be **the world's own agency** —
the one actor in the fiction who is not a person and who can therefore act when every person has a
reason not to. Delete it and the world becomes strictly reactive to persons, which is the design's
stated goal and also a genuine loss of one kind of story: the kind where the situation, not anybody,
runs out of patience.

**What partly substitutes, and should be counted before declaring a loss.** Three mechanisms let the
world press without anyone deciding to:

1. **P1 SETTLE** (`09` §1.2) — larders empty, bodies age, harvests fail. Material need is the one
   pressure that rises with no act, and `05` §1.1 turns it directly into petitions. Arc 1's south goes
   hungry whether or not anyone attends to it.
2. **Confidence decay** (`09` §7, mechanism 3) — *"You lose Goldenfurt by being forgotten."* An
   office-holder who does not act is not punished by a counter; his ward-holders simply stop asserting
   that he decides anything, and his share redistributes. Almud's restraint has a slow cost after all,
   and it is memory rather than instability.
3. **Dormant rows re-arming at magnitude** (`05` §8.1) — the accumulator that does not reset, held per
   person, inherited. This is the closest thing to the arcs' ambient tracks, and it is strictly better
   because you can name everybody in it.

None of the three *forces a position*. All three make the position more expensive to hold. That is a
defensible trade and the design makes it deliberately; it should be reported as a cost that was paid,
not as an oversight.

## 6. What the corpus wants that the design has no object for

Four recurring demands, ordered by how much of the corpus turns on them.

**6.1 A dying world.** Arcs 5 and 9 need Mending Stability; Arc 12 needs Thread-touched harm; the
corpus's own analysis calls this the game's central theme and Arc 5 the arc that must fire. The design
has **no world-state Thread quantity at all**. As §3's Arc 5 entry argues, this is an omission and not a
refusal: the design already holds non-social world state on a clock in P1, and a substrate condition
belongs there beside larders and bodies. **This is the lane's principal recommendation.**

**6.2 An off-board power that presses.** Arcs 7, 11 and 14 all price their stakes in Institutional
Pressure — Altonia leaning on the peninsula. The design has no producer, and `00_INDEX` §3.3 already
names it as open: *"Altonia and Schoenland exert real pressure from off the map. 'Generate a person' and
'allow an actorless pressure' are different games."* The arcs are evidence on that question and they
point one way: **three of eighteen arcs use off-board pressure as a term, and none of them uses an
off-board *person*.** What the fiction wanted was weather, not an ambassador. That does not settle the
question — an actorless pressure is exactly the shape §6 refuses everywhere else — but it is the corpus's
vote and it should be recorded.

**6.3 Something between a flaw and a stance.** Every arc in Batch 03 runs on documented NPC flaws —
OVERPERFORMER, PROCEDURALIST, CONSERVATIVE, PROFIT-MAXIMISING, EXTERNAL THREAT FIXATED — each of which
is a *behavioural commitment that persists against the person's own interest*. The design refuses a trait
vector (`01` §6) and keeps two scalars. In practice this worked better than I expected: PROCEDURALIST
became an act by a convener, flattery-vulnerability became credulity, OVERPERFORMER became shadow
standing. **But CONSERVATIVE and EXTERNAL THREAT FIXATED did not convert** — Almstedt's blocking and
Brandt's border fixation are dispositions no stance row quite holds, because they are stances toward a
*procedure* and toward a *class of threat* rather than toward a referent. The design's stance table takes
*"persons, factions, propositions and places"* as referents. **A procedure is not in that list, and
Almstedt is made of one.** Whether "the Parliamentary rules of order, as they stand" is a proposition is
a real question the suite should answer, because two of the corpus's richest NPCs need it to be yes.

**6.4 A legible clock the players can watch.** Arcs 3, 11, 13, 14 all get their table tension from
players watching a counter approach a number. The design publishes **bands with every input visible and
the trigger point never** (`07` §4.2, `09` §3, `12` §9.3), which is a considered answer to having no GM
and is probably right. But it changes the pleasure on offer from *dread of a known number* to *unease at
an unreadable one*, across four arcs. That is a deliberate design position; it is also the single most
frequently-relied-on thing in the corpus that the design chooses not to give.

## 7. Discipline notes — where this lane could be wrong

- **I have graded thirteen arcs REPRODUCED-BETTER, which is a suspiciously good result.** The
  mitigations: the corpus's arcs are *narrative* specifications and the design is an unusually complete
  *substrate*, so a match on story is easier than a match on mechanism would be, and I have marked
  mechanism NO nearly everywhere. Against that, I have not run anything — none of this executes, per
  `00_INDEX` §0 — so every "the design produces X" here is a reading of a document, not a measurement.
- **The strongest single result is a LOSS (Arc 5), and I have weighted it accordingly.** If the lane's
  finding were "everything reproduces," it would not be worth having.
- **Two arcs I nearly scored NEVER-WORKED and did not** — 8 and 12 — because in each case a real half of
  the arc works and discarding the whole would have hidden a result. Both are marked with their unbuilt
  legs inline. A reviewer who disagrees should move Arc 12 to NEVER-WORKED and re-read §4's Feldhaus row,
  which stands either way.
- **The Arc 1 calibration finding contradicts the play-space report's D-8** and I have not resolved it.
  That is deliberate: whether a dominated act is a defect depends on whether a player is sitting in that
  seat, and the two exercises sampled different seats.
- **Roster collisions were not adjudicated.** Maret Uln and Gerik Strand carry two incompatible lives
  across canon and the suite (play-space FINDING 1); I used canon's person for Arcs 4, 17 and 18 and
  flagged it where it changes the reading.

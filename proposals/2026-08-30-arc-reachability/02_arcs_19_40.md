| verdict | n | arcs |

Scored on the brief's two layers:

| | MECHANISM: can the design run the stated chain? | STORY: is the outcome reachable with nobody authoring it? |
|---|---|---|
| YES | 0 | 13 |
| PARTIAL | 2 (37, 39 — the TS registration floor survives almost verbatim) | 7 |
| NO | 20 | 2 (22, 27) |

**Twenty of twenty-two arcs have their mechanism refused, and twenty of twenty-two arcs still tell
their story.** That is the headline, and it is a win for the design of a kind the brief predicted:
the old machinery was scaffolding on all but two arcs, and on nine of them the scaffolding was
actively costing the story its causation.

**The losses have a shape, and it is not the shape the brief expected.** They are *not* concentrated
in ambient drift, faction scalars, or GM fiat — those three are replaced, and usually improved on.
Every loss and every downgrade in my range traces to exactly two absences:

1. **There is no world-side Thread substrate.** The design puts the Thread entirely on the person —
   Thread Sensitivity as a registration floor (03 §9), Coherence as a cost (02 §5, 10 §7),
   Thread-Read as one investigative act (03 §6.1). There is no Thread Tension, no Mending Stability,
   no Gap, no site, no operation vocabulary beyond reading, and no entity. Doc 02 §5.1 defers
   "Thread ops per doc 04" — and doc 04 is *The Hearth and the Community*, which owns none of it.
   **That is a dangling reference, not a refusal**, which makes this the most fixable loss in the
   exercise. It costs arcs 22 and 27 outright and downgrades 26, 28, 29, 30, 33, 35 and 39 —
   **nine of my twenty-two arcs**.
2. **An off-board polity cannot act.** The design names this open (`00_INDEX.md` §3.3;
   `15_adjudications.md:375`). It costs the Altonian invasion arm of arc 20, the Schoenland pivot's
   consequence in 25, and the entire mechanical content of 38.

Ambient drift, by contrast, comes back *stronger*: arc 36's tithe loop, arc 35's Cardinal, arc 37's
two half-cases and arc 40's five-way convergence are all better in the new design than in the old,
because the old ones were tracks and the new ones are people.

## 2. How I scored, and the one place I departed from the brief

The brief's discipline binds and I followed it: the arcs are not sacred, a deliberate refusal that
costs no story is a success, and an already-broken arc is not evidence.

One departure, stated so it can be overruled. **Arc 19 is scored NEVER-WORKED even though its story
is reproduced.** It is a rules exposition rather than an arc — no engine NPC, no narrative outcome,
and its pivot is a Game Master's private ruling — so scoring it a win would inflate the tally with a
document that was never a story. Its story is reproduced anyway, and I say so in the entry.

Two corpus artefacts a later reader will trip over. **"Institutional Pressure" (arcs 20–27) and
"Invasion Pressure / IP" (arcs 36–40) are the same track under two names**; and arcs 20–27 use
"Church Influence" in prose while their own mermaid nodes say `TC` and `CI` interchangeably. Neither
affects any verdict; both are evidence that the corpus was renamed mid-life and never swept.

## 3. The arcs

### Arc 19 — The Accounting Sequence · **NEVER-WORKED**

*Premise:* two factions are under Stability pressure in the same season; the strict accounting order
means your remediation arrives one phase after the check that needed it.

*Its own mechanics:* "Seasonal accounting strict order (Domain Echo outcomes → Stability checks →
clock drift → floors/ceilings → CP award) · Anti-death-spiral floor (Stability 2 = Ob 4 regardless of
actual pressure) · Seasonal cap (±2 per stat) · Zero-sum Stability intervention · Domain Echo from
personal actions reaching faction scope."

*Refused, and where:* the faction Stability scalar and the anti-spiral floor are both banned by
substrate §6 ("an unrest, loyalty or morale gauge… derived from the grievance stances of the persons
in it, or it is dead state that reads as mechanism"). The Domain Echo's pivot is explicit GM fiat —
"GM determines at accounting whether each personal action reached faction scope" — and there is no
GM.

*Why NEVER-WORKED rather than LOST:* no narrative outcome and no NPC. Its own "arc shape" line
concedes it — *"Runs continuously across every season — it is the underlying mechanism of all faction
arcs."* That is a rules chapter with a mermaid diagram.

*The story is reproduced anyway, and better.* The design's tick (09 §1.2) is a strict seven-phase
order with the same content and none of the fiat: **P1 is the only phase that changes the world with
no act behind it, and is restricted to metabolism**, so nothing social drifts; the zero-sum is **one
act per season**; and P6 WITNESS means you do not learn what your act did until claims come back —
arc 19's "players may not know the Echo fired until the accounting sheet is read", derived rather
than adjudicated. **What arc 19 wanted, the design supplies structurally — and D-2 threatens it**
(§5).

### Arc 20 — The Tutoring Demand · **TRANSFORMED**

*Premise:* Altonia demands Prince Torben for education at its court. Almud yields and Torben returns
a stranger, or refuses and the army comes.

*Its own mechanics:* "Institutional Pressure threshold (40 = Tutoring Demand) · Torben Loyalty
Clock · Institutional Pressure acceleration on refusal (1.5×) · Institutional Pressure 75 invasion
trigger · Border Pass (Territory 4, Altonian entry point) · Ehrenwall Coup Counter."

*Refused:* all five. IP is a faction-wide scalar that "accelerates regardless of player intervention";
the loyalty clock is a −1/season drift with no actor; 75 is a threshold that fires an invasion with
nobody deciding; the Coup Counter is stored state on an NPC.

*The design's route.* Torben-as-collateral is not an analogy — 06 §7's collateral table names him
("Hostage / fosterage … Prince Torben at the Altonian court"), and that section's worked trace *is*
Branch A: Alexios issues a local dispensation stripping Torben's honoured-guest mark, and Torben's
`opening_set` recomputes under house arrest. The loyalty decay needs no clock, because **his address
changed**: STANDING (02 §6) reads his siblings-in-container, now Altonian courtiers; COMMITMENT reads
a view assembled from claims that reach him, through channels Alexios controls (03 §8). *Torben
becomes Altonian because that is who is in the room and who is allowed to write to him.* Elske's
counter-move is 05 §6.4 intercession at rank gap 3.

*What it costs, and it is real.* The invasion arm has no route. IP 75 requires Altonia to act, and
an off-board polity acting without a person is the design's declared open question. So Branch B
stops at "the Guilds turn against the Crown" — which *is* reachable, as several small economic
factions whose members' subsistence and exposure needs recompute under a closed trade route — and
cannot proceed to the vanguard at Schoenland. **Ehrenwall's decision at the border is reproduced
better**: her Löwenritter is an office cluster whose conferral root is herself and whose warrant runs
to Crown-as-institution, "which is why she may lawfully refuse him" (14 §4). No counter needed.

*Conferral:* needs the **office-rooted** branch. Under person-rooted, the oath names a person and her
lawful refusal is unintelligible.

### Arc 21 — The Excommunication · **REPRODUCED-BETTER**

*Premise:* Cardinal Olafsson excommunicates Duchess Baralta on charges that are technically accurate.
Success spikes Church power; failure makes her a martyr and puts the Church on the defensive.

*Its own mechanics:* "Excommunication unique action (§8.3) · Baralta Church Influence suppression
condition (Mandate ≥ 5) · Church Influence +4 on Baralta excommunication · Church territorial seizure
at Church Influence 60 · Martyr effect (failure = Church Mandate −1, target +1) · Grand Debate
reversal (5 exchanges)."

*Refused:* Mandate and Church Influence are faction-wide scalars (substrate §6: *"a faction-wide
reputation scalar … destroys the case where the Church is loved in Himmelstift and hated three
valleys south"*), and CI 60 is a threshold.

*The design's route, and why it is better.* Excommunication is already in the design's dispensation
list (substrate §5.2): it travels by being noticed, distorts on the way, and lands as a per-person
compliance contest reading each person's stance toward *the issuer* (06 §3). **The martyr effect
stops being a rule and becomes an observation** — witnesses deposit divergent predicates, so Baralta
is simultaneously stronger in Hafenmark and weaker in Himmelenger, which a ±1 Mandate swap cannot
express. The reversal venue is a parameter row (Doctrinal Adjudication: floor G3, admissible source
*its own registers only*, Confessor's veto), and "no Sed Contra for the accused" is the `speak`
column of 14 §5. The arc's key sentence — *"The Church does not have to do anything else that season.
The accounting machinery does it for them"* — is what the design refuses, correctly: every seizure
now costs a named person an act at a venue against a holder who contests it.

*What it costs.* Pace. The arc's two-season seizure cascade to Valorsplatz needs four to six Church
acts in one season, and the Church has roughly five named persons, each with one act (§5). The
cascade becomes a decade, not two seasons. I count that an improvement in truth and a loss in
tempo, and the design should own it rather than discover it.

*Blocked:* the seizure half needs `sovereign_fraction`, which **D-6 says is undefined over every
Church office because the Church's conferral graph is a cycle.**

### Arc 22 — The Ceiral Ritual · **LOST**

*Premise:* years of prerequisites converge on one Weaving roll that either heals the wound at
Askeheim permanently or tears the outer winding and puts a Mode 3 entity in the room.

*Its own mechanics:* "Ceiral Ritual (§6.5) · Degree table (Overwhelming/Success/Partial/Failure) ·
Thread Tension/Mending Stability consequences · Mode 3 monstrous entity on Failure · Lead
practitioner incapacitation · Southernmost territory development (Overwhelming only) · Expedition
prerequisite cascade."

*What blocks it, precisely:* **the design defines no Thread operation other than Thread-Read, no
world-side Thread state for an operation to damage, no site, and no entity.** This is not a refusal —
nothing in 01 §6, 07 §8 or 12 §10 refuses it, and 02 §5.1's "Thread ops per doc 04" is a pointer to a
document that does not own them. It is an unwritten subsystem.

*Already partly broken in its own terms.* The Partial branch carries `[EDITORIAL: are there other TS
60+ practitioners in the campaign?]` and the Failure branch carries `[EDITORIAL: what does this
entity want?]`; arc 33's gap log records that Mode 3 stat blocks "are not in the current compilation."
So the arc's most consequential branch had no rules when written. **The Failure branch is
NEVER-WORKED; the arc as a whole is LOST**, and it would be dishonest to score the whole thing as
evidence against the new design.

*What survives:* the political consequence layer, and it survives well. "Observable Thread activity
that TS 30+ observers across the peninsula perceived" is the registration floor exactly (03 §9:
`P(register rendering facet) = 0 if TS < floor`). The design can express *who saw it*; it cannot
express *it*.

### Arc 23 — The General Falls · **REPRODUCED-BETTER**

*Premise:* Grandmaster Ehrenwall is wounded in a mass battle. One Medicine roll at Ob 2 decides
whether the army has a general next turn, and her death does not trigger the coup — it eliminates it.

*Its own mechanics:* "General two-stage incapacitation (§8.9) · Stage 1: Coherence Rating halved,
Morale floor suspended · Stage 2: −2 Morale outside cap, Coherence Rating = 0, all units uncommanded ·
Rout contagion brake · Leader defeat capture mechanic · Faction Military stat consequences ·
Ehrenwall as specific NPC."

*Refused:* Coherence Rating as a commander scalar, Morale as a unit scalar with a floor, and the
cap/contagion apparatus. 12 §3.1 refuses commander bonuses by arithmetic: *"a flat shift of size X is
worth X / (0.671·√Pool) standard deviations… a commander bonus is worth 2.5× as much to a weak side
as to a strong one — backwards from every intent anyone has when they add it. So commanders add
nothing."*

*The design's route is the same story with the numbers deleted.* A commander chooses a **gambit**
that adds acts to the option set and substitutes the pool source on the φ fraction only. Officers are
**channels**: `transmit(None) = 0` — *"a section with no living officer does not receive the gambit at
all. It performs its own default, chosen by its own cohesion: C ≥ 0.5 → hold, C < 0.5 → break."* That
is Stage 2, derived from a channel going dead rather than from a Coherence Rating set to zero — and
better in the way arc 23 cares about: *"Killing an officer makes a section deaf, which is why the
enemy shoots at officers, which is why officers die."* Promotion is derived too: the section promotes
the person holding highest regard among his own peers.

*Her death.* Arc 23's cleverest claim — *"the counter was hers, not the institution's. Without
Ehrenwall, there is no coup"* — needs no counter in the design. 12 §8's death cascade fires: the
patronage cascade voids, "one death fanning out into N individual demotions," and clients who had
clients **spin off as independent standing-holders**, which is the Löwenritter fragmenting. 14 §2.1
adds the thing arc 23 never thought of: her standing dispensations keep their terms and lose their
complier **at telling speed**, so *"the household that keeps the Duke's body quiet for a season keeps
his dispensations enforced for a season."* Arc 23's power vacuum becomes a map of where the news has
travelled.

*What it costs:* the single named dramatic roll. Survival is a per-person roll inside the casualty
step (12 §3.3), not a Phase-5 Medicine window everyone at the table watches. That is a table-feel
loss, not a story loss.

*Conferral:* office-rooted, for the same reason as arc 20.

### Arc 24 — The Faction That Breaks · **TRANSFORMED**

*Premise:* a faction's Stability reaches 0 and it fractures along its pre-existing fault lines. The
Church fractures three ways; the Guilds fracture into locality.

*Its own mechanics:* "Stability 0 = collapse event · Anti-spiral floor · Faction collapse
consequences differ by faction · Seasonal cap ±2 · Mandate floor at 0 → Faction Fracture · Mending
Stability spontaneous Gap generation during instability."

*Refused:* the whole apparatus, by substrate §6 and by 07 §8's refusal table (no tier, no scalar, no
merge/split/promote — *"`commit` in two directions"*).

*Branch A is reproduced, and reproduced better.* A schism is *"a subset of members whose commitment
migrates to a faction with a rival proposition"* (substrate §1.4) — no schism mechanic. The Templar
secession is a conferral-root conversion (14 §2.4, §4), a contestable act at a venue rather than a
consequence of a number hitting zero. Olafsson-as-freelance falls out of the epistemics: **his files
are rows in his own ledger**, so losing the office loses the warrant and keeps the intelligence,
exactly as the arc says. And Himlensendt keeping theology while losing enforcement is the design's
default statement about him — 14 §7: *"he holds no establishment of his own. He binds nobody by
presence. His entire power is conferral plus a veto at one venue."*

*Branch B is void, and not by the design's fault.* Jordan ruled 2026-08-30 that the Guilds are *"just
a loose collective of economic factions"* with no Council. There is no Guildmaster Council to
fracture, so "Economic Leverage becomes unavailable because it requires a unified Council" describes
an object canon has deleted. The story survives in a weaker form — several small economic factions,
each individually too small to hold a person who can act at the node it needs (07 §10.2, *"A large
faction that cannot act"*) — but it is a different story, and the four-season reconstruction race is
gone with the Council.

*Blocked:* Branch A's engine is **Jarnstal, the hardest BLOCKED verdict in the gap report** — *"no
act whose object exists at all"* — and canon never seats him (Finding 4). The secession can be
described and not performed.

### Arc 25 — The Schoenland Pivot · **TRANSFORMED**

*Premise:* a neutral trading power reads Valoria's political condition off the surface and aligns
with it or against it. Its read is not the players' read.

*Its own mechanics:* "Schoenland spoiler actor (§8.10) · 'Pro-war, anti-conquest' orientation ·
Diplomatic path (stable Valoria = naval ally, Institutional Pressure −2/year) · Fragmented Valoria =
Altonian alignment · Institutional Pressure threshold acceleration · Sea route."

*Refused:* IP −2/year is an ambient scalar; "Valoria reads as stable" is a read of true faction state.

*The design's route is its centre of gravity.* Substrate §1.3 forbids any read of the true profile
and supplies exactly one legal object: **an estimated profile computed from one observer's own claim
ledger.** *"Every observer therefore holds a different estimate of the same faction, and a covert
membership is absent from an estimate until somebody's claim names it. Underestimation is not a
special case; it is the default."* Arc 25's entire premise — the outsider misreads you and it costs
you — is the design's default behaviour rather than a spoiler actor's special rule. The passive
intelligence leak ("any Intelligence order placed in Schoenland reveals results to Altonia
automatically") is 03 §8's correspondence filtering, the servant who outranks the ministers.

*Why TRANSFORMED rather than better.* The arc says *Schoenland decides.* The design cannot let a
polity decide, so the decider becomes **Solberg** — Schoenland's trade factor in Valorsplatz, whom
arc 38 names and the gap report verdicts RICH on the strength of the channel he holds. His reports
are `tell`s whose `as_asserted` need not equal what he holds, which is the design's lie, performed at
a place, witnessable, traceable. That is a *better scene* and a *different story*: the pivot is now
one man's career self-interest, and Schoenland never quite knows what is happening on the peninsula.
The consequence — what Schoenland then does — still needs the off-board question answered.

### Arc 26 — The Duke Awakens · **REPRODUCED-BETTER**

*Premise:* Vaynard's undetected Thread Sensitivity crosses its Discovery Event. Success makes him the
campaign's most valuable ally; failure makes him its most capable actor working from wrong premises.

*Its own mechanics:* "Vaynard Thread Sensitivity 14 (Dormant) · Discovery Event trigger · Spirit
check TN 7 Ob 1 · Success: TS → 30 (Stirring), world reorganises · Failure: Certainty −1, new Belief
from ignorance · TK track consequences · Private Collection use adds to hidden Thread Sensitivity."

*Refused:* the Discovery Event as a GM-called check, and object-driven TS growth (there is no rule by
which handling a thing raises sensitivity).

*The design's route is a genuine improvement.* 07 §7 gives TS growth a real generator — `ts_gain`
accrues from **unresolved anomalous claims**, where a claim is unresolved if the person holds no
higher-confidence explanation entailing it. **Read that against Vaynard and the arc writes itself.**
He is the man who must have an account of everything; a man like that *resolves his own anomalies*,
and obstinacy resists re-opening them. His sensitivity is suppressed by the exact trait that makes
him formidable, and the person most likely to cross the floor is the one who can tolerate not
knowing. Better than a die at Ob 1, and nobody authored it.

*The failure branch is reproduced better still.* "A man who had a profound experience and constructed
an entirely wrong explanation, and will act on it effectively with his resources" is the design's
signature claim: substrate §3.2 — *"the true value is not in the decision path at all… there is no −2
and no cap. A well-placed lie can move a duchy."* Arc 26 needed a special rule to make wrong belief
consequential; the design cannot make it *in*consequential.

*Blocked:* **D-3.** `need(commitment)` and `need(exposure)` have no formula, and for a magnate the
other two return zero, so **100% of Vaynard's motivation is uncomputed.** The arc runs; its engine
has no motor.

### Arc 27 — The Dissolution · **LOST**

*Premise:* a practitioner chooses the operation the game warns about. On Failure a Gap tears open in
the room the scene was happening in.

*Its own mechanics:* "Dissolution operation (Threadweaving v2.5 §2.4) · Degree table (Overwhelming →
MS −3, micro-Gap; Success → MS −5, Gap 1 scene; Partial → Shifting Object; Failure → full Gap, MS −8,
Monstrous Incursion immediately, practitioner Incapacitated) · Coherence cost · Mode of Monstrous
entity · Political scene consequences from Thread event visibility · Axis 9 activation."

*What blocks it:* the same absence as arc 22 — no operation, no substrate to damage, no entity. The
Coherence cost is the one term that survives (10 §7: *"every Thread attempt costs −1 drift; a
contested Thread op costs −2"*), which makes the absence sharper rather than softer: the design
prices an operation it does not define.

*What survives, and it is the arc's best half.* "The micro-Gap's brief appearance is the political
problem… perceived, not identified" is the registration floor plus divergent witnessing, verbatim. A
Thread event in a Parliamentary chamber deposits a rendering-side facet only in the sensitives
present, and in everyone else's ledger it degrades to *"the nearest referent the hearer does have"* —
03 §9's *"religious poetry, produced with no suppression anywhere in the pipeline."* The Church's
consolidating response is then ordinary politics. **The consequence layer is reproduced better than
the arc wrote it; the cause has no expression.**

### Arc 28 — Vaynard at Coherence 0 · **TRANSFORMED**

*Premise:* the reckoner reaches complete understanding, remains entirely himself, and can no longer
be received by anyone he cares about.

*Its own pivot:* "Final operation crossing Coherence to 0 — the threshold at which his rendering
exceeds human limits." The file opens with an **Ontological Correction**: *"Coherence 0 does not mean
losing the ability to render… their rendering has expanded past the boundary of what human
consciousness can contain. More has become intelligible to them. Not less."*

*What the design does with Coherence 0, and this is the finding.* **The new design has re-adopted the
exact ontology this document was written to retract.** 02 §5.2: at Coherence 0, *"Severed — you stop
individuating. You return to cohort fidelity, cannot originate petitions, cannot hold office. A
person has become an object."* 10 §7 says the same in other terms: *"0 Severed (Thread Pool locked to
zero)."* Both are loss of capacity. Arc 28's whole premise is that this reading is wrong.

*The story survives on a different mechanism, and survives well.* The incommunicability is 03 §9,
done better than the arc did it: a rendering-side claim's subject is a configuration, *"a referent
class for which a non-sensitive's ledger has no address,"* and degradation happens **on every deposit
path**, so *"give him the sensitive's own written testimony, in the sensitive's own hand, and nothing
changes."* Arc 28's central image as a type conversion. The gap report reached the same place from
the other side: **TS is non-monotone, and the Warden-Chief at the highest living TS is "structurally
inaudible."** The design already contains a person in arc 28's position.

*What is specifically lost.* Two things, and they are precise. **(a) Expansion.** There is no state
in which a person's capacity grows past human limits and remains usable; the design's high-Thread
career terminates in de-individuation on a stated timetable (10 §7: Severed in ~10 seasons of routine
use). **(b) Calibration.** Arc 28's most beautiful claim — *"he can identify exactly what each person
can and cannot receive, and he shapes his communication accordingly… no more, no less"* — has no act.
The design's degradation is automatic and the speaker has no control over it. A person cannot choose
to transmit optimally-lossily; the ledger decides what arrives.

*Blocked:* D-3, as arc 26.

### Arc 29 — Almud at Coherence 0 · **TRANSFORMED**

*Premise:* the king who spent a reign holding incompatible things together can finally see past all
of them, and can no longer govern — because governance is a human-scale instrument.

*Its own pivot:* "Coherence 0 during active reign — a king who now renders past the limit of what
kingship means." Branch B vacates the throne.

*Mechanism:* refused, and refused twice — by the Severed band (above), and because the arc's Branch A
requires him to keep signing decrees while understanding them differently, whereas Severed explicitly
strips the capacity to hold office.

*The story splits cleanly.* **The cause is unreachable; the consequence is reproduced better than any
other succession treatment in the corpus.** 14 §2.1: every standing dispensation keeps its terms and
loses its complier *at telling speed* — *"the interregnum is not abolished; it is smeared along the
channels, arriving in the capital in three days and in the fjord hamlets in six weeks, and the map of
where obedience has lapsed is exactly the map of where the news has gone."* And 14 §2.2 supplies what
every succession arc here needed and none had: **no interim and no regent object.** A claimant seals
a paper and has a crier read it — a `tell` with `as_asserted = "by remit of the praefecture"`, an
ordinary lie whose discovery flips legitimacy retroactively because the ledger row names its
source.

*Blocked:* Almud has `goals: null` (Finding 2) and canon contradicts his Thread Sensitivity — registry
TS 28, timeline TS 0 (Finding 3, #4). The second is fatal to arc 29 specifically, because the arc
requires TS 28 → 30. **This arc cannot be run without ruling that contradiction**, and per the brief
I am not adjudicating canon.

*Conferral:* needs **office-rooted**, and needs it most. Person-rooted conferral means that at the
moment Almud dies, every office he conferred terminates at a dead man and the graph below him is
unrooted — arc 29 Branch B taken to absurdity, and `sovereign_fraction` undefined across the realm at
exactly the moment the game is about it.

### Arc 30 — Lenneth · **TRANSFORMED**

*Premise:* the queen who taught herself a dead practitioner's understanding from a 180-year-old
document reaches it, keeps her networks, and directs them with a precision the scholars she funds
cannot account for.

*Its own pivot:* "Coherence 0 while hidden networks still active."

*Mechanism:* refused as above.

*What is reproduced, and it is most of the arc.* The concealment half is fully expressible and needs
no new object. Secret alignment is standard (substrate §1.2, "membership is per-person, may be
secret"); 07 §1.3 owns secrecy as a property of who holds a claim rather than of the secret; the
funding is patronage, whose cascade and fission the design owns (06 §6, 12 §8). "Scholars begin
producing research that addresses exactly the gaps the Southernmost recovery requires; they do not
know why their work is suddenly this precise" is a patron's directed contributions plus the
recipients having no claim naming the director — clean. The archive document's persistence is 08
§4.1's instrument grade: reading it deposits `firsthand(the record says X)`, never `firsthand(X)`.

*What is lost:* the same two things as arc 28 — expansion and calibration — plus one specific to her.
Arc 30 Branch B's climax is two Coherence-0 beings communicating *without asymmetry*: "they can say
everything." The design's predicate vocabulary is **closed at fourteen forms** (03 §1.1), and none of
them can carry the content in question; two sensitives transmit perfectly *within the vocabulary*
(P-13's corollary), which is not the same as saying everything.

*Blocked:* the gap report's line on her is exact — **"Queen Lenneth is the richest character in her
lane and her stated goal needs a verb she cannot reach."**

### Arc 31 — The Quaestio of Baralta · **REPRODUCED-BETTER**

*Premise:* Church Influence reaches a number Baralta had set in her head; she invokes the Sovereign
Authority Doctrine; a Grand Debate follows and an operative is found dead in the corridor mid-sitting.

*Its own mechanics:* "Church Influence threshold (§7.2), Grand Debate (§9 / debate_system_redesign_v1),
Löwenritter Autonomy (§8.9), intelligence broker covert Network (§8.7), Personal Combat (§8), Mass
Battle (mass_battle_v3), Thread → Mass handoff (§11.3)."

*Already partly dead in its own terms.* The file's own header note: *"Arc 31 and Arc 34 reference
systems dissolved after this file was generated… retained as pre-dissolution illustrations of
emergent structure only — do not treat their mechanical seeds as current canon."* Niflhel was struck
by Jordan; the covert network is gone; so is the assassination arm.

*The debate is reproduced far better.* 08's argument system beats the corpus's on every axis that
matters here. The **stasis ladder** gives the argument terrain rather than a persuasion track;

## 4. Conferral: what the succession and coup arcs demand

Six arcs in my range turn on who confers what. They do not split evenly, and the answer they give is
one-sided.

| arc | the operation it needs | branch required |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Ehrenwall lawfully refusing the King at the border | **office-rooted** |
| 23 | the Löwenritter surviving, and fragmenting, when its Grandmaster dies | **office-rooted** |
| 24A | the Templars converting their conferral root away from the Confessor | **office-rooted** |
| 29 | the throne vacating without unrooting every office below it | **office-rooted** |
| 34 | military authority ceded "pending a settlement of the succession" | **office-rooted** |
| 21, 31, 40 | `sovereign_fraction` read over Church offices | **neither branch resolves — the cycle** |

**Every succession or coup arc I have needs the office-rooted branch, and none of them needs the
person-rooted one.** The mechanism is the same each time: person-rooted conferral makes the
interesting move *free*. Under it a Templar order secedes by simply ceasing to name a man (arc 24A
loses its drama), Ehrenwall's refusal is unintelligible rather than lawful (arcs 20, 34), and Almud's
death unroots the graph below him in the same instant (arc 29). The design's own note in
`00_INDEX.md` §3.2 cites the Löwenritter warrant as its evidence for office-rooted; **arcs 20, 23,
24A and 34 are four more independent instances of the same evidence, from fiction written years
earlier.**

**On the cycle (D-6), the fiction says something the audit did not.** The Church's graph closes on
itself — 14 §2.3's own table gives *"Confessor, in conclave | the four Cardinals | three of four,"*
and the Confessor confers the Cardinals. The realm's root then needs an external warrant which 14
§3.3 says *is* Church consecration, so the Crown's root points into the cycle. Both my Church-power
arcs (21, 31) and both my sovereignty arcs (34, 40) run into it.

But 08 §10.1 has already turned it into play:

> *"If the Church's own succession is contested, there is no determinate custody, so no ground can be
> graded G4, so article 3 cannot reach the floor for any claimant. The sitting cannot close on the
> merits… CARRIED-WITHOUT-FORCE, for whichever claimant has the seats."*

**The undefined sovereignty query is already rendered as a playable deadlock with a citable record
row.** So the fiction's advice on D-6 is not "break the cycle." It is: **rule that the cycle is
intended, keep it, and treat `sovereign_fraction` being undefined over Church offices as a
first-class political condition** — which *is* the Consecration Crisis, which is arc 21's Branch A and
arc 31's second act. That is a third option the gap report's "three-way question" framing has room
for and does not name.

## 5. The act economy at the arcs' stated pace

The contradiction is real (D-2): 09 §1.1 says *"every person and cohort commits exactly one act per
season"*, and 14 §8 works a season in which Duke Vaynard performs ten. Against my range:

**It does not block the multi-exchange arcs, and I expected it to.** 09 §1.3 resolves it: *"inside a
contest the tick subdivides, opening a nested loop of exchanges."* So arc 21's five-exchange Grand
Debate, arc 31's Quaestio and arc 32's tribunal are each **one act**. The debate arcs are safe under
either reading — which removes the objection I went in expecting to make.

**It bites in exactly three places, and each is instructive.**

1. **Sustained per-season maintenance.** Arc 20 Branch A needs covert contact with Torben *every
   season for six seasons*. Under one act, that is one person's entire life for eighteen months.
   Probably correct — an intelligence handler should be a full-time occupation — but it means the arc
   cannot be a side-thread for anyone doing anything else.
2. **Institutional tempo, and this is the sharp test.** Arc 21's seizure cascade reaches Valorsplatz
   "within two seasons" while the Church is also running a Grand Debate, a Heresy Investigation and a
   Templar deployment: four to six Church acts in one season, against roughly five named churchmen.
   **Under one act each, arc 21's two-season cascade becomes a decade.** Under the ten-act season it
   runs at the arc's stated pace. The two readings produce campaigns whose institutional clock speeds
   differ by roughly a factor of five.
3. **Arc 19's dilemma exists only under the one-act reading.** Its content is *"a Domain Action is one
   action… the other faction's Stability check fires regardless."* One act per season **is** that
   dilemma, generalised and made honest. **The ten-act season deletes arc 19 entirely** — a magnate
   who acts ten times a season never has to choose which faction to save.

So the recommendation my range supports: **keep one act per season, price the ten-act worked season
as the error, and expect institutional arcs to run several times slower than the corpus assumed — and
say so in canon rather than discovering it.** Arc 40 is the positive case (five persons, one act
each, a convergence nobody coordinated); arc 19 is the proof that relaxing it costs a real dilemma;
arc 21 is the honest cost.

## 7. The threshold question, answered

**The design can force a position. It has four devices, none of which is a threshold, and they are
sufficient for the institutional arcs and absent for the world-track ones.**

| device | where it lives | which arcs it terminates |
|---|---|---|
| **the standing date** | substrate §5.3 — *"both sides know when the argument ends"* | 21, 24A, 31, 32, 34, 40 |
| **the named fault / force-close** | 08 §3 — *"force-close is the normal ending. Most arguments end because somebody was caught doing something that has a name"* | 21, 31, 32 |
| **the larder** | 04 §1.2, 12 §7 — a siege is *"a clock made of two larders"* | 36; the material half of 24B, 25, 40 |
| **death** | 12 §8's fixed cascade | 23, 29; the succession half of 20, 34 |
| *(a postcondition, not a device)* `Press > Hold` | 12 §1.3 | 34, 36 |

**Which arcs have no terminus.** Exactly the ones whose old terminus was a world track crossing a
band: 22 and 27 (Thread Tension / Mending Stability), 33's return spike, and 20/38's Invasion Pressure
75. **In every one of those cases the missing thing is not the threshold — it is the quantity the
threshold was reading.** The design did not remove a forcing device from those arcs; it removed the
subsystem, and the device went with it.

So the answer is narrower and better than the question feared. **The refusal of thresholds costs no
story in my range.** What costs stories is that two subsystems are absent — one unwritten (the Thread
substrate), one openly undecided (the off-board polity) — and both will need forcing devices when
they arrive. The design's existing answer is available and good: **a site with a standing date is a
deadline, and a ritual is a prize allocated at one.**

## 8. What the corpus wants that the design has no object for

Five demands, ordered by how many arcs make them.

1. **A world-side Thread substrate — nine arcs (22, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30, 33, 35, 39).** Thread Tension,
   Mending Stability, Gaps, sites, an operation vocabulary beyond `Thread-Read`, entities. The design
   has the Thread entirely on the person and nothing in the world, and its own dangling pointer (02
   §5.1, "Thread ops per doc 04") shows this is an omission rather than a refusal. **Score it
   *unwritten*, not *lost*.**
2. **An off-board polity that acts — four arcs (20, 25, 34's Altonian arm, 38).** The design names it
   open. **Arc 38 is fiction-side evidence for "generate a person"**: its own content is that the
   pressure was always two men.
3. **A terminal state for a person whose understanding exceeds transmission — three arcs (28, 29,
   30).** The design's Coherence 0 is Severed: de-individuation into a cohort, "a person has become an
   object." The arcs' Coherence 0 is expansion, and their file opens by *retracting the reading the
   design has adopted*. Two incompatible ontologies, and the arcs are the later document. Somebody
   should decide which one Valoria has; the arcs make a case the design has not answered.
4. **An act whose object is an article or a doctrine — three arcs (21, 26's TK track, 31).** Baralta's
   Sovereign Authority Doctrine and Vaynard's Thread Knowledge track are both "a position a person
   advances over years." Whether propositions plus motions compose into an act whose *object* is the
   article is the open tension flagged at arc 31.
5. **Calibrated lossy transmission — three arcs (28, 29, 30).** *"He shapes his communication for
   exactly what each listener can hold — no more, no less."* The design's degradation is automatic and
   speaker-blind; a speaker cannot choose to transmit optimally within the hearer's referent space.
   Small object, large payoff, and the one place my range would ask for a mechanism if the brief
   allowed asking.

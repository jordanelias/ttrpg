# Arc reachability — synthesis and adjudication

## Status: ADJUDICATION (2026-08-30) — Fable 5, over three lanes, ~83 scored arcs from a 55-arc corpus.
## The lanes could not see each other. Where they converged, that is the strongest signal available.

**MECHANISM: NO on essentially the entire corpus.** Not one causal chain survives intact in lane 3;

**The scaffolding hypothesis is confirmed.** The old machinery — ambient tracks drifting with no
actor, faction-wide scalars, thresholds firing with nobody deciding — was scaffolding around stories
that survive without it, and usually improve. That is the headline, and it is a strong result for the
new design.

## 2. The one hole, confirmed three times independently

**There is no world-substrate quantity.** No Mending Stability, no Gap object, no Rupture condition —
nothing representing the state of the Thread substrate the setting's metaphysics rests on.

Three lanes reached it by three different routes:

| lane | route |
|---|---|
| 1 | Arc 5 is the single genuine LOST in the band, and the corpus's own analysis calls it *the arc that must fire* |
| 2 | doc 02 defers Thread operations to "doc 04" — which is **Hearth and Community** and owns none of it. **A dangling pointer** |
| 3 | five of seven LOST arcs die on it, and doc 09's otherwise-exhaustive refusal list **does not name it** |

**This settles it as an omission, not a refusal.** Every other absence in this design is argued for by
name. This one has a broken cross-reference pointing at where it was supposed to be.

And lane 1 supplies the argument that it belongs: the spine refuses gauges on containers **because
they are social** — a substrate's condition is the same class as larders and harvests, **which the
design already ticks** in the settle phase. *The shelf exists; nobody wrote the object.*

**Consequence stated plainly:** in this design the world is not dying. It is only being
misunderstood.

## 3. Conferral is answered — from the fiction side

The gap report left conferral open as a three-way question and named the Löwenritter warrant as its
single piece of evidence for the office-rooted branch.

**Lane 2 found four more, each independent, from fiction written years earlier: every succession or
coup arc in its range needs office-rooted conferral, and none needs person-rooted.** Person-rooted
makes the interesting move *free* — a Templar order secedes merely by ceasing to name a man, a
Grandmaster's refusal becomes unintelligible rather than lawful, and a king's death unroots the
entire graph beneath him in one instant.

**Adjudication: office-rooted, on the evidence.** Five independent fictional demands plus the warrant
is as much as this question is going to get short of Jordan ruling it.

### 3.1 And the Church cycle should be KEPT, not fixed

I filed the Church's conferral cycle as a defect that broke the sovereignty query. **Lane 2 supplies a
third option my framing did not contain: the design has already turned the undefined query into
play.** A contested Church succession means no determinate custody, which means the deciding article
is ungradable for every claimant, which means the sitting closes carried-without-force. **That is the
Consecration Crisis** — and it is one arc's Branch A and another's second act.

**Adjudication: rule the cycle intended.** Treat an undefined sovereign fraction over Church offices
as a first-class political condition rather than an error to repair. My defect filing was wrong.

## 4. The threshold question, answered precisely

Lane 1 got the sharpest form by adding a **closure axis** the story axis was hiding:

- **Thirteen arcs end at a scheduled sitting** — an examination, a summons, a year-end review, a
  conferral date. **Every one survives**, because the design has standing dates with named conveners
  who can be bought, delayed or killed.
- **Three arcs end at a counter reaching a number with nobody deciding.** All three lose their ending.

Lane 3 draws the same line from the other side: every threshold gating **a person's decision** is
replaced by something stronger; every threshold **reporting a world state** is lost — and **the loss
belongs to the variable, not the threshold.**

> **The honest cost: the design cannot resolve an arc whose premise is that nobody wants it
> resolved.** The threshold's real job in this corpus was to be *the world's own agency — the one
> actor that is not a person.*

Three mechanisms partly substitute — material need in the settle phase, confidence decay (*you lose a
settlement by being forgotten*), dormant rows re-arming — but none **forces** a position. They only
make holding one more expensive.

**And nothing measures whether confrontations arrive at all.** The old corpus guaranteed convergence
by authoring it: a 35-row trigger inventory, a season-by-season Convergence Timeline. The new design
guarantees only that *if* crises converge, a standing date forces them to fight. There is a
recoverability check and **no convergence check**. That is the sharpest unmeasured claim in the suite.

## 5. Where the two exercises disagree, and my adjudication

**Lane 1's calibration finding is the most uncomfortable result of either exercise:**

> The two acts that reproduce Arc 1 — the Confessor's `confer` compounding at zero cost, and the
> King's dominated decree — are **the two acts the play-space gap report files as design defects
> (D-8).** Fix them, and Arc 1 stops being producible.

Lane 1 declined to resolve it and recorded that *a season thin to play may be exactly the season that
makes the history.* That is right, and the resolution follows from Jordan's own definition.

**Adjudication: R is a test on the PLAYER's choices, and I applied it to NPC seats. That was a
category error in my gap report.**

Jordan's criterion is *"the thing offers choices with real impacts **for the player**."* An act that
dominates an **NPC's** menu is not a robustness failure — **it is characterisation.** The Confessor
*should* accumulate relentlessly and without deliberating; that is who canon says he is, and the
design reproducing it from his stance table rather than from a script is a success.

**D-8 is therefore narrowed, not withdrawn:**

| act | verdict now |
|---|---|
| the Confessor's `confer` | **characterisation, not a defect.** Leave it |
| the King's dominated decree | **a defect only if the player can be the King.** If the crown is playable, a dominated flagship instrument is a real R failure; if it is not, it is the same characterisation |
| Vossen's dominating `tell` | same test — she is a plausible player seat, so this one stands as a defect |
| inspired avowal unpriced | stands. That is a missing price, not a characterisation |

**The general rule this yields, which the gap report should have carried: apply the R test at seats a
player can occupy. Everywhere else, a dominant act is a portrait.**

## 6. The finding that joins the two exercises

Lane 2, and it is the most useful sentence either exercise produced:

> **A blocked core does not stop an arc from running. It stops it from terminating.**

A magnate has ten verbs and a duchess eleven, and none of what they do is aimed at what they want.
Combined with the threshold refusal, the result is **arcs that continue indefinitely** — which is
exactly the failure mode §4 predicts from a different direction. Twelve of lane 2's twenty-two arcs
run on such a character; lane 1 counts eleven of eighteen.

**This is why the blocked-core disposition matters more than its small backlog suggested.** Four
characters behind three edits is a cheap fix — and the cost of *not* making it is that a majority of
the corpus's arcs never end.

## 7. Two agreements reached independently, worth banking

- **The act economy: keep one act per person.** The fix lane reached it from coverage data (an empty
  establishment, not a small remit, predicts a thin office-holder). Lane 2 reached it from fiction:
  **arc 19's entire dilemma exists only under one-act**, because one act per season *is* "you can only
  save one faction," and a ten-act season deletes the arc. Two routes, same answer.
- **Feldhaus's undeliverable revelation.** Found by a play-space lane and an arc lane separately —
  the strongest single convergence across the two exercises.

---

## 8. What I would look at next, in order

1. **Write the world-substrate object.** It is the only confirmed hole, it is an omission rather than
   a decision, and it costs the arc the corpus itself calls the one that must fire.
2. **Rule conferral office-rooted**, and rule the Church cycle intended.
3. **Decide whether the crown is a playable seat.** It settles D-8 and it is a question about the
   game, not the engine.
4. **Add a convergence measure.** Not a threshold — a check that the world produces collisions at all.
5. **Calibrate the `requisition` obstacle's `burden` term.** Lane 1 flags it as doing more dramatic
   work than any other coefficient in the suite, and neither exercise has tested it.

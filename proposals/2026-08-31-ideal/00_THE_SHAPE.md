# THE IDEAL DESIGN SYSTEM AND ITS CODE SHAPE
## Status: DIRECT ANSWER (2026-08-31) to Jordan's question, from what the exercise established.
## Not a reconciliation. Not a defect list. **From scratch.**
## Cross-document contradictions in #342 (two pool formulas, two practice ranges, three signature
## spellings) are **not design findings** — they are artifacts of a seventeen-document suite and they
## do not exist in a single document. They are dropped here and not counted as work.

## 1. What the exercise actually established

Strip the process and six things survived attack. Only these are load-bearing.

| # | what survived | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The entry is already right.** An untrained attempt is legal and *"just a small pool"*; rank **adds** verbs rather than constituting the list; the postless fisher's shortfall arithmetic is **identical to a Duke's**; *"nothing consults post."* | The design's low end is not broken. **Do not fix it.** This was the session's largest false finding and its collapse is a positive result. |
| **2** | **The failure is TERMINATION.** Stories end at a scheduled sitting or they do not end. 13 of 18 arcs in one band close at a sitting and survive; 3 close at *a counter reaching a number with nobody deciding* and lose their ending. | The design starts things well and cannot stop them. |
| **3** | **There is exactly ONE position-forcing object: the standing date** — and it has a named convener who orders its items. Where everyone with power to convene prefers a matter unresolved, it stays unresolved. Falsifier run: five candidate forcing mechanisms checked, **none forces**. | One forcing object is not enough for a world that churns. |
| **4** | **Nothing anyone does degrades anything held in common.** No act damages a shared thing. | A whole story class — many rational private acts making everyone's practice worse, including the actor's — is unreachable. |
| **5** | **A want with no act whose object it is** does not stop a story running. **It stops it terminating.** | Blocked cores are a *termination* defect, not a content defect. Same disease as 2 and 3. |
| **6** | **`:219`** — *a personal effect on a group that is not a fraction of that group* is forbidden, and the design's own gloss is **"fractions rather than flat amounts, option-set changes rather than modifiers."** | This is the best rule in #342 and it is the constraint the world-substrate proposal failed. **It is not an obstacle to route around. It is the shape.** |

**Findings 2, 3, 4 and 5 are one disease.** Termination requires a moment where a matter is *decided*,
and the design has one such moment, reachable only by a person who wants it reached.

## 2. The ideal design system

### 2.1 What stays, because it earned its place

- **One actor: a person.** No faction acts. No leader modifier. No world agency.
- **Two structures over the same persons:** **containment**, a strict single-parent tree
  (Person → Hearth → Community → Settlement → Territory → Province → Realm); and **alignment**, an
  unconstrained set system where a faction is a proposition plus a commitment map. A faction is any
  aggregate at any scale, and scale is derived, never a field.
- **Three signatures.** `choose(person, view) → act` — **no world parameter.**
  `resolve(acts, world) → events` — **no person parameter.** `witness(person, event) → claims` —
  per-person; a consensus broadcast is a type error.
- **One resolver, one roll, one obstacle.** Everything else calls them.
- **Containers hold three things:** a stake, a judging set, standing dates. Persons hold everything else.
- **Knowledge lives only in ledgers.** Nothing knows anything except a person.

### 2.2 The one primitive the design is missing, and it already exists three times

The corpus already contains this object in three places and never names it:

- `banked_claims` — *"dormant claims on seats elsewhere; each is **a standing date with a watch
  predicate**"*
- a **vacancy** — death does not resolve succession, it *emits a standing date at a horizon*
- **dormant rows re-arming at magnitude** — a condition that puts a matter back in play

Three independent instances is the recurrence threshold. **Name it once:**

> **A WATCH is a predicate attached to a container that, when it becomes true, SCHEDULES A STANDING
> DATE. It never decides anything. It puts a matter on somebody's calendar.**

**This is the design's answer to the threshold, and it is not a threshold.** A threshold fires an
outcome; a watch schedules a decision. The number never decides — it only makes a person face the
question, and the person may still decide either way, or refuse.

**Why this is the right shape and not a smuggled gauge:**
- It adds **no** stored social quantity. A watch is a predicate over what the container can already
  read: its own stake, its own judging set, or an aggregate it computes on demand.
- It cannot resolve anything. **The decision remains an act by a named person at a sitting.** T1 holds,
  no-omniscience holds, no world agency is introduced.
- It is **published as a band with no trigger point**, exactly as the corpus already requires of
  larders and prosperity.

**What it buys — the three termination failures, all at once:**
1. The three arcs that ended at *a counter with nobody deciding* now end at **a sitting the counter
   scheduled.** The ending is restored and a person still makes it.
2. The closure limit **softens without being violated.** Where every convener prefers a matter
   unresolved, the watch still schedules — and the convener must convene it **or visibly refuse.**
   **A refusal is an act, and an act is witnessed.** *"Nobody wants this resolved"* stops being a dead
   end and becomes **visible obstruction that other people can hold against someone.** That is a
   better story than resolution.
3. A blocked core — a want with no act whose object it is — becomes a matter that can be *scheduled by
   someone else's watch* even when the wanter has no verb for it.

### 2.3 The one retyping that dissolves the worst structural gap

**A petition's respondent is a STANDING DATE, not a container.**

Today a petition needs a respondent *container*, so an office cluster — four Dicasteries, a knightly
order, every trans-settlement guild — has no owning node and **cannot be addressed at all.** That
removes a whole direction of play against every institution, and the obvious repair (type the
convener as a required Person) is refuted, because a convener is an **office**: conferred, revocable,
and **vacant-able**.

Retyping the respondent as a date fixes it without touching the office:

- Every body that can *hear* anything has sittings — clusters included.
- A vacant convener is no longer a type error. It is **a date nobody is ordering**, which is exactly
  the right behaviour: your petition is filed against a sitting that is not being scheduled, and
  *getting it scheduled becomes the play.*
- It unifies with §2.2: a watch schedules a date; a petition addresses a date. **One object.**

### 2.4 The one inversion that makes the commons expressible

The world-substrate proposal wanted a place-scoped scalar entering every actor's obstacle. That is a
**flat amount** read as a **modifier** — the wrong side of `:219` on both counts, which is why it had
no clearance argument.

**Invert it. Damage to a commons REMOVES AN OPTION; it does not add difficulty.**

This is the exact shape the design already licenses in the other direction — practice rank *adds
verbs to the option list* — and `:219`'s own gloss asks for **option-set changes, not modifiers.**

- A silted harbour does not make sailing harder. It makes **some cargo impossible**, so a different
  act must be chosen.
- A worked-out seam does not raise an obstacle. **The verb that drew from it is gone.**
- Degradation is **legible without a gauge**: you can see which verbs are missing.

**And it fixes leverage by construction.** One person's act removing one option from a place is a
change to *the place's* option set, sized to the place — a fraction of the container's own capacity,
which is precisely `:219`'s licensed route. **The rule stops being an obstacle to route around and
becomes the thing that makes the mechanic good.**

Second-order behaviour nobody designs: because the loss is an option and not a number, **the people
who notice first are the ones whose practice used that verb.** The commons becomes political through
`witness`, not through a meter.

## 3. The ideal code shape

### 3.1 The shape

- **The module tree IS the containment ladder.** Parent–child in code means containment in the world.
  Nothing else is a parent of anything. Alignment stays out of the tree.
- **A rung module** reads its own state and messages addressed to it. It may **compute** an aggregate
  over its descendants **on demand**; it may never receive a pushed one and never store one. It writes
  only its own state.
- **Down-stroke** = distributing at increasing granularity. **Up-stroke** = aggregating. A person's
  contribution to a container-scale outcome is **a fraction of that container's capacity, or a change
  to its option set. Never a flat modifier.**
- **One writing phase.** The resolver is the only thing that writes the world.
- **Three types carry everything:** an `Act`, an `Event`, a `Claim`.

### 3.2 What a container owns, in full

```
Container:
    stake          the contested thing at this rung
    judging_set    who decides here
    dates          [StandingDate]
```

```
StandingDate:
    convener       Office            -- may be VACANT; a vacant convener is a date nobody orders
    items          [Item]            -- ordered by the convener; capacity is finite
    watch          Predicate | NONE  -- pure over this container's own readable state
```

Three fields, and **`watch` is the only addition to #342's ownership table** — which the table already
admits, because a standing date is one of the three things a container holds and a watch is a property
of a date, not a fourth kind of container state.

**No aggregate, norm, density, unrest, cohesion, reputation or grievance field exists anywhere.**

### 3.3 The whole loop

```
P0  options      per person, from their view: verbs from practice, place, claims,
                 and the option sets of the containers they are in
P1  settle       matter and bodies only -- larders, production, wounds, ageing, travel
P2  choose       choose(person, view) -> act          NO WORLD
P3  resolve      resolve(acts, world) -> events       NO PERSON
                 conflicts route to contest(container, prize, claimants)
P4  witness      witness(person, event) -> claims     per person, never broadcast
P5  watch        every container evaluates its watches; true ones SCHEDULE dates
                 -- scheduling only. Nothing is decided here.
```

**P5 is the whole of the new machinery, and it decides nothing.** That is what makes it admissible:
it is not an actor, it is a calendar.

### 3.4 What this refuses, and keeps refusing

A second resolver · a threshold that fires an outcome · a stored aggregate or any gauge · a pushed
aggregate · a flat modifier from a person onto a roll · a tier/level/scale field · knowledge stored on
the thing known · a per-entity branch in the resolver · an authored per-person opportunity · **and any
apparatus whose subject is this repository's process rather than the game.**

## 4. The whole delta, stated so it can be attacked

**One primitive** — the watch, already present three times and never named.
**One retyping** — a petition's respondent is a date, not a container.
**One inversion** — commons damage removes options instead of adding difficulty.

Everything else in #342 stands, and the design's low end needed no repair at all.

**What would falsify this.** (i) If a watch predicate cannot be written for the three arcs that lose
their ending without reading a descendant's state, R-1 is violated and the primitive is wrong.
(ii) If any body that must be petitionable has **no** sittings, the retyping does not reach it.
(iii) If removing an option from a place cannot express a degradation somebody must be able to
express, the inversion is too weak and the scalar was right after all.

**These three are the next thing to attack, and they are the argument to run — not the seventeen
documents' disagreements with each other.**

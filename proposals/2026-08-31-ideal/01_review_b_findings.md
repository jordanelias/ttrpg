## THE RULING THAT MATTERS MOST — F-6: `:219` IS violated as filed, and IS repairable

Part 3 left `:219` as *"an unexamined row"* whose bite turns on an undefined word. **This review
rules further, on text the relay had and never composed:**

> **The row that decides is not `:219`'s undefined "group" — it is the design's own named precedent at
> `02_the_person.md:211-213`:** *"a person's contribution to a container-scale outcome is a **fraction
> of the container's own capacity**, because what they contribute is a verb the container can now
> attempt, **sized to the container**."*

A site's condition changing **is** a container-scale outcome. The proposed write is a scalar delta
with **no sizing rule anywhere** — nothing sizes one person's `alter` to the container, and nothing
bounds the fraction of a site's obstacle one act can move. `11:254-256` names the two licensed shapes:
*"fractions rather than flat amounts, option-set changes rather than modifiers."* An unsized additive
obstacle term is **the wrong side of both.**

**VERDICT: violated as filed. Repairable, not abandonable**, by either route — and **both routes end
at the same missing thing, a sizing rule for the write:**

1. **Repair into the licensed shape (recommended).** An `alter`'s delta is a **degree-scaled fraction
   of the site's current condition**, bounded so no single act at any rung moves more than a stated
   fraction; and the condition enters play primarily as **band-gated option closure**, with any
   `resistance_pool` contribution quantized to the published band, never the raw scalar.
2. **A written scope ruling on `:219`** — that it quantifies over effects on **persons** (social
   leverage), while effects on **physical state** are governed by material capacity, as the suite
   already does: `10:74`'s *"a lock's fineness, a wall's sheerness"* affects every subsequent climber
   and nobody reads it as leverage; `forestall` (`13:141-144`) lets one person remove a settlement's
   entire supply, bounded by `stores(hearth(person))`. **Material leverage bounded by material capacity.**

**Added falsifier, which no stage had:** *one person, one season, maximum-degree `alter` — what
fraction of the site's obstacle moved? If it can exceed the fraction any verb contributes to a
container outcome under `02:211-213`, the object violates the precedent.*

## F-6a — THE PROPAGATION GAP: the field's cross-rung semantics are UNWRITTEN

Walked at every rung. **Person** interacts by `alter` and `Thread-Read(place)` — sound. **Hearth and
community** are containment nodes (`09:684`, *"hearths on demand"*), so the S-G defect does **not**
recur in its original form. **But above the written node it is identically zero**, because:

> The proposal never fixes **which node an act's `touches:{(n, alter)}` names**, and never states the
> containment semantics between a node's condition and its children's. As filed, a settlement's
> condition and its hearths' are **independent primary fields with no relation** — a degraded hearth
> inside a pristine settlement, invisible to any settlement-level read.

**The licensed primitive exists and is exactly one — R-1's compute-an-aggregate-on-demand.** Three
sentences are required and all three compose on it:
1. primary state lives at **the finest node the act names**;
2. any coarser read is **R-1 compute-on-demand**;
3. **no coarser rung stores one.**

**Without these, the object recreates S-G in mirrored form — non-zero only at the rung an act happens
to name.**

## F-6b — THE SECOND COST IS UNDERSTATED BY EVERY STAGE, INCLUDING BOTH ANTAGONISTS

Priced as *"small"* because compute is trivial. **The real price is epistemic and it is not small.**

`10:180`, invariant 3: the odds table *"never calls `roll` … looking at the odds cannot consume the
die."* Composed with a new world-condition obstacle term:

> **A player can read any site's `thread_condition` repeatedly, at zero cost — no act, no witness, no
> claim, no Thread Sensitivity — by previewing a Thread act's odds and subtracting the known terms.**

Three collisions: `13` §5's own discipline for exactly this class of state (*"never published …
discoverable only by investigation"*); Arc 5's recovered theme (*the world is misunderstood while
everyone reports competence*) — which the proposal claims arrives *"for free"* and **the preview
channel deletes for the player specifically**; and `Thread-Read(place)`, which becomes partly
redundant against a free UI query.

**And it is asymmetric: `choose` has no `World`, so NPCs cannot preview. A player-only omniscience
channel into hidden world state — the T4 failure class in miniature.**

**Typed repair, one line:** route the pre-roll exposure of any world-condition obstacle term through
the band-publication rule the suite already owns — **the preview shows the band, never the scalar.**
The alternative (exempting Thread acts from exposure) is a per-act-class branch drifting toward
`:221`'s shape and must not be taken.

**What survives, restated so it is true:**

> The real cut is not *sitting vs counter*. It is ***a person's decision* — dated or not — vs *a state
> trigger with no decider*.**

Under that restatement all 12 stable members plus 7, 9 and 16 fall on the surviving side and the lost
side is **2½**. **The axis's direction is untouched; its arithmetic and its label are not citable as
filed.** Cite it as *12 stable + 3 label-disputed + 2½ lost, lane-1 scope.*

⚠ **Propagation note:** the axis was tested at institutional rungs only. `15` B-4 demotes the hearth

## F-3 — THE CLOSURE LIMIT survives its falsifier, but its FRAMING overstates the world's passivity

**The limit is true as scoped to positions**, and its falsifier was genuinely run — five candidate
mechanisms checked, none forces. **But the design already contains decider-free channels, and I-A-3's
wording denies them:**

- **Matter.** `13:182-186` — a storm plus a generation of silt closes Hafenmark's channel *"exactly
  when a famine elsewhere needs it."* **A decider-free state-plus-weather event forcing a realm-scale
  crisis.**
- **The calendar.** Lapse — *"not an act by anybody — it is the date passing"* — already resolves a
  matter against whoever needed the affirmative act.

> **So "the world's own agency" IS expressible inside the three signatures** — as world events over
> matter, and as expiry. **What is genuinely unspellable is a non-person mechanism that produces ACTS**,
> because acts come only from `choose(Person, View)`. *That* type error is the design's central
> deliberate commitment, and it is the only thing the limit should claim.

**What the design loses, argued honestly against itself:** the story where *the situation* runs out of
patience while every person prefers stasis (Arc 1's thesis, and the corpus's best arc); **a world-scale
failure state** — *"the campaign cannot end in a Rupture"* — so an unplayed region's default trajectory
is **stasis with rising costs, not crisis**; and a seat-level lockout, since a player whose opponent
benefits from irresolution has exactly one counter (reach a convener) and unpetitionable clusters can
close it. **The cost is asymmetric: it lands hardest on postless seats.**

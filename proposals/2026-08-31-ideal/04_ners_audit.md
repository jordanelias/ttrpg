ownership row**, and **the `carry`-standing rule restated for the office form** (§5.3 was derived for

**⚠ EXPIRY: REPAIR REQUIRED. The evaluator must know what nobody can know.**

The lapse analogy **fails on the predicate's type**: lapse's trigger is **a date** — container-local,
calendar-readable. Supersession's is *"the proposition is no longer live"* — **a world-scale semantic
judgment**. The design **stores no famine** (no gauges), and its epistemic discipline runs the other
way: a convener ranks *"the petitions he HOLDS A CLAIM OF — not the petitions that exist."*
**As filed, supersession is either a forbidden stored world-condition or an omniscient oracle.**

**⚠ AND §9.3 CONTRADICTS §12.4 — a contradiction I introduced.** §12 forbids dedup (*"none of them
cancels another … that would be an engine deciding a person's options"*) and celebrates the double
grant. But §9.3 supersedes a petition when *"the seat was filled by other means."* **If office B's
grant does not expire the petition at office A, then a seat filled by other means cannot expire a
conferral petition either.** The two rule the same fact pattern opposite ways, and **the contradiction
is the missing evaluator showing through.**

**Repair direction consistent with the design's own discipline:** supersession is **evaluated only at a
venue, from claims a named person holds** — the convener or any party *moves that the matter is moot*,
contestable like any claim — or at worst is scoped to **calendar-and-matter state only.**

**R for this enlargement CANNOT BE RULED until D-2 (the act economy) is.** Under one act per season,
three petitions cost three seasons of a famine — a true price. Under a multi-act reading,
**petition-spray dominates.**

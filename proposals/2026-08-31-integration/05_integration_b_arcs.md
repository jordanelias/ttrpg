### I-A-1 · A place-scoped Thread condition, written by acts

**(i) What it edits.** `13_material_life.md` — a new subsection alongside §5 (*The slow fuses*), which
is the document and the section that already hold primary physical state at a place. Consequential
one-line edits: `01_substrate.md` §4 (the Settlement rung's paragraph, which enumerates what a place's
material stake is); `11_code_shape.md` §3 (the ownership table — **AR-1**); `09_churning_world.md` §12
(the refusal list, which must say this was considered, so the next reader cannot read its silence as
a refusal again).

**(ii) The change.** A containment node may hold **one primary physical scalar recording the Thread's
condition there** — `thread_condition(n)`, in exactly the class of `13` §1's `stores(h)` and `13`
§5's `base(H_mine)`. Three properties, and they are the whole of the object:

```
thread_condition(n)   primary state, held at a place. Not an aggregate over persons.
                      Written ONLY by resolve(), in P5, in response to an act that
                      declares touches: {(n, alter)} — 09 §1.5's existing grammar.
                      Read as an obstacle term (10 §2) and as a facet by
                      Thread-Read(place) (03 §6.1, :531), which already exists.
                      Published as a band with its inputs and never a trigger point —
                      13 §1's own rule, applied unchanged.
```

**No new verb, no new phase, no new resolver, no threshold.** The operation that damages it is not a
new system: `09` §1.5 already declares every act's `touches: {(object, mode)}` with `mode ∈ {read,
alter, exclude}`, and `10` §8.1 already gives Thread acts a pool drawn through the identical `roll`.
`Thread-Read` is currently the only Thread act in the suite for one reason — **there is nothing at a
place for an act to alter.** Give places the field and `alter` falls out of the grammar that is
already written.

**Why place-scoped, argued rather than asserted (C-1's decisive clause).** A global scalar re-runs the
design's own anti-scalar argument in physical form: `01` §6 refuses a faction-wide reputation scalar
because *"a single number destroys the case where the Church is loved in Himmelstift and hated three
valleys south — which is most of the setting"*, and a peninsula-wide Thread number destroys the
identical case for the Thread. The corpus itself supplies the evidence that this is a real loss and
not a purity argument: `03_arcs_41_55_and_emergent.md:160` records that the old global MS *"unravelled
him peninsula-wide the season the band changed"* and that the local replacement is *"more causal, more
local"*. And **the arcs asked for place-scoping directly**: arc 22's blocker is stated as *"the design defines
no Thread operation other than Thread-Read, no world-side Thread state for an operation to damage,
**no site**, and no entity"* (`02:183–184`), and lane 2's demand list repeats it — *"no Gap, **no
site**, no operation vocabulary beyond reading, and no entity"* (`02:42`). So the reviewer's
constraint and the corpus's demand ask for the same object. **I do not claim these converged
independently, and P-2 is why:** the reviewer of body (b) read the lane reports, so it had access to
arc 22's "no site" and this is a shared-source agreement, not a rediscovery. What it does establish is
that place-scoping costs the arcs nothing — the units that need the object were asking for the
place-scoped version of it.

**Why this is the omission, not a refusal — and one piece of evidence the arc lanes did not have.**
The suite prices an operation set it never defines. `10` §8.3 (`:201`) charges *"every Thread attempt
costs −1 drift … a contested Thread op costs −2"*; `10` §8.3 (`:199`) closes *"some Thread ops"* at
Fragmented; `02` §5.1 (`:435`) writes *"Thread ops per doc 04"*; and doc 04 is *The Hearth and the
Community* (`00_INDEX.md:31`), which contains one incidental use of the word "Thread"

second, checkable in one line: **doc 02's header mispoints two of its three deferral targets.**

`00_INDEX.md` lists 07 as *Alignment* and 08 as *Argument*. A header whose cross-references were
written against a different document ordering is evidence that a document was planned and not filed;
it is not evidence of a decision. Set against `09` §12, whose refusal list is otherwise exhaustive
enough to name simulation radii and event inboxes and does not name any world quantity, **omission**
is the reading the tree supports.

**(iii) N/E/R/S.**

### I-A-2 · Promote the precondition/postcondition test out of doc 05 and make it the general rule

**(i) What it edits.** `01_substrate.md` §6 (the final bullet, *"A grievance-to-revolt threshold"*)
and `09_churning_world.md` §12 (the clause *"A grievance threshold, unrest trigger point or revolt
gauge"*). The text being promoted already exists at `05_up_stroke.md` §5.3 (`:402–440`).

**(ii) The change.** Both refusals are currently **subject-scoped** — they name grievance, unrest and
revolt. Replace the subject with the structure, using the suite's own words: *A threshold is a
**precondition**: state is measured, a line is crossed, and something is caused. What this design
refuses is the precondition. A **postcondition** — acts chosen for other reasons, and a comparison
describing how they came out — is not refused anywhere and is used throughout.* Keep §5.3's three
falsifiers as the test: no signature returns "revolt"; the compared quantities never appear in a
precondition; the band is publishable and the trigger point does not exist.

**Why this is the right integration of the variable-not-threshold survivor.** Lane 3 reached the same
distinction from the opposite side: *"Every threshold that reports a world state — the MS bands, RS 0,
the Rupture — is lost, **and the loss belongs to the variable, not to the threshold**"* (`03:432–441`),
with the demand that the two be *"reported as two independent findings"*. Note that lane 3 **does**
point at the design's own rule for it, at `03:438` — *"`05` §5.2 already shows how to make a comparison
a postcondition rather than a trigger"* — so the discriminator was found, in the up-stroke, by a lane
reading arcs. Lane 2 landed in the same place: *"in every one of those cases the missing thing is not
the threshold — it is the quantity the threshold was reading"* (`02:941`). **The design already owns
the discriminator and files it where nobody looking at a refusal list will find it**, which is the
whole of the defect this edit closes; the three falsifiers that make the test checkable rather than
asserted are one section further on, at `05` §5.3.

**This edit is also the precondition of I-A-1 not being misread.** A reader who meets
`thread_condition(n)` under the current wording has to decide for themselves whether a place-held
quantity that an obstacle reads is "an unrest gauge". Under the promoted test the answer is
mechanical: `choose` cannot see it, no branch producing acts reads it, and the band is published
without a line under it — so it is a postcondition and it passes.

**(iii) N/E/R/S.** **N** — cut the promotion and the suite keeps a rule that is stated once, in the
wrong document, in subject terms; the emergent loss is not in play but in the *next* object, which
will be argued about on vocabulary rather than on structure. **E** — this is the proposal's one
strictly-shrinking edit: two subject-scoped bullets become one structural test that already exists,
so the vocabulary goes down by one term. **R** — none directly; it is a rule about rules. **S** —
unchanged; it moves no quantity.

**(iv) Cost.** Nil in objects. One real cost in reading: a structural test is harder to apply from
memory than a banned word, and a later session may need the falsifiers to apply it. They are three
lines and they move with the text.

**(v) Falsifier.** Apply the promoted test to every quantity the suite already ships — larder bands,
Prosperity/Order/Defense bands (`13` §2), Coherence bands (`02` §5.2), `force`/`hold` (`05` §5.2). If
any one of them fails the test, the test is wrong or those objects are, and this edit must not be
made until that is resolved.

### I-A-4 · Answer the off-board polity with the generation rule the suite already has

**(i) What it edits.** `00_INDEX.md` §3.3, first bullet — *"An off-board polity acting without a person
to carry it … 'Generate a person' and 'allow an actorless pressure' are different games."*

**(ii) The change.** Rule it: **generate the person.** Altonia and Schoenland act through persons the
world mints when they act, under the suite's existing generation rule — `09` §8.1 (*"The clock never
mints a person here … A **record** is minted only when: an event names them · a telling puts them in
someone's ledger · **they occupy a role or office** · they enter a Knot · they are individuated as
decisive in a contest"*), restated in `11` §6 as *"a person is generated when something needs them to
exist, and is then made consistent with the address, marks and history they must already have had."*
An envoy carrying a demand occupies a role and is named by an event, so he is already on `09` §8.1's
minting list. No new object; the open question is answered by rules the suite already ships.

**Why this way round, given that the corpus votes the other way.** The two lanes disagree, and I say so
before recommending. Lane 1 (`01:730`): *"three of eighteen arcs use off-board pressure as a term, and
none of them uses an off-board **person**. What the fiction wanted was weather, not an ambassador."*
Lane 2 (`02:963`): *"**Arc 38 is fiction-side evidence for 'generate a person'**: its own content is
that the pressure was always two men."* The tie is broken by the code shape, not by the vote count. An
actorless pressure has to reach a person somehow, and the only channels are `witness` and `tell`,
both of which carry **claims with referents**. A claim can already have a faction or a place as its
referent, so *"Altonia is massing"* is expressible today. What is **not** expressible is Altonia
*acting*: `06` §1 types a Dispensation's issuer as *"a person holding office"*, `14` §2.3 makes
conferral `admit()` performed by persons, and `09` §6 refuses a leader as a modifier. An actorless
pressure would therefore need either a second issuing path or a scalar with no carrier, and both are
on `11` §7's forbidden list under *a pushed aggregate* and *a stored aggregate*. Generating the person
costs nothing and forbids nothing.

**(iii) N/E/R/S.** **N** — cut it and three LOST units stay lost with no route (rows 7, 8, and the
Blocker-B half of row 10 in §2.0), and every arc that prices a stake in off-board pressure has no

## 4. Live choices

**L-1 · Does the Thread's condition decay on a clock, with nobody acting?**
*Option A (recommended for now — decline).* Act-driven only. Every movement of `thread_condition(n)`
has a named actor and an event. Four of five Blocker-A units recover. `09` §8.3 is untouched, and the
design's refusal of the world-as-actor stays intact and consistent with I-A-3's stated limit.
*Option B (grant).* Add a baseline decay term in P1 SETTLE. Arc 5 — which the old corpus's own analysis
calls *"the most thematically essential arc in the game. If this arc doesn't fire, something is wrong
with the campaign"* (`01_arcs_01_18.md:275`) — becomes reachable in its stated form, and the setting
acquires a world that is dying rather than merely misunderstood.
**These are materially different games.** Under A the peninsula's condition is a ledger of what people
did; under B it is a fate people are inside. I recommend A because it is reversible — B can be added
later as one term and A cannot be recovered once B is in — and because B is exactly the object
`04_SYNTHESIS.md` §4 identifies as the thing the design gave up on purpose. **This is a Jordan call.**

**L-2 · Is conferral office-rooted?**
The arc corpus votes one way and does not close. Lane 2 (`02:817–841`) finds five arcs in its range
requiring office-rooted conferral (20, 23, 24A, 29, 34) and none requiring person-rooted, on a
consistent mechanism: person-rooted makes the interesting move free — a Templar order secedes by
ceasing to name a man, a Grandmaster's refusal becomes unintelligible rather than lawful, a king's
death unroots the graph beneath him in one instant. **C-7** bounds this: *"needs" does double duty*
(two of the five are structural necessities, one is a drama argument), and another lane's succession
material was never put through the test. **The evidence points one way; the sweep does not close.**
`00_INDEX.md` §3.2 says of this question: *"Two defensible answers, materially different games. Not
an audit's call."* **I therefore do not rule it.** Recommendation, offered as evidence rather than as
a verdict: office-rooted, with one qualification about what kind of evidence this is. The arc
*documents* were written years before the design, so their demands are not a re-reading of it — but
the lane that extracted the demands read the design while extracting them, so the extraction is not
blind and P-2 applies to the framing even though it does not apply to the source.

**L-3 · Is `13_material_life.md` the right home, or should the Thread condition wait for a document of
its own?** Housing it in *Material Life* makes AR-2a's ruling structurally rather than by declaration
(see §3). The alternative — a sixteenth document owning the Thread world-side — is the shape 00b's
P-5 exists to catch, and I do not recommend it. But if AR-2a is refused, L-3 collapses: there is no
home, and I-A-1 should not be built.

**L-4 · Which seats can a player occupy?** Not my lane's question to answer, and it gates
lane (a)'s D-8 rows and my §1.4 closure. Recorded here because both exercises now depend on it:
00b's RULE 3 makes *"is this seat playable?"* load-bearing, and **C-5** shows the last attempt to
apply the rule settled one seat's row while leaving an identically-undecided seat's row open. The
answer needed is a list, not a principle.

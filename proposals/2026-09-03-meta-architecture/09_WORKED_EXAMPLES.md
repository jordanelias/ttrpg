# §1 · Political strife among ruling factions

**A faction is a Proposition somebody uttered plus the `commit` edges of everyone who signed it.**
So *multiple ruling factions* is several propositions with overlapping commitment sets and
persons holding seats — and **a person may commit to two that conflict, which nothing forbids.** They
are a live contradiction, and their own scoring will make them betray one.

> ⚠ **AMENDED 2026-09-03.** This paragraph read *"Not a type."* **It is a type** — a `Faction` view
> resolved from that proposition at a barrier (Stage 4 §B.6.1), so consumers get a roster instead of
> recomputing one. **Nothing in the strife below changes**, because the view carries no state: every
> mechanism in this section still runs on the propositions and the `commit` edges themselves.

## §1.1 · Seven sources of contention, none of them implemented

| source | mechanism |
|---|---|
| **scarcity** | two people reach one granary; the fold gives the second a **different Event** |
| **one seat, many claimants** | `hold` is 1-per-object; a stranger takes it and your act is refused |
| **overlapping purview** | two seats whose scope reaches one rung. Nothing resolves it but who acts first, or a contest |
| **de jure vs de facto** | the sworn edge says loyal; the commitment overlap says otherwise. **The gap is the strife** |
| **incompatible honest claims** | neither can read the other's ledger. **They act on different worlds** |
| **revocation asymmetry** | a King with authority but not holdings **cannot unmake a Duke.** A structural stalemate, free |
| **custody** | whoever holds the document gates the act |

## §1.2 · Contested issues the primitives already spell

**A vacant seat** — and `conferral` decides which act fills it: `confer` by a superior, `determine` by
a judging set, or `succeed` by an heir. **Three genres of succession crisis from one field.**

**An edict** — a dispensation does not apply; it lands as **a compliance contest per executor.** One
decree, N contests, N outcomes.

**A sitting** — the judging set is a Query over seats whose remit covers the matter. **If two seats
both claim it, the sitting is contested before anything is decided.**

**A maturing term** — an obligation ending on a schedule somebody set, which gives it handles: bribe
the clerk who set it, burn the record carrying it, reach the man who must renew it.

## §1.3 · One trace, four seasons

**S1** · A Count dies. `destroy` closes every tenure naming him. CALENDAR makes the vacancy a **fact,
not an occasion**. ⚠ **Nobody convenes. The seat stays empty** — if no person acts, the thing does not
occur.

**S2** · Someone with the `convene` remit pays a scene to schedule the date. Two claimants each form a
question from a claim that landed; each files a petition — now **Records**, holdable and burnable.

**S3** · The date fires. Meanwhile a Grandmaster has forsworn — a named act, at a venue, witnessed —
so the overlap Query falls and **a band crossing changes who may act.** The sitting determines.

**S4** · The loser witnessed it firsthand; their stance moves; their scoring now ranks a different
candidate highest. They spend a scene to `tell` someone, **and the claim distorts in transit.** That
person forms their own question and acts.

> **That is the loop closing across three people, and it is the story.** Nobody scripted it. The
> grievance is claims in a ledger that **decay unless renewed** — do nothing for four seasons and it
> lapses, which is *he loses the town by being forgotten.*

## §1.4 · The uncomfortable consequence

> **A CIVIL WAR IS: ENOUGH NAMED PEOPLE, EACH CHOOSING, IN A SEASON.** There is no other spelling. No
> faction declares. No unrest meter rises. A crossing may **compel someone to choose**, never decide
> for them.

**That refusal is why a third of the arc corpus is unrunnable, and it is the price, not a bug.**

# §2 · A settlement at risk of revolting

⚠ **THIS IS THE HARDEST CASE FOR THE DESIGN, and the answer is that the question must be re-asked.**

**There is no unrest value and there cannot be one** — a Rung owns no social aggregate. So a
settlement cannot *be* at risk as a property. **Risk is a QUERY.**

**And it may not be "sum everyone's grievance"** — no resolver-side Query may aggregate per-person
tallies across holders. That is the banned ratchet.

> **What is lawful to count is `commit` edges, because they are structural and live:**
> **risk of revolt = the share of persons at and under that rung whose LIVE commitments are to a
> proposition incompatible with the holder's.**

Reversible, because a Query can fall. Composes over **live edges only**, so it is not a ratchet.

## §2.1 · How it gets fed — every link an act or a licensed motion

> bad harvest → **subsistence crosses a floor** → that person has a question → among their candidates
> is `commit` to an `OUGHT` somebody uttered → **each commitment is an act, by a named person, costing
> a scene, witnessed**

**So a revolt is ORGANISED, literally.** Somebody uttered the proposition; somebody spent scenes
recruiting. **There is no ambient anger.**

When the share crosses a declared band, the crossing **emits and changes what may be chosen and by
whom** — acts needing co-committed persons present become formable. ⚠ **It does not cause the revolt.
The revolt is people choosing, in a season.**

## §2.2 · The part that makes it drama

**That Query is resolver-side, so NO PERSON CAN READ IT.** The mayor does not know how bad it is. He
acts on his own claims and on what people have told him — **which distorts.**

> **The gap exists because the aggregate is UNOWNABLE, not because it is hidden.** Nobody chose to
> conceal it; there is simply nothing to conceal.

⚠ **AND WHAT THE PLAYER IS OWED, ADDED 2026-09-03 — because the sentence above answers `AX-2` and
leaves a second question standing.** Stage 4 `§C.11` (the explanation contract) says there is no
referee, so the engine inherits the referee's other job: *why did that happen*. **Its split is on WHAT
IS SHOWN, never on who is looking** — the engine owes the **arithmetic of what the character already
holds**, and nothing else. **That does NOT reach this Query**, and `§C.11` says so in terms: the mayor
who holds claims about three angry guilds is owed the sum of *those claims*; the mayor who holds
nothing is owed nothing. **So the number stays unreadable, and the reason the player gets is not the
share but their own ledger.** A preview that named a group the mayor has no claim about would be a
breach of `AX-2`, not a courtesy — which is why this section's *"nothing to conceal"* is the whole
answer to the epistemics and only half the answer to the player.

# §3 · Excommunicating a mayor on an inquisitor's findings

## §3.1 · The chain

**Investigation** — six acts available to **any** person; **eligibility never consults office.** The
Inquisitor uses acts a merchant could use. Findings land as **claims in his own ledger**, which may be
wrong.

**`open_case`** — here the office bites: it needs the `determine` remit, and **the act declares the
stages and their terms.** The case is a **Record**, so it ripens while the accused does nothing —
*because the Inquisitor is not doing nothing* — and every maturation cites the act that wound it.

> **Which means it has handles: burn the record, bribe the clerk who set the term, reach the man who
> must renew it. A case advancing is clerks filing, not weather.**

**The sitting** — a Date fires; the judging set is the seats whose remit covers the matter;
`determine` writes a degree.

## §3.2 · Excommunication is not a flag

> **It is an `issue` — a dispensation whose scope enumerates EXECUTORS, not places. IT DOES NOT
> APPLY. It lands as a compliance contest, per person**, and each recipient may `comply`, `evade`,
> `defy` or `refract`.

**That is historically exactly right, and it falls out of the mechanism rather than being modelled.**
A sentence is only as strong as the people who honour it: **a mayor excommunicated and ignored is a
mayor who is fine.**

And it **travels by being noticed, distorting in transit** — so the version reaching a distant hamlet
is not what the Confessor signed, and *never heard* stays distinct from *heard and refused*.

## §3.3 · What the mayor actually suffers

**Not a status.** People who complied refuse to deal with him, **so his acts needing their cooperation
are refused by the fold.** If he also holds a Church-conferred seat, the revocation path fires
separately — and needs **rank + containment + holdings**, so a Confessor without the holding cannot
unmake him.

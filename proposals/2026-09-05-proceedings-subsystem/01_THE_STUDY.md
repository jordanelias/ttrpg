# 01 · THE STUDY — what the source establishes, and what it licenses a design to do

## Status: **PROPOSED (2026-09-05). HELD BACK IN FULL. NOTHING RATIFIES ON MERGE.**
## This file is the REQUIREMENT SOURCE. It is not the design; the design is `00_DERIVATION.md` onward.

---

The subsystem in this directory is a mechanisation of one document: **"Standing, Persuasion and the
Actor — eight findings from setting the pre-modern literature of political, forensic and diplomatic
proceeding against modern evidence on what a person can do"**, assembled September 2026, 33,395
words in ten files, supplied to this session as the substantive input.

**This file exists because a design that cites its source loosely will be read loosely.** Every
mechanism proposed downstream traces to a numbered result here, and where it does not, that is said.

⚠ **AND THE FIRST THING TO SAY IS THE THING THE STUDY SAYS ABOUT ITSELF, because it constrains this
design more than any single finding does.**

> **The corpus records failure TYPES exhaustively and failure FREQUENCIES not at all.** No source in
> the survey reports a base rate, a failure count, a comparison case or a counterfactual. **No figure
> in the study carries a probability, a base rate or an expected value**, and the study says why: *"a
> decision tree with likelihoods on its branches would be invention, and the absence is the reason
> Part III is a requirement analysis rather than a decision procedure."*
>
> **The consequence for us is exact and it is not negotiable.** The study can supply **what a
> proceeding is made of**, **what goes wrong at each part of it**, **what a person must be able to do
> to clear it**, and **how bad each failure is**. It cannot supply **a single number**. Every
> threshold, weight, band edge and cost in this design is therefore either derived from a structure
> the study does state, or it is `assumption`-grade with a named injection site and a sweep
> (`ID-6`), and it is marked as such at the point of use. **A number presented here as though the
> study supplied it would be a fabrication of exactly the kind `§0.1` point 4 exists to catch.**

## 1 · The pincer — why the study takes the form it does, and why that form suits us

The founding fact is a two-sided result, and the design depends on **both** sides of it.

**Side one — the person is large.** Rotating negotiators across counterparts and partitioning the
variance directly rather than measuring traits and hoping, Elfenbein and colleagues (2008) found
individual differences accounting for **25 per cent** of objective performance in distributive
bargaining, **46 per cent** in integrative bargaining, **19 per cent** of subjective value; a
behavioural follow-up reported **52 per cent**. A twins study (2018) partitioned the same way.

**Side two — the traits are not where the instruments were pointed.** In the same paper, a battery of
existing trait measures revealed few reliable associations with the differences the design had just
demonstrated. The later meta-analysis (Sharma, Bottom & Elfenbein 2013) restored trait validity at
modest effect sizes — and found effects **stronger in field data than in the laboratory**, because a
laboratory negotiation is a strong situation by construction and a strong situation is built to
remove the person.

> ### **WHY THIS MATTERS TO A GAME AND NOT ONLY TO A STUDY**
> A design that models a proceeding as *a contest of two stats* is modelling the half of the variance
> the instruments could find. A design that models it as *a sequence of steps, each of which can be
> failed in a named way, by a person who can see the failure coming and do it anyway* is modelling
> the half that is actually large. **The study's own remedy — stop measuring dispositions, code what
> the consistently good ones do differently at each step — is the shape of the subsystem.**

## 2 · The fifteen steps — the requirement table, which is the spine

Part III.2. Fifteen rows. Every row carries a **fault the corpus names with a source in every cell**,
the **discriminating capacity** that fault implies, an **acquisition verdict**, a **recoverability
grade**, and a **discretion sensitivity**.

| # | step | the fault, in one phrase | capacity | recoverability | discretion sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | whether to enter at all | the meeting that converts a negotiable difference into a question of honour | **tempo** | **TERMINAL** | **highest** — this step *is* the exercise of latitude |
| 1 | audit one's own standing | presumption; performing above one's attained rank; visible effort | calibration of reception · rank-calibration | low | high |
| 2 | classify the body | arguing justice to a body choosing an action | **institutional classification** | moderate | **low** — and it does most of the work where latitude is least |
| 3 | find who actually decides | persuading the assembly when the inner council has settled it | structural inference | moderate | high |
| **4** | locate what may not be said | the scale that grows backwards; profit urged on one who publicly seeks fame | **elicitation** | **TERMINAL** | high |
| 5 | inventory the proofs | mistaking a symmetric argument for evidence; a confident witness for a document | evidential discrimination | high | low |
| 6 | fix the concession in advance | conceding reactively under pressure, or taking the impasse | prior specification under low arousal | high | moderate |
| 7 | choose a register, pre-empt its misreading | every manner invites a specific misreading (Fig. 8) | knowing which misreading one's manner invites | costly | moderate |
| 8 | open | the turnable opening; the too-elaborate opening; opening into a house that has not settled | restraint against one's own default; tolerance of dead air | costly | moderate |
| 9 | arrange | burying the strongest proof; diluting it with the weak | willingness to discard one's own work | costly | low |
| 10 | retrieve | the inaptly chosen ode; the precedent not remembered | ***memoria*** | costly | moderate |
| 11 | hold position under attack | temper; answering more than was asked; being drawn off the matter | **voluntary control of display** | costly | high |
| 12 | supply a favourable construction | the fact is proven and nothing remains to be said about what kind of thing it was | generative reframing under time pressure | costly | moderate |
| 13 | attack | the attack read as personal where character is not the issue | licence assessment | costly | high |
| **14** | close, leaving the other party a way down | winning, and being repaid later; belittlement as the cause of anger | **allocation of attention under load** | **TERMINAL** for the relationship | high |

**Three properties of this table are load-bearing for the design and each is easy to lose.**

1. **The three terminal rows carry the least text and the most weight** (Fig. 21). Rows 0, 4 and 14
   are the rows for which no tradition can write a rule, and they are therefore the rows the written
   corpus says least about. *Any* design built from the corpus inherits that misallocation unless it
   corrects for it deliberately. **What the three have in common: each is a decision ABOUT the
   encounter rather than a move within it — whether to have it, what its boundary is, and how it
   ends.**
2. **The fault column, read down, names one capacity seven times.** Do not deny in detail · do not
   lose temper · do not answer what was not asked · do not fill the silence · do not include the
   weak argument · do not open before they have settled · do not perform above one's rank.
   **INHIBITION.** And in most of these *the actor is not failing to perceive* — the perception is
   available and does not govern the action. **The constraint sits downstream of perception**, which
   is why training perception does not help.
3. **The requirement is control of display, not low reactivity.** Displayed anger extracts
   concessions from a counterpart who cannot afford an impasse. What is scarce is the capacity to
   **withhold, and to release on purpose**.

## 3 · The nine capacities, and what is known about acquiring each

Part III.3, drawn as Fig. 12. The **grouping is the finding**; the list is subordinate to it.

| | capacity | acquisition | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | calibration of reception | **evidenced** | Mellers et al. 2015. ⚠ hazard: instructed perspective-*taking* raised confidence without accuracy |
| 2 | institutional classification | **evidenced**, weakly | propositional — the cheapest thing in the set. **Includes knowing which other capacities this arrangement leaves live** |
| 3 | elicitation | **evidenced** | the one positive result in twenty-five experiments — perspective-*getting*, not taking |
| 4 | evidential discrimination | **evidenced**, weakly | a rule, not a perception; the cleanest trainable item |
| 5 | voluntary control of display | **partly evidenced** | Antonakis et al. 2011 is adjacent, not direct |
| 6 | rank-calibration | tradition-only | what size of claim one is entitled to make. Distinct from 1: different failure, different remedy |
| 7 | generative reframing | tradition-only | the tradition supplies a drill corpus and no modern evidence was located |
| 8 | *memoria* | tradition-only | **the capacity on which the corpus is most confident and the modern literature most silent** |
| 9 | tempo | tradition-only | the rate at which a relationship is built, and the timing of entry |
| — | **allocation of attention under load** | **DOUBLE-ABSENT** | named by neither tradition nor located evidence — and it is what rows 0 and 14 require |

**The two rows of the map behave differently and the design must preserve the difference.** The
bottom row of Fig. 12 — institutional classification, evidential discrimination, generative
reframing, *memoria* — **survives a tight arrangement**. The top row does not: where latitude is low,
capacities 1 and 2 do the work and the rest barely operate.

## 4 · The discretion gate — the join, and the key to covering many proceedings with one mechanism

Fig. 4, which the study calls *"the diagram this study turns on."*

> ### **THE PERSON MATTERS MOST EXACTLY WHERE THE ARRANGEMENT CONSTRAINS HIM LEAST.**

```
  LOW LATITUDE ◄──────────────────────────────────────────► HIGH LATITUDE
  scripted audience · written-only     ranked speaking      autocrat with few
  channel · fixed warrant · paired     order · advocate     constraints · private
  envoys · written summary ·           pleading · council   firm · envoy alone
  silent sovereign                     of state             abroad
  ─────────────────────────────────    ────────────────     ────────────────────
  LIVE: capacities 1 and 2, little     most live            LIVE: all nine, and
  else                                                      the entry step
```

**The measured evidence under it.** Leader effects are **present in autocracies and absent in
democracies**, larger where constraints are fewest, one standard deviation in leader quality moving
growth ~1.5 pp/yr (Jones & Olken 2005). Chief-executive effects are substantially greater in United
States firms than German or Japanese ones, and six national institutions predict the *latitude*,
which **mediates** the effect (Crossland & Hambrick 2007, 2011). Private firms **23.8 %** of variance
in return on assets against public firms' **16.6 %** (Quigley, Chirico & Baù 2022).

**Every interposition device in the corpus moves a setting leftward** — the advocate who pleads, the
envoy who absorbs the insult, the paired envoys watching each other, the office of censor, the
written summary returned with a written ruling, the sovereign who does not speak, the ceremonial that
places a visitor in a relation before a word is said, the consensus council.

**And interposition has a measured price**: third-party representation raises the price obtained and
raises the rate of impasse; representatives under constituency pressure shift toward contentious
tactics and away from exchanging information, producing fewer integrative agreements and more
deadlocks (Bazerman et al. 1992). **Interposition buys protection and distributive advantage at the
cost of joint gain and of the deal happening at all.**

> ### **THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT STRUCTURAL FACT THE STUDY GIVES US.**
> A trial, an embassy, a parliamentary debate, an inquisition hearing and a haggle over a barn differ
> **in how much latitude the arrangement allows and in what is interposed** — and the study says that
> this difference is *what determines which of the actor's capacities operate at all.* **So the
> arrangement is the datum, and the ten proceeding kinds are ten values of it, not ten mechanisms.**

### 4.1 · The interposition taxonomy, and the column that is easy to miss

Fig. 24 gives six kinds, and for each **what it can absorb against what it cannot** — the second
column being the part a design will otherwise invent wrongly.

| interposition | absorbs | **cannot** absorb | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a person** — advocate, envoy, vizier | defeat on the merits · the insult · the refusal | **a discovered lie**, which attaches to the principal; **a concession**, which only the principal can make | contentious drift: price up, impasse up, joint gain down |
| **an office** — censor, remonstrance official | the personal affront in criticism, by converting it to a duty | a ruler's decision that the office itself is the problem | the criticism is expected, and therefore discounted |
| **a document** — written summary, written ruling | **visible defeat on both sides at once** | the exchange in which unshared information would have been pooled | it is also a channel decision, and channels differ measurably |
| **a procedure** — inner council · consensus without recorded division | disagreement, by removing the record of who lost | the informational work dissent does | hidden profiles: what only one member knows stays unsaid |
| **a ceremony** — designed reception · the silent sovereign | **the contest over relative standing, settled before a word** | a substantive concession | a sovereign who does not speak is never contradicted and never tested |
| **a pair** — envoys sent in twos | anxiety about a representative who cannot be observed | **collusion**, which is the failure the device invites | two men must agree before anything is reported |

> ⚠ **AND WHAT NONE OF THEM ABSORBS: a terminal failure.** No interposition repairs a discovered lie,
> a scale touched, or grace once seen as effort. **The three terminal steps are the three where
> nothing can be put between the actor and the consequence.**

## 5 · Standing — four provenances, and the cut that actually predicts

Part II.1, Figs. 3 and 25.

```
A1 ASCRIBED     birth, office, rank, precedence
                ▸ contested PROCEDURALLY, BEFORE ANY CONTENT: seating, order of
                  speaking, forms of address, who is held by the arms
A2 ACCUMULATED  the record of offices, obligations owed, consistency
A3 PERFORMED    what this room sees now — practical wisdom · virtue · goodwill
A4 STAGED       ceremony, setting, retinue, controlled access
```

**Provenance arranges the sources; it does not predict behaviour. The cut that does is PRESTIGE
against DOMINANCE**, and it runs across all four:

| | **prestige** — freely conferred for demonstrated competence | **dominance** — extracted by fear and the capacity to impose costs |
|---|---|---|
| erodes | **slowly**, by inconsistency, because it is conferred and can be re-conferred | **fast**, by a single called bluff, because it is extracted and the extraction can be refused |

**The cell that makes the matrix worth drawing** is A4: Byzantine reception maximizes distance,
Carolingian audience minimizes it. **Both are staged; provenance cannot tell them apart and route
can.** One stages competence and goodwill, the other the capacity to impose.

**Erosion list** — inconsistency · visible self-interest attached to advice · **visible effort once
seen (TERMINAL)** · claiming more than one's place warrants · **the DISCOVERED lie, not the lie
(TERMINAL)**.

**Two bounds the corpus does not state and the study adds:**
- ▣ **bounded by elaboration** — source credibility operates as a peripheral cue where motivation or
  ability to process is low, and biases processing rather than replacing it where they are high.
  **Position dominates an inattentive room and merely tilts an attentive one.**
- ▣ **bounded in time** — the suppression a discounting cue imposes can dissipate while the message
  survives (Kumkale & Albarracín 2004). **Low standing suppresses reception NOW and does not reliably
  suppress it LATER.** A speaker with no standing may still place an argument that outlives the
  discount on its source.

**And standing is a stock filled earlier.** *"An encounter freezes the clock."* Machiavelli's
memorandum of October 1522 is explicitly about filling the reputation that will later present itself
as a given.

> **Jordan's direction that titles impact standing and influence lands here, on A1, and the study is
> unusually precise about the mechanism: an ascribed position is contested PROCEDURALLY AND BEFORE
> ANY CONTENT.** Liudprand, seated below a Bulgarian envoy in 968, understood correctly that *the
> seating was the negotiation*, and recorded that he said nothing at the time because of the pain in
> his heart. **A title's effect is therefore not a bonus to a roll. It is a settlement reached before
> the first word, and it is contestable as such.**

## 6 · The three ladders, which share one shape

Figs. 5, 26 and 27. The study draws them together and the shared property is a mechanism:

> **Each is descended only when forced, and EVERY DESCENT CONCEDES SOMETHING THE ROOM CAN SEE.**
> *"A speaker who begins at 'it did no harm' has conceded the fact before anyone asked him to."*

**(a) Locating the issue** — the procedural objection (*is this body competent to hear this at all?*
— cheapest win available, signals evasion, spent early or not at all) → **conjecture** (is the fact
disputed) → **definition** (fact admitted, its name disputed: theft or sacrilege, error or fraud,
gift or bribe) → **quality** (fact and name admitted: justification · excuse · transfer ·
mitigation — the province of the favourable construction).

**(b) The licensing conditions on frank speech** — four cumulative conditions, each of which
**removes one available reading of the act**: goodwill demonstrated first (removes *hostile*) ·
private (removes *competing*) · unrepeated (removes *characterizing*) · the speaker's own advantage
absent and seen to be (removes *bargaining*). What remains is the reading the speaker wants. **Three
institutional substitutes when the conditions cannot be met: an office, a proxy, displacement.**

**(c) Dissolving a prejudicial attack** — six methods **ordered by what each concedes**: deny the
fact (concedes nothing) · cite something incompatible (nothing, but shifts to character) · impugn the
accuser's motive (nothing on the fact, but signals one cannot) · concede the fact and deny the harm ·
concede the harm and deny the intent · **hand the error back to the audience** (concedes everything
except the relation to the room, and is the only method that can turn the room *against* the accuser
rather than merely away from him).

## 7 · The genre triangle — what the body's office requires it to decide

Fig. 23. **The species of a speech is not chosen by the speaker.**

| the body must decide | the question | the species | the hearer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the past** | did it happen, and what was it | **forensic** | the juror |
| **the future** | shall we do it | **deliberative** | the assemblyman |
| **the present** | is this worth honouring | **epideictic** | the spectator |

**Only the forensic runs the issue ladder.** The deliberative has no issue ladder — the fact is not in
dispute and the question is what follows. The epideictic amplifies. **Inverted, the case is lost
regardless of its merits, because the speaker is answering a question the body has no office to
decide.**

⚠ **And the study names three cases where the scheme BENDS, which a design must be able to express or
it has flattened its own source:** a trial that is really about honour (Demosthenes on the crown wins
by refusing the forensic frame); an assembly asked to judge a past act (the scrutiny of an outgoing
magistrate — **the speaker faces both ladders at once**); and epideictic working inside the other two.

## 8 · Evidence, concealment, and the mirror test

**The mirror test (S8), which is the study's one-line operational result:** *before advancing an
argument, ask whether your opponent could advance its mirror image. If he could, it is not evidence.*
An argument both parties can run has a likelihood ratio at or near unity.

⚠ **The ranking of proofs is CONDITIONAL and the study corrects the corpus here.** Aristotle's
inartificial proofs — laws, witnesses, contracts, testimony under torture, oaths — are ranked first;
the modern hierarchy partly **inverts** it. Mistaken eyewitness identification is implicated in
roughly seventy per cent of US DNA exonerations. **Throughout, "inartificial proof" reads as
documents, records and contracts; a confident witness is a probable sign wearing a necessary sign's
clothes.**

▣ **Adding weak arguments to strong is conditional too**: the dilution of diagnostic information by
non-diagnostic information holds under **high** elaboration; under **low** elaboration the sheer
number of arguments works as a cue and additions help.

**The concealment decision** — silence and ambiguity are cheap, recoverable and deniable; **asserted
falsehood is expensive and, once discovered, terminal.** Three tests, all of which must pass: can it
be cheaply checked · will this party be needed again · does exposure cost more than the concession
avoided. **The traditions disagree violently about whether deceit is permitted and agree almost
entirely about when it fails.**

## 10 · What the study explicitly refuses to supply, restated as a compliance list

| the study says | what a design may therefore NOT do |
|---|---|
| no source reports a base rate, failure count, comparison case or counterfactual | quote a probability as sourced |
| no figure carries a probability, base rate or expected value | draw a decision tree with likelihoods and call it derived |
| the corpus supports description and requirement analysis; **it cannot support procedure** | present a move list as the study's recommendation |
| a type inventory is not a frequency inventory | claim any misreading is the likeliest |
| the transfer from negotiation, executive and head-of-state data to tribunals, embassies and parliaments **is inference** | cite the discretion evidence as if measured in a courtroom |
| four of the nine capacities have **no modern evidence at all** | grade them as trainable |
| the nine capacities are **not claimed independent**, and the count of nine is a judgment graded as a synthesis-gloss | build a nine-axis stat block and call it evidenced |

> ### **AND THE STUDY'S OWN ADMISSION, WHICH IS THE ONE THIS DESIGN INHERITS WHOLE**
> *"This study holds that judgment is what decides proceedings and that nearly every tradition
> surveyed declares it irreducible to rule — and then presents a list of capacities in a table. The
> form asserts something the content denies."* **A subsystem is a stronger form of the same
> assertion.** What mitigates it there mitigates it here and does not dissolve it: **a requirement
> claim is falsifiable where a prescription is not.** The design that follows is built so that its
> claims can be wrong in checkable ways — which is the whole of the defence, and it is enough to be
> worth having.

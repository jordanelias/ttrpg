# PART B · THE SIX MAPPINGS THAT ARE ARGUMENTS RATHER THAN TRANSCRIPTIONS

## B.1 · Latitude is DERIVED from what is interposed — it is not a number a designer sets

The lazy reading of Fig. 4 is a scalar: `latitude: 0..10`. **Refused, on `T-a`.**

The study does not say *latitude is high here*; it says **latitude is what is left when you count what
stands between the actor and the exchange**, and Fig. 24 enumerates the six things that can stand
there. A scalar beside the list is **two homes for one fact** (`ID-2`) that can disagree the moment a
designer edits one — and it is a **stored aggregate**, which `T-a` refuses outright because a value
over many owners has no owner.

```
latitude(game) := a Query over interposed[] — ownerless, computed, stored nowhere
```

> ### **AND THIS IS WHAT MAKES THE PARAMETER SPACE A DESIGN TOOL RATHER THAN A DIAL.**
> A designer does not set how much the person matters. **They say what stands between the person and
> the exchange** — an advocate, a written channel, a ceremony, a paired envoy — **and how much the
> person matters falls out.** That is the study's actual finding, mechanised: *every interposition
> device is a discretion-reducing design, and that is what it is for.*
>
> **The measured price comes with it and is not optional:** interposition raises the price obtained,
> raises the impasse rate, lowers joint gain, and makes the deal likelier not to happen. **A designer
> who interposes to protect a principal buys the impasse.** ⚠ **The MAGNITUDES are not in the study**
> and are `assumption`-grade with a sweep — see `10_LOOPS_AND_GAPS.md` row `P-11`.

## B.2 · The capacity map becomes almost NOTHING, and that is the most important refusal here

Fig. 12 lists nine capacities. **The obvious move is nine stats. It is refused, on four independent
grounds, and the refusal is the design.**

| ground | why it is fatal to the nine-stat reading |
|---|---|
| **`§A.2` — eligibility is never capability** | a capacity that gated an option would be `if skill < N: return []`, which *"re-implements the gate the design deleted"* and *"deletes the best thing about the option set"* |
| **`§B.2` reader clause, split** | a *held* field is admitted when a **decision** reads it; a *read-off* field when a **resolver** does. Six of the nine are things a person **does**, not things they hold. **A capacity is not a field at all — it is what a step REQUIRES** |
| **the study's own text** | *"the requirement must be a capacity, not a disposition"*, and *"it does not claim the nine are independent… no factor structure is attempted, because nothing located would support one"*. **The count of nine is a judgment graded as a synthesis-gloss** |
| **`S4`, which is the study's own finding** | *"in most of these the actor is not failing to perceive — the perception is available and does not govern the action."* A perception stat models the half that is not the constraint |

> ### **SO THE NINE CAPACITIES BECOME REQUIREMENTS ON STEPS, NOT ATTRIBUTES ON PERSONS.** Each is
> either **an act the person may take** (elicitation, control of display, prior specification), **a
> propositional fact the person may hold or not** (institutional classification, evidential
> discrimination — these are *claims in the ledger*, and a person who holds a false one classifies
> the body wrongly, which is `AX-2` doing the work), or **a dimension of `capability`, which already
> exists as a dict on `Person` and supplies dice at RESOLVE and gates nothing** (`§D.1.1`'s fifth
> category).

⚠ **AND THIS IS WHERE JORDAN'S DIRECTION ON ATTRIBUTES LANDS.** *"They will have attributes that
influence their efficacy."* **Efficacy, not eligibility** — which is exactly `capability`'s existing
contract: it *"supplies dice at RESOLVE and gates nothing."* The direction and the axiom agree
precisely, and **no new attribute carrier is needed**: `Person.capability` is already a dict, and
which keys it carries is content by `ID-12`.

## B.3 · The genre is DERIVED from the bench's remit — and the three bends must survive

Fig. 23's own headline: *"the species of a speech is not chosen by the speaker. It is fixed by what
the hearer's office requires him to decide."*

**So `genre` is not a field on the game. It is a Query over the bench's remit acts** — the same walk
`judging_set` performs, reading what those seats are empowered to decide:

| the bench may decide | genre | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| a **past** matter — did it happen, and what was it | **forensic** | **the issue ladder runs** |
| a **future** matter — shall we do it | **deliberative** | **NO issue ladder.** The fact is not in dispute |
| nothing — there is no bench | **epideictic** | amplification; the only prize is standing |

**A verb that mismatches the genre is refused, and the refusal emits.** Arguing what should be done
to a bench empowered only to find a fact is not a penalty applied to a roll — it is an act whose
`requires` fails, producing an Event that every witness deposits. **The corpus's rule — *inverted, the
case is lost regardless of its merits* — is the refusal channel (`§B.3`), not a modifier.**

⚠ **THE THREE BENDS, AND ALL THREE MUST BE EXPRESSIBLE OR THE MAPPING HAS FLATTENED ITS SOURCE.**

| the bend | how the derivation handles it | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **a trial that is really about honour** — Demosthenes on the crown | the bench's remit is forensic; the **prize** is standing, which is not the bench's to dispose of. **Two games run on one occasion**: a forensic game with a bench, and an epideictic game with an empty bench and the same floor | ✅ expressible, and it explains why Demosthenes *wins by refusing the frame* — he plays the second game |
| **an assembly asked to judge a past act** — the scrutiny of a magistrate | one bench, whose remit covers both; `genre` resolves to a **set**, not a value; **the speaker faces both ladders at once** | ⚠ **expressible only if `genre` is a set.** It is declared as one. **A `genre` typed as a single value would have failed here**, and this bend is why |
| **epideictic inside the other two** | amplification is not a game; it is what `charge` and `utter` do to standing regardless of bench | ✅ needs nothing |

## B.4 · Fig. 26 is a four-conjunct `requires` with a refusal kind per conjunct — the tightest fit in the study

The licensing conditions on frank speech are **cumulative**: all four hold, or the speech is received

| conjunct | fails as | and the study's own gloss on why |
|---|---|---|

| it is private | **a standing contest** | removes the reading that he is competing |

| the speaker's own advantage is absent, and seen to be | **self-interest** | removes the reading that he is bargaining |

> ### **THAT IS LOADER INVARIANT 4, WIDENED (`F7`), WRITTEN OUT BY A HUMANIST IN THE FIRST CENTURY.**
> `04_CODE_ARCHITECTURE.md` §B.13 invariant 4: *"every failable clause has a refusal kind — not only
> a verb with a `requires`, but **each conjunct of it**."* The first wording covered `requires` as a

>
> **Fig. 26 supplies four conjuncts and four refusal kinds, matched.** The design does not have to

| conjunct | form |
|---|---|
| goodwill demonstrated | **form 5** — a relation between actor and subject, over prior acts |
| private | **form 1** — existence over an edge kind: no third party present at the venue |

⚠ **`private` is form 1 only if presence is an edge, and it is** (`contain`, walked). **Recorded

warns about.

## B.5 · Concealment and the lie need no mechanism, and the reason is the strongest emergence result here

Fig. 6's concealment branch — silence · ambiguity · asserted falsehood, with three tests — looks like
it wants a `deception` subsystem. **It wants nothing.**

- **A lie is `utter` of a Proposition that is false.** The Proposition is immutable and carries its
  utterer (`AX-6`). Nothing marks it false; **the world simply does not contain a fact matching it.**
- **Its danger is that it can be DISCOVERED**, and discovery is an ordinary investigation act
  producing a claim that contradicts it. `AX-2` is what makes the lie work in the first place: *a
  false conclusion is indistinguishable from a true one to the person holding it.*
- **Silence is not acting.** It costs nothing, is deniable, and leaves no Event — which is precisely
  the study's *"cheap, recoverable, deniable."*
- **The three tests are the player's reasoning, not the engine's.** *Can it be cheaply checked* is
  whether a record exists; *will this party be needed again* is whether an edge persists; *does
  exposure cost more than the concession avoided* is a comparison of two futures. **All three are
  things a player weighs. None is a rule.**

> **And the terminal grade is what makes the lie a real decision:** the discovered lie is TERMINAL and
> **no interposition absorbs it** — Fig. 24 is explicit that an advocate absorbs defeat *"but not a
> discovered lie, which attaches to the principal."* That single cell is worth more than a deception
> subsystem would be.

## B.6 · Two ladders, one primitive — and the third "ladder" is not one

The study groups Figs. 5, 26 and 27 as *"the three ladders, which share a shape."* **On inspection two
are ladders and one is a gate, and conflating them would have cost a mechanism.**

```
LADDER   := ( id, rungs: [ (name, concedes, track) ] )     -- ORDERED. Descended only when forced.
                                                          -- Each descent is an ACT, and is WITNESSED.
```

| | rungs | what a descent concedes | which `AX-3` track it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`issue`** (Fig. 5) | procedural · conjecture · definition · quality | the rung above | **procedural: neither · conjecture: TRUE · definition: the hinge · quality: RIGHT** |
| **`charge`** (Fig. 27) | deny · cite incompatible · impugn motive · concede-fact-deny-harm · concede-harm-deny-intent · hand back to the room | nothing → nothing-but-character → nothing-but-capacity → the fact → fact and harm → everything but the relation to the room | the same descent, applied to a **person** rather than a case |

**Fig. 26 is not a ladder.** It is a conjunction evaluated before an act, with no descent and no
concession — a `requires`, per §B.4. **The study calls it a gate in its own prose and a ladder in its
index, and the design follows the prose.**

⚠ **RECORDED, because it is a real disagreement with the source rather than a reading of it.** The
study's stated commonality — *"whether a speaker knows which rung he is standing on is the capacity
all three require"* — holds for all three. What does not hold is that all three are **descended**.
**A gate is entered or refused; a ladder is descended at a price.** Merging them would have produced
one primitive that could not express the price, which is the mechanism.

# PART C · VENUES AND ROLES — the two the study does not enumerate and the architecture does

## C.1 · The venue — **any rung above the person tier, and the ordinal is the parameter**

> **JORDAN, 2026-09-05, correcting a live data row:** *"convene could happen at any scale except
> individual/person"* — and, on the reason: *"unless you mean convene as limited by physical location
> of participants, but this game can assume people will travel to somewhere."*

`verb_table.yaml:137-147` carries `convene` with **`scale: "settlement"`**. That is wrong, and it is
wrong in the direction the architecture predicts: **a single roster member standing in for a
relation.**

**The lawful expression is an ordinal comparison, which `02_HIERARCHIES.md` §C.2 explicitly
licenses:**

```
convene.requires :  rank(venue.kind) > rank(person)          -- form 2, a computed scalar against a threshold
                    -- NOT  kind == "settlement"             -- which is scripting drift with a schema's face
                    -- NOT  kind in [settlement, province, duchy, realm]   -- the same defect, enumerated
```

`rung_kinds` is an **ordered** roster (Stage 1 §E.3: *the kinds are ORDERED, because a title's rank is
its domain's ordinal*), so the comparison is available with nothing stored, and **it stays correct if
somebody adds a sub-settlement tier** — which a list of names would not. That is `§C.2`'s falsifier
run in advance: *change the roster's membership and see what breaks.*

⚠ **AND THE GROUND FOR EXCLUDING THE PERSON TIER IS A TYPE FACT, NOT AN OCCUPANCY FACT.** The
tempting derivation — *a person-rung holds one person, so a convening there has nobody to gather* —
**smuggles in a co-location premise, and Jordan refused it in the same breath.** The correct ground is
§D.2's: *"the `person` kind is the address slot and the `Person` is who stands in it."* **A proceeding
convenes at a place; a person-rung is an address, not a place.**

### C.1.1 · What follows from *people will travel*, which is three things and the third is free

1. **Convening puts nobody in the room.** `convene` writes `Date.due_at` and attaches a
   `ConveningCondition`. **Being present is the attendee's own later act** — `move`, and the `contain`
   edge. `AX-1` at the door.
2. **So the arrangement never declares a cast.** It may say who is **admitted** (the closed-floor
   parameter); it may not say who is **present**. *Present* is whoever travelled. **A convened
   proceeding nobody attends is a legitimate outcome**, exactly as an undecided fight is (Jordan,
   2026-06-02) — and the path already exists: CALENDAR appends a `DocketItem` when a fired date is
   **vacant** (`shape.py:5363-5381`).
3. ⭐ **The venue's ordinal produces the draw radius with no rule at all.** A hearth arbitration, a
   settlement tribunal, a duchy assize and a realm parliament differ in **how far a person must
   travel to attend**, therefore in **who realistically comes** — out of containment, travel and the
   scene budget. **There is no `catchment` parameter and none is wanted.**

> ### **AND THIS IS WHERE TRIAL IN ABSENTIA STOPS BEING A SPECIAL RULE.**
> A proceeding about a person who is far away and does not come is **the ordinary case of an attendee
> who did not travel.** The excommunication row below carries **no `subject_absent` flag**, because
> there is nothing to flag: the subject is not on the floor, the floor is whoever came, and a body
> disposing of an absent person is what that produces. ⚠ **A co-location model would have needed a
> rule here.** Jordan's refusal of the co-location premise is what makes the absence free.

## C.2 · Roles — what a person IS at a proceeding, which is nothing they carry

> **Jordan's direction:** *"characters will be enacting the system as participants, audience,
> adjudicators etc."*

**A role is not a field, not a type, and not a conferred thing.** Under `AX-1` and `T-a` it cannot be
any of those: a role is a **relation between one person and one occasion**, and every candidate role
below resolves to membership in a set that already exists.

| role | what it IS mechanically | conferred by | closed by (`ID-14`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **participant / party** | holds a live `commit` to a disposition of the matter | **their own `commit`** | `repudiate` — `T-m`, their own discretion |
| **adjudicator** | holds a seat whose remit covers the matter, and acts **`via`** it | `confer` (the seat), long before | `revoke` — `T-o`, the seat's basis |
| **audience** | present at the venue, holding no `commit` and no covering seat | **travel** | leaving |
| **advocate / proxy** | holds a `commit` to another's disposition, plus an `oblige` to that person | their own two acts | `repudiate` + `release` |
| **witness** | present, and **`tell`s** a claim they hold about the matter | nothing — it is an act, not a status | — |
| **subject** | the person the matter's Proposition is about | **the matter**, not the occasion | the matter's disposal |
| **clerk / recorder** | holds the case `Record` | `carry` | handing it on, or `destroy_record` |

> ### **THREE OF THE SEVEN ARE THE SAME EDGE AT DIFFERENT DISTANCES, AND THAT IS THE RESULT.**
> Party, advocate and audience are distinguished entirely by **which `commit` edges a person holds** —
> one to their own disposition, one to another's, or none. **There is no role field, no role enum, and
> nothing to keep in sync.** A person who commits mid-proceeding *becomes* a party; one who repudiates
> stops being one; and neither transition needs a mechanism.

⚠ **AND THE ADVOCATE'S DEFINING PROPERTY COSTS ZERO LINES.** Fig. 24: *an advocate absorbs defeat on
the merits; the principal is not humiliated.* Under `AX-1`, **consequences attach to the actor** — the
advocate spoke, the advocate lost, and standing is read off **their** acts. **The principal's standing
is untouched because nothing touched it.** The corpus's most-cited institutional device is a
consequence of the first axiom, and any explicit interposition mechanism would have been a second
implementation of it.

⚠ **AND THE OTHER HALF OF THAT CELL COSTS ZERO LINES TOO, IN THE OTHER DIRECTION.** *An advocate
cannot absorb a discovered lie, which attaches to the principal.* A lie is an `utter` by whoever
uttered it — **so if the principal uttered it, it is theirs, and no advocate stands between.** Both
halves of Fig. 24's hardest cell fall out of *the actor is the author*.

| **eloquence vs consequence** | `\|bench\|` and `latitude` | Fig. 14's pairing: *eloquence requires many who must be persuaded; consequence requires few who can constrain* |

⚠ **THREE KEYS ARE THE LEAST DEFENSIBLE AS ARCHITECTURE AND ARE FLAGGED RATHER THAN DEFENDED**
(`G.1.6`: *a shape decision is one that would be correct for ANY membership of the data it governs*):

| key | the doubt |
|---|---|
| **`registers[]`** | the seven manners are Han Feizi's, and a different tradition might carve them differently. **If the roster's membership changed, would any mechanism break?** It should not — nothing branches on a member, and the misreading map is a lookup — **but that has not been tested, and it is `10_LOOPS_AND_GAPS.md` row `P-07`** |
| **`order`** | five values, and the fold's canonical order is `(stratum, actor-hash, intra-person position)` (`F.26`). **`order: rank` needs the fold to sort by rank within a stratum, which is a change to a shared mechanism, not a parameter of this one.** Registered as `P-08` |
| **`stakes_grade`** | it is a *grade*, and a grade is a summary. The honest form may be that the stake is simply **what `disposes` writes**, with terminal/costly/free falling out of whether that write is closable. **If so, delete the key.** Registered as `P-09`, and it is the one most likely to be deleted |

# PART E · THE TWELVE GAMES — twelve rows, and the proof obligation discharged

**The claim under test:** *a proceeding is just defined parameters.* **The test:** twelve rows, no
code branch, and every difference between any two of them locatable in the row.

## E.1 · The twelve at a glance — the six keys that do the separating

| game | `disposal` | `bench ∩ parties` | `floor` | `interposed` | `order` | `stakes` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **negotiation** | **mutual** | *is* the bench | closed | — | alternating | costly |
| **arbitration** | bench | ∅ | closed | — | alternating | costly |
| **legal trial** | bench | ∅ | **open** | person · procedure | rank | **terminal** |
| **tribunal** | bench | ∅ | admitted | **office** · procedure | rank | **terminal** |
| **interrogation** | bench | **the questioner** | closed | — | **scripted** | **terminal** |
| **inquisition hearing** | bench | **the accuser sits** | closed | office · procedure · **document** | **scripted** | **terminal** |
| **excommunication deliberation** | bench | ∅ | closed | procedure · **ceremony** | rank | **terminal** |
| **parliamentary debate** | bench | **⊇ parties** | **open** | procedure | rank | costly |
| **council of state** | bench | **⊇ parties** | closed | procedure | rank | costly |
| **audience / embassy** | bench | ∅ | admitted | **ceremony · person · document** | **scripted** | **terminal** |
| **appeal to authority** | bench | ∅ | admitted | **document** | written_only | costly |
| **public debate** | **none** | **no bench at all** | **open** | — | free | costly |

⭐ **Read the first two columns together and the taxonomy is there** — and it takes two, not one.
`bench ∩ parties` **is not a key**: it is a set intersection over edges that already exist, and it is
what makes an interrogation asymmetric and a council collegial. **But it cannot separate a negotiation
from a public debate**, because both have an empty bench and an empty bench intersects everything in
the empty set. **`disposal` is what does that**, and it is declared. ⚠ **A first draft of
`00_DERIVATION.md` claimed the sets alone were sufficient and was wrong for two of the twelve rows;
the correction is recorded there rather than overwritten.**

## E.2 · The rows in full, each with what makes it play differently

### **negotiation** — *the deciders are the parties*
```yaml
disposal: mutual · bench_basis: none · floor: closed · records_dissent: false
venue_min_rank: hearth · term_required: false · appeal_basis: none
interposed: [] · order: alternating · registers: [all seven] · proofs: [record, testimony]
disposes: oblige · verdict_reasons: given · stakes_grade: costly
```
**Why it plays as it does.** `disposal: mutual` means nothing settles until **both parties commit to
the same Proposition** — so there is no bench to persuade and no verdict to lose, only an agreement
to reach or an impasse to take. `latitude` is **maximal** (`interposed: []`), so **all nine
capacities are live** and the entry decision (row 0) is the largest lever in the game. **`proofs`
admits records, so the mirror test bites**: a probability argument both sides can run is worth
nothing against a counterpart who can run it back.

### **negotiation through envoys** — *the same row, one key changed, and the game inverts*
```yaml
interposed: [person, pair]        # ← the ONLY change
```
**This is the parameterisation working, and it is the row to check the whole design against.** Adding
two interpositions **lowers latitude**, which by §B.1 lowers the person-effect — and it buys exactly
what Fig. 24 says it buys and costs exactly what Bazerman et al. measured: **the envoy absorbs the
insult and the refusal; the price obtained rises; the impasse rate rises; joint gain falls; the deal
is likelier not to happen.** ⚠ **And `pair` cannot absorb collusion, which is the failure the device
invites** — two envoys must agree before anything is reported, which is a `requires` conjunct and a
new way to lose.

### **arbitration** — *one decider, chosen by the parties, and no appeal*
```yaml
disposal: bench · bench_basis: determine · floor: closed · venue_min_rank: hearth
term_required: true · appeal_basis: none · interposed: [] · order: alternating
proofs: [record, testimony, oath] · disposes: oblige · verdict_reasons: given · stakes_grade: costly
```
**`appeal_basis: none` is what makes it arbitration** rather than a trial, and it is one key. The
arbiter is named in the **opening act's terms** (`T-n`) — so *who arbitrates* is itself negotiable
before the game starts, which is a game before the game.

### **legal trial** — *the issue ladder, in public, with advocates*
```yaml
disposal: bench · bench_basis: determine · floor: open · records_dissent: true
venue_min_rank: settlement · term_required: true · appeal_basis: determine
interposed: [person, procedure] · order: rank · registers: [restricted]
proofs: [record, testimony, oath] · disposes: <tenure> · verdict_reasons: given · stakes_grade: terminal
```
**`floor: open` is the parameter that does the most work.** An open floor means **every descent of the
issue ladder is witnessed by everyone present**, so conceding the fact to argue quality is expensive
in a way it is not in a closed room. **`interposed: [person]` admits the advocate**, which absorbs
defeat and cannot absorb a discovered lie. **`appeal_basis: determine` opens the nesting** — and the
depth cap is declared by the opening act, so *how many appeals this matter admits* is a term somebody
set and somebody can be reached about.

### **tribunal** — *a standing commission, admitted floor*
```yaml
floor: admitted:<basis> · interposed: [office, procedure] · appeal_basis: none
```
**Differs from a trial in three keys and the difference is real.** `office` interposed means the
tribunal's members criticise **as a duty**, which absorbs the personal affront — **and is therefore
discounted** (Fig. 24's cost column). `floor: admitted` narrows who witnesses a descent, so
concessions are cheaper. `appeal_basis: none` makes the finding final, which under `AX-6` means the
**disposal itself** must be closable by some other act or the row is a ratchet — flagged in
`10_LOOPS_AND_GAPS.md` `P-12`.

### **interrogation** — *the questioner decides, and the subject cannot win*
```yaml
disposal: bench · bench_basis: determine · floor: closed · records_dissent: false
venue_min_rank: hearth · term_required: false · appeal_basis: none
interposed: [] · order: scripted · registers: [restricted] · proofs: [testimony, confession]
disposes: standing · verdict_reasons: withheld · stakes_grade: terminal
```
**The asymmetry is not a key — it is `bench ∩ parties = {the questioner}`.** The questioner is both a
party and the only person who may dispose, so **the subject has no third party to persuade.**
`order: scripted` means the subject does not choose when to speak; `registers: [restricted]` means
they do not choose their manner. **What is left to the subject is Fig. 27's ladder and nothing else**
— deny, cite something incompatible, impugn the motive, concede the fact, concede the harm, hand the
error back — **and there is no room to hand it back to.** `verdict_reasons: withheld` means the
subject cannot even learn what moved it, which by Fig. 13 means **they cannot improve at this.**

### **inquisition hearing** — *an interrogation with a bench, and the terminal failure is being made the subject*
```yaml
bench_basis: determine · floor: closed · records_dissent: false · venue_min_rank: settlement
term_required: false · appeal_basis: determine · interposed: [office, procedure, document]
order: scripted · registers: [restricted] · proofs: [testimony, confession, record]
disposes: <tenure> · verdict_reasons: withheld · stakes_grade: terminal
```
**`term_required: false` with a bench and a closed floor is the whole horror of it, and it is one
boolean.** By `T-n`, an act that opens something without declaring its end has opened something with
**no declared end** — so the proceeding does not conclude; it persists until somebody acts.
⚠ **This is a place where the architecture and the fiction agree so exactly that it is worth flagging
as a check rather than a triumph:** `AX-6` says nothing becomes permanent without an author, and an
indefinite hearing is **authored indefiniteness**. It is lawful, it is contestable, and *whoever set
it can be reached* — which is `T-c`'s dividend and the only thing that makes the row playable rather
than a trap.

### **excommunication deliberation** — *a body disposing of a person who is not there*
```yaml
disposal: bench · bench_basis: determine · floor: closed · records_dissent: false
venue_min_rank: settlement · term_required: true · appeal_basis: determine
interposed: [procedure, ceremony] · order: rank · proofs: [record, testimony]
disposes: <tenure> · verdict_reasons: given · stakes_grade: terminal
```
⭐ **There is NO `subject_absent` key, and that is the result.** The subject is absent because they did
not travel (§C.1.1). **A trial in absentia and an excommunication deliberation are the ordinary case
of an attendee who did not come**, and they need nothing. `interposed: [ceremony]` settles relative
standing before a word is spoken — and Fig. 24 is precise that a ceremony **cannot absorb a
substantive concession**, so the ceremony buys the body its authority and buys the absent person
nothing. **`disposes` severs a `commit`, which under `T-m`/`AX-6` is closable — so a restoration is
a later proceeding, not a mercy.**

### **parliamentary debate** — *many who must be persuaded, and no issue ladder*
```yaml
disposal: bench · bench_basis: determine · floor: open · records_dissent: true
venue_min_rank: duchy · term_required: true · appeal_basis: none
interposed: [procedure] · order: rank · registers: [all seven] · proofs: []
disposes: issue · verdict_reasons: given · stakes_grade: costly
```
**`proofs: []` is not an oversight — it is Fig. 23.** A deliberative body decides the **future**, and
*the future admits no witnesses*; **the fact is not in dispute and there is no issue ladder to
descend.** So the whole forensic game is absent and what replaces it is amplification and the
weighing of advantage. ⭐ **And Fig. 14's pairing falls out of two keys:** many on the bench and low
latitude means **eloquence matters and consequence does not** — *"the republic maximizes the first
and minimizes the second."* `records_dissent: true` means dissent does its informational work and a
loser is on the record as having lost.

### **council of state** — *the same shape, and the opposite trade*
```yaml
floor: closed · records_dissent: false · venue_min_rank: duchy · verdict_reasons: withheld
```
⭐ **Four keys differ from the parliament and they produce the Fig. 14 inversion exactly.** A closed
floor conceals disagreement from outsiders **while permitting it in the room**; `records_dissent:
false` — the consensus requirement — **discourages the disagreement from forming at all**, which the
study argues suppresses dissent *more thoroughly* than secrecy does. **The hidden-profile cost is the
mechanism**: what only one member knows stays unsaid, and the body chooses the inferior option with
nobody having made a mistake.

### **audience / embassy** — *one hearer, heavy staging, and the argument is not the game*
```yaml
disposal: bench · bench_basis: <any remit> · floor: admitted · venue_min_rank: realm
term_required: false · appeal_basis: none
interposed: [ceremony, person, document] · order: scripted · registers: [restricted]
proofs: [record] · disposes: issue · verdict_reasons: withheld · stakes_grade: terminal
```
**Three interpositions is the lowest latitude on the board, and Fig. 14's pairing inverts:** one
person who can constrain means **consequence matters and eloquence does not.** `interposed:
[ceremony]` means **relative standing is settled before anyone speaks** — Liudprand seated below the
Bulgarian envoy, understanding correctly that *the seating was the negotiation*. ⚠ **And the row
carries the cost Fig. 24 names: a sovereign who does not speak is never contradicted, never caught
in error, and never tested.**

### **appeal to a higher authority** — *a proceeding whose matter is a prior finding*
```yaml
disposal: bench · bench_basis: determine   # at a bench whose remit REACHES the first bench
floor: admitted · interposed: [document] · order: written_only
proofs: [record] · disposes: <the prior tenure's closure> · stakes_grade: costly
```
⭐ **This is the only row that is not a sibling of the others — it is one of them, NESTED.** The matter
is the disposition of a prior game; `seam.contest` already carries `depth` and `max_depth` and
already returns a typed `Refusal` at the cap. **`order: written_only` is the *telhīs*** — counsel
removed from face-to-face exchange, so **neither party is seen to yield** — and its cost is exactly
the exchange in which unshared information would have been pooled.

### **public debate** — *no bench, and the only prize is standing*
```yaml
disposal: none · bench_basis: none · floor: open · records_dissent: n/a
venue_min_rank: settlement · term_required: false · appeal_basis: none
interposed: [] · order: free · registers: [all seven] · proofs: [record, testimony]
disposes: standing · verdict_reasons: n/a · stakes_grade: costly
```
**`disposal: none` is the key that makes it a different game entirely.** Nothing is settled; nobody is
bound; **what moves is what the floor holds about the speakers.** Fig. 23's epideictic. ⚠ **And it is
the row that most tests the design**, because a game that writes only standing must still be worth
playing — see `07_THE_GAME.md` §6, where the answer is that **standing is an input to every other
game's eligibility and reception**, so a public debate is how a person becomes able to enter rooms
they are currently refused at the door.

# PART F · THE THIRTEENTH GAME, AND THE ONES THIS STRUCTURE CANNOT HOST

> **`G.1.4`: a shape is a set of refusals, each with what pays for it. A design that cannot say what
> each refusal costs has not refused anything — it has merely not built something yet.**
>
> **And a parameter space with no inexpressible neighbour has not been bounded.** So the closure claim
> is tested in both directions: one game the study never describes, authored without a code change;
> and three the structure genuinely refuses, each with its price.

## F.1 · The thirteenth — an EXAMINATION, chosen because it is the hardest of the easy cases

Stage 3 `§E.1` names it in the sentence that sets the whole bar: *"a battle, a hearing, an examination
and two siblings arguing over a barn are the same call with different vocabularies."* **So an
examination is a case the architecture has already promised, and the study never describes one.**

```yaml
- id:              examination
  disposal:        bench          # the examiners dispose
  bench_basis:     determine
  floor:           admitted
  records_dissent: false
  venue_min_rank:  settlement
  term_required:   true           # an examination has a closing day
  appeal_basis:    none
  interposed:      [procedure, document]     # the sealed paper; the fixed form
  order:           scripted
  registers:       [restricted]
  proofs:          [testimony]    # ⚠ see below — this is where it strains
  disposes:        <tenure>       # the qualification conferred, and thus closable
  verdict_reasons: withheld
  stakes_grade:    costly
```

**It works, and it strains in exactly one place, which is the informative part.**

| | |
|---|---|
| ✅ **the candidate is both subject and sole party** | `bench ∩ parties = ∅` and `\|parties\| = 1`. **Nothing in the structure assumed two parties**, and the negotiation row's `alternating` order is what would have assumed it — a key, not a mechanism |
| ✅ **there is no adversary** | so no descent, no Fig. 27 ladder, no attack. **The absence needs no representation**: the ladder is descended by acts and nobody takes them |
| ✅ **`interposed: [document]`** | the sealed paper removes the examiner's knowledge of who wrote it, which **lowers latitude and therefore the person-effect** — which is what an anonymised examination is *for* |
| ⚠ **`proofs: [testimony]` is a stretch** | what an examination weighs is a **performance**, and `proofs` was derived from Fig. 6, which enumerates what a *forensic* proceeding admits. **Either `proofs` needs a `performance` member — a data edit — or the key is narrower than it looks.** Recorded as `10_LOOPS_AND_GAPS.md` `P-13`; **it is a roster edit, not a code change, so the closure claim survives it** |

> **Verdict: expressible, with one roster member added.** `ID-12` says a roster lives in data and
> changing one is a data edit, so this is the closure claim holding rather than bending. **And the
> strain was worth finding: it says `proofs` is a forensic-shaped key, and a design that had only
> tested it on forensic games would not have known.**

## F.2 · Three games this structure REFUSES, and what each refusal costs

### **(1) A disposal with no author — decision by lot, or a self-executing ordeal**

**Refused by `AX-1` and `T-b`.** A threshold may change what can be chosen; **it may never produce an
outcome**, because an outcome is what a decision produces and only persons decide. A lot that decides
a case is a non-person acting; an ordeal whose result *is* the verdict is a threshold producing one.

| | |
|---|---|
| **what it costs** | Athenian allotment; trial by ordeal as the law understood it; any sortition. **These are real institutions and the game cannot host them as written** |
| **the decomposition that recovers most of it** | an **ordeal is two games**: a physical contest at the seam, whose outcome deposits claims — and then a bench that **reads those claims and determines**. The iron is carried; the burn is witnessed; **a person still decides what it meant.** ⚠ That is arguably *better history* than the self-executing reading, and it is certainly better play, because it leaves somebody to persuade |
| **what is NOT recovered** | a lot that binds with nobody reading it. **If Jordan wants sortition, `AX-1` is the axiom to break, deliberately, and the breach should be stated as a chosen different game** — `G.1.2`: *break an axiom and you have chosen a different game; break a theorem and you have introduced a contradiction* |

### **(2) A shared transcript — common knowledge of what the room concluded**

**Refused by `AX-2`, and this is the largest refusal in the design.** There is no fact of the matter
about *what the room decided* apart from **what each person present now holds.** WITNESS deposits per
person, per channel; the document holder learns that the record changed and **not who changed it**;
the co-located learn attribution; the absent learn what they are told, when they are told.

| | |
|---|---|
| **what it costs** | any UI that shows *the verdict* as a fact; any quest state reading *the trial's outcome*; any two characters who can be assumed to agree about what happened. **A great deal of convenience** |
| **what is bought** | ⭐ **every mechanism the game has for politics.** A verdict can be **denied, misreported, concealed, and believed by the wrong people** — and none of those needs a mechanism, because the alternative was never representable. `§B.1` of Stage 3: *"a design with only effects has no politics"* |
| **and the honest residue** | **a player will want to know what the court decided, and cannot be told.** `07_THE_GAME.md` §4 is entirely about what is offered instead, and whether it is a better game or merely a more principled one. **It is the design's sharpest open question and it is not resolved by an axiom** |

### **(3) A proceeding that advances because time passed and nobody acted**

**Refused by `AX-5` and `T-c`** — a clock nobody wound. A session that moves to a second reading after
N days on its own is a fourth motion.

| | |
|---|---|
| **what it costs** | **almost nothing, and the recovery is the good part.** `T-n`: the opening act **declares its terms**, and MATTER matures what an act wound, citing the act that wound it |
| **what is bought** | *"a wound clock can be bribed, delayed, burned, or killed — bribe the clerk who set the term, burn the Record that carries it, kill the man who must renew it. An unwound clock is unbuyable, undelayable and unkillable, which is what a GM is."* **Every deadline in every proceeding becomes a target** |
| ⚠ **and it is not built** | `Tenure` has no `term` field in the tracer (`shape.py:2066-2091`). **The refusal is sound and its recovery is unimplemented.** `10_LOOPS_AND_GAPS.md` `P-04` |

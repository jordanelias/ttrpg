# PART A · THE ONE SENTENCE

> ## **THE PLAYER IS DECIDING WHAT TO SPEND AND WHAT TO KEEP, IN A ROOM WHOSE MIND THEY CANNOT SEE.**

Not *which argument is strongest.* The study is emphatic that this is not where the variance lives:
the scarce capacity is **inhibition** — it appears seven times down the fault column — and *"in most
of these the actor is not failing to perceive. The perception is available and does not govern the
action."*

**So the game is a spending game with an information problem**, and it has exactly four kinds of
decision:

| | the decision | why it is hard |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **whether to be there at all** | terminal, free if right, and it costs a scene out of five |
| **2** | **whether to descend** | descending lowers the obstacle **now** and concedes something the room can see, **forever** |
| **3** | **whether to spend a proof or hoard it** | an unused proof costs nothing; a spent one can be answered |
| **4** | **whether to say the thing** | the tempting move is a competent thing done too hard |

**Everything else in this document is those four, at different scales.**

# PART B · THE LOOP THE PLAYER IS IN

## B.1 · At the season scale — triage, which is the only reason the budget exists

`§C.2` of Stage 3: the budget counts **scenes**, ~5 per character per season, and *"triage is the
mechanism the budget exists to create. At one scene per person a King's scarcity is identical to a
copyist's, and nobody ever chooses what to leave undone — which is most of what high office is."*

```
A date is set somewhere.  You learn of it — or you do not.
   │
   ├─ you hold a claim that it is happening ──── you may go
   ├─ you hold a claim, and it is WRONG ──────── you may go to the wrong place
   └─ you hold nothing ───────────────────────── it happens without you
   │
   ▼
GO?          costs a scene, and the travel to get there
SEND?        an advocate goes.  Lower latitude · you cannot concede in absentia ·
             a defeat does not touch you · a lie you told still does
NEITHER?     it proceeds.  You will hear about it, from somebody, later, at their
             confidence, and possibly wrong
```

⭐ **Sending an advocate is not a separate mechanic. It is somebody else committing to your
disposition** (`03_PARAMETERS.md` §C.2), and Fig. 24's whole absorbs/cannot-absorb column falls out of
*consequences attach to the actor*. **The player's decision is a real one because the trade is real:
protection and distributive advantage, bought with joint gain and with the deal happening at all.**

## B.2 · Inside the proceeding — one turn, and what is on the screen

The nested run reaches the player in the arrangement's order. **What they may take is a Candidate set
formed from what they hold** — `AX-2`, and the same function an NPC uses.

| the player may | which is |
|---|---|
| **press the matter** at a rung, in a register | `speak` |
| **produce a proof** they hold | `tell` |
| **ask** — act on someone to produce a read | `interview` |
| **take or abandon a position** | `commit` / `repudiate` |
| **release** something they could have held | `release` — and this is the close |
| ⭐ **say nothing** | **not an act.** It emits nothing, writes nothing, and is sometimes correct |

# PART C · THE FOUR DECISIONS, AS MECHANISMS

## C.1 · Whether to be there — the terminal decision that is free if right

Row 0 is **terminal** and the study calls it *"the largest lever in the corpus."* Its faults: the
meeting that converts a negotiable difference into a question of honour; committing early to a
faction; intimacy too fast or too slow; **a room already lost.**

**Mechanically it is the absence of `move`, which costs nothing and emits nothing** — and that is
exactly right: *"the unused proof, the unspent objection to the forum… cost nothing; the corpus holds
that they are hoarded."*

⚠ **AND THE GAME MUST NOT TELL THE PLAYER THE ROOM IS LOST.** That is the room's disposition, which
`AX-2` forbids. **What the player has instead is what they were told, by whom, and how long ago** —
which is why `interview` before the day is a use of a scene, and why *the envoy who arrives early and
observes* (Nizām al-Mulk's envoy, requirement row 3) is a strategy rather than flavour.

## C.2 · Whether to descend — the core tactical decision, and the one the corpus is loudest about

```
   the matter stands at CONJECTURE — "it did not happen"
   the obstacle is high, because you are denying a thing they may prove
        │
        │  DESCEND?  the obstacle falls.  You may carry the room at DEFINITION.
        │            and every person present deposits a claim that you
        │            stopped denying it.
        ▼
   DEFINITION — "it was a gift, not a bribe"
```

> **Every descent is visible and is read as a concession that was not announced.** *"A speaker who
> begins at 'it did no harm' has conceded the fact before anyone asked him to."*

**And it is one-way within the proceeding**, because the claims are already in the ledgers. **The
player is spending something irrecoverable to make the next roll easier — which is the cleanest
possible expression of the study's whole account of a proceeding.**

⚠ **AND THE DESCENT IS FREE WHEN NOBODY IS WATCHING**, because the cost is the witnessing. **A closed
room is a room where you can concede cheaply**, and an open floor is where you cannot. **That is not a
rule; it is `floor:` interacting with WITNESS**, and it is `00_DERIVATION.md` `D-6` — a live
prediction, flagged because it may be a defect rather than a feature.

## C.3 · Whether to spend a proof — and why hoarding is correct more often than it feels

**An unused proof is FREE. A spent one can be answered, and a spent weak one makes a strong one
worse.**

▣ The dilution effect, with the boundary the corpus does not state: **under high elaboration, adding
weak arguments to strong depresses the whole; under LOW elaboration, the sheer number of arguments
works as a cue and additions help.** So *whether to pile on* has **opposite correct answers depending
on how hard the room is thinking** — and how hard the room is thinking is a property of the
arrangement, which the player can see.

⭐ **This is a decision with no dominant option, decided by a parameter the player can read.** It is
the design's best answer to `§0.06`'s *E* test: *allows the player to intuit complex outcomes from
simple choices.*

**And the mirror test is the player's, not the engine's:** *before advancing an argument, ask whether
your opponent could advance its mirror image.* **The engine does not check this.** A symmetric
argument simply does not move the room, and the player who works out why has learned something the
game never told them.

## C.4 · Whether to say the thing — and the design's obligation to make it tempting

> **If the overshooting move is obviously bad, the game has not modelled the study's central finding.
> `S3`: the fault is a competent thing done too hard, too soon, or too visibly.**

**So the tempting moves must be genuinely tempting, and the design's job is to put the cost where the
corpus puts it: later, elsewhere, or invisible at the moment of choosing.**

| the tempting move | what it buys, now | what it costs, and when |
|---|---|---|
| **the detailed denial** | it answers the charge, in full, and the room hears it answered | ⚠ **not a memory effect** — the study is explicit that *"repeating a claim while denying it raises belief"* has **repeatedly failed to replicate**. **The cost is standing**: a long defence reads as anxiety and cedes the floor to the accuser's framing |
| **the joke** | ⭐ **the highest return in the corpus** — it redistributes standing **without conceding the point**, and no other device does that | **the highest variance.** The joke against the wretched, against what the room loves, or that costs the speaker his dignity — and *the joke requiring explanation* |
| **displayed anger** | ⚠ **it works, and the corpus is wrong about why.** Negotiators concede more to an angry counterpart | it works **only** against a counterpart who cannot afford an impasse; against one with alternatives it buys impasse and relational damage. **The requirement is control, not calm** |
| **the frank criticism** | it is true, and it is the thing that needs saying | **the four conjuncts of Fig. 26**, each of which fails in a *named* way. Miss one and it is priced as an attack |
| **speaking first** | the initiative, and the framing | *"opening into a house that has not settled"* — costly, and **tolerance of dead air is not a perception**: one can tell the room is not ready; **the failure is beginning anyway** |
| **descending early** | the obstacle falls | you conceded before anyone asked |

⭐ **THE DESIGN RULE THAT FOLLOWS, AND IT IS THE ONE MOST EASILY LOST IN IMPLEMENTATION:** *the cost of
an overshoot is a WRITE to standing and to other people's ledgers, not a lower roll.* A player who
sees `-2 to your check` has been told the answer. **A player who sees the room hear them, and finds
out three seasons later what the room concluded, has played the study's game.**

# PART D · WHAT THE PLAYER IS SHOWN — the hardest constraint in the design

`§C.11` — the explanation contract — governs this, and it forecloses the obvious formulation by name:
*"the player is not an actor, so `AX-2` does not reach them"* fails immediately, **because the player
CONTROLS a person.** For an NPC, `choose` is the decision procedure; **for a player character the
player IS the decision procedure.**

> ### **THE SPLIT IS ON WHAT IS SHOWN, NOT ON WHO IS LOOKING.**
> **The engine owes the ARITHMETIC of what the character already holds, and nothing else.**

| ✅ owed | ⛔ forbidden |
|---|---|
| the claims in your ledger about this matter — their **source**, **confidence**, and **when** | the bench's leaning, in any form |
| which of them are firsthand and which you were told | the other party's reservation point |
| your own convictions and stances, and how they weigh the axes | your odds, your margin, or a preview of the band |
| the **arrangement** — it is how the room is physically and procedurally set up, and it is visible | who is lying |
| **the rung the matter stands at** — it was witnessed | what an absent person knows |
| who is present | anything from a resolver-side Query |

```
explain(p : PersonInterior, v : Value) -> Derivation        -- person_q. NO World parameter.
```

**By `T-f` it cannot reach `World`**, so it cannot leak a resolver-side Query even by accident. **And
it may not walk `causes[] → Act.actor`**, because that path reaches the attribution `T-d` forbids.

⚠ **AND *HIDDEN ACTORS ARE HIDDEN IN THEIR EXISTENCE, NEVER IN THEIR ARITHMETIC.*** *"Your standing
before this room fell, from a cause you cannot name"* is **admissible** — the character can see the
fall. What they are additionally given is **that it has an author they do not know**, and *that is
what makes investigation worth a scene.*

## D.0 · The two presentation rules — **MOVED HERE 2026-09-06, because this part owns the surface**

⚠ **These were written in `06_RESOLUTION.md` §B.1c, which is the wrong home.** That section owns
*what `discernment` does*; **this part owns what a player is shown**, and two files stating rules about
the same screen is `T-k` one level up. They are reproduced here in full and §B.1c now points at them.

**Jordan:** *"the limitations of the character can impose limitations on the player. with the right UI,
this will feel natural."*

**That is licensed by the table above rather than excepted from it.** The engine owes *the arithmetic
of what the character already holds*. **A read is something the character holds** — so it is owed, and
it is owed **as they hold it**, including when it is false.

> ### **1 · ⛔ A MISREAD IS PRESENTED INDISTINGUISHABLY FROM A TRUE READ.**
> No hedge, no *"you are not certain"*, no confidence bar. **The moment the UI marks a read as
> unreliable, the player routes around the character and the fog is gone.** The player learns their
> character reads rooms badly the way a person does — **by being wrong and finding out afterwards** —
> never by being shown a number.
>
> ### **2 · Nothing is greyed out.**
> Every lawful move stays selectable. What differs between a sharp character and a dull one is **what
> the room is DESCRIBED as**, not what the list contains.

⭐ **AND WHAT MAKES THE LIMITATION READ AS CHARACTER RATHER THAN AS PUNISHMENT IS THAT IT CAN BE
PLAYED AGAINST.** A player who suspects their own read can send an advocate whose `discernment` is
better, spend a season reaching a bench member before the sitting, or go and find out what the room
was told. **A limitation you can act against is characterization; one you can only suffer is a gate.**

⚠ **RULE 1 HAS NO ENFORCER, AND THAT IS THE HONEST STATE.** It is a constraint on a renderer nobody
has written, in a repository whose §0.05 ruling says prose is not a mechanism. **What would make it
one is a single presentation call that takes a claim and CANNOT take its confidence** — the same
shape as `explain`'s missing `World`. Until that exists, rule 1 is a convention, stated at that
strength. Registered `P-46`.

## D.1 · The honest residue — the design's sharpest open question

> **A player at a trial will want to know how it is going, and cannot be told.**

**This is not resolved by an axiom and should not be presented as though it were.** What is offered
instead:

1. **The arithmetic of their own hand** — every claim they hold, graded, sourced, dated. *This is a lot.*
2. **The rung**, which is public and is the single best summary of where a case stands.
3. **A read they get for free, and one they can go and buy.** In the room, `discernment` returns *who
   decides* and *how the body is set*, at their character's fidelity — **which may be wrong and which
   they cannot tell is wrong** (`06_RESOLUTION.md` §B.1c). Out of the room, an investigation act buys
   a better answer at the price of a season and of being seen asking. ⚠ **That second half is the
   `FI` lane's to build** — this proposal withdrew its own investigation rows on 2026-09-06
   (`04_VERBS.md` §B.3) — **so today only the free half exists**, and item 3 is weaker than it reads
   until `FI` lands the acts.
4. **The room's reaction as fiction, not as numbers** — what people did, which is public, rather than
   what they think, which is not.

⚠ **AND WHETHER THAT IS A BETTER GAME OR MERELY A MORE PRINCIPLED ONE IS NOT SETTLED HERE.** It is
the design's largest experiential risk, and `11_NERS.md` scores it under **E** rather than hiding it.
**The strongest argument for it is negative and worth stating plainly: a design that shows the odds
has made the study's central finding unplayable, because inhibition is only a decision when the
consequence of failing to inhibit is not yet visible.**

# PART E · LEGIBILITY WITHOUT A NARRATOR — three ways to learn what happened, and they differ

There is no GM and no transcript. An Event carries **no actor, no target, no subject**; what a person
ends up holding is what **their channel** minted (`§C.6`).

| your relation to the hearing | what you end up holding |
|---|---|
| **you were there** (`co_located`) | the changes **and the attribution** — you saw who did it |
| **you hold the record** (`document_key`) | ⭐ **that it changed, and NOT who changed it.** You know a finding was entered; you do not know who entered it |
| **you were told** (`chronicle`) | what the teller held, at **their** confidence, and it may be false |
| **it was about you and you were absent** | ⭐ **NOTHING, until somebody tells you** |

> ### **SO A PLAYER CAN BE CONDEMNED AND NOT KNOW IT, AND THAT NEEDS NO MECHANISM.**
> It is what happens when the subject did not travel, the floor was closed, and nobody has yet had a
> reason to `tell` them. **Finding out is a scene. Finding out too late is the game.**

⚠ **AND COVERT INFLUENCE NEEDS NO FLAG.** An act performed where nobody is co-located has **no
firsthand attribution anywhere**; false attribution is a `tell` carrying a claim whose subject is the
wrong person. **A bench that was reached before the sitting leaves no record of having been reached.**

# PART F · THE TWELVE, AS GAMES

**Each is the same structure with different parameters, and the parameter that dominates is named.**

### **negotiation** — *the game of what you will trade*
`disposal: mutual`, no bench, no floor, **no interposition, so all nine capacities are live.** Nobody
to persuade but the person opposite. **The characteristic decision is row 6 — fixing the concession
before you are asked for it** — and the characteristic failure is conceding reactively under pressure.
**Terminal risk:** none inside the room; **row 0** — the meeting that should not have happened.

### **negotiation through envoys** — *the same game, played by someone else*
**One key changed and the whole thing inverts.** You are not in the room. You get the price and the
protection; you get the impasse; **and you cannot concede, because only a principal can.** The
player's game moves up a level: **what instructions, and how much rope.**

### **arbitration** — *the game before the game*
`appeal_basis: none` makes the finding final, so **the real contest is over who arbitrates**, and that
is a term of the opening act. **A player who wins the choice of arbiter has usually won.**

### **legal trial** — *the ladder, in public*
`floor: open` makes every descent expensive; `interposed: [person]` puts an advocate between you and
defeat; **`appeal_basis: determine` means losing is not final.** The characteristic decision is **C.2**,
and the characteristic terminal risk is **the discovered lie, which no advocate absorbs.**

### **tribunal** — *the game against a standing body*
`interposed: [office]` means the tribunal criticises as a **duty** — so the affront is absorbed **and
the criticism is discounted.** `appeal_basis: none`. **The characteristic decision is whether to
contest at all or to accept and preserve the relation.**

### **interrogation** — *the game you cannot win*
`bench ∩ parties = {the questioner}`. No third party. `order: scripted`, `registers: restricted`,
`verdict_reasons: withheld`. **All that is left is Fig. 27's ladder, descended under pressure, with
nobody to hand the error back to.** ⭐ **The player's real decision is C.3 inverted: what NOT to say**,
and the win condition is not winning — **it is reaching the end still holding something.**

### **inquisition hearing** — *the game where being the subject is the loss*
An interrogation with a bench and `term_required: false` — **an authored indefiniteness.** The
terminal failure is not losing; **it is having been made the subject at all.** `AX-6` is what keeps it
playable: **somebody set this going and can be reached.**

### **excommunication deliberation** — *the game about someone who is not there*
The subject did not travel. **They may not know it is happening.** For the player as a bench member,
this is the purest test of C.4 — **you are deciding about an absent person on claims you cannot check,
and your own convictions are doing most of the work.** For the player as the subject, it is Part E:
**you find out, or you do not.**

### **parliamentary debate** — *the game of many*
`proofs: []` — **there is no evidence and no ladder**; the fact is not in dispute. Fig. 14: **many who
must be persuaded means eloquence matters and consequence does not.** `records_dissent: true` puts
losers on the record. **The characteristic decision is which of many to address**, and the
characteristic failure is arguing what was right to a body asking what it will cost.

### **council of state** — *the game of the closed room*
The same shape, four keys different, **and the trade inverts.** `records_dissent: false` — the
consensus requirement — **discourages the disagreement from forming at all.** ⭐ **The player's
characteristic dilemma is the hidden profile: you know something nobody else does, and saying it makes
you the one who broke the consensus.** *What only one member knows stays unsaid, and the body chooses
the inferior option with nobody having made a mistake.*

### **audience / embassy** — *the game where the argument is not the game*
Three interpositions; the lowest latitude on the board. **Relative standing is settled by the ceremony
before anyone speaks** — Liudprand seated below the Bulgarian envoy, understanding correctly that *the
seating was the negotiation*. **The player's decisions are almost all row 0 and row 1**, and the
characteristic move is Liudprand's actual one: **lose the room, and change the room** — say nothing
here, and write for an audience whose opinion you can still affect.

### **appeal to a higher authority** — *the nested game*
`order: written_only` — the *telhīs*. **Neither party is seen to yield**, and the cost is exactly the
exchange in which unshared information would have been pooled. **The depth cap is a term somebody
declared**, so *how many appeals this matter admits* is itself contestable.

### **public debate** — *the game with no prize but standing*
`disposal: none`. Nothing is settled and nobody is bound. ⭐ **And it is worth playing because standing
is an input to every other game's reception and to the order in which you are called** — so a public
debate is **how a person becomes able to enter rooms they are currently refused at the door.**

# PART G · THE PLAYER AS ADJUDICATOR AND AS AUDIENCE

## G.1 · Sitting on the bench is a game, not a cutscene

You hold a seat whose remit covers the matter. **You must `determine`, `via` that seat, with your name
on it — and `AX-6` means it can be revoked, which means it can be held against you.**

| what you have | what you do not |
|---|---|
| the claims **you** hold about the matter, sourced and graded | any fact of the matter |
| your convictions, which weight the axes | any indication that they are biasing you |
| what was said in front of you, if you were there | what was said before you arrived |

**The decisions:** determine on what you hold, or **`interview` first and spend the turn**; determine now
or let the date lapse (**not determining is not acting, and the vacant date is already a mechanism**);
determine against your convictions because the evidence is overwhelming, or against the evidence
because your convictions are what you are.

> ⭐ **AND `AX-3` IS WHAT MAKES THIS A GAME RATHER THAN A SUM.** Evidence moves what you hold **true**.
> Argument and consequence move what you hold **right**. **They are different stores, they move at
> different steps, and they can disagree.** A judge who believes the accused did it and believes it
> was not wrong is a coherent position the design can represent — and a design with one
> guilt-conviction scalar could not.

## G.2 · Being in the audience is being a source

You hold no `commit` and no covering seat. **You cannot dispose of anything — and you leave holding
firsthand claims with attribution**, which nobody who was not there can have.

**So the audience's game is what you do with them afterwards.** `tell` is a verb; a claim you hold is
a proof somebody needs; and **you may be the only person who can say who was in the room.** ⚠ **And
anyone may `speak`** — eligibility is `own`, never a seat — **so an audience member becomes a party
the moment they take a turn.** *There is no spectator mode.*

# PART H · WHAT MAKES TWO PLAYTHROUGHS DIFFER

`§0.06`'s **R**: *provides emergent and compelling narrative hooks WITHOUT player involvement*, and
*allows creativity and variety in approach and resolution.*

| the source of variation | needs a mechanism? |
|---|---|
| **who travelled** | no — each person's own decision from their own frozen view |
| **what each person holds, and what is false in it** | no — `AX-2` and the deposit channels |
| **the order** | no — a parameter, and at `rank` it is a public ruling about the people who came |
| **which rung it settled at** | no — the descents that were taken |
| **who was reached beforehand** | no — an act with no co-located witness leaves no attribution |
| **whether anybody bothered** | no — **a convened proceeding nobody attends is a legitimate outcome** |

> ### **AND THE HALF OF R WITH NO PLAYER IN IT IS THE HALF THIS DESIGN IS BEST AT.**
> Dates fire; benches resolve; NPCs travel or do not; matters are pressed, descended, determined, and
> appealed — **all of it through the same acts a player takes, with nothing scripted and nobody
> narrating.** The world holds hearings when nobody is watching, and **a player learns of them the
> same way they learn of anything: somebody tells them, and may be wrong.**

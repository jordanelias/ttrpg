# 15 · WHY IT IS A GAME AND NOT A FLOWCHART — and the one change that decides it

## Status: **PROPOSED (2026-09-06). HELD BACK IN FULL. NOTHING RATIFIES ON MERGE.**

> **Jordan, 2026-09-06:** *"So if we know that we are working on a game structure with the proceedings
> as games, how do we make this a game rather than just following a flowchart for optimal gains?"*

---

# PART A · ⚠ **AS SPECIFIED IN THE PRECEDING FILES, IT IS A FLOWCHART. HERE IS THE SOLVER.**

**The honest thing is to write the exploit out, because a design that cannot state its own solved line
has not looked for one.**

```
each turn:
  1. list the apt speech kinds        -- genre × rung × registers[] × proofs[]   ALL VISIBLE
  2. for each, compute margin = pool − Ob
        pool = brought + conduct × latitude            -- MY OWN, KNOWN
        Ob   = their score/2 ± latitude ± reception
                              ± rung ± register ± proofs told   -- ALL VISIBLE OR MINE
  3. descend a rung whenever the Ob drop exceeds the concession's cost
  4. spend the proof with the best Ob-per-cost ratio
  5. pick the register with the best fit
  6. repeat until the ladder bottoms out
```

> ### **THE DEFECT IN ONE LINE: EVERY INPUT TO THE OBSTACLE IS EITHER MINE OR PRINTED ON THE ROOM.**
> The arrangement is a data row the player can read. The rung is public. The proofs are in my hand.
> The opposition's score is a number. **Nothing in the obstacle is hidden, so the optimum is
> computable, so the player computes it.**

⚠ **AND `AX-2` DOES NOT SAVE IT, WHICH IS THE PART WORTH NOTICING.** The design has hidden information
everywhere — you cannot see the bench's mind. **But nothing hidden enters the resolution.** Hidden
information that no outcome depends on is **decorative**: it makes the fiction opaque and leaves the
arithmetic transparent. **A player does not need to know what the bishop believes, because what the
bishop believes does not appear in the margin.**

# PART B · WHAT ACTUALLY MAKES SOMETHING A GAME, AND WHICH ONES THIS HAS

Five conditions. **The design has two, half of a third, and is missing the one that matters most.**

| | condition | state |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **the optimum is not computable, because inputs are hidden AND load-bearing** | ⚠ **MISSING.** PART C |
| **2** | **irreversible commitment before the uncertainty resolves** | ⭐ **HAVE IT.** The descent |
| **3** | **the cost lands outside the current frame** | ⚠ **HALF.** PART D |
| **4** | **incommensurable goods — no single quantity to maximise** | **HAVE IT, thinly.** PART D |
| **5** | **other minds reasoning about you** | **downstream of 1** — an NPC whose scoring function you can reconstruct is a lookup table |

# PART C · ⭐ **THE ONE CHANGE: THE OBSTACLE MUST DEPEND ON WHAT THE ROOM HOLDS**

> ### **`reception` MUST BE COMPOSED FROM THE HEARERS' OWN LEDGERS AND CONVICTIONS — WHICH ARE
> ### RESOLVER-SIDE, AND WHICH `T-f` MAKES UNREACHABLE FROM ANY DECISION.**

**Not from a printed "standing" score. From what these particular people, in this particular room,
hold about you and hold to be right.**

```
Ob = their score/2
   ± reception( THE HEARERS' CLAIMS ABOUT THE SPEAKER,        ← hidden. Resolver-side.
                THE HEARERS' CONVICTIONS ON THIS MATTER'S AXES ) ← hidden. Resolver-side.
   ± latitude ± the rung ± register fit ± proofs told
```

**Three things change the moment this lands, and they are the whole answer to Jordan's question.**

### C.1 · The optimum stops being computable — for a reason inside the fiction

A bench that heard a rumour about you last season is a **harder** room, and **you do not know they
heard it.** You cannot compute the margin because **you cannot see the term.** This is `AX-2` moving
from decoration to load-bearing in one edit.

### C.2 · ⭐ Elicitation becomes an information economy, which is what the study says it is

`interview` currently *"emits a degree nothing consumes"* (`P-05`) — **and that is why it is
pointless.** Once the Ob depends on what they hold, **buying a read is buying an estimate of the
obstacle**, and every clause of the *Guiguzi* loop earns its keep:

| the act's declared cost | what it now means in play |
|---|---|
| **it costs a turn** in a proceeding with a finite order | you are trading a **speech** for a **read** |
| ⭐ **"they learn what you are asking"** | **asking about the bribe tells them you suspect the bribe.** The information purchase is *itself* a move in the game, visible to the person you bought it from |
| ⭐ **`Read` and `Misread` emit IDENTICALLY** | you cannot tell whether you bought truth. **Confidence is not accuracy** — which is the study's own hazard on capacity 1, in the engine |
| *"their `SAID` row — which may be a lie"* | the counter-play is **feeding a false read**, and it needs no deception mechanic |

> **So `P-05` is not the largest hole because it blocks discovery. It is the largest hole because
> without it the design has no reason for anyone to ask anybody anything** — and the study's one
> evidenced capacity is the one that makes proceedings a game rather than a calculation.

### C.3 · The bench stops being a difficulty number and becomes people

Two tribunals with identical arrangement rows now **play differently, because different people are
sitting on them** — and *who is sitting* came from conferrals, deaths and travel nobody scripted.
⭐ **This is also the ruling of 2026-09-06 arriving in the resolver:** *enacted by characters OF the
world.* **A bench composed of a Query over seats is characters of the world only if their interiors
reach the outcome.** Until they do, they are a number wearing names.

# PART D · THE THREE THAT COST NOTHING TO STRENGTHEN

## D.1 · The commitment is already irreversible — keep it that way

**The descent is the design's best game object and it needs no repair.** You lower the obstacle **now**
by conceding something **permanently**, in front of witnesses, **before the draw tells you whether you
needed to.** That is a real decision under uncertainty, and no solver removes it — a solver can only
compute the expected value of a gamble it still has to take.

⚠ **The one thing that would ruin it: showing the player the post-descent margin before they choose.**
`eff_ob` is display-only by ruling; **this design goes further and does not display it at all.** See
PART E.

## D.2 · The cost must land outside the room — and the season loop already provides it

| the cost | where it lands |
|---|---|
| **a scene, out of ~5** | *"triage is the mechanism the budget exists to create"* |
| **a proof spent** | it is answerable now and **not available at the next occasion** |
| **an enemy made** | a person you charged is a person who **convenes something next season** |
| ⭐ **standing** | it is the `reception` term at **every future room** — so today's overshoot is next year's harder obstacle, **and you will not be told which room it cost you in** |

> **This is why a proceeding cannot be optimised in isolation even if its arithmetic were open: the
> quantity you are maximising is not in this room.**

## D.3 · The goods are genuinely incommensurable, and the design should stop pretending otherwise

**Win the matter · keep your standing · keep the relationship · keep the proof · keep the scene.**
Five currencies, no exchange rate, and the corpus is explicit that the last one is hoarded. **A design
that gave them an exchange rate would have built the flowchart on purpose.**

---

# PART E · THE UI RULE THAT FOLLOWS, AND IT IS THE EASIEST THING TO GET WRONG

> ### **NEVER SHOW THE PLAYER A NUMBER THEY COULD OPTIMISE AGAINST.**

| ⛔ never shown | ✅ shown instead |
|---|---|
| the obstacle, effective or otherwise | **the arrangement** — who sits, what is interposed, what may be proved here |
| the margin, or a preview of the band | **the rung**, which is public because it was witnessed |
| the bench's disposition, in any form | **your own claims about them** — sourced, dated, graded, and possibly wrong |
| a percentage | **what you were told, by whom, and how long ago** |

**`eff_ob()` exists, is P-232-floored, and says in its own docstring that it is display-only. The
finding to look for is a caller RESOLVING on it. This design adds a second rule: do not DISPLAY it
either** — because a displayed obstacle is a solved proceeding, whatever the resolver does with it.

⚠ **AND THE HONEST COST, WHICH IS THE DESIGN'S LARGEST EXPERIENTIAL RISK:** a player who cannot see
the obstacle cannot tell a bold line from a stupid one **until afterwards**. **That is the study's own
subject** — *"the perception is available and does not govern the action"* — but it is also how a game
becomes frustrating rather than tense. **The mitigation is not a number; it is that the world tells
you afterwards, through people, and that you can go and ask.**

# PART F · THE RESIDUE — what stays solvable, and why that is acceptable

**A determined player will still play strong lines, and should.** What the changes above buy is that
the line is:

| | |
|---|---|
| **not transferable** | a walkthrough cannot tell you what to do, because it does not know **what your character holds, what this bench heard, or which axes they weight** |
| **not stable** | the same arrangement with different people is a different obstacle, and the people change every season without anyone scripting it |
| **not free to compute** | the inputs cost turns to acquire, and acquiring them is itself witnessed |

> ### **AND THE DEEPEST ANSWER IS THE STUDY'S, NOT THIS DESIGN'S.**
> *"This study holds that judgment is what decides proceedings and that nearly every tradition
> surveyed declares it irreducible to rule — and then presents a list of capacities in a table. **The
> form asserts something the content denies.**"*
>
> **A subsystem is a stronger form of the same assertion, and the resolution is the same one the study
> reached: do not try to make judgment computable. Make the situation genuinely underdetermined, and
> let the player's judgment be the thing that is scarce.** The flowchart is not defeated by hiding the
> arithmetic. **It is defeated by making the arithmetic depend on other people's minds.**

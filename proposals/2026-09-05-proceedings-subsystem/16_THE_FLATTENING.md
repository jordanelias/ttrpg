> ### WHY THIS PASS EXISTS, AND WHY THE FIRST ONE WAS NOT IT
> `03_PARAMETERS.md` PART A maps each of the twenty-seven figures **to a parameter** — one row per
> figure. **That is a transcription: it preserves the figures' own carving and never asks what they
> are made of.** It is also why three of them were flattened without anyone noticing (`13_ADVERSARIAL.md`
> #17) — a one-row-per-figure mapping can silently drop a figure's interior and still look complete.
>
> **Flattening asks the opposite question.** Decompose all twenty-seven into atoms — one node, one
> branch, one cell, one rung, one condition — tag each, and count what recurs. **Every atom has to land
> somewhere, so a dropped one is visible.** That is `§0`'s *build bottom-up from primitives*, run for
> the first time in this directory.

## D.2 · The recurrence caveat, which the pass supplied against itself

⚠ **Twenty-six recurrence groups met the bar, and the pass warns that seven of the largest are not
independent convergence** — they are *"the same claim deliberately restated"* wherever a fault table
needs the capacity it is missing. **That is a property of how tightly the study cross-references
itself, not evidence that nine independent lines converge on nine capacities.**

**Three groups are a different and more interesting kind** (7, 10, 21): the *same historical material*
— the inverse-scale parable, the four-provenance matrix, the Japanese/Carolingian contrast — **pressed
into service for more than one argument.** ⭐ **An example serving several claims is evidence that the
thing underneath it is more primitive than any of them**, which is exactly what a primitive census is
looking for, and it is why groups 7, 10 and 21 informed PART D more than the six larger ones did.

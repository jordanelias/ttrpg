# Figure Atoms — flattened decomposition of the 27 figures

**Total atoms: 591.**

**Method.** Each of the twenty-seven figures in `09-figures.md` (cross-checked against the fifteen-step requirement table in `04-part-III-actor.md`, which several figures cite by row) was decomposed into its smallest constituent assertions — one node of a tree, one branch, one cell of a matrix, one row of a fault table, one condition of a gate, one item of a list, one arrow of a cycle. Nothing was summarised into a single sentence per figure; a fault table with N cells yields N atoms, a matrix with N populated cells yields N atoms plus a note where a cell is empty and that emptiness is itself informative. Purely editorial/bibliographic notes about this study's own drafting history ("an earlier version argued X and was wrong," "restored," "rebuilt here") were folded into the content claim they carry when one exists, and otherwise not separately atomized, since the task is the corpus's assertions about standing, persuasion and the actor — not this document's revision history. Every atom carries the six required fields; `source` is `—` wherever the figure names no tradition, author or study-section for that cell. Two atoms (Fig. 3, Fig. 11 — both "the discovered lie, not the lie itself") would not sit inside a single one of the eleven kind tags and are marked `AMBIGUOUS (ACT/PERCEPTION)`, per the task's instruction not to force them.

**What this recurrence count shows, and does not show.** Several of the largest groups (1, 2, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12) are not independent discoveries — they are the same claim deliberately restated at its point of first appearance, in the capacity map (Fig. 12), in the five-canons audit (Fig. 22), and again wherever a fault table's cell needs the capacity it is missing. That repetition is a property of how tightly this study cross-references its own figures, not evidence that nine independent lines converge on nine capacities. A few groups (14, 15, 17, 23, 25) are genuine internal recurrences where one figure compresses a claim that another states in full. And at least three groups (7, 10, 21) recur because the *same historical material* (the inverse-scale parable, the four-provenance matrix, the Japanese/Carolingian contrast) is pressed into service for more than one argument — which is a different and more interesting kind of recurrence than restatement, since it shows the study's own examples are overloaded across its claims.

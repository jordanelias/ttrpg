# PART I — The eight findings

Each section has the same shape: the **Finding**, stated once; the **Derivation**, naming which of the three sources — corpus, evidence, interrogation — support it and which does not; what it **withdraws**, since every finding here replaced an earlier claim of this study's; what it **requires** of an actor and where in Parts II and III that requirement is worked out; its **limit**; and a **crosswalk** to the seven corpus-doctrines that the earlier version of this study organized itself around.

The order is the order of dependence. S1 licenses the actor as an object of study at all. S2 says where he operates and how much room he has. S3, S4 and S5 say what he does and what he fails at. S6 says whether he can learn it. S7 says where the corpus, and this study, go quiet. S8 is a single test that runs through the whole of it.

---

## S1. The person is large and psychometrically opaque

### Finding

Individual differences account for between a quarter and a half of the variance in negotiation outcomes, and the traits that can be measured account for almost none of that share. The corpus's intuition — that some people are simply better at this — is correct, and correct by a wide margin. The modern project of saying *which* people is unfinished, and the reason it is unfinished is itself informative: it looked for dispositions where the differences live in step-level behaviour. Anyone who reads the failure of trait measurement as a verdict on the actor has made a category error, and an earlier version of this study made it.

### Derivation

**Evidence.** The decisive design is not a trait study at all. Elfenbein, Curhan, Eisenkraft, Shirako and Baccaro (2008) rotated negotiators across multiple counterparts and used the Social Relations Model to partition the variance directly, rather than measuring traits first and hoping they predicted. Individual differences accounted for 25 per cent of objective performance in distributive bargaining, 46 per cent in integrative bargaining, and 19 per cent of subjective value; a follow-up that coded transcripts, linguistic style and nonverbal behaviour reported 52 per cent; a twins study in 2018 partitioned the same way against dyadic interaction effects.

Then the pincer. In the same paper, a battery of existing trait measures revealed few reliable associations with the consistent individual differences the design had just demonstrated. The effect is large, stable across partners, and does not sit where the instruments were pointed.

The broader trait picture is better than that suggests and still modest. Sharma, Bottom and Elfenbein's meta-analysis (2013) overturned an older consensus that individual differences barely matter in negotiation: cognitive ability, emotional intelligence and most of the Big Five predicted at least one outcome, conscientiousness excepted. One moderator in that analysis matters more than the main effects. **Field data showed stronger effects than laboratory studies.** Laboratory negotiations are strong situations by construction, and the place where evidence about the person is cheapest to gather is the place designed to remove him.

**Corpus.** The corpus addresses a single reader — a prince, a son, a troupe, an heir — and possesses no comparative vocabulary of individual difference, because it has no population to compare. Where it describes a person it gives a normative role-portrait: Quintilian's good man skilled in speaking, Castiglione's courtier, the ideal grand vizier of the *Āsafnāme*, the *junzi*. That is a specification, not a distribution. And where the corpus does theorize individual difference, it theorizes **fixity**: Machiavelli's claim that men cannot change their natures because past success habituates them to the method that produced it; Ibn Khaldūn's decay of inherited prestige across roughly four generations; Han Feizi's Mi Zixia, in which merit is not the relevant variable at all. Zeami is the exception. A large, stable, unexplained effect is exactly what this looks like from inside a tradition with no means of measuring it.

**Interrogation.** The response within the negotiation literature to its own pincer was itself the step-level turn: stop measuring dispositions, code what the consistently good negotiators actually do. That is the method of Part III, arrived at independently by the researchers who created the problem it solves.

### Withdraws

That the failure of "social astuteness" to predict task performance in the political-skill meta-analysis showed that reading people does not matter. It is a self-report measure, tested post hoc, against aggregate job performance in ordinary organizational roles — a low-discretion setting, and one where the pincer predicts exactly such a null.

That two independent literatures agreed on a situationist reading. They were one literature — trait measurement — and one design artefact — the laboratory as a strong situation.

More broadly, the situationist drift that this study fell into once it began importing outcome evidence, in which a series of null results for named traits was read as a verdict on the contribution of the person.

### Requires

Part III exists because of S1. Every capacity listed there is a behavioural residue of a variance that trait measurement could not find, and the requirement analysis is the form the question has to take once the disposition question has failed.

### Limit

Negotiation, executive performance and the effects of national leaders are proxies. Nothing located measures person-effects in a tribunal, an embassy or a parliament. The transfer to those settings is inference, and Part 0.6 states it once.

### Crosswalk

New. No corpus-doctrine corresponds, because this is what a method organized around recurrence could not see.

## S2. Discretion is the join between the scenario and the actor

### Finding

The person matters most exactly where the arrangement constrains him least, and the corpus's richest material describes the machinery by which societies decide how much that is. Every interposition device the traditions catalogue — the advocate who pleads so the principal need not, the envoy who absorbs the insult, the paired *missi* watching each other, the office of censor that converts an affront into a duty, the Ottoman written summary that lets neither party be seen to yield, the sovereign who does not speak, the ceremonial that places a visitor in a relation before a word is said, the council that decides in place of the lord — is a **discretion-reducing** design. An earlier version of this study presented that material as its most confident and did not notice what it was confident about.

The corpus's own migration is a migration between discretion regimes. Tacitus says eloquence is the child of licence and disorder and withers when one man decides; the leader-effects literature says the individual's *impact* is greatest when one man decides. Both hold, because they concern different quantities. **Eloquence requires many people who must be persuaded; consequence requires few people who can constrain.** The Roman republic maximizes the first and minimizes the second; the sultanate does the reverse; and the literature migrated from rhetoric to mirrors-for-princes as the ratio moved.

### Derivation

**Evidence.** Jones and Olken (2005) used the deaths of leaders in office as exogenous variation in leadership and asked whether these transitions shift national growth rates. They do: one standard deviation in leader quality moves growth by about 1.5 percentage points a year. The result is present in autocratic settings, absent in democratic ones, and larger where constraints on the leader's power are fewer.

The same shape appears in firms. Crossland and Hambrick found the effect of chief executives on performance substantially greater in United States firms than in German or Japanese ones (2007), and then showed that six national institutions — individualism, tolerance of uncertainty, cultural looseness, dispersed ownership, common-law origin, employer flexibility — predict the latitude available in a country, and that this latitude mediates the size of the leader's effect (2011). Quigley, Chirico and Baù (2022) found the effect on return on assets larger for privately held firms than publicly traded ones, 23.8 per cent against 16.6 per cent of variance.

And interposition has a measured price. Third-party representation raises the price obtained and raises the rate of impasse; representatives under constituency pressure shift toward contentious tactics and away from exchanging information, producing fewer integrative agreements and more deadlocks (Bazerman, Neale, Valley, Zajac & Kim 1992).

**Corpus.** Tacitus in the *Dialogus*. Hincmar's inner council of the elder counsellors, which sets the agenda and takes the decisions while the general assembly hears, discusses and is seen to consent, with secrecy enjoined lest resentment and revolt follow. The Ottoman *telhīs*, in which counsel is written and the ruling written back, so the vizier is not seen to be overruled and the sultan is not seen to be persuaded. The Roman patron who pleads while the client stays silent. Commynes' rule that two great princes should never meet, because a personal encounter converts a negotiable difference into a question of honour.

And the actor's own contribution to the constraint layer, which is made before that layer begins. Standing appears as a fixed given only because an encounter freezes the clock. Machiavelli's memorandum to Raffaello Girolami of October 1522 is explicitly about *filling* the reputation that will later present itself as a constraint: be thought liberal, honest and wise, because that reputation buys access and information.

**Interrogation.** The discretion gate, worked out in III.5: institutional classification is not merely knowing what a body decides but knowing which of one's other capacities this arrangement leaves live.

### Withdraws

That the limit on interposition is that it costs judgment — a sovereign who never speaks is never contradicted and never tested. True, and unmeasurable. The measured cost is joint gain and the deal happening at all.

That the four settlements of the counsel problem — Athenian, Roman, Carolingian, Ottoman — sit on one axis running from candour to protection.

### Requires

Part III rows 2 and 3, and the discretion gate at III.5. Part II.2 sets out the interposition catalogue and the figures.

### Limit

The proxies are executive and head-of-state. The *telhīs* is also a channel decision — written, asynchronous and face-to-face exchange produce measurably different negotiation outcomes — and treating it only as a screening device leaves it undescribed in that respect; logged as a gap. The pairing of Tacitus with the leader-effects result is this study's synthesis and appears in neither source.

### Crosswalk

Absorbs the corpus-doctrine on interposition entire, the institutional content of the doctrine on standing — the Roman order of speaking, Liudprand's seating — and the second axis of the dispute over public and private counsel.

## S3. Every move carries its characteristic failure, and the failure the corpus names is overshoot

### Finding

There is no safe register. Han Feizi's chapter on the difficulty of speaking demonstrates that each of seven manners of speaking invites a specific misreading, so choosing a manner is choosing which misreading to pre-empt, not finding one that carries no risk. And across seven traditions the named fault is excess of a virtue rather than deficiency of it. The corpus almost never warns that a competent practitioner will do too little. Its consistent expectation is that his failure will be a competent thing done too hard, too soon, or too visibly.

Both halves follow from a single observation about what kind of literature this is. **The corpus is a fault catalogue, and an earlier version of this study read it as a move catalogue.** Reading it correctly is what makes the backwards interrogation of Part III the corpus's native method rather than something imposed on it.

### Derivation

**Corpus.** The overshoot vocabulary, across traditions that name it independently:

| Tradition | Fault | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Quintilian VIII.3 | *cacozelia* | perverse affectation — treated as the worst fault, and it is excess in the direction of a virtue |
| Aristotle *Rhet.* III.3 | *psychron*, frigidity | the failed reach for elevation: compound words, strange words, unseasonable epithets, far-fetched metaphor |
| *Ad Herennium* I.11 | *nimium apparate compositum*, *nimium longum* | too elaborately worked up, too long |
| Castiglione | *affettazione* | visible effort; the negation of grace |
| Cicero *De or.* II | the joke that costs the speaker his dignity | wit overrun |
| Confucius | *ning*, *qiao yan ling se* | glibness; clever words and an ingratiating expression |
| Zeami | the temporary flower taken for the true one | performing above one's attained rank |
| Han Feizi 12 | intimacy achieved too fast | pace overrun |

One passage fixes the continuity. The *Ad Herennium* instructs that the opening use plain speech and ordinary words **so that the oration should not appear worked up** — *ut non apparata oratio videatur esse* — and then names as faulty the exordium *nimium apparate compositum*. That is Castiglione's doctrine of concealed effort, in a Latin schoolbook of the 80s BC, sixteen centuries before *sprezzatura* acquired its name.

Then the register matrix. Han Feizi's *Nan yan* is not a list of mistakes but a demonstration that no manner is safe: smooth and fluent speech is heard as ostentatious and insincere; sincere, blunt and careful speech as awkward and unsystematic; richly illustrated and analogical speech as empty and impractical; plain and concise speech as simple and undiscerning; shrewdness about human nature as conceit; erudition as boastful and useless; detailed and itemized speech as vulgar.

**Evidence.** Two results bear on the characteristic failure of the indirection device specifically. O'Keefe's meta-analytic review of conclusion articulation (1997) tested the received view that omitting a conclusion recruits the hearer's own inferential effort and therefore persuades harder. Messages with explicit conclusions were more persuasive than messages from which the conclusion was omitted, and more specific recommendations outperformed vaguer ones. The device was not selected for persuasive yield, and mistaking one's own objective is itself a characteristic failure of a move. And Bartsch (1994) supplies the failure in the other direction: under the Principate the audience took the initiative in hunting for subtexts, so that dangerous meanings were read into innocent speech and Pliny had to labour to prevent sincere praise being received as irony. **Where the room is already decoding, indirection stops protecting and starts manufacturing charges nobody made.**

**Interrogation.** The second rule: derive the requirement from the failure mode, never from the ideal.

### Withdraws

That the entry decision is a binary between direct and oblique openings. It is a choice among manners each of which invites a nameable misreading.

That the Greek and Chinese indirection devices are identical in structure. The shared canon on which recitation of the *Odes* rests is the decisive difference, not a decoration, and it is why Bartsch's objection bites the Roman case and not the Chinese one.

That the corpus records no failures. It records types exhaustively and frequencies not at all.

And the move-catalogue reading of the corpus that governed this study's first four stages.

### Requires

Part III rows 7, 8 and 13; and the extension of anticipation from the opponent's argument to one's own manner. The corpus already contains the figure — *procatalepsis*, also called *prolepsis* or *praemunitio*, prescribed in the *Rhetoric to Alexander*, discussed by Cicero, treated systematically by Quintilian with the warning against handing an opponent an argument he had not thought of. An earlier version of this study reported that the corpus raises the question of two opposed arguments through Protagoras and never answers it. It answers it, and the omission was this study's.

### Limit

A type inventory is not a frequency inventory. Han Fei's seven registers exhaust the ways a manner can be misread and say nothing about which misreading is likeliest before a given ruler.

### Crosswalk

Absorbs the corpus-doctrine on indirection, with its split into a safety claim and a persuasion claim intact, and the rewritten account of what the corpus records.

## S4. The scarce capacity is inhibition — controlled withholding and deliberate release

### Finding

Run down the fault column of the requirement table and one capacity appears seven times. Do not deny in detail. Do not lose your temper. Do not answer what was not asked. Do not fill the silence. Do not include the weak argument. Do not open before they have settled. Do not perform above your rank.

In most of these the actor is not failing to perceive. One can tell that the room has not settled, which of one's arguments is weakest, that the question was not asked. **The bottleneck sits downstream of perception, in whether the perception governs the action** — which is why training perception does not help, and why an earlier version of this study, having named perception as the scarce faculty, could not explain the evidence against it.

And the requirement is not low reactivity. Displayed anger extracts concessions from a counterpart who cannot afford an impasse. What is required is voluntary control over display: the capacity to withhold, and the capacity to release on purpose.

### Derivation

**Interrogation.** The pattern was found by running the modern-annotated sequence step by step and observing that six consecutive requirements came out as negations.

**Corpus.** The same result arrives independently from the other side, in the overshoot vocabulary of S3 — which was derived by reading the fault catalogues, not by running the sequence. Two derivations, one from each direction, converging on one capacity. Beside them stands the reserve doctrine proper: Zeami's interval of no-action, in which nothing visible occurs and the audience is moved in the gaps; the Confucian preference for slowness of speech over fluency; Kai Kāvus on saying less than one knows and not answering at once; Kekaumenos on inconspicuousness as a form of standing; Guicciardini's reserve of the man who does not reply; *insinuatio*, the oblique opening that declines the frontal meeting; and Quintilian's treatment of the rapid hostile exchange, where the danger is named precisely as temper.

**Evidence.** ⚠ The flat prohibition on anger is not sustained. Negotiators concede more to an angry counterpart than to a happy one (Van Kleef, De Dreu & Manstead 2004), and the effect is conditional in ways the corpus could have used: power moderates it, with low-power counterparts conceding and high-power ones not (Van Kleef et al. 2006); perceived authenticity moderates it; and it carries the costs the corpus is right about — higher impasse rates, relational damage, worse subjective valuation of the deal by the counterpart (Wang, Northcraft & Van Kleef 2012). **The corpus is right that anger is expensive and wrong about why.** It reads anger as a forfeit of standing, following Aristotle's analysis of belittlement; the measured mechanism is inferential, the counterpart reading anger as information about your limits and conceding if he cannot afford the impasse. The two accounts give opposite advice against a weak opponent.

⚠ And the ground given for not denying a charge in detail is wrong as stated. The premise underneath it is that repeating a claim while denying it raises belief in it, and that effect has repeatedly failed to replicate, including under conditions built to favour it. Corrections reduce belief in every condition tested, and detailed corrections do better than brief ones. Quintilian's defensible ground is different and is a claim about standing rather than memory: a long defence reads as anxiety and cedes the floor to the accuser's framing.

▣ On acquirability, the nearest evidence is that specified behavioural tactics train. Antonakis, Fenley and Liechti (2011) taught a defined set of charismatic tactics in a field experiment and a laboratory experiment; coders confirmed increased frequency, and observers rated the trained speakers higher, with a mean effect of about .62 on charisma ratings. That is behaviour under deliberate control, which is the class of thing controlled display belongs to.

### Withdraws

The flat rule against anger. The cognitive framing of the rule against detailed denial. And the title under which this material previously stood — that reserve is a move among others. It is the capacity that the fault catalogues name most often.

### Requires

Part III rows 8, 9 and 11, and capacity 5. The fault table for conduct under attack is placed at row 11.

### Limit

The charisma result is adjacent, not direct: tactics for a platform speech are not discipline in a hostile exchange. And whether controlled *release* trains as well as controlled withholding is not established by anything located.

### Crosswalk

Absorbs the corpus-doctrine on reserve, and the inhibitory half of the doctrine on perception.

## S5. The read is made, not taken

### Finding

Persuasion is intervention that modifies the room, not navigation of a fixed one, and the read of the other party is produced by acting on him rather than inferred from his type. What an earlier version of this study called perception was a portmanteau covering at least four capacities with different acquisition profiles: calibration, elicitation, control of display, and allocation of attention. Only one of them has evidence that it can be trained, and it is the one the corpus almost never names.

**The corpus's least respectable text contains its best-supported method**, and three independent lines converge on it: the procedure itself, which is iterative rather than diagnostic; the positive result in the perspective-taking experiments, which was for getting a perspective rather than taking one; and the asymmetry that no other text in the survey treats elicitation as a capacity at all.

### Derivation

**Corpus.** The *Guiguzi* is the only operational procedure in the survey and it is generative throughout. Opening and closing — draw the other party out to learn what he wants, close down to conceal what you want. Reversing and responding — say something calculated to provoke a reply, because the reply reveals; if the reply does not fit, say the opposite and listen again; cast a net and keep speaking until something is caught. Estimating circumstances and probing disposition, approaching when the other is elated or frightened, because the true wish shows then. Weighing words: breadth with the learned, plainness with the unlearned, directness with the brave. Then back to the beginning with the reading revised.

The same generative logic, unnamed, elsewhere. Mencius leads King Xuan not by classifying him but by extracting an admission — the king's own recorded pity for a sacrificial ox — which alters what he can afterwards deny. Aristotle's chapters on the emotions are instructions for *changing* an audience's condition, giving for each emotion its object, its cause and its cure; they are not a diagnostic key. Zeami's flower is the alteration of the house's state, and its governing property is novelty, which is why a device that has worked will not work again on the same audience.

Against these stands the passive line, which the earlier version of this study took for the whole: Aristotle's typology of hearers by age and fortune, and Plato's demand in the *Phaedrus* that the practitioner classify kinds of soul and kinds of speech and recognize the type in front of him.

**Evidence.** ⚠ Eyal, Steffel and Epley (2018) ran twenty-five experiments on the instruction to adopt another's perspective, scored against what targets actually reported — predicting emotions from faces and postures, genuine from feigned smiles, lies from truths, a spouse's preferences. They found no consistent gain, an overall decrease in accuracy, and occasional increases in the predictor's confidence. What improved accuracy was **getting** the perspective: being allowed to ask.

One scope qualification is honest and one is not. The unearned one is that the instruction was given to naive participants rather than practised over a career; that is not a rescue but the substance of S6. The earned one is that Zeami's discipline is not quite the instruction tested. Seeing oneself from where the audience sits asks the performer to perceive his own visible form as an external object; the experiments tested inference about another's private mental content. Self-appearance and other-mind inference are not the same operation, and the result lands squarely only on the part of Zeami's discipline that requires modelling how the house is receiving him — which is a substantial part, and not the whole.

▣ For the calibration component of the bundle, the geopolitical forecasting work supplies the positive evidence: accuracy was predicted by a combination of dispositional, situational and behavioural variables, with training in probabilistic reasoning among the situational predictors (Mellers et al. 2015).

**Interrogation.** Row 4 of the requirement table — locating what may not be said — is one of only three steps whose failure is terminal, and elicitation is the only capacity with evidence of clearing it.

### Withdraws

That the scarce faculty is perception. That the *Guiguzi* is best characterized as sinister — a fact about its reception in orthodox Confucian eyes, which an earlier draft allowed to stand where an evidential ranking belonged. And the framing of the audience throughout as a type to be read rather than a state to be altered.

### Requires

Part III row 4 and capacity 3; and the restatement of every step previously written as "read the room" into a form that says act on the room and observe what returns.

### Limit

Elicitation requires someone safe to ask. Where the forbidden subject is the ruler's own and no third party will name it, row 4 is a guess, and the corpus says as much: the dragon may be ridden and tamed, but there is a scale beneath its throat that grows backwards, and whoever touches it dies.

### Crosswalk

Absorbs the active half of the corpus-doctrine on perception, which it retitles.

## S6. Trainability belongs to the environment, and the environment can be built

### Finding

The dispute between Machiavelli and Zeami over whether adaptability can be acquired dissolves into a claim about feedback, and then a third possibility opens that no text in the corpus proposes.

Skilled intuition forms under two conditions: an environment of high validity, in which relations between cue and outcome are stable and learnable, and prolonged practice with feedback that is rapid and unequivocal. Where either fails, what develops is confidence rather than skill. Zeami's domain has both. Machiavelli's has neither. Both men report accurately about their own environments, and their apparent disagreement about human nature is a real difference in how quickly the world tells a practitioner that he was wrong.

The corpus's own pattern confirms it and was previously reported without being interpreted. **Every tradition here that declares the faculty unteachable by rule is political, forensic or diplomatic. The one that claims a training is theatrical.**

Then the third leg. **The environment is itself a design variable.** A geopolitical forecasting tournament is the deliberate conversion of a low-validity domain into a high-validity one: attach resolvable questions, deadlines and scores to political judgment, and trainability appears. The largest lever available to an actor in a domain that does not supply feedback is to build the feedback, and no source in this survey proposes it.

### Derivation

**Evidence.** Kahneman and Klein's adversarial collaboration (2009) settled the general form. Mellers and colleagues (2015) concluded that superforecasters are **partly discovered and partly created**, with the best model combining dispositional, situational and behavioural variables at a multiple correlation near .64, and the top performers substantially outperforming intelligence analysts with access to classified material. Antonakis, Fenley and Liechti (2011) showed that technique trains even where perception does not. And a thin but pointed result: within-person *variability* in personality predicts negotiation gains and satisfaction better than average trait levels do — adaptability measured as an individual difference. Two studies in one paper, dyadic, not replicated at scale; if it holds it vindicates Machiavelli's *Prince* against his own *Discorsi*.

**Corpus.** *Discorsi* III.9 against *Prince* 25: match your proceeding to the times and you succeed, but Fabius could not become Scipio, and the conclusion drawn is institutional — a republic outlives a prince because it can replace the man rather than change him. Against this, Zeami's treatises, which name a training and describe its stages. And between them the case that makes the resolution a gradient rather than a coin toss: forensic oratory, where feedback is fast — an Athenian jury voted within hours — and its meaning is uninterpretable, since 501 jurors deliberated not at all and gave no reasons. Cicero and Quintilian came from that world and think most of the art teachable. Three environments, three verdicts, one variable.

```
                  validity of cues     feedback speed     feedback clarity
ZEAMI             high                 same evening       unequivocal
                  → skill develops

FORENSIC          moderate             same day           unequivocal but
ORATORY                                                   uninterpretable
                  → partial; and the practitioners think it teachable

MACHIAVELLI       low                  years              confounded, and
                                                          ascribed to fortune
                  → confidence, not skill
```

**Interrogation.** The acquisition column of Part III can only be written once S6 is in place, because it is the column that says what a domain permits.

### Withdraws

The dispute over adaptability as open. And the reading of the corpus's institutional designs as a crutch for those who failed to train: in a low-validity environment they are the correct response. Also the claim, made in an earlier draft, that a sequence is simply what a domain produces when it cannot produce expertise — true but incomplete, because a domain's validity can be altered.

### Requires

The acquisition column throughout Part III, and the three legs of D3.

### Limit

The forensic intermediate case is this study's judgment, not a finding of any source. Environment-construction is demonstrated for probabilistic judgment about resolvable questions; whether it transfers to the interpersonal capacities of Part III is not shown by anything located, and the capacities most in need of it — reframing under pressure, tempo, allocation of attention — are precisely those where no evidence exists.

### Crosswalk

Absorbs the dispute over adaptability, and with it the whole person-against-scenario question, which is that dispute in other words.

## S7. The corpus is silent where failure is terminal

### Finding

Failures grade into three classes, the corpus marks the grading, and the distribution of its attention runs opposite to it.

**Terminal.** The scale that grows backwards. The discovered lie — and note the precise form of Machiavelli's warning to his envoy, which is not that concealment must not occur but that it must not be *noticed*. Visible effort once seen, since grace perceived as contrivance cannot be re-perceived as grace. Zeami's false flower once exposed. *Lian* forfeited.

**Costly but recoverable.** Lost temper, wrong genre, the failed joke, the frontal entry into hostility, leaked counsel.

**Free.** The unused proof, the unspent objection to the forum, the reframing that proved unnecessary. The corpus is emphatic that these cost nothing and should be hoarded.

Effort belongs at the terminal steps. Those are exactly the steps for which no tradition can write a rule. **So the written material concentrates on the recoverable middle, and any study built from it — including the earlier version of this one — inherits the misallocation.**

The sharpest symptom is a capacity that no tradition names and no located evidence measures: the allocation of one's own attention under load. It is what steps 0 and 14 require, and those are the two moments — entry and close — at which an actor's attention is most committed elsewhere.

### Derivation

**Corpus.** The terminal markers listed above, each from a different tradition and each stated as one-way. Against them, the *Ad Herennium*'s catalogue of exordium faults, every one of which is recoverable within the same speech. And the devices that keep another party's failure from becoming terminal: Aristotle's account of how anger is appeased, which is a doctrine of giving an offended party a way down; Sunzi's instruction not to press a surrounded enemy; Shōtoku's tenth article, which defuses disagreement by denying that either party's status is at stake in it, since we are all merely ordinary men and neither is certainly wise nor certainly foolish; and the fiction, maintained jointly and known by both sides to be one, in which a payment made to avoid war is recorded as a gift.

**Evidence.** Two absences, one deliberate and one structural. A search for experimental work bearing directly on face-preserving exits and graceful yielding found nothing contradicting the doctrine and nothing that would refine it beyond the negotiation literature's general support for subjective valuation and for the durability of agreements — reported as a clean result rather than padded into a finding. And a search of both bodies of material for anything treating the management of one's own attention under adversarial load found nothing in either.

**Interrogation.** The recoverability column, and rows 0, 4 and 14.

### Withdraws

The uniform treatment of steps in the master sequence, which weighted them by how much the corpus says about each. And the presentation of building the other party an exit as one doctrine among seven: it is the device that prevents a costly failure from becoming a terminal one for the other side, which is a different order of thing.

### Requires

Part III rows 0, 4 and 14; the recoverability column; and the paragraph at III.4 on where effort belongs.

### Limit

The grading is the corpus's own and is unverified against outcomes. And the inference from a double absence to "hard to see rather than unimportant" is weak; it is stated as weak and rests only on the fact that two bodies of material with different biases are silent on the same thing.

### Crosswalk

Absorbs the corpus-doctrine on building the other side a bridge, and the recoverability structure.

## S8. Symmetry is the test of evidence, applied first to one's own material

### Finding

An argument that both parties can run has a likelihood ratio at or near unity and carries no evidential weight, however persuasive it sounds. The corpus ran this test routinely, and ran it on the speaker's *own* material before the paradox that generates it had a name.

The *Ad Herennium* marks as faulty the exordium *quo nihilo minus adversarius potest uti* — which the adversary could use no less well — and the one *quo adversarius ex contrario poterit uti*, which he could turn to the contrary. Aristotle's chapter on apparent enthymemes is a check on one's own case before it is a weapon against another's. *Topics* VIII distinguishes real refutation from apparent refutation and asks who is blamed when an argument fails, which is a doctrine of failure attribution.

The modern hierarchy of evidence inverts the ancient one on witnesses and coerced testimony. What survives the inversion is the test: **before advancing an argument, ask whether your opponent could advance its mirror image. If he could, it is not evidence.**

### Derivation

**Corpus.** The reversal reported by Aristotle and credited to the art of Corax: the small man accused of assaulting a large one argues that he is unlikely to have done it, and the large man accused of assaulting a small one argues that it would have been too obviously suspicious. The *Ad Herennium* and *De inventione* fault lists. Quintilian's doctrine of the issues, whose operative rule is never to conjecture where one can prove.

**Evidence.** ⚠ Mistaken eyewitness identification is implicated in roughly seventy per cent of United States DNA exonerations, and the National Research Council's assessment (*Identifying the Culprit*, 2014) is in large part a catalogue of the ways in which a confidently delivered identification is the least trustworthy item in the room; the witnesses in those cases almost without exception expressed complete confidence. Aristotle's list places witnesses and testimony taken under torture at the top of the ranking of proofs; the modern one places them near the bottom. ▣ And the corpus's corollary — that arguing probability in addition to a document weakens the case — has modern support in the dilution of diagnostic information by non-diagnostic information, together with a boundary the corpus does not state: under low elaboration the sheer number of arguments works as a cue, and additions can help. The rule is conditional on how hard the room is thinking.

**Interrogation.** Row 5, and capacity 4, which is the cleanest trainable rule in the set because it is a rule rather than a perception.

### Withdraws

That the inartificial proofs stand first in a strict ranking issued as procedure. The ranking is conditional, and "inartificial" must be read as documents and records; a confident witness is a probable sign wearing a necessary sign's clothes.

### Requires

Part III row 5, and the redirection of "inartificial proof" throughout.

### Limit

Reversibility makes a probability argument worth least against a skilled opponent and most against an unskilled one, which is the opposite of the intuition that a clever argument is a strong one. But the test says nothing about which of two mirror-image arguments will land with an unskilled hearer, and the corpus contains a good deal of advice premised on their landing.

This is the narrowest of the eight. What it withdraws is smaller than what the others withdraw, and a stricter reading would fold it into S3 as the same fault-check applied to one's own material. It is kept separate because it is the only finding here with a one-line operational test attached, and because the *Ad Herennium* evidence shows the corpus treating it as a distinct discipline rather than as an instance of restraint.

### Crosswalk

Absorbs the corpus-doctrine on proof and probability.

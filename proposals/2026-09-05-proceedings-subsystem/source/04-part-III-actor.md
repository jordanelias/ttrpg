# PART III — What the actor must be able to do

---

## III.1 Why the question takes this form

Trait measurement failed at exactly the point where the person-effect is largest, and the response within the literature that discovered the failure was to stop measuring dispositions and start coding steps.

The pincer is set out in S1 and its two halves need holding together. Rotating negotiators across counterparts and partitioning the variance directly, Elfenbein and colleagues found individual differences accounting for 25 per cent of objective performance in distributive bargaining, 46 per cent in integrative bargaining and 19 per cent of subjective value, with a behavioural follow-up reporting 52 per cent. In the same design, a battery of existing trait measures revealed few reliable associations with the differences just demonstrated. The meta-analysis that followed restored trait validity at modest effect sizes, and found effects **stronger in field data than in the laboratory**.

So: large, stable, and not where the instruments were pointed. The authors' own response was to code transcripts, linguistic style and nonverbal behaviour, and ask what consistently good negotiators do differently. That is the method of this part, and it is also the corpus's method, since the corpus is a fault catalogue (0.4).

**The three rules**, restated because everything below is built on them:

1. A step earns a requirement only if failure at it is observable and attributable.
2. The requirement derives from the failure mode, never from the ideal.
3. The requirement must be a capacity, not a disposition.

**What this licenses.** A requirement claim is falsifiable where a prescription is not. "Open obliquely into a hostile room" carries no warrant that this study can supply. "Clearing this step requires the ability to detect hostility before speaking" can be wrong in checkable ways — the step may not exist, the failure may not be attributable to it, the named capacity may not discriminate. That is the whole of the improvement, and it is enough to be worth having.

## III.3 The nine capacities

Grouped by what is known about acquiring them. The grouping is the finding; the list is subordinate to it.

### Evidenced

**1. Calibration of reception.** Forecasting one's own standing and one's own read, and knowing the width of the interval. Mellers et al. 2015 supply the positive evidence — accuracy predicted by dispositional, situational and behavioural variables together, with probabilistic-reasoning training among the situational predictors, and superforecasters "partly discovered and partly created." The hazard attached to this capacity is specific and documented: instructed perspective-taking raised confidence without raising accuracy.

**2. Institutional classification.** Knowing what this body's office requires it to decide, who in fact decides, what licence one holds, and — see III.5 — which of the other capacities this arrangement leaves live. Propositional, and the cheapest thing in the set to acquire.

**3. Elicitation.** Producing the read by acting on the other party rather than inferring it from his type. The one positive result in twenty-five experiments on interpersonal accuracy, and the operational content of the only procedure in the corpus. S5 sets out why three independent lines converge on it.

**4. Evidential discrimination.** The mirror test, and the distinction between a document and a confident witness. A rule rather than a perception, which is why it is the cleanest item here.

**5. Voluntary control of display.** Withholding, and deliberate release. Antonakis et al. 2011 is the nearest evidence and it is adjacent rather than direct: charismatic tactics for a platform speech are not discipline in a hostile exchange, and whether controlled release trains as well as controlled withholding is not established.

### Tradition asserts it can be trained; nothing located in modern work

**6. Rank-calibration.** Not how one will be received but what size of claim one is entitled to make. Zeami's doctrine of attained rank; the *sprezzatura* and *affettazione* pair; the Roman fit between claim and *dignitas*. Distinct from capacity 1: different failure, different remedy.

**7. Generative reframing.** The favourable construction produced under time pressure. The tradition is confident and supplies a drill corpus; no modern evidence was located, and the search for it is logged.

**8. *Memoria*.** A stocked mind with retrieval under pressure. The corpus makes this a canon; the requirement analysis vindicates it, since the favourable construction, the ode, the precedent, the exemplum and the anecdote all require it, and the failure mode of recitation from a shared canon is a retrieval failure. **This is the capacity on which the corpus is most confident and the modern literature most silent.**

**9. Tempo.** The rate at which a relationship is built and the timing of entry. Han Feizi's too-fast-fatal and too-slow-useless; Commynes on the meeting that should not happen. An earlier version of this study treated every encounter as a single occasion and had nothing corresponding to it.

### Double-absent

**Allocation of attention under load.** Neither tradition nor located evidence names it. What makes the absence worth stating rather than passing over is where it bites: steps 0 and 14, entry and close, the two moments at which an actor's attention is most committed elsewhere. Two bodies of material with different biases, silent on the same thing, is weak evidence that it is hard to see rather than unimportant. Stated as weak.

## III.4 Two findings that appear only at step level

### Inhibition is the recurring capacity

Read down the fault column: do not deny in detail, do not lose temper, do not answer what was not asked, do not fill the silence, do not include the weak argument, do not open before they have settled, do not perform above one's rank. One capacity, appearing seven times, and the overshoot vocabulary of S3 is the same result reached from the corpus's side by a different route.

**In most of these the actor is not failing to perceive.** The perception is available and does not govern the action. That is why training perception does not help — the constraint sits downstream of it — and it is the substance of S4.

### Recoverability, graded by the corpus itself

```
TERMINAL      the scale that grows backwards · the discovered lie · visible
              effort once seen · the false flower once exposed · lian forfeited
              → no later step repairs these
COSTLY        wrong genre · lost temper · the failed joke · frontal entry ·
              leaked counsel
              → recoverable at a visible price
FREE          the unused proof · the unspent objection to the forum · the
              favourable construction not needed
              → cost nothing; the corpus holds that they are hoarded
```

**Effort belongs at the terminal steps, and those are exactly the steps for which no tradition can write a rule.** The written material therefore concentrates on the recoverable middle, and a study built from it inherits the misallocation — which is S7, and which is why rows 0, 4 and 14 carry the least text in this table and the most weight.

## III.5 The discretion gate

Which capacities are live is set by the arrangement, and knowing which is part of capacity 2.

Where the arrangement is tight — a scripted audience, a written-only channel, a body with a fixed warrant, a role with no latitude — capacities 3 through 9 barely operate, and capacities 1 and 2 do the work. Where it is loose, the reverse. **Capacity 2 therefore includes knowing which of the other capacities this arrangement will let one use**, and that is the step the earlier version of this study lacked entirely.

The arrangements are themselves designed, and Part II.2 sets out the designing. The evidence there — that the effect of the person is present in autocracies and absent in democracies, greater in private firms than public, greater in some national settings than others, and reduced by every interposition device the corpus catalogues — is what determines how much of this table is live in a given room. **S2 and S6 meet here: the arrangement fixes how much the actor can do, and, per S6, the arrangement is itself something an actor may sometimes build.**

## III.6 What this part does not claim

**Two rows are expanded elsewhere.** Row 11, conduct under attack, and row 13, attacking without cost, each carry a fault table too large for this one. They are Figures 9 and 10 in `09-figures.md`, with a source in every cell and both contradicted branches marked. Rows 5, 7 and 12 are likewise expanded, as Figures 6, 8 and 5.

It does not claim the nine capacities are sufficient. It claims that failure at a named step implies the absence of the capacity named beside it, which is a weaker and checkable thing.

It does not claim frequencies. The corpus supplies a type inventory and no base rates, and no modern work located supplies them for these steps.

It does not claim any acquisition verdict beyond the evidence cited. Four capacities are tradition-only and one is double-absent, and those verdicts are not inferred from the neighbouring ones.

It does not claim the nine are independent. Several plainly covary — rank-calibration with calibration of reception, tempo with allocation of attention — and no factor structure is attempted, because nothing located would support one. The count of nine is a judgment, and it is graded as a synthesis-gloss in VII.3.

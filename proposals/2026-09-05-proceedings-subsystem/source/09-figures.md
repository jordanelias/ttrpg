# FIGURES — the trees, ladders and tables, collected

Every diagram in this study, in one place, with a pointer to where each is discussed.

**Register.** Nothing here is an instruction. Twenty-seven figures. Each is one of three things, and the label says which:

- **Anatomy** — a decomposition of a thing into its parts.
- **Reconstruction** — what the corpus prescribes, compressed into its decision structure so that it can be inspected. Read every branch as prefixed by *the corpus holds that*.
- **Fault table** — what the corpus names as going wrong at a point, with the source in every cell, and the capacity that failure implies.

▣ marks a note where modern work bounds or explains a branch. ⚠ marks a branch that modern work contradicts. Absence of a marker means no evidence was located, not that the branch is sound.

## FIG. 1 — Anatomy: the three layers

*Discussed at: Part 0.5, S1, III.5.*

```
MASTERY OVER SOCIAL PROCEEDINGS
│
├─ [A] STANDING — the credit brought in the door
│   ├─ A1 ascribed ......... birth, office, rank, precedence
│   ├─ A2 accumulated ...... record, obligations owed, consistency
│   ├─ A3 performed ........ what this room sees now
│   └─ A4 staged ........... ceremony, setting, retinue, controlled access
│       ▸ fails as: contempt, ridicule, being ignored
│       ▸ not repairable inside the room — but A2 is a stock filled earlier (S2)
│       ▸ the cut that predicts erosion is not provenance but PRESTIGE against
│         DOMINANCE, which runs across all four (Fig. 3)
│
├─ [B] JUDGMENT — knowing which move this occasion admits
│   ▸ an earlier version of this study named this layer "perception" and
│     called it the scarce faculty. It is a portmanteau. It decomposes into
│     four capacities with different acquisition profiles (Fig. 12):
│   ├─ calibration ......... of reception, and of one's own rank
│   ├─ elicitation ......... producing the read by acting on the other party
│   ├─ control of display .. withholding, and releasing deliberately
│   └─ allocation .......... of one's own attention under load
│       ▸ only elicitation and calibration have evidence that they train
│
└─ [C] TECHNIQUE — the moves themselves
    ├─ C1 construction ..... issue location, proof, arrangement
    ├─ C2 refutation
    ├─ C3 defence of one's own position
    └─ C4 offence without cost
        ▸ fully teachable; the entire written corpus is C
```

**The asymmetry.** [C] is what gets written down. The capacities under [B] decide outcomes. [A] determines whether either gets a hearing. Isocrates complains that the technique-sellers market [C] and pretend to teach [B]; Han Feizi observes that persuaders die of failures in [B] believing they failed at [C]; Ibn Khaldūn explains [A] as a material fact about kin-solidarity that no quantity of [B] or [C] overrides.

## FIG. 5 — Reconstruction: locating the issue

*Discussed at: Part II.3, S8. Three rational issues; the procedural objection disputed.*

```
WHAT IS ACTUALLY IN DISPUTE?
│
├─ Is the tribunal competent to hear this at all?
│   └─ THE PROCEDURAL OBJECTION — forum · standing · jurisdiction
│      ▸ Quintilian doubts this is a true issue
│      ▸ cheapest available win; but it signals evasion, and the corpus
│        holds that it is spent early or not at all
│
├─ Is the FACT disputed?  →  CONJECTURE
│   ├─ documents and records exist? → led with, and conjecture abandoned
│   └─ otherwise, the argument runs from:
│        ├─ motive — prior enmity, gain, who benefits
│        ├─ capacity and opportunity — could he? was he there?
│        ├─ signs — before, during, after
│        └─ consistency of character — has he done such things?
│      ⚠ all four are reversible. The mirror test (Fig. 6) applies to each
│
├─ Fact admitted, its NAME disputed?  →  DEFINITION
│      theft or sacrilege · error or fraud · gift or bribe
│
├─ Fact and name admitted, its QUALITY disputed?  →  QUALITY
│      the province of the favourable construction              [row 12]
│      ├─ justification — it was right
│      ├─ excuse — ignorance, necessity, accident
│      ├─ transfer — another is responsible
│      └─ mitigation — it was less than alleged
│
└─ The corpus's rule: this ladder is descended only when forced, because
   every descent is visible and is read as a concession not announced.
```

## FIG. 6 — Reconstruction: evidence, and the concealment decision

*Discussed at: Part II.3, S8, D2.*

```
WHAT IS PUT FORWARD?
│
├─ DOCUMENTS OR RECORDS available?
│    → led with; probability not argued in addition
│    ⚠ "inartificial proof" reads as documents, records and contracts.
│      Mistaken eyewitness identification is implicated in roughly 70% of
│      United States DNA exonerations, and those witnesses almost without
│      exception expressed complete confidence. A confident witness is a
│      probable sign wearing a necessary sign's clothes
│    ▣ the corpus's rule against arguing probability in addition has modern
│      support — dilution of diagnostic by non-diagnostic information — and
│      a boundary the corpus does not state: under LOW elaboration, number
│      of arguments works as a cue, and additions can help
│
├─ A NECESSARY SIGN available?
│    → used, and named as necessary so the hearer knows the difference
│
├─ Only PROBABLE SIGNS?
│    │  THE MIRROR TEST: could the opponent advance this argument's image?
│    │  If he could, its likelihood ratio is near unity and it is not evidence
│    ├─ against a skilled opponent → reversal expected; the case not staked
│    └─ against an unskilled one   → usable; several stacked, not one
│
└─ NOTHING?  →  THE CONCEALMENT DECISION
     │
     ├─ Silence or ambiguity available?   (concealing what is)
     │    → cheap · recoverable · deniable · widely licensed
     │
     ├─ Asserted falsehood?               (feigning what is not)
     │    three tests, all of which must pass:
     │      1. can it be cheaply checked?              → if yes, not done
     │      2. will this party be needed again?        → if yes, not done
     │      3. does exposure cost more than the
     │         concession being avoided?               → if yes, not done
     │    ← the DISCOVERED lie is terminal. Machiavelli's instruction to his
     │      envoy concerns being NOTICED, not concealing
     │
     └─ Otherwise → concession, with something bought by it
```

## FIG. 8 — Fault table: the register matrix

*Source: Han Feizi 3, verified in Liao's translation. Discussed at S3, Part III row 7.*

There is no safe register. Choosing a manner is choosing which misreading to pre-empt.

```
  MANNER ADOPTED                          HOW IT IS HEARD
  ─────────────────────────────────────   ──────────────────────────────────
  smooth, fluent, well-ordered        →   ostentatious and insincere
  sincere, blunt, straightforward     →   awkward and unsystematic
  richly illustrated, analogical      →   empty and impractical
  plain, concise, summary             →   simple and undiscerning
  shrewd about human nature           →   self-assuming and conceited
  erudite and profound                →   boastful but useless
  detailed, itemized, numerical       →   vulgar

  CAPACITY IMPLIED: knowing which misreading one's own manner invites, and
  pre-empting it — which extends procatalepsis from the opponent's argument
  to one's own manner. The figure is in the corpus already: prescribed in the
  Rhetoric to Alexander, discussed by Cicero, treated systematically by
  Quintilian, with his warning against handing an opponent an argument he
  had not thought of.
```

## FIG. 9 — Fault table: conduct under attack

*Sources in each cell. Discussed at S4, Part III row 11. Rebuilt here in the register of a fault table; an earlier version of this study rendered it as a decision procedure in the imperative.*

```
UNDER ATTACK OR REFUTATION — where the corpus says it goes wrong
│
├─ Q1  Is the attack TRUE?
│   ├─ NO, and provable
│   │     FAULT: elaborating past the proof
│   │     CAPACITY: restraint — one denial, with the proof, and no more
│   │
│   ├─ NO, but unprovable
│   │     ⚠ the corpus holds that detailed denial is avoided, and the
│   │       ground it gives — that denial dignifies the charge and supplies
│   │       its vocabulary — is a cognitive claim and is NOT supported.
│   │       The premise underneath it, that repeating a claim while denying
│   │       it raises belief, has repeatedly failed to replicate; corrections
│   │       reduce belief in every condition tested and detailed ones do
│   │       better. Quintilian's defensible ground is different and is about
│   │       standing: a long defence reads as anxiety and cedes the floor
│   │     FAULT: anxiety displayed; the accuser's framing accepted
│   │     CAPACITY: control of display
│   │     ▸ the corpus's own repertoire here (Aristotle III.15 on dissolving
│   │       a prejudicial attack): the fact denied · the fact conceded and
│   │       the harm denied · the harm conceded and the intent denied · the
│   │       accuser's motive impugned · something known of oneself cited that
│   │       is incompatible with the charge · the error handed back to the room
│   └─ YES → Q2
│
├─ Q2  Is it MATERIAL to the matter in hand?
│   ├─ NO   FAULT: answering it anyway, and thereby conceding its relevance
│   │       CAPACITY: institutional classification — Quintilian's limit on
│   │       invective works for the defender as well as against him
│   └─ YES → Q3
│
├─ Q3  Is a FAVOURABLE CONSTRUCTION available?
│   ├─ YES  FAULT: contesting the fact when the fact is proven
│   │       CAPACITY: generative reframing under time pressure    [cap. 7]
│   │       ▸ conceding the undeniable buys credit for the contested part
│   └─ NO → Q4
│
├─ Q4  Can the room be made to LAUGH without cruelty?
│   │     FAULT (Cicero, De or. II): the joke that strikes the genuinely
│   │     wretched, since pity outweighs laughter; the joke against what the
│   │     audience loves, since it attacks their affection; the joke that
│   │     costs the speaker his own dignity; the joke requiring explanation
│   │     CAPACITY: rank-calibration — knowing what one may spend
│   │     ▸ highest return and highest variance in the corpus: a joke
│   │       redistributes standing without conceding the point, and no
│   │       other device does that
│   └─ → Q5
│
├─ Q5  Is there a THIRD PARTY who can carry the defence?
│   │     FAULT: vouching for oneself
│   │     CAPACITY: licence assessment — an advocate can be defeated
│   │     without the principal being humiliated
│   └─ → Q6
│
└─ Q6  SILENCE, and relocation
      FAULT (Quintilian VI.4): temper · answering more than was asked ·
      being drawn off the matter
      ⚠ the flat prohibition on anger is not sustained. Negotiators concede
        more to an angry counterpart than to a happy one; power moderates
        it, with low-power counterparts conceding and high-power ones not;
        perceived authenticity moderates it; and it carries the costs the
        corpus is right about — impasse, relational damage, worse subjective
        valuation. THE CORPUS IS RIGHT THAT ANGER IS EXPENSIVE AND WRONG
        ABOUT WHY: it reads a forfeit of standing where the measured
        mechanism is inferential — the counterpart reads anger as
        information about your limits
      CAPACITY: voluntary control of display — withholding, and releasing
      deliberately. Not low reactivity                          [cap. 5]
      ▸ and where the room is lost, the room is changed. Liudprand, unable
        to win before the Byzantine court, said nothing and wrote afterwards
        for an audience whose opinion he could still affect
```

## FIG. 10 — Fault table: attacking without cost

*Sources in each cell. Discussed at S3, Part III row 13.*

```
ATTACKING, CRITICIZING, OR CONVEYING WHAT WILL BE PUNISHED
│
├─ P1  Does an OFFICE license it?
│      censor · remonstrance official · advocate · appointed critic
│      FAULT: speaking plainly without the warrant, so that a discharge of
│      duty is received as a personal affront
│      CAPACITY: licence assessment
│      ▸ Wu Jing's casebook records what Wei Zheng said to Tang Taizong,
│        in what form, and how narrowly he survived saying it
│
├─ P2  Can a PROXY carry it?
│      ally · subordinate · letter · written summary
│      FAULT: carrying it oneself when it could be disavowed
│      CAPACITY: institutional classification
│
├─ P3  Is the target SUPERIOR?  → indirect forms only
│      ├─ figured speech — said so that the hearer completes it
│      ├─ quotation from a shared canon — recitation of the Odes
│      ├─ historical anecdote — Nizām al-Mulk's method, in which the story
│      │  carries the criticism and the ruler may take or leave it
│      ├─ a fixed argumentative form — the circle of justice, which lets
│      │  oppression of subjects be raised as an observation about revenue
│      └─ the fault located in their counsellors rather than in them (Alcuin)

│      ⚠ AND THE OPPOSITE FAULT, which the corpus does not name: where the
│        room is already hunting for subtexts, indirection stops protecting
│        and starts manufacturing charges nobody made. Under the Principate
│        the audience took the initiative in decoding, and Pliny had to
│        labour to prevent sincere praise being received as irony. The
│        protection is a property of the audience's disposition, not of
│        the utterance
│      ▣ and the persuasive premium attributed to hearer-completion is not
│        there in the general case: explicit conclusions outperform omitted
│        ones, and specific recommendations outperform vague. The device was
│        selected for the speaker's survival before a tyrant, not for
│        persuasive yield before a jury
│      CAPACITY: licence assessment, plus a second read of whether the room
│      is decoding
│
├─ P4  Is the target an EQUAL, before an audience?
│      FAULT: attacking the person where character is not the issue —
│      Quintilian's limit, and it injures the attacker
│      FAULT: conceding nothing first, so that the room extends no trust
│      FAULT: invective where wit would serve; invective is expensive and
│      often recoils
│      FAULT: attacking what the audience loves
│      CAPACITY: rank-calibration, and control of display
│
└─ P5  Is the target INFERIOR or dependent?
       FAULT: doing it publicly · doing it twice · taking visible advantage
       from it · doing it without demonstrated prior goodwill
       ▸ the four licensing conditions on frank speech, from the ancient
         literature on frankness: goodwill demonstrated first, never before
         an audience, never repeated, and never with one's own advantage
         visibly attached. Violate any one and frankness reads as attack
         and is priced as one
       CAPACITY: tempo, and control of display
```

## FIG. 23 — The genre triangle

*Source: Aristotle, Rhetoric I.3, and the topics at I.4–8. Discussed at Part II.3, Fig. 2 step 1.2, Fig. 17 branch B1, requirement row 2.*

The species of a speech is not chosen by the speaker. It is fixed by what the hearer's office requires him to decide, and the whole of the rest follows from it.

```

   │ TOPICS        │          │ TOPICS           │          │ TOPICS          │

   ISSUE LADDER                   NO ISSUE LADDER                AMPLIFICATION
   (Fig. 5)                       the fact is not in             the fact is not
   the fact is                    dispute; the question          in dispute; the
   in dispute                     is what follows                question is what
                                                                 it is worth
```

### The characteristic fault of each

| Genre | Fault | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Forensic | arguing what should be done | a defence that pleads the policy instead of the fact, and concedes the fact by doing so |
| Deliberative | arguing what was right | a proposal defended on its justice when the body is asking what it will cost — and Aristotle notes the deliberative is the harder art, because the future admits no witnesses |
| Epideictic | arguing at all | a eulogy that reasons where it should amplify |

**The rule the corpus draws.** Expediency is argued to a parliament and fact to a court. Inverted, the case is lost regardless of its merits, because the speaker is answering a question the body has no office to decide.

### Where the triangle bends

Three cases the scheme handles badly, and they are the interesting ones.

**A trial that is really about honour.** Demosthenes on the crown is nominally forensic — the legality of a decree — and is conducted as a contest over two men's standing across a lifetime. Demosthenes wins by refusing the forensic frame and arguing that a counsellor is answerable for judgment rather than for outcome, which is a deliberative claim made inside a court.

**An assembly asked to judge a past act.** The scrutiny of an outgoing magistrate, and the Athenian prosecution of a man for having proposed an unlawful decree, put a forensic question to a deliberative body. The speaker there faces both ladders at once.

**Epideictic inside the others.** Amplification is not confined to its own genre; it is the instrument by which the other two are made to feel like what they are. Aristotle treats it as belonging to praise and it does most of its work elsewhere.

## FIG. 26 — The licensing conditions on frank speech

*Sources: Philodemus on frank criticism; Plutarch on distinguishing a flatterer from a friend. Discussed at S3, Fig. 10 branch P5, requirement row 13.*

Frankness toward someone who can be hurt by it is not a right and not a temperament. It is a **licence**, and the ancient literature specifies four conditions on it. They are cumulative: all four hold, or the speech is received as an attack and is priced as one.

```
                    MAY THIS BE SAID PLAINLY?
                              │
                              ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 1. HAS GOODWILL BEEN DEMONSTRATED FIRST?                        │
  │    not felt — demonstrated, in acts the hearer can point to     │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
       │ yes                              │ no
       ▼                                  ▼
                          FAILS AS: an attack. There is nothing
                          in the record to make another reading
                          available, and the hearer will choose
                          the reading that costs him least
       │
       ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 2. IS IT PRIVATE?                                               │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
       │ yes                              │ no
       ▼                                  ▼
                          FAILS AS: a standing contest. Before an
                          audience the hearer must defend his
                          position rather than consider the point,
                          and he will. The correction is lost and
                          the relation is spent
       │
       ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 3. IS IT UNREPEATED?                                            │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
       │ yes                              │ no
       ▼                                  ▼
                          FAILS AS: a characterization. Said once
                          it is about the act; said twice it is
                          about the man, and he will answer it as
                          such
       │
       ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 4. IS THE SPEAKER'S OWN ADVANTAGE ABSENT — AND SEEN TO BE?      │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
       │ yes                              │ no
       ▼                                  ▼
   LICENSED                   FAILS AS: self-interest. And note that
                              this is on the erosion list at Fig. 3
                              in its own right: visible self-interest
                              attached to advice damages standing
                              whether or not the advice is sound
```

### When the conditions cannot be met

The corpus supplies three substitutes, and they are the material of Figs. 10 and 24.

| Substitute | How it works | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **An office** | the licence is institutional rather than personal, so conditions 1 and 4 are satisfied by the appointment itself, and 2 and 3 cease to apply — a censor is expected to repeat himself in public | the Censorate; the remonstrance officials; and the casebook of what one of them said and how narrowly he survived |
| **A proxy** | someone else holds the licence, or can be disavowed for lacking it | the advocate; the letter; the written summary |
| **Displacement** | the fault is located in the counsellors around the ruler rather than in the ruler, which preserves condition 1 by not making the hearer the target | Alcuin's method: affection established, something specific and recent praised, the fault placed beside the throne rather than on it, and the close on duty owed to God rather than to the writer |

### Why these four and not others

The conditions look like courtesy and are not. Each removes one available reading of the act:

- **Goodwill** removes the reading that the speaker is hostile.
- **Privacy** removes the reading that he is competing.
- **Non-repetition** removes the reading that he is characterizing.
- **Absence of advantage** removes the reading that he is bargaining.

What remains, when all four hold, is the reading the speaker wants — that he is telling the truth at cost to himself. **The conditions are a construction of the only interpretation that makes plain speech survivable**, which is the same manoeuvre as the favourable construction at row 12, run in advance rather than in repair.

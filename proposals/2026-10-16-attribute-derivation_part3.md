## 6. STAGE 3: COLLATION

### 6.1 The rule

Every one of §1's 46 aptitudes goes into exactly one cluster, by **what it would measure about a
person**. **The clustering is a judgment call.** The aptitudes are fine. The clusters are this
proposal's reading, and §1 is kept unmerged so Jordan can cluster differently.

- **PRA domains**, **verbs** (with distinct resolvers in brackets) and **subsystems** count the items
  that draw at least one member of the cluster.
- **draws** and **distinct reasons** follow §1: a reason (one source statement or one inference) is
  counted once per member aptitude.
- **draws as** counts roles over all the cluster's draws.
- **maps to the nine today** gives each member's code from §1's last column (REC, TMP, RES and TS are
  the template's candidates).

### 6.4 What each cluster moves

The *draws as* column of §6.1 gives the role mix.

- **Body clusters split by role.**
  - K4 Constitution is mostly **difficulty/cost**: 17 of 24 draws (wounds, fatigue, exposure).
  - K2 Agility is all **odds** (14 of 14).
  - K1 Force is all **effect size** (8 of 8).
- **K14 Will is the one cluster spread over odds (15), cost (12) and eligibility (9).** It supplies
  the pool, a contact cap, a cost resistance and the defence of one's convictions.
- **K16 Thread sensitivity is odds and eligibility** (9 and 7). The clusters most often used as a
  **gate** are K8 Application and K14 Will (9 eligibility draws each: Focus's operations-per-contact
  rule, and contact endurance) and K16.
- **Social and perceptual clusters are mostly odds** (K9: 19 of 24; K12: 16 of 20; K10: 14 of 21;
  K11: 14 of 19). In a contest-shaped game, they are what makes a pool bigger.

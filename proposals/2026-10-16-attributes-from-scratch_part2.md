## 3. How each subsystem could work if fully designed

Each sketch is a first-principles account, built from the verb inventory, language, history and
general knowledge of games. None of it comes from the repository's design documents, which this pass
did not read. Every sketch ends with **"the sheet should predict"**: what a player should be able to
foresee about a person from his sheet alone. Thin knowledge is marked **[INFERRED]**.

### 3.1 Personal combat (PC)

**What people are trying to do:** kill, capture, drive off, survive, or win a point of honour (a duel
settles a claim). **The loop:** someone challenges or ambushes. The parties close (rush, shoot at
range). Then come exchanges of moves: strike, guard, parry, feint, grapple, disarm, dodge. Wounds and
fatigue build up until someone yields, disengages, flees or falls. Others may interpose or rescue.
**Where success comes from:**

- **Timing:** who acts first and lands. The old fencing masters built their teaching on *Vor*, *Nach*
  and *Indes* (before, after, in the same moment). Initiative is reading plus reaction.
- **Reading the opponent:** a parry or a feint works on what the other is about to do.
- **Force:** what a landed blow does, and who wins a grapple.
- **Trained technique and kit:** reach, armour, the weapon.

**Cost:** fatigue (fights are short because they are exhausting) and wounds. **Risk:** death, maiming,
capture, shame.

**Who is the better duellist:** the one who reads the other's intent and answers first, keeps his
technique while afraid, and lasts the exchange. The stronger man wins grapples and breaks guards. The
quicker man sets the timing. The braver man accepts the fight at all, and that is a decision before
the dice. **The better archer** has a steady eye and hand, and, with a war bow, the force to draw it.

**The sheet should predict:** who strikes first, how hard he hits, how much he can take, whether he
will stand or accept, and how well trained he is.

### 3.2 Mass battle (MB)

**What people are trying to do:** take or hold ground, destroy or preserve an army, force terms.
**The loop:** levy and recruit, then muster and drill, then provision. March (attrition, disease,
desertion), reconnoitre, encamp, fortify, forage. Choose ground, then marshal the order of battle.
Engage: missile, shock, the line that withstands, the commander who rallies. Then rout, pursuit or
orderly retreat, or the long sit of a siege, a storm, and a sack or a surrender on terms.

**Where success comes from:** numbers, supply, ground and cohesion, and **command**: seeing the
ground, timing the reserve, holding men by presence. Historically, more armies were lost to supply and
disease than to battle. The quartermaster matters as much as the general.

**Cost:** stores, men, the land foraged over. **Risk:** rout, which turns a loss into a massacre in
pursuit.

**Who is better:**

- **The general** reads ground (fieldcraft on top of watchfulness), plans supply (forethought,
  reckoning), decides in time (composure under pressure), and holds men to him (presence, nerve shown
  in the front rank).
- **The quartermaster** reckons, sees ahead, and is honest with stores (constancy).
- **The scout** is watchful, knows terrain, moves unseen, and lasts the ride.
- **The sergeant who drills** is patient and imposing.

**Troops differ** in their training and experience (drill, campaigns survived), their kit, and their
commander. That is a lane demand too, but the verbs reach it independently through drill, muster and
command. **The sheet should predict:** how many he can order well, whether men hold under him, how he
reads ground, and how well he feeds an army.

### 3.3 Social contest (SC)

**What people are trying to do:** win a decision from an audience, a bench or a counterparty; bind
someone to terms; get a petition granted; move a belief. **The loop:** a matter is set (open a case,
convene, petition, challenge). Exchanges follow: argue, refute, plead, persuade, flatter, threaten,
testify, lie, concede, insult. Then a determination by the bench, a vote, or a mutual covenant. The
result is fixed in a record and a binding (oblige).

**Where success comes from:**

- **What the audience already believes and owes:** standing and relationships. These are holdings, not
  capacities.
- **The quality of the case:** reasoning, law and custom, memory for what was said.
- **Delivery:** presence and fluency.
- **Fit to the hearer:** reading people, warmth.
- **Credibility:** a record of kept faith, plus standing.
- **Composure under attack.**

Classical rhetoric's three proofs line up with this: *logos* (the case), *ethos* (credibility,
standing) and *pathos* (the hearer moved).

**Cost:** standing spent, debts incurred, enemies made. **Risk:** humiliation, a duel provoked, being
bound to terms one cannot keep.

**Who is better:**

- **The negotiator** reads people, judges value, outwaits (constancy) and does not show his hand
  (composure).
- **The advocate** reasons, knows the law, remembers, and speaks well.
- **The orator or preacher** has presence and conviction, and moves crowds.
- **The courtier** is warm, reads people, knows manners, and flatters without being seen to.

**The sheet should predict:** whether a crowd will listen, whether a bench can be moved, whether a
counterparty will believe him, and whether he can keep a straight face.

### 3.4 Fieldwork and inquiry (FI)

**What people are trying to do:** learn what happened, who did it, what a document says, where a
person is; or keep those things hidden. **The loop:** notice and open a case. Gather by examining,
searching, tracking, interviewing, interrogating, surveilling, shadowing, eavesdropping, researching
and auditing. Assemble by reconstructing, construing and deciphering. Act on the finding: charge,
tell, testify.

**Every gathering act has a hiding act opposed to it:** conceal, forge, lie, destroy, disguise,
smuggle, infiltrate. How well a thing was hidden should set what the seeker faces. The FI lane asks
for this too (M-FI-4), but the symmetry follows from the verbs alone.

**Where success comes from:**

- **At the scene:** senses and fine discrimination.
- **With witnesses:** reading people and composure.
- **With documents:** letters and reasoning.
- **Throughout:** memory and patience.

**Cost:** time; exposure (watching risks being watched). **Risk:** a false conclusion held
confidently, being found out, retaliation.

**Who is better:**

- **The investigator** is watchful, discriminating, a reader of people, and a reasoner with a good
  memory.
- **The spy** moves unseen, keeps composure, takes another's guise, and has connections. The verbs make
  this a different person from the investigator.
- **The forger** has a fine hand, letters, and knowledge of real documents' forms.

**The sheet should predict:** what he will notice, whom he will see through, what he can hide, and
how long he will keep at it.

### 3.5 Threadwork (TW) [INFERRED throughout]

All I have are the verb names (`thread_read`, `tie / knot`, and the lane's Weave, Pull, Knot/Lock,
Loosen/Sever/Dissolve, Mend, the Leap) and "Coherence" as a cost. **What people seem to be trying to
do:** perceive and alter strands that, in this setting, bind persons, places and oaths; mend or
harm; cross distance (the Leap).

**The loop could be:**

1. Perceive (`thread_read`, trace).
2. Choose an operation (weave, pull, lock, loosen, sever, mend, ward, anchor).
3. Pay in self-integrity.
4. Change the world.

**Where success might come from:** perceiving the strands (a gift?), fine control (interlacing and
undoing without tearing), lore (what a strand is), and the capacity to bear the cost without coming
apart.

**Who is better:** a seer who perceives much but handles little; a weaver with fine control and lore;
someone who can do much because he holds together.

**The etymology adds this.** The thread verbs share root senses with the social binding verbs (§5.5).
*bind*: oblige, tie, rally, marry, weave, lock. *loosen*: release, waive, loosen, sever. *draw*: train,
pull, trace. The setting's threadwork reads as the binding metaphor of oaths and obligations made
literal. Whether that metaphor is one property of a person is tested in §5.5, and the answer is no.

**The sheet should predict:** whether he can see the threads, how fine his work is, and how much it
will cost him.

### 3.6 Settlements and governance (GV)

**What people are trying to do:** hold a seat; keep a place fed, ordered and paying; widen the remit;
survive rivals and the ruled.

**The loop, a season:**

- **Collect:** tax, requisition, levy.
- **Spend:** build, found, provision, relieve, ration.
- **Hear:** petitions; open a case; determine; pardon; detain or ban.
- **Appoint and remove:** establish, confer, revoke.
- **Enforce:** arrest, patrol, guard.
- **Answer the ruled:** they comply, evade or defy.
- **Succession:** succeed, abdicate, depose.

**Where success comes from:** the seat's remit, the right to do these acts at all. That is a holding.
The **personal** differences are:

- **forethought and reckoning**: stores across seasons, a budget that holds;
- **judgement of people**: honest appointees;
- **credibility**: legitimacy is what the ruled believe;
- **presence**: orders obeyed without force;
- **constancy**: the honest steward against the embezzler;
- **warmth**: relief given, levies that come willingly.

**Cost:** stores and goodwill. **Risk:** defiance, flight, unrest, deposition.

**Who is better:** **the governor** foresees, reckons, judges people and is believed. **The steward**
reckons and is constant. **The judge** discriminates finely, knows the law, and is believed to be
fair.

**The etymology adds this.** The governance verbs are almost all Latinate, and their root senses are
bodily acts. *confer* is "bear together". *establish* is "make stand". *oblige* is "bind". *revoke* is
"call back". *arrest* is "make stay". *levy* is "raise". *charge* is "load". *determine* is "set the
boundary stone". The body's demand has been replaced by the seat's: a man needs arms to carry, and a
seat to confer. **The office works as a borrowed body.** This bears on whether standing should be an
attribute (§6.4).

**The sheet should predict:** how well he keeps a place in stores and order, whom he would appoint,
and whether he is believed.

### 3.7 Faction actions (FA)

**What people are trying to do:** shift power among organized bodies (houses, a church, guilds, the
crown). **The loop:**

1. A head utters a goal and members commit.
2. Factions covenant, marry, negotiate.
3. They recruit and suborn, conspire, infiltrate.
4. They declare and dispatch, march, seize, raze.
5. Someone may betray, avenge or ransom.

**Where success comes from:** a faction's following, connections and means; these are holdings. The
**head's** person shows through leadership (presence, warmth), judgement of people (who will betray),
and secrecy (composure, stealth). The rank and file show through constancy (who keeps the covenant).

**Cost:** means, trust. **Risk:** a betrayal that carries a bloc; war.

**Who is better:**

- **The plotter** reads people, keeps composure, is believed by fellow plotters, has connections, and
  is constant inside the cabal.
- **The head** has warmth and presence (loyalty) and forethought.
- **The envoy** has fluency, reads people, and judges value.

**The sheet should predict:** whom he can hold, whom he will see through, and whether his word holds.

### 3.8 Economy, trade and craft (EC)

**What people are trying to do:** make, keep and grow goods. **The loop:** produce (cultivate, craft,
build, work, restore). Move (haul, transfer, sail). Value and trade (appraise, bargain, exchange,
lend, hire). Keep (hoard, steward). Evade (smuggle).

**Where success comes from:**

- **For the maker:** craft knowledge, a fine hand, force, stamina.
- **For the merchant:** judgement of value, reading people, reckoning.
- **For the labourer:** stamina.
- **For the steward:** reckoning and constancy.

**Cost:** time, materials, labour. **Risk:** loss, fraud, debt, a bad season.

**Who is better:** the master smith (craft, a fine hand, force); the merchant (value, reading people,
reckoning, patience); the quartermaster (forethought, reckoning, honesty).

**The sheet should predict:** what he can make and how well, whether he will get a good price, and
whether he can be trusted with stores.

### 3.9 World, travel and survival (WT)

**What people are trying to do:** get there, alive and on time. **The loop:** plan the route. Move by
road, river or sea (ride, sail, swim, climb, march). Endure weather and want. Forage and hunt. Find
the way (navigate, track). Shelter and recover.

**Where success comes from:** toughness (privation, exertion), fieldcraft (terrain, signs),
riding and watercraft (skills), watchfulness (danger seen in time), and quickness (footing on a
cliff).

**Cost:** fatigue, health, stores, days. **Risk:** illness, injury, ambush, getting lost.

**Who is better:** **the scout or guide** has toughness, fieldcraft and watchfulness. **The hunter**
reads signs, has a steady aim, and is patient.

**The sheet should predict:** how far and how fast he can go, what the road will cost him, and
whether he sees trouble first.

### 3.10 NPC life (NP)

**What people are trying to do:** live. Form and keep bonds, raise children, keep faith, settle
grievances, pursue an ambition. **The loop:**

- **Bond:** court, tie, marry, commit.
- **Keep or break:** keep a covenant, or betray, release, repudiate.
- **Care:** rear, tend, heal, mourn.
- **Grievance:** avenge or forgive.
- **Faith:** pray, confess, bless.
- **Learn:** study, train.
- **The body:** recover, age.

**Where difference comes from:** warmth (bonds form), constancy (bonds hold), and nerve (confession,
open defiance). **Above all, from values:** whether a man forgives or avenges, shows mercy or is
ruthless, believes or doubts. **Most of what makes NPC lives differ is what they WANT, not what they
CAN do.** The sheet needs a values block separate from its capacities.

**Cost:** time, grief. **Risk:** betrayal, feud, loss.

**Who is better:** **the healer** has knowledge of the body, care, a fine hand and patience. **The
preacher** has presence, conviction, doctrine and fluency. **The parent or guardian** has care and
patience.

**The sheet should predict:** who will stay loyal, who will forgive, who will avenge, and who is loved.

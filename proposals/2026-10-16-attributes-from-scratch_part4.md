## 6. The from-scratch attributes

### 6.1 Selection principles (stated before the sets)

| # | principle | test |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Breadth of demand** | at least 10 verbs and at least 4 subsystems draw on it as performer |
| P2 | **Independence between persons** | for each nearest neighbour, a recognizable person exists who is high in one and low in the other (named in each cut test) |
| P3 | **Readable** | a player can guess from the plain name which acts it helps, without a table |
| P4 | **Stable to carry** | it changes over seasons and years, not scenes; anything spent and refilled in play is a track |
| P5 | **Absorbs specific demands as uses** | specialties sit under it as skills; it names a capacity, the skill names a training |
| P6 | **Personal, not positional** | if it would pass to an heir or a successor with the seat, the purse or the retinue, it is a holding (§3.6: the office as a borrowed body) |
| P7 | **Capacity, not preference** | it says what a person *can* do; what he *wants* to do is a value (§3.10) |
| P8 | **Opposition symmetry** | every contested act's seeker and hider, attacker and defender, each stands on an attribute (§3.4; the 47 patient-side demands) |
| P9 | **A clean draw** | it moves mainly one kind of quantity (odds, cost, access or effect); a merger that mixes kinds needs a reason |
| P10 | **Root-sense support** (weak, tie-breaking) | a verb family whose original sense is the property exists in the language (§5.5) |

**P6 and P7 decide the biggest question before any attribute is chosen.** The holdings clusters (K23,
K24, K25) are larger than most personal clusters, but they fail P6, and the values (K22) fail P7. All
the brief's candidates were weighed: learned skills, resources and tracks could be primaries if the
principles favoured them. **These principles do not favour them.** The case for each is in §6.4.

### 6.2 The recommended set: fourteen

> **Bodily force · Quickness · Fine hand · Toughness · Watchfulness · Reading people · Reasoning ·
> Forethought · Nerve · Constancy · Composure · Presence · Warmth · Thread perception**

Served counts (verbs, performer and resister, with the resister-only count) and draws are computed in
§6.5's table. For each attribute below: what it measures, what it does not, what it serves, what it
moves, the cut test with its false-N-line check, and what it leaves to skills, holdings or computed
reads.

### 6.3 The fourteen, defined

**1. Bodily force.** *Measures* the force a body can put out in a moment: the weight of a blow, a
lift, a grip, the draw of a heavy bow. *Not* how long it lasts (toughness), nor technique (skills).
*Serves* 15 verbs in PC, MB, GV, EC, WT: fight, strike, grapple, rush, rescue, arrest, haul, work,
craft, climb, shoot (war bow). *Moves* effect (13), plus odds when resisting a grapple or arrest (5).
*Cut test:* fold it into toughness, and the wiry long-distance man and the heavy wrestler become one
person; hauling and blow weight are read off how long someone lasts. *False-N check:* could skill and
kit absorb it? Partly: a trained cut needs little force. But grappling, hauling, labour output and the
war bow cannot be skilled around. The N-line holds in PC, MB, EC and WT, and is **narrowed** in SC, FI, TW,
FA and NP, where nothing draws it. *Leaves:* load carried (computed: force with toughness), reach (kit).

**2. Quickness.** *Measures* the speed and balance of the body's response: reacting, dodging, timing
a blow, keeping footing, leaping, climbing, keeping a saddle. *Not* speed of thought (composure), nor
precision of the hand (fine hand). *Serves* 16 verbs in PC, MB, TW, GV, WT. *Moves* odds only (20 of
20). *Cut test:* fold it into force, and the heavy-slow against light-fast trade dies. That trade is the
most legible choice in personal combat. Fold it into fine hand, and dodging reads off a jeweller's
precision. *False-N check:* the N-line is real in PC and WT and **narrowed** elsewhere. Quickness is a
fighting-and-travel attribute: nobody negotiates or governs with it. Kept because PC needs an odds
attribute that is not force. *Leaves:* stealth's movement half (stealth as a skill riding quickness
and composure), horsemanship (a skill).

**3. Fine hand.** *Measures* precision of hand and eye: a fair hand, a forger's line, a surgeon's cut,
a picked lock, an aimed shot, a woven pattern, a strand undone without tearing. *Not* speed (quickness),
nor knowledge of the craft (skills). *Serves* 11 verbs, spread over nine subsystems (the widest spread
per verb of any attribute). *Moves* odds (7) and effect (4). *Cut test:* fold it into quickness, and the
forger, the cutpurse and the surgeon read their art off reaction speed. Fold it into skill, and equally
trained craftsmen cannot differ by talent. *False-N check:* holds. It is the craft economy's and the
inquiry's talent attribute, and the forger who sets the examiner's obstacle stands on it (P8).
*Leaves:* each trade as a skill.

**4. Toughness.** *Measures* what the body can bear: sustained exertion, hunger, cold and the road,
harm taken and recovered from. *Not* the courage to bear pain (nerve), nor the refusal to give up
(constancy). *Serves* 26 verbs in nine subsystems: march, migrate, move, work, build, haul, forage,
besiege, patrol, endure, fight (as harm taken), recover. *Moves* cost (23) and effect (5). *Cut test:*
without it, the march, labour, the road, the siege and the wound cost everyone the same. Mass-battle
attrition and travel lose their person-level variance. *False-N check:* the strongest N-line among the
body attributes. Nothing else moves cost. *Leaves:* fatigue and harm as **tracks** that toughness sets
the size or recovery of. *Note:* splitting it into stamina and hardiness fails P1 for hardiness (§6.5).
Being hurt is not a verb, so the verbs under-sample it.

**5. Watchfulness.** *Measures* what a person notices and how long he keeps noticing: keen senses and
sustained vigilance. *Not* interpreting what was noticed (reasoning), nor reading intent in faces
(reading people). *Serves* 17 verbs (4 as resister), nine subsystems. *Moves* odds (17) and cost (6).
*Cut test:* fold it into reasoning, and the hawk-eyed unlettered sentry and the short-sighted scholar
are the same; the concealment acts (steal, shadow, ambush, eavesdrop, smuggle) lose their opposing
number. *False-N check:* holds on P8. The *watch* family supports it (P10). *Leaves:* fieldcraft
(reading terrain and signs) as a skill riding watchfulness and reasoning. Field sense (K06, 13 verbs)
is learned: a townsman and a forester with the same eyes read a wood differently.

**6. Reading people.** *Measures* seeing what another wants, fears and intends, whether he lies, and
what he is good for. *Not* being liked (warmth), nor knowing what to say (a skill), nor what one shows
(composure). *Serves* 23 verbs, eight subsystems: interview, interrogate, persuade, flatter, bargain,
negotiate, bribe, suborn, mediate, lend, parry, feint, and the choosers' verbs (confer, designate,
hire, recruit). *Moves* odds (22). *Cut test:* fold it into warmth, and the cold manipulator and the
kindly dupe collapse into one person. Lie, feint, betray and flatter lose their opposition. Choosing an
appointee stops depending on who chooses. *False-N check:* holds in SC, FI, FA, GV and PC (parry and
feint).

**7. Reasoning.** *Measures* assembling meaning from parts and weighing evidence finely, with the
memory to hold the particulars: the interpreter, the investigator, the scholar, the advocate, the judge.
*Not* planning and reckoning (forethought), nor improvising under pressure (composure). *Serves* 20
verbs, eight subsystems: construe, reconstruct, examine, research, argue, refute, decipher, determine,
testify, tell, thread_read. *Moves* odds (16), effect (7). *Cut test:* fold it into forethought, and the
scholar and the steward merge; inquiry and the contest of argument read off a logistician's number.
*False-N check:* holds in FI and SC; **narrowed** in MB and WT, where nothing draws it. *Leaves:* letters, law, lore
(skills); memory, folded in (§6.5 splits it).

**8. Forethought.** *Measures* seeing ahead and reckoning: planning, counting stores, judging value,
choosing the moment. *Not* understanding a text (reasoning), nor keeping to the plan (constancy).
*Serves* 25 verbs, seven subsystems: provision, found, establish, dispatch, besiege, tax, ration, audit,
appraise, exchange, bargain, ransom, counsel, abdicate (the right moment), withdraw. *Moves* effect
(22): the largest effect-mover of any attribute. *Cut test:* fold it into reasoning, and the
quartermaster, the merchant, the steward and the planning general become scholars. The economy's and
logistics' person-level variance is gone. *False-N check:* holds in MB, GV and EC; **narrowed** in PC
(only yield and surrender). The *price* and *see ahead* families support it (P10). *Leaves:* command of
many (computed from presence, forethought, knowledge of war and following).

**9. Nerve.** *Measures* willingness and ability to act in the face of danger, shame or a stronger
opponent: to accept, challenge, defy, rescue, interpose, storm, confess, refuse the powerful. *Not*
functioning well once in it (composure), nor keeping at it (constancy). *Serves* 19 verbs in all ten
subsystems. *Moves* **access** (16 of 20). *Cut test:* fold it into composure, and the brave hothead
and the cool coward collapse. Every challenge, defiance and confession reads off coolness. *False-N
check:* holds. **But its draw says where it lives.** Nerve decides *whether*, not *how well*. It may
belong in the chooser (a threshold on which acts a person will consider) rather than in a resolution
roll. This is left open; the same attribute serves either use.

**10. Constancy.** *Measures* holding a course over time: keeping one's word, outlasting, patient
application, and not being worn down, persuaded or bought. *Not* courage in the moment (nerve), nor the
body's endurance (toughness). *Serves* 30 verbs in all ten subsystems: commit, covenant, steward,
besiege, withstand, pursue, research, study, train, drill, cultivate, search, audit, anchor, lock, avenge;
and as resister, persuade, interrogate, bribe, suborn. *Moves* cost (19), odds when resisting (6).
*Cut test:* fold it into nerve, and the timid but steadfast clerk and the brave oath-breaker collapse;
every patient act (research, siege, training, farming) costs the same. *False-N check:* the strongest
N-line among the personal attributes: all ten subsystems draw it. It has the most root-sense support
(17 R tags). *Leaves:* a **record** of kept and broken word, which is world state. Credibility is
computed from that record and from standing (§6.4).

**11. Composure.** *Measures* keeping one's head under scrutiny and pressure: no tell, a ready answer,
a decision in time, a convincing other self. *Not* daring (nerve), nor reading the other (reading
people). *Serves* 17 verbs, eight subsystems: lie, conceal, disguise, infiltrate, feint, betray (until
the moment), threaten (no bluff shown), concede, yield, retreat (no panic), command (orders in time),
smuggle (at the gate); and as resister, interview, interrogate, insult. *Moves* odds (19). *Cut test:*
fold it into reading people, and the liar and the lie-detector become one number, so the opposition
dies (P8). Fold it into nerve: see nerve. *False-N check:* holds. *Includes* quick wits (invention
under pressure, quick judgement; 6 verbs), which fails P1 on its own (§6.5).

**12. Presence.** *Measures* the bearing and voice that make others attend and obey: the order
followed, the threat believed, the crowd stirred, the proclamation heard. *Not* being liked (warmth),
nor holding a rank (standing, a holding). *Serves* 20 verbs, eight subsystems: command, rally, muster,
levy, proclaim, preach, ban, declare, utter, challenge, threaten, train, drill. *Moves* effect (16). *Cut
test:* fold it into warmth, and the feared captain and the loved priest are the same person. Fold it
into standing, and the commoner who commands a mob and the king nobody heeds both disappear. *False-N
check:* holds. The *proclaim* family supports it (P10).

**13. Warmth.** *Measures* being liked and caring for others: bonds that form, patients tended, levies
that come willingly, a gift that lands. *Not* reading people, nor presence. *Serves* 14 verbs, nine
subsystems: give, tie, court, persuade, flatter, recruit, levy, relieve, tend, heal, rear, mourn,
interview. *Moves* effect (12). *Cut test:* fold it into presence or reading people (see each). Without
it, NPC life loses the only capacity that explains who is loved. *False-N check:* holds in NP, SC and
FA; **narrowed** in PC, where nothing draws it.

**14. Thread perception [INFERRED; the weakest foundation in the set].** *Measures* the gift of
perceiving the threads: whether and how well a person sees them, the ground on which handling is
learned. *Not* the lore (a skill), the handling technique (a skill on fine hand and thread lore), nor
the cost borne (a track). *Serves* 12 verbs, six subsystems (all of TW's, and PC, FI, EC, WT and NP
through ward, trace, mend and the leap). *Moves* effect (11), cost (5). *Cut test:* remove it, and
threadwork becomes pure skill that anyone may learn to see. Whether that erases a distinction the
setting depends on, I cannot check in a blind pass. *False-N check:* **unverified.** Its case rests on
the guess that perception of the threads is a gift, not a training.

### 6.4 What the fourteen leave to other layers (and why none of these is a primary)

| layer | what it holds | from which clusters | why not an attribute |
|---|---|---|---|
| **skills** | weapon craft · horsemanship · watercraft · letters · law and custom · doctrine and rite · each trade · the body and its cures · war · thread lore · rhetoric · fieldcraft · stealth · speech | K21, K06, K18, K20 | learned (P4, P5); each rides one or more attributes. Fluency (K20, 10 verbs) is the closest call: §6.5 promotes it |
| **values** | mercy against ruthlessness · conviction · restraint · readiness to let go | K22 | preference, not capacity (P7). They move access (14 of 21): they belong in the chooser |
| **holdings** | rank and office · a seat's remit · a lawful claim · means · arms and kit · a place held · a following · connections | K23, K24, K25 | positional (P6). They pass with the seat, the purse or the retinue. §3.6: the office is a borrowed body |
| **computed** | credibility (record of kept word + standing + the hearer's regard) · command of many (presence + forethought + war + following) · load carried · who acts first | K26, K16 | depend on the moment and on another person; storing them would double-store world state |
| **tracks** | harm · fatigue · stores · self-integrity under the threads · reputation as it moves | parts of K04, K19, K24 | spent and refilled in play (P4) |
| **situation** | knowledge of the matter · being at the place | K27 | world state, not person |

**Are standing or means a case for a primary?** The brief invites it, and standing has the second-largest
cluster (40 verbs). It fails P6 and P9: it moves access (37 of 40) and passes to a successor. A sheet
should *show* rank, means and following. They are read from holdings, not carried as attributes.
**Are skills a case for primaries?** Skills are the largest cluster (49 verbs), but no single skill
reaches P1. Letters and law and custom are the broadest, at 8 verbs each. Each is narrow, and as a
group they are what attributes absorb (P5).

### 6.5 A smaller and a larger alternative

**Smaller: eight.** *Brawn* (force + toughness) · *Deftness* (quickness + fine hand) · *Perception*
(watchfulness + reading people) · *Mind* (reasoning + forethought) · *Heart* (nerve + constancy) · *Poise*
(composure) · *Bearing* (presence + warmth) · *Thread perception*.

- **Gains:** every attribute serves 12 verbs or more in six subsystems or more. It is quick to read
  and familiar from many games.
- **Loses six contrasts the subsystem accounts named:**
  - the heavy-slow fighter against the light-fast one;
  - the jeweller against the brawler;
  - the sentry against the confessor;
  - the scholar against the steward;
  - the brave oath-breaker against the timid steadfast man;
  - the feared lord against the loved one.
- **P9 suffers.** *Heart* moves access and cost. *Brawn* moves cost and effect. *Bearing* mixes command
  and affection.

**Larger: twenty.** The fourteen, with toughness split into **stamina** and **hardiness**, reasoning
split into **reasoning** and **memory**, and composure split into **composure** and **quick wits**.
Plus **fluency** (the gift of words), **unobtrusiveness** (the forgettable face, the quiet foot: K18
promoted from skill), and **standing** (rank that others recognize, promoted from holding).

- **Gains:** the wounded-but-unwearying man, the forgetful genius, the eloquent clerk and the man no
  one remembers seeing become expressible.
- **Costs:**
  - *hardiness* (8 verbs counting resister, 5 as performer) and *quick wits* (6) fail P1;
  - *standing* fails P6;
  - *unobtrusiveness* duplicates stealth's skill;
  - twenty numbers cannot be read at a glance (P3).

# THE NERS AUDIT BRIEF — pessimistic steelman, E as a ratio

## WHAT YOU ARE AUDITING

Read 01 (the spine) first, then your assigned documents, then 15 (the adjudication register, which

## THE METHOD: STEELMAN FIRST, THEN ATTACK. BOTH ARE MANDATORY.
For each object you assess: state the strongest case FOR it in its own terms, as its author would
put it and slightly better. Only then attack. A critique that skips the steelman is not admissible,
because the failure this audit exists to catch is OVER-DISTILLATION, and you cannot detect
over-distillation without first understanding what an object was doing.

## JORDAN'S CRITERIA, AND THE ONE RULE THAT CHANGES HOW YOU SCORE
N NECESSARY - cannot be removed without affecting EMERGENCE
E ELEGANT   - as distilled as possible WITHOUT AFFECTING FUNCTION
R ROBUST    - offers CHOICES WITH REAL IMPACTS for the player
S SMOOTH    - integrates well and PROPAGATES AS REQUIRED ACROSS SCALES

*** E IS NOT AN INDEPENDENT AXIS. IT IS A RATIO AGAINST N AND R. ***
Jordan's ruling verbatim: "distill as much as possible without losing emergent possibilities or
robust choosing for the player." That is E CONSTRAINED BY N AND BY R, in one sentence.

YOU MAY NOT SCORE FOUR AXES INDEPENDENTLY AND AVERAGE THEM. An averaging audit scores an amputated
design as elegant, which is the exact failure mode this rule exists to prevent. Instead:

  For every object, compute the RATIO:  what it costs in complexity  AGAINST  what dies without it.
  - An object that cannot name a lost emergent possibility is E-SURPLUS. Say so and cut it.
  - An object whose loss is named AND REAL is N-NECESSARY and MAY NOT BE DISTILLED AWAY, no matter
    how much simpler the result would be. Simplicity is not a defence against necessity.
  - THE MOST VALUABLE FINDING YOU CAN RETURN IS A FALSE N-LINE: an object whose claimed lost
    possibility ACTUALLY SURVIVES THE CUT via some other object. That is how surplus gets kept and
    how the elegance test gets cheated. Hunt these specifically.

*** S IS T5 AND T6 RESTATED. IT IS NOT A STYLISTIC JUDGMENT. ***
Decide S by exactly two questions, and answer them concretely with a traced example:
  S-UP   can a DEMAND travel up the ladder, and be FILTERED BY A NAMED PERSON at a rung?
  S-DOWN can an OPPORTUNITY travel down and reach A PERSON WHO HOLDS NO POST?
The prior suite failed BOTH structurally. Verify, do not assume, that this one does not.

*** R IS STRUCTURAL DOMINANCE DETECTION, NOT BALANCE. ***
A STRUCTURALLY DOMINANT OPTION IS A DESIGN FAILURE, not something to tune later.
For every player fork in your documents, compare THE SHAPE OF GAIN against THE SHAPE OF COST OVER
TIME. Decaying gain against compounding cost = dominance = FAIL.
Jordan's worked case: a Comply/Defy fork where Defy's gain decayed geometrically while its costs
were durable and compounded into next season's obstacle.
Also apply the precedent test: RUN MAXIMUM AVAILABLE MITIGATION AGAINST MAXIMUM ACCRUAL and check
the net is recoverable. A mechanism tuned never to reach its failure state is indistinguishable from
one that does not exist.

## EMERGENT NARRATIVE MUST BE PRESERVED — this is a hard constraint, not a preference
Flag as a DEFECT any mechanism that:
  - special-cases a named entity or a scripted outcome;
  - resolves a throughline by ENUMERATING CASES rather than by COMPOSING PRIMITIVES;
  - would produce the same story every campaign;
  - forecloses a class of story the setting obviously wants (a slow revenge, a secret that surfaces
    twenty years late, a lie that works, an institution harming people nobody intends to harm).
Conversely, CREDIT specifically where a mechanism produces a named setting outcome WITHOUT that
outcome being authored. Those are the suite's best moments and the audit should identify them, not
only its failures.

## THE NINE THROUGHLINES — what the design must express
T1 all actions performed by characters · T2 memories, feelings, beliefs that change · T3 fallible
memory, bias, multiple perspectives · T4 no one is omniscient · T5 upward aggregation and filtering
· T6 downward ripple reaching individuals · T7 events/clocks/gates as the basis of debate and
negotiation · T8 the world churns without the player · T9 field investigations first-class

## RETURN FORMAT — fixed, at most 1,600 words
1. VERDICT PER THROUGHLINE you can assess: SUPPORTED / PARTIAL / BLOCKED, one sentence each.
2. THE E-RATIO TABLE: your assigned documents' major objects, each with cost, what dies without it,
   and a verdict of NECESSARY / SURPLUS / FALSE-N-LINE.
3. FALSE N-LINES you found, with the object that actually preserves the named possibility. Rank
   these first if you found any -- they are the highest-value output of this audit.
4. R-FAILURES: forks where gain decays while cost compounds, or where a failure state is unreachable.
5. S VERDICT with a traced example each for S-UP and S-DOWN.
6. EMERGENCE DEFECTS, and EMERGENCE CREDITS.
7. WHAT YOU WOULD CUT and WHAT YOU WOULD REFUSE TO CUT even under pressure to simplify.
8. THE ONE THING MOST LIKELY TO BE WRONG in what you read.

Be adversarial. Do not be encouraging. Do not propose replacement designs -- find defects.

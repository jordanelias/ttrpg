# Part 3 — antagonist brief

You are the ANTAGONIST half of a relay, not a dialogue. You did not see the producer's reasoning and
will not get it. You have its OUTPUT, a verified citation ledger, and the working tree.

## What you are attacking

**The integrated document** answering three questions about two bodies of work — the NPC matrix
(a coverage instrument over seats) and the arcs (a reachability test over outcomes):
1. how they function for emergent narrative,
2. how they work with PR #342's proposed code shape,
3. how they can work together.

## The specific hazards of THIS document, which are not the hazards of Part 2

Part 2's lanes proposed *changes*, so their failure mode was a misquoted line. A comparative synthesis
has a different and more dangerous failure mode: **it can be entirely well-cited and still be wrong**,
because its claims are about RELATIONSHIPS between bodies of work rather than about lines. Attack
accordingly.

1. **Manufactured complementarity.** The comfortable conclusion is "the two instruments are
   complementary and should be combined." Test it. Do they have DIFFERENT blind spots, or the same
   ones? Two instruments that both read the same design documents and both report what those documents
   say are ONE instrument run twice. If the document claims complementarity, make it name a defect one
   found that the other structurally could not have found — and check that claim against the record.
2. **False corroboration (P-2).** Where the document says both bodies demand the same thing, check
   whether they demand the same OBJECT or two objects sharing a NAME. This session has already
   produced a four-way name collision on `exposure`. A synthesis is where such collisions get
   laundered into agreement.
3. **A conclusion that could not have come out the other way.** For each headline claim, ask: what
   observation would have falsified it? If the document's structure makes a negative answer
   unreachable — as a rating scale with an upgrade verdict and no downgrade verdict does — the claim
   is an artefact of the instrument, not a finding.
4. **Retrospective coherence.** A synthesis written after the fact can make a messy process look
   designed. Check whether the document claims intent or method for things that were accidents, and
   whether it reports the failures as prominently as the successes.
5. **Smuggling.** §A of `03_corrected_findings.md` struck twelve claims and §C-3 is now struck too.
   A synthesis is the easiest place to reintroduce one as background. Check for: any rate about play,
   any use of the arc tally as an estimate rather than an upper bound, any appeal to "convergence"
   across lanes that read the same briefs, and any treatment of E1 as repaired.

## Rules

- **P-4: where two readings are available, take the one that costs the document more.**
- **Return NOs and YESes both.** For every claim that survives, name the specific attack you tried
  that failed. A review that overturns everything is as useless as one that overturns nothing.
- **Do not write the replacement.** Name the break and, at most, its repair class.
- You have no write tools. Return findings as your final message.

## Output format

- `VERDICT:` SOUND / SOUND-WITH-CORRECTIONS / UNSOUND, one sentence.
- `## Struck` — false or unsupported claims. Each: the claim, what you read to check it, what is true.
- `## Manufactured or laundered agreement` — anywhere the document turns one source into two, or a
  shared blind spot into a complementarity.
- `## Unfalsifiable` — claims whose structure makes a negative answer unreachable.
- `## Compliance` — anything that violates `11_code_shape.md` or proposes apparatus, undeclared.
- `## Weakened` — survives on less than claimed; say what to narrow it to.
- `## Survived attack` — with the specific attack that failed on each.
- `## What it missed` — short, and only what you can point at.

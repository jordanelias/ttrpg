# D-2, THE ACT ECONOMY — a proposed resolution
# Licensed by Jordan mid-session: "you can propose ways to resolve D-2 act economy."
# This is a PROPOSAL with its alternative stated, not a ruling.

## The fork as the design states it

`10_SUPERSEDING.md` §6.1: #342 asserts one act per season (`09:33`) and then narrates a Duke taking
seven verbs in one turn under a header claiming ten (`14:562`) — "wrong under every reading, including
its own". `14:91-92`'s `seat_items` "consume the holder's own hours", already a fourth per-holder
capacity against `14 §1`'s "three quantities and nothing else". §16 states the stake: **a personnel
game or a decree game**, and whether a player-Duke experiences the top of the ladder as a demotion.
D-16 adds the exploit any answer must price: **individuate your own cohort and get eleven acts.**

## The diagnosis: the fork is false because ONE WORD is doing two jobs

*Act* is being used for two different scarce things, and the design already distinguishes them
everywhere except here.

| | what it is | genuinely scarce? |
|---|---|---|
| **personal attention** | what YOU do with your season | **yes, and identically at every rung.** A Duke has the same hours as a fisher |
| **institutional throughput** | what your OFFICE does, performed by the people it employs | **yes, but it is a different quantity** — it scales with the establishment, not with the holder |

§4.4 already ships this distinction and stops one step short of it: *"when an act is performed **by
remit**, the pool is drawn from the **establishment** — the named persons the office employs — not from
the holder's own capability … **Choosing which of your people performs the act is the whole of a
leader's tactical choice.**"*

> **If the POOL for an act by remit comes from the establishment, the ACT does too.** That is the whole
> proposal. The design already moved the dice off the holder and left the act on him.

## THE PROPOSAL

> **ONE ACT PER PERSON PER SEASON. Universally. No office, rank, or holding changes it, ever.**
>
> **An office's throughput is its ESTABLISHMENT's acts** — and every member of an establishment is a
> named person who has exactly one act, their own stance, their own ledger, and the standing option to
> refuse, comply badly, or defect.

So a Duke does not take thirty-five acts. He takes **one** — `dispatch` — and thirty-five named people
each spend **their** one act deciding what to do about it. That is `§9.3`'s one-order-many-executors,
already shipped, now paid for out of a budget that exists.

### What it does to the fork

**It is the personnel game, and it removes the demotion.** The Duke's leverage was never more hours; it
is that *his one act moves other people's acts*. A fisher's `alter` moves a boat. A Duke's `dispatch`
moves thirty-five seasons. **Same allowance, incomparable reach** — which is exactly Jordan's *"factions
are only as strong as the people under their purview, and the extent to which those people can
influence beyond themselves."*

### What it does to D-16, and this is the part that earns it

The cohort exploit **prices itself, and turns into content.** Individuating your cohort to farm acts
gives you eleven *persons* — each with a ledger, a stance toward you, needs of their own, and the
ability to refuse. **You did not buy eleven acts; you created eleven people who might hate you.** The
loophole becomes the most characteristic political decision in the game: raising men gives you reach
and gives them names. No rule forbids it and none needs to.

### What it does to `seat_items` — the fourth quantity dissolves

`seat_items` was never a per-holder allowance; it is **the sitting's throughput**. Re-read `14:91-92`'s
own gloss under this proposal: *"Holding two offices does not double a day"* is **the one-act rule
already**, stated in the vocabulary of hours — you cannot be at two sittings. So:

> **One allowance — the act, one per person per season. One cap — items a sitting processes.**
> `seat_items` and `capacity(date)` are the same quantity seen from the office's side and the date's
> side, which is why the adversarial review found `capacity(date)` double-counted (spent at `carry`,
> then used again as the admission cap at `compose_agenda`). **Unify them and the double-count cannot
> be written.** `carry` spends the carrier's act; the sitting admits what it can process.

### Three shipped mechanisms that stop being in tension

- **§8.3 multi-petition** keeps its scarcity intact — three petitions still cost three seasons, so
  petition-spray never dominates. The §16 warning that *"under a multi-act reading, petition-spray
  dominates"* is answered by not taking that reading.
- **§11.3's lord paying his retinue** stops eating his season: he `dispatch`es a steward, and the
  steward spends the act. The objection §11.3 raises against one-act disappears without weakening it.
- **§17.4 item 1 — the missing "finite, contested, durable capacity object"** is **the establishment**,
  and under this proposal it finally is one: finite (named persons), contested (they can be bribed,
  killed, or turned), durable (they persist and remember). The design's own open finding closes here.

## THE ALTERNATIVE, stated fairly, because this is a proposal and not a ruling

**Several acts for an office-holder, scaling with rank.** It is defensible: it makes the top of the
ladder feel powerful *directly* rather than through other people, it needs no establishment roster to
be authored before a Duke is playable, and it matches the strategy-game convention where a bigger
polity simply does more per turn.

**What it costs, and why I do not recommend it:** scarcity is the only thing making refusal, delay and
obstruction matter, and it is the same scarcity at every rung that makes the low end playable — which
§17.1 records as the single largest thing the testing got right. A Duke with ten acts does not need his
people; he routes around them, and the establishment, the compliance contest and the whole
one-order-many-executors register become decoration. It also leaves D-16 unpriced, so it must then
*forbid* the cohort exploit by rule — a special case, which §14 row 13 refuses.

## What this proposal does NOT settle

It does not decide **how large an establishment is**, who authors the first one, or what an office
costs to staff — that is `upkeep` and it is unspecified. It does not decide whether a person may *bank*
an unspent act (recommend: no — an unspent season is the design's characteristic outcome, not a
resource). And it does not touch the reserved forks: conferral basis, `stores`-as-denominator, S19,
Coherence-0, off-board polities.

## 1. What a holder may read of another person

**The tree today** (opened, not recalled): `person_q.known_persons` is a sorted set from three sources — a truthy `exists:Person` reading, a `seen` claim's `who`, a told claim's last teller (`queries/person_q.py:1950-1985`); CR-81 writes its acquaintances as the first source (`boot/realm.py:768-783`, faction co-members and high-status seat holders, `:744-765`). `regard` scores a referent's deeds by the HOLDER's own pursuits (`person_q.py:599-619`; `deeds_judged`, `:103-149`; the deeds are `attributed_deeds`, `:192-208`). Nothing reads another person's pursuits: every pursuit reader takes the person himself (`crisis_weights`, `:1142`; `projected_axes`, `:1232`).

**What he may read: the other's pursuits, as his own ledger evidences them.** Every deed claim already carries its pursuit content: `data/verbs.py::align_kind(kind, axis)` (`:2445-2457`) is the alignment of an event kind on each pursuit axis — the table `deeds_judged` reads to score a deed by the judge's pursuits. Read the other way round it says which pursuits a deed shows in its doer. So:

```
inferred_pursuits(p, x)[axis] = Σ_{c ∈ attributed_deeds(p.ledger, x)} w(c) · align_kind(c.predicate, axis)  /  Σ_c w(c)
w(c) = the weight `p` already reads `c` by: `ledger_weigh(p, fx, tick)` (teller_weight, `decision/options.py:1582-1600`), 1.0 where it is None
```

— a vector over the existing pursuit axes, in `[-1, 1]` per axis, `None` where `p` attributes no deed to `x`. `p`'s told `self:` claims about `x` (what `x` said of himself, B10.65's stem, `person_q.py:1558`, `:1729-1731`) join as evidence on the axes their key names, at their told weight. Convictions, ambitions, goals, obligations and demands (ED-IN-0324's list) are read the same way only where a deed or a `self:` claim names them: an `oblige` edge he saw opened (`seen` on the seat, with `who` `x`) is an obligation read; a `commit` he saw is a conviction read; nothing else is guessed. **Person-side, no World, no `decision/` import:** `attributed_deeds` and `align_kind` are `queries/` and `data/`; the weigh closure is passed in, as `said_of` takes it (`person_q.py:1724-1728`).

**What he may not read:** anything his ledger does not hold. A stranger's pursuits are `None`, not a default; the true `Person.pursuits` is world truth and no reader here touches it (AX-2).

## 2. How the reading grows with what he holds: familiarity

```
familiarity(p, x) = 1 - 1 / (1 + D(p, x))           in [0, 1); 0 at no evidence
D(p, x) = the count of evidence items `inferred_pursuits` itself reads:
          distinct deed claims attributed to x (`attributed_deeds`; a `seen` sighting counts only through the deed it pairs, `deed_doer`)
        + told `self:` claims about x
        + p's own live Tenures read in §1 as an obligation or a conviction of x's
```

**The cut, taken (the lighter option, named):** familiarity counts only what evidences the reading it grades, i.e. it ranks pairs by attributed-deed count (plus the two small terms above). A first draft counted every source the charge lists — sightings with no deed, told claims whose teller is `x`, distinct origins, CR-81's `exists:Person` acquaintance claim — and so read one deed plus twenty bare sightings near 1, a confidence the inference had not earned (the critic's finding, accepted). Those other sources do not evidence a pursuit; they evidence acquaintance, and they enter where acquaintance is read: CR-81's claim and shared faction membership through §7's know-OF grade (membership is a live `commit`, world truth, `boot/realm.py:748-750`, so person-side it is reachable only through CR-81's seeded claim); his own tenures with `x` through the third term only where §1 reads them. **The N-line this narrows:** the charge's *"how the reading grows with what he holds of him"* holds for what he holds that evidences a pursuit, not for every claim that names `x`; a holder who has merely seen a man twenty times knows his face, not his aims. The `1 - 1/(1+D)` form needs no ceiling nobody chose and reads 0 at no evidence exactly; its slope is the build's one sweep point (`familiarity_scale` dividing `D`, swept 0.5 / 1 / 2) `[ASSUMPTION; medium; Jordan to correct; revert: D itself, uncapped]`.

**How familiarity sharpens the inference:** `inferred_pursuits` is already sharper with more evidence (more deeds, more axes touched); familiarity is the CONFIDENCE a consumer gives it. A consumer reads `familiarity(p, x) · inferred_pursuits(p, x)`. Because `D` counts exactly the inference's inputs, `familiarity` 0 and `inferred_pursuits` `None` coincide by construction: that is a definition, not a finding, and §5 does not test it. What §5 tests instead is that nothing ELSE leaks into a no-evidence reading.

**Attunement (ED-IN-0326) sharpens the person inference** as a `judgement` row of a new use site `person_read` on `rosters.yaml: tables.capability_uses` (key `attr.social.attunement`, side `actor`, gain `person_read_gain`, shipped `0`), read by `options.py::_judgement_factor` (`:1660-1674`) — the existing reader of a judgement row — and applied by the decision-side consumer to `familiarity`, never inside `person_q` (which may not import `decision/`, and a second reader of judgement rows there would be a second owner): a perceptive holder reads more from the same evidence. One site, one row, the shape `told_claim_credit` already has (`rosters.yaml:1566`, `:3771`). Capped at 1 as `competence` is (`options.py:1692-1696`), so `familiarity` stays below 1.

**The closure the charge names.** Item (2)'s *"passed to `queries/` as a closure so `decision/` is never imported (`world_q.py:2672`)"*: that line now reads `upkeep_of` (`world_q.py:2669`), so the cite is stale.

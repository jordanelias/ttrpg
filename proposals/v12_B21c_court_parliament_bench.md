## 1. The derivation: the bench is read off the title seats, and no seat is authored

**Claim.** A row reading the Court Parliament's bench seats **the live holders of a `hold` granted `BENCH_BASIS` (`determine`; `state/gate.py:200-204`, the one owner) on an Office whose `post` is a governance-ladder title** (`offices.yaml: titles.domains`, `:150-161`, through `data/rosters.py::title_domain`, the one owner) **governing a rung kind at or above `territory`**, and whose `rung` lies **inside the realm root `R`** of the matter's place: `R = ancestry(w, place_of(w, matter))[-1]` (`state/containment.py:136`, which returns the walk up to the root), and "inside" is `R in ancestry(w, office.rung)`. `R` is read off the containment tree, not off the row: `disposal_reach` is ruled to mean *who learns the ruling* (`proposals/2026-09-05-proceedings-subsystem/03_PARAMETERS.md:490`, `:287-293`: it scopes the outcome, not the sitting), so the bench does not key on it. A detained holder does not sit (`restrained(w, holder, DETAIN_KIND)`, the rule `judging_set` applies at `queries/world_q.py:1089`). The members are distinct persons, so a person holding two such seats is one member, as `bench.size` already counts them (`world_q.py:6523-6528`).

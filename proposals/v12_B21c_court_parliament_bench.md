## 1. The derivation: the bench is read off the title seats, and no seat is authored

**Claim.** A row reading the Court Parliament's bench seats **the live holders of a `hold` on an Office whose `post` is a governance-ladder title** (`offices.yaml: titles.domains`, `:150-161`, through `data/rosters.py::title_domain`, the one owner) **governing a rung kind at or above `territory`**, and whose `rung` lies **inside the sitting's reach** (`R`). `R` is the ancestor of the matter's place (`place_of`) whose rung kind is the row's own `disposal_reach` (`realm` for `parliamentary_debate`, `arrangements.yaml:354`), and "inside" is `R in ancestry(w, office.rung)` (`state/containment.py:136`, the one walk). A detained holder does not sit (`restrained(w, holder, DETAIN_KIND)`, the rule `judging_set` applies at `queries/world_q.py:1089`). The members are distinct persons, so a person holding two such seats is one member, as `bench.size` already counts them (`world_q.py:6523-6528`).

- **"Landed aristocrats" is the title seats, not rung holdings.** Jordan's message (1) in H-90 lists the titles as King/Queen, Duke/Duchess, Count/Countess and Lord; message (3) carries the ladder on down to mayor, community leader, family head and a person. The aristocratic titles therefore stop at Lord (`territory`), which sets the floor [ASSUMPTION; medium; Jordan to correct; revert: the floor at `duchy`, `boot/realm.py:741`'s `HIGH_STATUS_FLOOR`, the CR-81 precedent]. In the realm today the floor changes no member, because no Count or Lord seat is built. Reading rung holdings instead would seat the Confessor (`terr_T9`) and Altonia's `p_npc_089` (`terr_ALT`) (readings above), so one body would sit on the motion and also concur in it, which is the separation M8's single-signer arm protects (`engine/season/tests/test_cr37_deposition.py::test_a_planted_single_signer_is_refused`).
- **No `determine` grant is required to sit.** The duchy heads hold `determine` only by B21.53's revertible assumption (`offices.yaml:223, :236, :249`, "revert: drop `determine`"), so the bench's membership must not depend on it. The guard in `judging_set`'s docstring (`world_q.py:1032-1037`: a remit edit must reach the bench through an act, never through a hand-mutated field) still holds: a title seat's holder changes only through `confer`, `succeed`, `revoke` or `release`.
- **"Distinguished service" is not derived.** The only carrier that could read it is B21c.01's ladder posts. They mark rank, not distinction. In this tree two seats carry one (`off_senior_inquisitor`, `off_grand_master`), and seating either would rest on a rank cut-off that no source authors (§6, step 5).
- **Expected in the realm:** at the King's place this reading seats `{p_npc_020, p_npc_050, p_npc_052}`, three members through four seats.

**Falsifier.** On `build_realm(0)` with `parliamentary_debate` reading this bench, the bench for a matter docketed under it about `p_npc_020` is `{p_npc_020, p_npc_050, p_npc_052}`. The design is wrong if the bench lists anyone else (the Confessor, `p_npc_089`, any holder of a seat whose `body` is `Court Parliament`, of which there is none) or misses one of the three. **Control.** (a) After a hand-built `confer` that moves `off_duchess_hafenmark` to another person, that person replaces `p_npc_050` on the bench with no data edit: the bench is derived from the seats, not authored. (b) A `council_of_state` matter at the same place still reads `['p_npc_020']`, because that row keeps today's reading.

## 2. What each route costs

**(i) Grant `determine` to the duchy-head seats.** On this tree the edit has already been made: B21.53 (A-37) put `determine` on all three duchy-head rows (`offices.yaml:223, :236, :249`), so the cost the route names (the dukes judge trials in their duchies, which moves every determination in a duchy) has already been paid. It still seats no parliament. `judging_set` admits a seat only if its `scope_rung` contains the venue (`sits_over`, `state/gate.py:989-1007`, H-32's ruled default), so at the King's place the bench is the King through his two seats (readings above). To seat the dukes there, the route would have to change H-32's containment test, which moves every bench in the realm.

**(ii) A bench reading at the arrangement level.** One key on the `arrangements:` row (named here `bench_seats:`, values `over` and `within` [ASSUMPTION: the key's name; the two values are the decision]). `over` is today's reading on every row; `within` is §1's reading. The key is required of every row and refused outside its two values. `within` is also refused on a row whose `disposal_reach` is not a rung kind (`room`, `body`) or whose `disposes` is `hold`, because the electorate decides those rows. The loader refuses the key today (`data/arrangements.py:339`), so it enters `_ARRANGEMENT_KEYS` in the same edit, the way B05.21's `standard:` and CR-34's `quorum:` did. A key is needed because the readings already on the row cannot select the bench: `council_of_state` has the same `authority`, `disposes`, `venue_min_rank` and `disposal_reach` as the parliament (`arrangements.yaml:365-381`), and keying the bench on `floor` or `records_dissent` would tie it to an unrelated field. The rule is one predicate in `state/gate.py` beside `sits_over` (§1's test), asked by the three readers CR-35's electorate branch switches in the same way:
  1. `judging_set` (`world_q.py:1074-1092`);
  2. `sigma._bench_of` (`seam/wrappers/sigma.py:1739-1751`), which would otherwise narrow the bench again by `sits_over` and authority, as it now skips for the electorate;
  3. the precondition's `bench` stem (`world_q.py:6511-6522`), so the presiding actor is a member. Without this, a motion against the King in the realm has no presider: the King is its only `sits_over` seat-holder, and `may_determine` refuses an actor who is the party (`state/gate.py:1060`).

  `declared_votes_by_member` reads `judging_set` and follows without an edit. **Reach:** only a row declaring `within` moves. While H-80 holds, no computed docket item names an arrangement (`arrangements.yaml:654-655`; `petitioned_seat`'s docstring, `world_q.py:721-722`), so the realm reach is 0. The prediction is that B21c.07's realm hash at one season is EQUAL, not the "declared MOVER" that B21c.07's entry states (to be read at B21c.07, not asserted here).

**(iii) Keep the interim realm bench.** No build. In the realm a motion against the King cannot be brought at all, because no member other than the party can preside (above). Where a hand-built seat presides (B21c.05's `off_regent`), the motion carries on a seat that holds no land. With §3's flip at `quorum: 2`, every realm `parliamentary_debate` is refused `short_bench` (`sigma.py:1950-1961`).

**Falsifier.** (i) is wrong if `judging_set(w, place_of(w, "p_npc_020"))` on `build_realm(0)` lists `p_npc_050` or `p_npc_052` (it reads `['p_npc_020', 'p_npc_020']`, reading @8fe3b44). (ii) is wrong if a row carrying `bench_seats:` loads before the key is added to `_ARRANGEMENT_KEYS`. (iii) is wrong if a realm motion against the King is admitted with no hand-built seat. **Control.** For (i): `judging_set` at `duchy_hafenmark` reads `['p_npc_020', 'p_npc_050']`, so the grant is live and only the containment keeps the Duchess off the King's bench. For (ii): the tree as it stands loads, so the refusal is a refusal of the key and not of the row. ## 3. `parliamentary_debate` flips to `close: declared`, read with A-14

**Claim.** The row moves from `close: bench` to `close: declared` with `quorum: 2` (the loader requires `quorum:` exactly when the close is `declared`, `data/arrangements.py:418`), plus `bench_seats: within` (§2 (ii)). The obstacle CR-34 recorded against this flip, *"it disposes a `Record`, which `_eff_determine` declines"* (`arrangements.yaml:511-512`), is gone: B21c.04's `_eff_determine` mints a `resolution` under a row that disposes Record. Under A-14 (*"a draw on the voter, inside the presiding person's act"*) each member's ballot is a draw in the one sitting (`sigma.act_band`'s `declared` close, `sigma.py:2096-2097`: the margin of the quorum-th best seat, the presiding seat one vote with no veto). It is no count and stores no tally. At three members, `quorum: 2` is a majority. It is a fixed count and does not scale with the bench, the way `excommunication_deliberation`'s does not [ASSUMPTION; medium; Jordan to correct; sweep {2, 3}; revert: `close: bench`, no `quorum:`]. Each ballot starts level: `declared_votes_by_member` matches a Proposition whose predicate is the row's `disposes` kind (`world_q.py:1116-1126`), and no Proposition names `Record`. The King sits as party and member, under the rule that a party who sits does not step down (`sigma.py:1947`, A-139 as corrected); the design does not change it.

**Falsifier.** After the flip, a sitting of the three-member realm bench (or §5's court) whose two non-party members favour the motion carries at the second-best seat's margin and mints the `resolution`, and a one-member sitting is refused `short_bench` before any draw. The design is wrong if a one-member sitting draws, or if a three-member sitting reads only the presiding seat's verdict. **Control.** At `close: bench` the same sitting reads the presiding seat's one verdict (`sigma.py:2094-2095`).

## 6. The route taken, by CLAUDE.md §0's ladder

| route | step that decides | reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| (i) the remit grant | **1, superseded** | B21.53 (A-37) made the grant (`offices.yaml:223, :236, :249`). **TAKEN: route (ii).** Step 1 decides the route, because it closes the other two. Step 4 settles the form. The sub-choices inside it are answered lower down the ladder. The floor at `territory` is step 3 (Jordan's message (1) lists the titles down to Lord). Leaving out "distinguished service" is step 5: no carrier marks distinction, and the ladder posts would need a rank cut-off that nobody authored, while the landed half needs only `title_domain`. A service clause can be added later as a disjunct in the same predicate without changing the key. Not requiring the grant is step 5: membership stays independent of B21.53's revertible remit assumption. **No choice survives all five steps; this design raises no `needs_jordan` flag.**

**Falsifier.** The choice is wrong if a ruling later than A-76 restores the interim bench as final, or if B21.53's grant, on its own, seats the duchy heads at a realm venue (the §2 reading says it does not). **Control.** On this tree, reverting route (ii) means deleting the key, the predicate and the three switches. `parliamentary_debate` then returns to `over` and `close: bench`, and the realm hash returns to the pre-B21c.07 reading.

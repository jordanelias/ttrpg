## 0. VERDICT

**The mass-battle engine is the most history-cited code in the repository, but the game never puts that grounding into play.** The `march` verb builds one Line infantry subunit per side whose troop count is the side's summed `Person.weight`. Every side the realm can field is below the engine's rout floor of 80 from the first tick, so an opposed season march has one outcome, Lost for the attacker, whatever the relative strength. On the populated realm no field is fought at all (measured 2026-10-01). The grounding itself is mostly self-written, its numbers come from no cited source, and nothing in CI gates it.

Problems, worst first:

1. **An opposed season march has one outcome: Lost.**
   - Construction: `resolve_field` builds each side as one `Line`/`infantry` subunit (power 4, command 4, discipline 5) with `troops` = the summed `Person.weight` (`modules/mass_battle/massbattle.py:170-232`) [CHECKED].
   - Floor: inside `run_battle` the check `atom.troop_total() < SUBUNIT_ROUT_FLOOR` (80; `config.py:117`) runs at the end of every tick from tick 1, and is not gated on contact (`orchestration.py:1850-1853, 2262-2269`) [CHECKED].
   - Grade: `_run_and_grade` sets `attacker_wins = (not unit_a.routed) and (...)` (`massbattle.py:287`), and `field_degree` maps a false `attacker_wins` to LOST (`engine/season/seam/ladder.py:204-206`) [CHECKED].
   - Measured on `build_realm(0)`: 83 persons; total weight 120; largest faction weight 11 (`fac_crown`); largest side `mustered` at any of the 37 settlements, 6 [CHECKED by run, this pass].
   - Measured on `_run_and_grade` (WALLS, seeds 0–4): 6-v-2, 11-v-1, 2-v-6 and 79-v-1 give `attacker_wins` False on every seed; 200-v-1 gives True on every seed [CHECKED by run, this pass].
   - The module's docstring says such a side "routs on contact regardless of who wins" (`massbattle.py:198-212`). `engine/season/tests/test_march.py:407-408` puts its `field.lost` down to "this fixture's 2-v-6 mismatch", and cannot tell that from the floor [CHECKED].
   - Consequence: formations, cavalry shock, envelopment, octagon facing, brace and the 15–30% rout band all sit behind this path.
2. **No realm field is fought.**
   - H-149: the realm declared 11 marches, and ENCOUNTER refused all 11 because their targets were hearths or persons, not settlements. H-150: "the realm fights no field" (`engine/season/hole_register.yaml:3144-3181, 3215`) [CHECKED].
   - H-175's `march` row reads `att 6 ex 6 ref 6 both 6` (`:3798`). In `engine/season/harness/aperture.py:161-171`, `both` counts acts that executed and were also refused, so all six were refused [CHECKED].
   - `engine/season/requirements.yaml:1124-1125`: "Declared 11 / no field fought" [CHECKED].
   - The rows answer different questions (is a march attempted; is a field fought). They agree that no field is fought.
3. **The gauge bands are numeric, but none of their sources supplies a number.**
   - MBG §3 sets bands such as H3 55–72 and H6 48–60 from qualitative sources ("reliably tells", "modest").
   - Biddle and Sidnell are cited second-hand, through reviews that MBG labels openly: "rev. Wirtz (2006)" and "rev. Basler (2008)" (MBG:31, :35) [CHECKED].
   - The C5 ceiling was raised from 90 to 98, and C7 from 90 to 100, after the emergent 95.6 and 100.0 (MBG:70, :72, :93, :95) [CHECKED]. MBG's stated reason for C5 is that "the Phase-2 ceiling was set when the shock was inert" (MBG:70, :115). That explains why the old ceiling was stale. It does not explain why the new one sits at 98, just above the emergent value. C7's stated reason, "encirclement of the immobile is total" (:72), gives a ceiling of 100, which cannot fail upward.
   - Its §1 takes a high draw rate from Lanchester and Hillestad, which is a misreading (§1.2).
   - Its §2 hangs "engine command-decisiveness (cmd6-vs-2 → 40-0) is correct" off a bullet citing PW (MBG:32), next to Biddle, a study of 20th-century battle (MBG:31) [CHECKED].
4. **The gauge bands gate nothing.** `.github/workflows/valoria-ci.yml` never runs `gauge_mb.py` [CHECKED].
   - The CI reader is `tests/valoria/test_gauge_invariants.py`, which imports `gauge_mb` (:49). Its two engine-against-band tests are `xfail`, "TRACKING tests, not gates" (:16-31, :64-82, :103-113). Its three other tests (:85, :116, :127) check the instrument's own consistency, not engine output [CHECKED].
   - The other CI reader is a report-only `lanchester_signature … || true` (`valoria-ci.yml:522`) [CHECKED].
5. **The largest historical corpus, PFF (16 files), was written from memory and never independently verified.** File 12 is titled "Primary-Source Reconstruction" but declares "training-data familiarity rather than re-fetching" (R2 K). File 13 is the author's own check: 5 of 9 battles against Wikipedia, Medium and Britannica, with two P1 errors found in Panipat (R3 K13-14). Files 13–16 are `[SELF-AUTHORED]` and defer to an independent audit that none of them records as run (R3 T). The extractions log over 100 internal contradictions (R1 K, R2 K).

The wrapper keeps that result "for introspection" (`seam/wrappers/mass_battle.py:64-71, :221-223`) [CHECKED]. So the season grade has three bands (Won/Lost/Unopposed), and the 0.75/0.50/0.25 ladder is computed and then discarded on that path. It is not a live two-ladder S defect.

### 1.3 Critical evaluation of the historical research

- **Method: one author, from memory, self-checked.**
  - PFF was produced chunk by chunk with "no repo write" and no verifier. Its only check (file 13) was run by the same author against encyclopaedia-grade web pages for 5 of 9 battles (R3 K14). That check still found two P1 errors in a single battle (Panipat). This is direct evidence that the unchecked chunks hold errors of the same size.
  - The repo's own rule is that a correction caught by re-reading is weak; the throughlines chapter (R8 T11) found none of its 11 corrections by re-reading. PFF 13–16 is exactly that method.
- **Marks are missing where they matter most.** PFF 01–08 carry no DOCUMENTED/REPORTED/INFERRED marks at all (R1 global note). MBG carries a single `[SELF-AUTHORED]` header mark, and its per-band rationale carries none. By contrast, the `.audit` engineering files use five verdict tags and admit how few tags they opened (E1b P, H).
- **Numbers are not grounded by qualitative sources.** Every MBG band is an interval such as 48–62. The sources support only directions: "modest edge", "reliably tells", "repels". The bands are therefore the author's design targets with historical illustrations attached; they are not measurements. The repo's later practice (`gauge_mb.py:440-441`: "any interval in turns would be fabricated to look grounded") applies the same reasoning to duration and declines to band it. MBG's win-share bands have never been re-read on that standard [CHECKED].
- **Bands were adjusted after measurement.** MBG's header says bands are "not set by engine output". Yet C5 (90 → 98) and C7 (90 → 100) were raised after the emergent 95.6 and 100.0, and C1 was rebaselined to a band containing the emergent 45.7 (R3 K1; MBG :68-72, :93, :95 [CHECKED]). MBG gives a reason for C5: "the Phase-2 ceiling was set when the shock was inert" (:70, :115). That is a fair reason to revisit the old ceiling, but it does not derive the new one, which sits just above the emergent value. C7 at 65–100 is saturated by construction and cannot fail upward (R3 K2). Only the C1 move was against the engine's interest.
- **Anachronism.**
  - The gauge's "pre-firearms" model takes its key anchors from 1757 (Leuthen), 1780 (Baillie), 1815 (Waterloo), Civil War data (Weiss), 1904–1991 data (DLEDB) and modern force employment (Biddle).
  - PFF 05/06 spend whole sections on firearm battles (Cerignola, Pavia, Mohács) under a "pre-shot" scope (R1 K).
  - None of this is flagged where a band cites it.
- **Surface-analogy hooks.** PFF 16 maps threadwork onto the "firearm transition" (Drift 6). It rests on a chat statement, with the "mass-X doctrine" left blank (R3 K20). PFF 11 maps morale onto Coherence by label only (R2 C). Neither reaches any owner.
- **Internal contradictions that reach code.** Only the rout band and the Cannae shock constants carry PFF/MBG-era history into `config.py`. The rout band is a Jordan design value with no historical source, credited to du Picq, and the shock constants' design document is forked. The many PFF contradictions (R1 K, R2 K) do not reach code. That is not a defence: none of the PFF content reaches code at all.
- **Reach (the test of §0.05).**
  - MBG reaches `tests/sim/gauge_mb.py`, which is not a gate (§0 item 4).
  - The du Picq, Sabin and Dupuy material reaches `config.py` mechanisms, but behind the degenerate season construction (§0 item 1).
  - PFF reaches nothing: no `config.py` tag, gauge row or plan row cites it [CHECKED: `grep pre_firearms` across `modules/` and `tests/sim/` finds no citation; `00_INDEX` §6.3].

### 2.3 Critical evaluation of the game precedent

- **The division of labour is sound.** Formation and combat mechanics take their grounding from history (MBG, du Picq, Sabin). Game precedent is used for structure: fidelity, auto-resolve, orders and the seam. That is where game precedent has authority.
- **The one convergence that reached the architecture was applied correctly:** FM, XCOM and Total War all point to one engine run headless, and the season and the harnesses do share one engine. Its failure-mode test ("test the failure mode, not the mean") has no consumer for MB. `arms.py` sweeps `field_casualty_model` only, and H-150 itself calls a realm-level arm a fake control "today -- the realm fights no field" (`hole_register.yaml:3215`) [CHECKED].
- **The sourcing floor is breached by dropping marks.** The Dominions rule ("fraction, never flat") is the precedent most relevant to MB's personal-to-field seam. It rests on player-community consensus that the companion first marked `[UNVERIFIED]` and then quoted without the mark (R4 K7). Despite that, the engine's own general rule (`Command=0 → instant rout`) is the very shape the rule warns against, and nothing records the conflict.
- **Nulls are the honest finding here.** D3 (no game keeps personal leverage meaningful from N=1 to N=1000+) and D4 (no game calibrates auto-resolve) mean that no precedent can justify MB's seam either way. The season path avoids D3 because no person is in the field: a side is a sum of weights. That solves nothing; the seam is simply absent.
- **Squad-engagement borrowings were named, not surveyed.** SQ5 lists nine or more games with no fetch, no marks and no survey program. SQS adopted and built several shapes from them (ED-MB-0069…0077). That is a design choice Jordan ruled, and it is legitimate as a ruling. As precedent it carries no evidential weight beyond its being named.
- **The COMP-3 §7 verdict on the mass-battle seam ("below floor") is dated,** and the AP re-score keeps it: "Finishing the plan consolidates the engine; it does not change this verdict" (R8 T10). The working tree supports this (§3.1).

### 3.4 Critical evaluation

- **Grounded in code, ungrounded in the game.** The lane's repository work is real engineering: an executable tactical engine with invariants, honest debt tags and self-correcting audits. But it is an oracle with no player. The only path from the game into it passes through an army builder that its own docstring calls a GAP. That builder discards every quality the engine models (type, formation, discipline variation, equipment, multiple subunits, a general). It also feeds troop counts (at most 6 per realm side, measured) below the engine's own rout floor of 80, so every opposed march grades Lost and relative strength never enters the outcome (§0 item 1).
- **The grounding chain runs from code to prose.** `config.py`'s `[canonical: …]` tags say which document a number came from. For the historically framed constants those documents are, in turn:
  - self-authored and quarantined (MBG, `.audit` files);
  - forked away (`cavalry_shock_design.md`, `mb_lanchester_design.md`, `movement_model_design.md`);
  - snapshot-only (`sim_mb_06_v9_historical_spec.md`);
  - or a Jordan statement recorded in code and ledger with no document behind it (the 15–30% band: `config.py:163`, ED-MB-0031).

  Under §0.05 the code is the fact, so this is legitimate. The chains can be named: a forked file resolves through `restructure_ledger.md`, and the rout band resolves to a ruling. What is missing is the evidence, not the provenance. The most "historical" constants (`ROUT_ONSET_FRAC`, `ROUT_CAP_FRAC`, `MB_SHOCK_REAR`, `K_LINEAR`) all rest on a file outside the working tree or on a ruling with no historical source. The rout band's ruling is also credited to du Picq, who gives no figure.
- **The gauge is an instrument with no gate, and its targets are self-set.** The repo knows this. The CI comment refuses to make the Lanchester check blocking until "fork #2 is ruled" (two incompatible 2:1 targets), and the invariants are xfail (`valoria-ci.yml:503-509`) [CHECKED]. So no historical band currently constrains the shipped engine, and none constrains the shipped game.
- **Muster has no season mechanism.** Every muster design (ML §1.4–1.5 Muster Ob table; SQ5 §10.2; PMM's three models; the board-game Muster Ob 2) is quarantined, retired or conceptual. In the season, the army is whoever is present at the origin, so "mustering" is presence, not an act. That is a coherent design choice (VCD reads Muster as `march` plus `oblige`/`transfer`), but no research or precedent defends it. It is also the reason an army's size is a sum of `Person.weight`s that the engine was never calibrated for.
- **Plan reach is thin and partly stale.** Two MB rows, neither about the season gap; citations into a pre-`31c` path. IN-13 and IN-52 carry the season-facing MB work in another lane.

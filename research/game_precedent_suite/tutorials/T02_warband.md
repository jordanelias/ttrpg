### Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord - facts line
TaleWorlds Entertainment; real-time strategy/action-RPG sandbox (campaign map plus personally fought battles). Stable base-game line 1.3.x since 26 Nov 2025; newest patch found v1.4.8, 18 Aug 2026 [V2 b2, b6]. Tutorial assumes: Campaign (not sandbox-skip) start, a new wanderer, Warrior-ish difficulty, culture of your choice, no mods. One session = 2-3 h gets you from the opening raid to clan tier 1-2 [M]; a campaign is 100+ h [M]. Source mix: Gamer Guides (updated 21 Dec 2022), Fextralife (undated), 2018-19 dev blogs, 2020-22 Steam threads.
#### 1. Setup choices
- **Culture (6)**: Vlandia, Sturgia, Empire, Aserai, Khuzait, Battania. A permanent small bonus plus your starting region; "not a commitment to serve that faction" [V3 B6]. Guide advice: take a movement-speed culture for a first run (speed decides whether you can catch bandit bands); Aserai (cheaper caravans, lower trade penalty) is "entirely viable"; Empire's build/repair bonus pays only mid-game [V3 B6].
- **Background, six stages**: Family, Early Childhood, Adolescence, Youth, Young Adulthood, Story Background [V3 B17]. Each answer grants starting skill levels, focus points and sometimes an attribute point, e.g. "+10 Skill Level and 1 Focus Point to Riding and Polearm, 1 Attribute Point to Social" [V3 B7].
- **Attributes (6) / skills (18) / focus**: Vigor, Control, Endurance, Cunning, Social, Intelligence, three skills each [V3 B7, B17]. Focus points are per skill (max 5); attributes raise learning limit and rate of all three skills under them [V3 B7].
- **Difficulty presets**: Freebooter / Warrior / Bannerlord. Freebooter = you take 25% damage, troops take 50% reduced damage, +2 troops offered at settlements, +10% map speed, +10% persuasion, no hero or clan deaths in battle [V3 B16]. Warrior and Bannerlord values were not read; per v2, hero death is on at Bannerlord only [V2 b19].
- After the opening you name your family, pick banner and colours [V3 B1]. Appearance/name are cosmetic.
#### 2. The loop
Clock: real-time map with pause [M]; a day passes in seconds; year = 84 days, four 21-day seasons [V2 b24]. Battles are a separate real-time scene.
1. Click a destination or a party on the map (pause to plan). Your party moves; speed falls with party size and carried weight [V3 B2] and varies with terrain [V3 B6].
2. Each day the game, unasked: eats food from your inventory, pays wages (unpaid or unfed troops lose morale, then desert) [V3 B2], moves every AI party, grows or starves towns, runs caravans, advances fief projects, accrues renown/influence.
3. Contact with a party opens a menu: fight, avoid or talk [M]. Fight = play it yourself, send troops, or let the game auto-resolve [M]. If you touch several groups at once you fight them all at once [V3 B1].
4. In a battle you deploy, give group orders (F1 order menu, F3 charge in the tutorial; number keys select formations [V3 B1, V2 b22]) and fight in person. Your troops do nothing until told [V3 B1].
5. Result screen (TAB): kills per unit, upgrades, dead/wounded/routed; then capture-or-free prisoners and take loot [V3 B1]. Overloading with loot makes you "extremely slow" [V3 B1].
6. Settlement stop: market (food, goods, horses), recruit from notables, tavern (companions, mercenaries), arena/tournament, wait/rest to heal, keep (lords, quests) [V3 B1, B10; tavern/arena M].
7. Between: quests and "issues" arrive from notables; as a vassal the Kingdom screen offers votes (see the policy, army and fief rows of the table); AI lords raise armies and declare wars without you.
Automated: wage payment, food draw, simulated battles, AI troop behaviour (until ordered), governors/caravan leaders, markets, tax income. Interrupts: bandit ambush, a lord's army meeting you, a village asking for help, war declarations, rebellion in a low-loyalty fief.

#### 4. First hour, step by step
1. Finish the training/combat tutorial; you are placed on the map near your culture's region [V3 B2]. Pause. Hold Left-Alt to highlight names of every interactable [V3 B1].
2. Go to the nearest village; talk to the headman (quest hook: your missing siblings) [V3 B1].
3. Open the village menu (hold TAB): hire as many troops as affordable, "as early as possible" so they train in your first fights; buy food [V3 B1]. Unfed or unpaid troops desert [V3 B2].
4. Buy grain plus a second food type later; variety adds morale [V3 B2].
5. Hunt the raider groups the quest marks (groups of 6 against your 8); engage them one at a time, wait for groups to split [V3 B1].
6. In the battle press F1, then F3 to charge, and join in; try other orders freely [V3 B1].
7. TAB at the end: read the breakdown, free or take prisoners, loot. Sell loot and ransom prisoners to fund more recruits [V3 B1, B9].
8. Buy a pack animal/mule when loot slows you [V3 B1]. Rest by "wait here" in a settlement [V3 B1].
9. Raid the hideout named by the quest prisoner: clear groups in turn, then duel the boss for more renown/morale (you can fail and be dragged out) [V3 B1].
10. Name the clan, banner, colours [V3 B1]. Spend level-up points; note the displayed learning limit on each skill's perk tree [V3 B7].
11. Keep farming bandits on horseback (community advice: horse-archery beats cavalry-less looters, but steppe bandits are fast) [V3 B9]. Aim at clan tier 1 (renown 50 [V2 b8]).
12. At tier 1 the mercenary option opens; at tier 2 vassalage and a second party slot [V3 B5; V2 b8]. Find any lord of the kingdom (Encyclopedia, key N, shows locations) and use the dialogue line "I would like to enter the service of ..." [V3 B4].
13. As a mercenary, daily influence converts to denars; as a vassal it accumulates and buys votes [V3 B5, B3]. Join the kingdom's armies for the big-battle renown.
14. Open Kingdom > Clans to compare influence; do not vote against powerful lords repeatedly (relation falls; they may secede) [V3 B3].
15. Vote or ask for the next captured fief; first fief usually comes this way [V3 B5].
16. Add companions with roles; a clan party leader and a caravan are the first delegations [V3 B2; V2 b9].
#### 5. What changes later
- Clan tier is the gate: renown 50/150/350/900/2,350/6,150 for tiers 1-6 (undated table) [V2 b8]; each tier raises party size, party slots and companion cap [V2 b14]. Past tier 3, solo bandit-fighting becomes "tedious"; the guide steers you to kingdom service for bigger battles [V3 B4].
- Land: fiefs bring tax, loyalty drift toward 50, rebellion risk below 25 [V2 b31], per-fief project queues [V2 b13]. Policies (35 on the Fextralife list) change influence, tax, loyalty and party size [V3 B8].
- Wages become "the most expensive thing in the game" with thousands of troops [V3 B2]. Influence becomes the scarce currency; every army, vote and fief revocation spends it [V3 B3, B11].
- 1.3 adds stealth/disguise, alliances and trade agreements (beta text) [V2 b10]; War Sails naval DLC arrived 26 Nov 2025 [V2 b2].
#### 6. Failure and ending
No scripted victory or ending. Older guidance says the Dragon Banner main quest gates kingdom creation [V3 B9]; the encyclopedia-style text lists no quest requirement [V3 B13]. Loss = hero dies of age/disease or in battle (preset-dependent) with no heir, load or watch as observer [V2 b20, b19]; or bankruptcy (desertion spiral) [V3 B2]; or losing fiefs to rebellion or conquest. Mostly recoverable: a captured hero can be ransomed [M]; if you lose the kingdom but keep a town and tier 4, the creation rule above suggests you can found again (my inference, not tested).
#### 7. Consequential vs cosmetic choices
- Consequential: troop upkeep versus income (the daily food/wage drain forces tempo) [V3 B2]; relation with notables (recruit tier) [V3 B12]; which kingdom and clan tier you reach (gates every later option) [V3 B5]; influence spending, because votes decide land and war [V3 B11].
- Dominant answer, community consensus: farm bandits on horseback early; speed-bonus culture for the first run [V3 B6, B9].
- Mostly flavour: banner, family background beyond its skill grants, difficulty presets beyond their stated sliders.
- Contested value: AI vote logic. The dev blog says AI weighs interest, available influence and relation to the sponsor [V3 B11]; players call votes against them "reflexive" [S3, no page opened].

### Mount & Blade: Warband - facts line
TaleWorlds Entertainment; same genre, one generation earlier. Windows 30 Mar 2010 (NA) / 2 Apr 2010 (EU) [V2 w2]; final patch `?`. Tutorial assumes: Native (vanilla, no Diplomacy mod), single-player Campaign, default-ish difficulty. One session = 2-3 h to reach a first vassalage; reaching kingship = many sessions [M]. All beginner material is Steam-guide/forum grade (guides 2017-2022, threads 2014-2021); there is no official manual I could open. One popular guide assumes the Diplomacy mod [V3 W8], so I used it only where another source agrees.
#### 1. Setup choices
- **Gender**: male STR+1 CHA+1, female AGI+1 INT+1; base STR 5 / AGI 5 / INT 4 / CHA 5 [V3 W1]. A female hero can marry into a kingdom and may wait longer for a first fief; the king says fiefs for women make nobles think he is "bewitched" unless renown is high (700+) [V3 W2, W3, community advice].
- **Background (4 questions)**: "Your father was", "Early life", "Adulthood", "Reason for adventuring"; they set starting attributes, skills, gear and proficiencies, not only flavour [V3 W3, W1].
- **Starting town (6)**: Praven (Swadia), Reyvadin (Vaegirs), Tulga (Khergits), Sargoth (Nords), Jelkala (Rhodoks), Shariz (Sarranids). Guide: Praven is best for newcomers; Tulga and Shariz bandits ride horses (faster than you); Reyvadin/Sargoth sit by sea raiders [V3 W3].
- **Options**: campaign difficulty (fief limit before tax inefficiency 6/4/2 from easy to hard [V3 W3]), battle difficulty (guide: set at least medium or soldiers "barely attack more than once every 5 seconds"), defence by mouse direction vs auto [V3 W3]. Full option list `?`.
#### 2. The loop
Clock: real-time map with pause [M]; days pass, with a **weekly** tick for wages and income [V3 W3, W2]. Year length `?`.
1. Move on the map to a town, village or castle, or toward a party. Party size cap rises with Leadership (+5 per level) and Charisma (+1) [V3 W1, W3].
2. Daily: food is eaten; "Trainer" skill trains troops lower than the trainer; AI lords move, siege, recruit [V3 W3].
3. Weekly: wages for every soldier and companion are paid, fief taxes arrive automatically (no visit needed) [V3 W2, W3]; debt lowers morale [V3 W1].
4. Meeting a party: attack, flee, or (some bandits) pay to be left alone - sea raiders will not take payment [V3 W3]. Battle: your orders, loot and prisoners (sell at a ransom broker) [V3 W3].
5. In a town: market, tavern (companions, rumours, claimants located for 30 denars [V3 W1]), guild master (quests almost always [V3 W6]), arena, lord's hall (relations, votes, quests).
6. As a vassal/king: the marshal summons armies; each captured castle or town triggers a vote on who gets it; you ask lords whom they support and may persuade [V3 W2, W3].
Automated: AI armies, sieges, caravans, tax, upkeep, weekly balance. Interrupts: war declared, marshal's summons, feast invitation, lord asks for help, a lord defects.

#### 4. First hour, step by step
1. Pick gender, background answers and a start town (Praven suggested) [V3 W3].
2. Expect an ambush at the start and then a merchant's quest to rescue his brother (~700 gold total) [V3 W3, W1 "given a mission"].
3. Recruit about five men with "recruit volunteers"; stay under ~20 soldiers for now (upkeep, speed) [V3 W3].
4. Kill the 4 looters, then the lair (preset map); loot everything; sell it [V3 W3].
5. Return for 100-300 gold (more kills = more) and reputation with the town [V3 W3].
6. With no quests left, grind looters near the town; take most kills yourself, peasants clean up [V3 W3]. Let troops reach tier 2/3.
7. Bandit camps are good early loot, but cavalry cannot fight in them (narrow entrances); shield + one-handed is advised [V3 W1].
8. Grow to 30-40 tier-3 troops before hunting sea raiders for top gear [V3 W3].
9. Hire companions from taverns; give crossbows and gear; their roles: medic, scout, tactician, cavalry [V3 W3].
10. Level: one attribute point per level; skill level capped at a third of its attribute [V3 W3]. Win tournaments for gold and meeting ladies [V3 W1].
11. When vassalage is offered early, decline (weak army = losses) [V3 W3]; accept at ~50-60 hardened troops, preferring a kingdom that holds your businesses and has vassals to cover its land [V3 W3].
12. Swear to the king; you get the poorest village; build improvements there; keep relation with the village for recruits [V3 W2, W3].
#### 5. What changes later
- **Fiefs**: villages bind to a castle/town; capture the castle and villages follow. Fief awards weigh renown, current holdings and luck: (renown+500) / (1 + 3 towns + 2 castles + villages) x random 50-100, x1.5 for the conqueror, + 2x relation with the king [V3 W2]. Tax is weekly and automatic; tax inefficiency from holding too many (cap 35% [V3 W3]; v2's 65% [S2] conflicts).
- **Lords and marshal**: personalities (upstanding, good-natured, martial, ...) govern who defects; relation is the political currency; marshal controls field armies but "controversy" rises with enemy successes until the king replaces him [V3 W3].
- **Becoming king**: Right to Rule (about 50 needed) and a rebel capture of a castle/city; low claim means every kingdom treats you as a bandit and lords desert [V3 W3, W1]. Differences from Bannerlord: no influence currency (relation with each lord does the work), no clan tiers or kingdom policies, fief votes happen as one-to-one conversations rather than an influence-weighted council [V3 W2, W3]; marriage exists but children/heirs are Bannerlord additions [V2 w2, M].
#### 6. Failure and ending
No scripted ending; common goal is conquering Calradia [V2 w2]. Troops die; the hero is knocked out and may be captured [M]; companions "can't die" [V3 W3]. Failure = debt and desertion, or a weak claim: lords defect, sieges take your fiefs [V3 W3, W1]. Recoverable: peace by emissary, rebuild vassals.
#### 7. Consequential vs cosmetic choices
- Consequential: renown and relation with the king and lords (vassalage, fief votes, defections) [V3 W2, W3]; Right to Rule and fief count (survival and tax) [V3 W3]; wage load versus income [V3 W3].
- Dominant, one-source opinions: tournament betting for money [V3 W1]; farming sea raiders for gear [V3 W3]; female hero marrying into a kingdom [V3 W3].
- Low-yield: a feast gives +2 relation per vassal talked to [V3 W3] or about +1 per day [V3 W7] [CONTESTED], small against the 2-3 lost per passed-over lord.
- Flavour: background answers beyond stats, faction colour choices for a start.
- Personality matching of lords matters but only via guide advice and forum agreement [V3 W3, W5].
#### 8. Open items
- v2 w4 (claimant: must you give up your kingdom?): guide says yes, you give up your kingdom; fiefs convert to the rebel faction if you belong to the target faction [V3 W1]; a 2017 thread agrees and adds you cannot back a claimant while king [V3 W4]. Needed renown 200 [V3 W1] (v2 said `?`).
- v2 w7 tax inefficiency "up to 65%" [S2] vs 35% max [V3 W3] [CONTESTED]; guide says limits 6/4/2 by difficulty.
- Feasts: relation per feast contested (+2 per vassal talked to [V3 W3] vs about +1 per day [V3 W7]); who may host: castle/city plus marriage per forum [S3].
- Not found: year length, exact option list, vassalage renown threshold (~150 only as [S3]), final patch number.

### Battle Brothers - facts line
Overhype Studios; turn-based tactical combat inside a mercenary-company management sandbox. Current patch found: v1.5.2.3, 24 Jun 2026 (fixes after v1.5.2.2 of 18 Jun 2026, which added weapons/enemies and an Executioner background) [V3 X2]. Tutorial assumes: standard "A New Company" origin, Beginner or Veteran combat difficulty, no Ironman, base game plus free DLC, 2020s patch. One battle = 10-20 min [M]; one sitting = 3-6 contracts; a campaign = 100+ in-game days. Mostly forum/guide-grade: no official manual found; the Steam page and patch posts are the only first-party text.
#### 1. Setup choices
- **Origin** (starting roster, funds, special rules): in the 2021 list, Rebuilding a Company (tutorial), A New Company (1 skull), Southern Mercenaries (1), Peasant Militia, Band of Poachers, Trading Caravan, Deserters, Northern Raiders, Manhunters, Davkul Cultists (2 each), Beastslayers, Gladiators, Lone Wolf (3) [V3 X4]; 1.5 (Mar 2022) added Anatomists (three modest men, high funds, unique mechanic) and Oathtakers (two skilled warriors, good gear, a banner, oath system) [V3 X2, dev blogs #145/#146]. Lone Wolf: single hedge knight whose death ends the run [V2 x5].
- **Combat difficulty, economic difficulty, starting funds, optional Ironman, end-game crisis (pick or leave random), world seed** (a code you can share) [V3 X5]. Beginner combat is "still challenging" [V3 X3]. Fan-wiki funds 1,500/2,000/2,500 and Beginner +5 hit/avoid are [S3] only.
- Guide advice for a first run: tutorial origin first, then A New Company, Beginner difficulty [V3 X3]; community rankings call Peasant Militia easy and Band of Poachers strong [V3 X4, opinion].
#### 2. The loop
Clock: real-time travel on a procedural world map with pause [V3 X1 "travel in real-time"]; days pass; wages and food are paid daily [V2 x11]. Combat is separate and turn-based.
1. Open the world map; pick a town or site and walk there (terrain and roads change speed).
2. Each day: provisions are eaten, wages paid, wounds heal, prices drift; world parties (bandits, beasts, nobles, caravans) move [V3 X1; M for detail].
3. In a settlement: market (buy/sell gear, food, tools, medicine), tavern (recruits with random backgrounds, rumours), the lord or elder's contract list [V3 X1].
4. Take a contract; travel; a random event may interrupt; at the target a battle starts or an item is delivered.
5. Combat: place the brothers you bring (default maximum 12 in battle [V2 x4, x8]); units act one at a time using Action Points and Fatigue [M]; morale can break either side; terrain height matters [V3 X1, X3].
6. After the fight: loot, dead are gone for good, wounded carry temporary or permanent injuries [V3 X1; V2 x2]; survivors gain XP and, at level-up, pick stat rolls (and later perks) [V3 X3].
7. Return, collect pay (contract + relation with the faction), repair with Tools, heal with Medicine, restock, repeat. Ambitions give mid-term goals; crises arrive later [V3 X5].
Automated: world AI, daily accounting, market prices, event rolls. You control every unit in battle (no auto-resolve) [M].

#### 4. First hour, step by step
1. New Campaign: take the tutorial origin or A New Company; Beginner difficulty; Ironman off; leave the crisis random [V3 X3, X5].
2. Read the opening event: your company is mostly dead and you rebuild [V3 X5].
3. Hire 3-4 good recruits, not the cheapest; read the star icons beside stats [V3 X3].
4. Give every front-liner a round shield and a spear (or sword); keep spare shields because shields break [V3 X3, X4].
5. Buy food, tools (~90-120 coins per 10) and medicine (~110-135 per 10); hold cash buffers of 500, then 1,000, then 2,000 [V3 X3].
6. Take a delivery or caravan contract first: "almost always free money", and you do not eat your own provisions on caravan jobs [V3 X3].
7. Move to bandit-thug or "retrieve stolen item" contracts; avoid beasts and monster hunts early [V3 X4, X3].
8. In battle: form a shield wall and let the enemy come (they tire); take high ground; keep brothers adjacent; focus one enemy [V3 X3, X4].
9. Chase down fleeing enemies; take all loot; sell what you do not need [V3 X3].
10. Level up: favour defence, fatigue, health with the best rolls; "Colossus" is a safe early perk [V3 X3].
11. Repair with Tools and heal; do not take a second fight with wounded front-liners.
12. When income allows, grow toward 12 fighters plus reserves and repeat with harder contracts. Expect to lose the first run; it is normal [V3 X4].
#### 5. What changes later
- Roster and gear: more brothers (roster cap 18 or 20, contested; 12 per fight) [V2 x7, x9]; named loot; armour tiers; retinue [V3 X2].
- Pressure: contracts scale; champions appear more at higher contract difficulty [V3 X2]; Beasts, orcs, undead rise.
- Crisis: noble war, greenskin invasion or undead scourge reshape the map; a player-quoted timeline is foretold ~day 50-70 and starts ~day 80-100 [S3 earlyguides.com/battle-brothers], and forum posters say there is no fixed standard [V3 X4]. Ranking by players: noble war easiest, undead hardest [V3 X4 thread, opinion]. Destroyed towns stay destroyed (2017 dev statement via forum) [V3 X4].
#### 6. Failure and ending
Soft win = surviving or beating the crisis; no hard ending [V3 X5]. Loss = company wipe or the Lone Wolf dying; in Ironman, no reloading [V3 X5; V2 x5]. Running out of food makes brothers weak and they can leave [V3 X3]. Not recoverable after a wipe; start again with a new seed or origin. Individual deaths are permanent, injuries may be permanent [V2 x2].
#### 7. Consequential vs cosmetic choices
- Consequential: front-line equipment and positioning (one or two hits kill a brother) [V3 X4]; contract choice against current strength; provisioning buffers; origin (changes caps, funds and special rules).
- Dominant (consensus): shields plus spears, bandit contracts first, avoid beasts [V3 X3, X4].
- Flavour: names, banner, most ambitions' wording. Seed is consequential only for the first recruits (seed lists exist) [V3 X4].
- Level-up rolls are consequential but not strategic: take the best rolls in defence/fatigue/health [V3 X3, guide opinion].
#### 8. Open items

- Not established: shipped default roster cap, daily wage scale, crisis day numbers (only [S3]/forum), exact start roster of A New Company, Beginner combat modifiers.

### Kenshi - facts line
Lo-Fi Games (development primarily led by one person over twelve years, per Wikipedia); real-time-with-pause squad RPG/RTS sandbox. Early Access Mar 2013, full release 6 Dec 2018 [V3 K7]. Latest numbered patch found: 1.0.68, 1 Apr 2024 (free camera mode), with 1.0.64/FCS 2.14 on 6 Feb 2024 [V3 K2]; Kenshi 2 is being built in Unreal Engine and old-game bug fixes are "very unlikely" [V3 K2]. Tutorial assumes: the default Wanderer start, default advanced settings, no mods. No start called "Drifter" appears in any source I read; Wanderer (single character, The Hub) is the nearest. One session = first 1-2 h are scraping money and food; reaching an outpost = 5-15 h [M]; campaigns run hundreds of hours.
#### 1. Setup choices
- **Start**: Wanderer (default; "intended way to play"; one character in The Hub with about 1,000 Cats) [V3 K3, K4]. Others: Empire Citizen, The Slaves, The Wandering Trader, The Holy Sword, The Cannibal Hunters, The Hive Exile, Rock Bottom, Holy Nation Citizen, Guy with a Dog, The Freedom Seekers, Nobodies [V3 K4]. Nobodies spawns in The Hub, Stobe's Garden or Venge (Venge worst) [V3 K5]. Each start begins differently then "merges into" the same mid-game [V3 K4].
- **Character creation**: several races with subraces, each with stat boosts and food needs (Shek eat most, Hive least); Greenlanders get farming, cooking, science boosts and 100 hp [V3 K3].
- **Advanced settings**: speed of tasks, damage taken, chance of death, hostile fauna nests, prospecting, and whether "life is fair" (with it on, bandits loot your characters) [V3 K3]. Exact slider ranges `?`.
- No difficulty that scales to you: "no hero characters", "you are not the chosen one" [V3 K1].
#### 2. The loop
Clock: real time; Space pauses; F2 normal to F4 fastest (watching workers is done at F4) [V3 K3]. Days and night matter (stealth, sleep).
1. Select a character (number keys); right-click to move or give a one-off job; hold Shift + right-click to put a job in the permanent job queue [V3 K3]. Queue order is priority; drag to reorder [V3 K2, K3].
2. The world runs: hunger (eats food from inventory automatically; no thirst), bleeding, healing (beds heal 8x faster [V3 K6]), skills rise by doing [V3 K3].
3. NPC groups roam and fight each other; the eye and sun icons show who sees you; yellow = about to be spotted, red = seen [V3 K3].
4. Meeting trouble: sneak away, run (drop gear to run faster [V3 K2]), or fight. Fighting is real-time and AI-driven once ordered [M]; you pause to re-order. Stances: passive, block only (+20 melee defence), hold position, ranged [V3 K2, K3].
5. Injuries: every limb has health; crippled limbs impair; severed limbs are permanent unless replaced by a prosthetic; heavy bleeding kills [V3 K3, K1].
6. If the whole squad is down, survivors play dead until the enemy leaves; slavers and cannibals may capture you; shackles come off with lockpicking or a saw [V3 K2].
7. In a base: raids arrive and production chains run on assigned jobs [V3 K6].
Automated: medic/rescue jobs, food sharing, hauling, machine work, NPC factions. Interrupts: raids, ambushes, weather such as acid rain (tent protects) [V3 K2].

#### 4. First hour, step by step
1. New Game, Wanderer; glance at advanced settings; design the character [V3 K3].
2. In The Hub hold Alt to highlight items: white names are free, red names are someone's property [V3 K4]. Loot, then go to the bar: buy dried meat (cheapest food) [V3 K3].
3. Open the map (M); order a long move to Squin (safe Shek town) with Sneak on; flee if threatened; save before each town run [V3 K3, K4].
4. At Squin buy food and a medkit for each character [V3 K3].
5. Click Prospect; find the copper node outside the south gate (always present); mine until the node holds 5 ore; use F4; sell [V3 K3].
6. Spend first Cats on food, then hire companions to about 10 plus a pack animal [V3 K4]. Open the AI window (M then AI) and enable Share Food [V3 K4].
7. Give everyone armour in each slot and a better weapon; tiers Prototype to Masterwork [V3 K4].
8. Hire a mercenary group each time you leave town; avoid Beak Thing zones until Toughness is 25+ [V3 K4].
9. Buy a ruined building in a town, add a Research Bench and Books, research with T [V3 K4, K6].
10. Test the squad on the Dust King Tower (Toughness 25+ advised), later the Tower of Ninjas (35+) [V3 K4].
11. Research to tech level 4, gather materials, then choose a plot (The Border Zone suggested for a first base) [V3 K4, K6].
12. Drop Beds and a Food Store first, then Stone Mine/Processor, Iron Refinery, power (wind or generator plus biofuel), Storage, walls [V3 K6]. Raids begin as soon as it exists; hire mercenaries for the first week [V3 K6].
#### 5. What changes later
- Squad becomes several squads; base specialises (farming, cactus rum, skeleton repair kits, weapon smithing) [V3 K4, K6]. Gear tiers climb to Mk I-III, Edge types and unique Meitou weapons from bosses [V3 K4].
- Pressure: raids grow; food stolen from stores; Elder monsters; faction hostility depends on your race and actions (Holy Nation hostile to non-humans: [S3, pindrop.gg/kenshi/beginners-guide]). World reacts to notable deaths with new locations or takeovers [V2 k2].
- Self-set goals: Almar's own "end of game" is killing a late boss; the game does not mandate one [V3 K4].
#### 6. Failure and ending
No scripted ending. Characters die permanently (chance of death is a setting); limbs lost stay lost; capture or slavery is a state you can escape; a fully dead squad is game over [M]. Recovery is common: rescue companions, rebuild [V3 K1, K2, K3].
#### 7. Consequential vs cosmetic choices
- Consequential: food and medkit discipline; squad size and gear before leaving town; where you place a base (raids move to it) [V3 K6]; research order; which race you play (hostility, hunger) [V3 K3].
- Dominant (community): mine copper/iron first; buy a town house + Research Bench before building outside; Border Zone first base; mercenaries as insurance [V3 K4, K6, single-author guide].
- Largely cosmetic: appearance; which of the many starts you take, since they merge into the same mid game [V3 K4, author's view].
- Unverified: Almar's claim that you "can only fight enemies 1 on 1" unless modded [V3 K4]; neither the official tips nor Wikipedia mention such a rule, and I recall groups attacking together [M].

### Europa Universalis IV - facts line
Paradox Development Studio (Paradox Interactive); real-time-with-pause grand strategy, 1444-1821. Patch relied on: **1.37.x; the last patch note on Steam is the 1.37.4 hotfix of 2024-09-18, and the Steam news feed up to 2025-12-18 has none newer** (Europa Universalis V launched 2025-11-04 as a separate game) [V3 https://api.steampowered.com/ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp/v2/?appid=236850 fetched 2026-10-22]. Since 2024-10-17 four DLCs (Rights of Man, Art of War, Digital Extreme Upgrade, Common Sense) are in the base game [V3 same feed, "Free DLCs for all Europa Universalis IV Players", 2024-09-03]. Assumed: the default 1444 start, a Christian monarchy (Ottomans per the veteran guide; France per a 2022 walkthrough), non-Ironman, Normal difficulty. Session: 2-4 h; a full 1444-1821 campaign is many sessions [M]. **Caveat**: the Paradox wiki (eu4.paradoxwikis.com) served only a bot challenge to my fetch, so rules text is from Paradox dev diaries and patch notes in the Steam feed, Steam forum threads, one 2022 walkthrough and search-tool summaries of the wiki (tagged [S3]); the loop in section 2 and several rows are [M]. Everything uses the terms "monarch power" (ADM/DIP/MIL points; the community says "mana").
#### 1. Setup choices
- **Nation** (hundreds of tags; veteran advice: Ottomans for a first run because no dangerous neighbours and a strong start; Sweden if you own the DLC because its mission tree teaches; Portugal and France also suggested): picks government, estates, mission tree, neighbours, and rivals [V3 Steam threads 2024-06-08, 2025-03-27, 2024-12-26; Superjump 2022-05-18 https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/intro-to-europa-universalis-iv-the-first-turn/].
- **Start date** (default 11 Nov 1444; other bookmarks exist) [M]. **Difficulty and AI aggression, Ironman, game rules** [M, options not read this session].
- **Before unpausing**: set message settings to "Pop up and pause" for armies arriving, battles starting/ending, sieges ending, war start; pick a campaign goal; choose rivals when the red alert flag asks [V3 Steam 2024-12-26 pasted guide by "Marquoz"; Superjump].
#### 2. The loop
1. **Clock**: real time, pausable at any moment, five speeds; the author of the 2022 walkthrough plays at speed 3, drops to 2 in complicated wars and goes to 4 when bored [V3 Superjump]. One tick is one day; once a month (the 1st) income, expenses, monarch power, manpower recovery and estate loyalty drift update [M].
2. **You act any time, paused or not**: spend monarch points, set diplomacy, move armies, build, hire advisors, manage estates, raise/lower maintenance and take loans [V3 guides above; M for loans].
3. **The game automates**: AI diplomacy and war, daily battle resolution and sieges, trade, rebels, institution spread, estate drift, random events, disasters [M].
4. **Interrupts**: pop-up messages you configured, event windows needing a choice, AI proposals (alliances, marriages), war declarations on you, battle results; pausing is how control returns [V3 Marquoz: unattended armies "cost you wars"].
5. **Monarch power** comes from the ruler's three stats, advisors (+1 to +5 per month each), estate privileges (up to +1 per category each) and power projection; all three types buy technology and ideas; ADM also coring, DIP annexing vassals and hiring admirals, MIL hiring generals [V3 Marquoz].

#### 4. First hour, step by step (the in-game tutorial is called "bad and somewhat broken now"; veterans still say play part of it to find buttons) [V3 Steam 2024-12-26, 2024-12-05]
1. Set all war/army messages to pop-up-and-pause; choose a goal [V3 Marquoz].
2. Pick Ottomans (or France, as in the walkthrough) at the 1444 start; do not unpause yet.
3. Read the top bar: ducats, manpower, ADM, DIP, MIL; hover each to see monthly gain and loss [V3 Superjump].
4. Check alerts (green/yellow/red), set rivals, give each army a general [V3 Superjump].
5. Open the Estates tab: assign privileges so each estate's loyalty equilibrium reaches about 45-50, keep crownland above 30%; seize land when all estates are at 50+ and at peace (loyalty then falls to 30); summon the Diet and choose an agenda [V3 Superjump; Marquoz].
6. Diplomacy: send a diplomat to ally a strong neighbour (Tunis for the Ottomans); count relations against the cap of 4 (France's "Strong Duchies" privilege adds 3) [V3 Superjump, Marquoz].
7. Open the mission tree and pick the nearest mission [V3 both].
8. Unpause at speed 3. Hire advisors you can afford; spend the first monarch points on technology; take Admin/Diplomatic/Influence ideas when a slot opens [V3 Marquoz].
9. A war will arrive (France's first event is the Surrender of Maine). Unprotected provinces fall to one regiment in 30 days; forts must be besieged (6-9 months in the example) [V3 Superjump].
10. Open the peace screen: war score 33 in the example but only 17 points of demands were taken; the AI accepts by war length, relative strength and allies [V3 Superjump].
11. Check relations and AE afterwards; members leave a coalition once AE is low (1.37.3) [V3 Steam 2024-09-10]; coalitions forming against high-AE nations is the usual rule [M, not in that note].
12. Keep stacking points into tech to stay ahead: "even a difference of one point can have a huge impact" [V3 Marquoz].
#### 5. What changes later
Institutions appear and spread (feudalism, renaissance...), so tech costs rubber-band (a missing institution is +50% per tech) [V3 2021-09-28 diary]. Age of Absolutism: most privileges lower absolutism, so players revoke them (needs Loyalty > Influence) [V3 Steam 2020-06-23 replies]. Colonisation, trade nodes, Reformation, large coalitions, revolts, estate disasters. Pressure ramps because the AI's alliance blocs and your own AE grow [M].
#### 6. Failure and ending
Annexed or destroyed nation ends your run unless you continue as another tag; wars lost cost provinces or vassals. Estate disasters: influence reaching 100 starts them, and a timer runs while an estate has under 50% loyalty and you are not at war; "quite difficult to start" [S3 wiki search summary]. No in-game final victory beyond the 1821 end date, achievements and a score [M]. Recovery from a bad war is usually possible by reloading in non-Ironman [V3 Steam 2024-10-13 advice: save before wars].
#### 7. Consequential vs cosmetic choices
- **Consequential**: technology lead, alliance web, terrain and combat width in wars; veterans give these as the main factors of battle results [V3 Marquoz; community].
- **Consequential**: estates: privileges give up to +1 monarch power per category each, crownland above 30% avoids penalties, and "Supremacy over the Crown" raises all equilibria by 10 for letting estates call Diets [V3 Marquoz].
- **Dominant answers (community)**: "Big Three" first ideas; Ottomans as first nation; always attack on plains, defend on hills [V3 Marquoz; community consensus, one author].
- **Cosmetic**: most flavour privileges and events; the choice among three Diet agendas [M].

### Dominions 5 and Dominions 6 (two separate games, kept apart below) - facts line
Illwinter Game Design; simultaneous-turn fantasy grand strategy (one turn = one month). **D5**: last Steam patch 5.61, 2023-07-25 [V3 steam-news appid 722060 fetched 2026-10-22]. **D6**: released 2024-01-17 [V3 same D5 feed, item "Dominions 6 released"], latest 6.37, 2026-09-09 [V3 steam-news appid 2511500]. Assumed: single-player vs 1 AI, Early or Middle Age, random map ~20-25 provinces/player, Thrones-of-Ascension victory (the community guide's own recipe [V3 IW5:bcnoexceptions-new-player-guide, IW6:new-player-guide]). Session: first hour = turns 1-13 of the guide; a full single-player game is tens of turns at 5-20 min each, multiplayer runs weeks on turn timers [M]. Sources are the community wiki "illwiki" (IW5 = https://illwiki.com/dom5/ , IW6 = https://illwiki.com/dom5/dom6/ ; fetched via `?do=export_raw`, all [V3] 2026-10-22), not the Illwinter manual (illwinter.com was down; manual unread). Only one editor's guide; opinions flagged.
#### 1. Setup choices
- **Age** (Early / Middle / Late): sets the nation list and tone; EA and MA play alike, LA differs (humans, heavy armour, rarer magic) [V3 IW5 guide; IW5:introduction_to_dominions].
- **Nation** (one per player, ~dozens per age): fixes your recruitable troops, commanders, mages, sacred units and fort buildings. Guide: easy EA = Caelum, Fomoria; easy MA = Ulm, Caelum; avoid underwater, Blood nations, EA Ubar/Yomi, MA Ermor/Asphodel/Ind (D6 adds Phaeacia); Astral nations add "Communions" [V3 guides].
- **Map and opponents**: provinces per player (guide: 20-25), AI count, "Random Land Nation" for the AI. D6 also asks for world/underground layout (defaults) and E/W + N/S wraparound [V3 IW6 guide]. D5 offers "<Random Map>" directly [V3 IW5 guide].
- **Pretender god design** (the main decision): chassis = what the god is. D5 groups by dominion strength: immobile statues (Dom 4), titans (3), monsters (2), humans (1); a new magic path costs 40 / 60 / 80 / 10 points respectively [V3 IW5 guide]. D6 same four classes (first level of a new path 40/60/80/10) and a budget of **450 design points minus the chassis** [V3 IW6:pretenders]. D5's budget: `?` (not on any page I read).
- **Magic paths**: D5 has 8 (Fire Air Water Earth Astral Death Nature Blood); D6 has 9 (adds Glamour) [V3 diff of the two guides]. Cost rises each level; a high path is cheaper if the chassis starts with it [V3 IW5/IW6].
- **Bless** (buffs for your "sacred" troops, cast by priests): D5 needs >=4 in one path, or >=3 in all four elemental or all four sorcery paths; D6 gives 1 bless point per path level above 1 [V3 diff]. "Incarnate-only" blesses need an Awake god.
- **Dominion candles** (5-7 typical in D5 guide) cap sacred recruits per site per turn and spread your faith faster [V3 IW5 guide]. **Scales** (six, each +/-40 pts per click): Order/Turmoil (gold, recruit points), Production/Sloth (gold, resources), Heat/Cold (design points either way, income penalty), Growth/Death (income, population), Luck/Misfortune (random events), Magic/Drain (research) [V3]. D6 caps most scales at 2 clicks from default (3 for some chassis) [V3 IW6:pretenders].
- **Awakening**: Awake (god on map turn 1), Dormant (appears ~turns 11-14, +150 pts in D6), Imprisoned (turns ~29-42, +350 pts in D6) [V3 IW6:pretenders; D5 timings in IW5:pretenders]. Guide: take Awake first game, avoid Dormant.
- **Game rules**: random events common/rare, story events, research start, renaming, and **Thrones**: number per level (1-3) and ascension points needed (guide: one of each level, win at 3) [V3 IW5 guide].
#### 2. The loop
1. **Clock**: one turn = one month; all nations give orders at the same time; "End Turn" (or the host's timer in multiplayer) resolves everyone at once [V3 IW5:introduction_to_dominions; IW5:turn-order-sequence].
2. **Your orders**: each *commander* gets one order (move, attack, research, recruit, rituals, forge, site-search, build, preach...). Troops are grouped into squads under commanders and follow them. Before moving you set **Army Setup**: squad placement, formation, battle orders; mages get a **spell script** (specific actions for 5 rounds then a general order) [V3 IW5 guide].
3. **Resolution (automated, ~45 numbered steps in D5)**: research, empowerment, recruitment, forging, preaching, throne claims, rituals; then magic battles; "godly intermezzo" (site search, prophets, god awakening); horrors/assassination/raids; movement (friendly first, then into enemy provinces); **main battles in random order**, fort storms; events; global enchantments; sieges; construction; **income, unrest, starvation, upkeep, dominion spread** [V3 IW5:turn-order-sequence]. D6 reorders recruitment before empowerment and adds a "mount admin" step [V3 diff, IW6:turn-order-sequence].
4. **Battles are never played live**: the engine runs them from your army setup and scripts; you watch a replay next turn (pause, speed, per-unit log) and revise [V3 IW5 guide turn 4].
5. **Interrupts**: none mid-turn. Control returns only at the turn boundary, with a message list (battles, events, new gods, thrones). Against AI there is no diplomacy at all: nations start at peace and a war lasts "to the bitter end"; human multiplayer diplomacy happens outside the game [V3 IW5:introduction_to_dominions].

#### 4. First hour, step by step (guide turns 1-13; D5 unless marked)
1. Create world, pick Age, a nation, 1 AI; design the pretender (>=5 candles, scales to taste, Awake) [V3 guide].
2. Turn 1: troop-leader commander -> "Become Prophet"; scout sneaks to a neighbouring farm province; an Awake god chooses Research [V3].
3. Recruit: a mage commander plus your sacred or high-protection troops; click "repeat recruitment"; research queue; End Turn [V3].
4. Turn 2: Army Setup: put the new troops in a squad on the green deployment area, battle orders "Hold and attack" / "Hold and fire", script the prophet (Divine Blessing, then Smite x4) [V3].
5. Turn 3: attack a neighbour with <=~40 defenders, preferably a farm or forest, not Barbarians/Heavy Cavalry/Bone Tribe [V3].
6. Turn 4: read the battle reports, open View Battle, pause, read the log; fix flanks with "Guard Commander" or "Attack Rear" [V3].
7. Turn 5: Mercenaries tab; recruit a commander with Leadership >=40 (>=80 unlocks line formations) and gather 30-40 troops [V3].
8. Turns 6-9: order a unit to build a Palisade (4 turns) in a second province; add a temple and lab by sending a priest and a mage [V3].
9. Turn ~10: send mages out to search every province for magic sites (gems); fill labs with mages [V3].
10. Turns 10+: cast rituals, forge items; transfer gems to mages at a lab (or let a script use "conservative gem usage") [V3].
11. Turn 12+: find Thrones of Ascension (strong indie guards); build a fort on it, send a Holy-3 unit to claim it [V3].
12. Turn 13+: grow, conquer, repeat; try Communions, thugs, stealth. **D6 differences**: size squares hold 10 size points (D5 6), humans size 3 (D5 2), 9th path, new bless rule, dearer buildings [V3 diff].
#### 5. What changes later
Magic overtakes troops: battles are decided by gem-fuelled mage scripts, so large fights need dozens of mages plus scouts ferrying gems [V3 IW5:wiki/common_beginner_mistakes, quoting Taorec]. New systems: item boosters, summon-climbing, empowerment (15 gems per level, 50 for a new path in D5) [V3 guide], global enchantments and domes [V3 IW5 index], thugs (single supercombatants), Blood hunting. Pressure ramps with neighbours meeting (war is permanent vs AI), gem upkeep, and Thrones that "need several years" of conquest and research before anyone can take them [V3 intro].
#### 6. Failure and ending
You are eliminated when you hold no provinces or no dominion on the map [V3 intro]. A killed pretender is not permanently dead: priests spend ~50 priest-level turns on "Call God", with a chance to lose a magic path level or a point of dominion (immortal gods exempt) [V3 IW5:pretenders]. Win: reach the ascension-point target with claimed thrones; other victory modes exist [V3 IW5:thrones; the "vast majority" use thrones]. Recovery from a collapsing front is possible; from elimination no.
#### 7. Consequential vs cosmetic choices
- **Consequential**: pretender build (chassis, paths, bless, candles, scales). The guide treats some answers as near-dominant: >=5 candles, never Dormant on a first game, and in D5 "Growth 3 ... take as much growth as you can afford"; the D6 guide drops that line and only warns that Death costs income [V3 diff].
- **Consequential**: expansion tempo (≥20 provinces in the first year is the community test), forts/labs/temples timing, gem economy (mid-game wars go to whoever converts gem stock best) [V3 common_beginner_mistakes; all opinion pages tagged by author on the wiki].
- **Consequential in battle**: leadership and commander survival (section 8 below); formations; scripts (the wiki lists "armies and mages not scripted at all" as a classic beginner error) [V3].
- **Cosmetic/dominated**: renaming commanders (used for reminders or taunts), most blessed "***Weapons" for beginners (guide says avoid) [V3]. Community consensus, not measured.

### Heroes of Might and Magic III, V and VII (one town/hero/map/battle loop, three entries) - facts line
**III**: New World Computing / 3DO, 1999 (RoE, Armageddon's Blade, Shadow of Death) [M]; rules read from the fan wiki heroes.thelazy.net (H3W = https://heroes.thelazy.net/index.php?title= ... &action=raw, fetched 2026-10-22, covers SoD with Horn of the Abyss "HotA" fan-expansion notes) and the original manual's tutorial transcribed there [V3 H3W:Tutorial]. **V**: Nival / Ubisoft, EU 2006-05-19, NA 2006-05-23; expansions Hammers of Fate (2006), Tribes of the East (standalone, 2007) [V3 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroes_of_Might_and_Magic_V]. **VII** (Might & Magic Heroes VII): Limbic / Ubisoft, 2015-09-29, Windows; Trial by Fire (2016-08-04) added a Fortress faction and a random-map generator; Metacritic 67 [V3 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Might_%26_Magic_Heroes_VII]. **Mostly [M] for V and VII**: I could not open a V or VII rulebook or wiki (Fandom/StrategyWiki blocked); V is backed by one 2005 designer Q&A, VII by an undated gamepressure guide. III is well sourced. Assumed: III tutorial map and a normal skirmish at Normal (100%) difficulty. Session: the III tutorial is 11 game days (about 30-60 min); a full scenario is 1-4+ hours [M].
#### 1. Setup choices
- **Scenario/map** (or campaign): map size, players, win/loss rules set by the map author; special victory conditions listed in section 6 [V3 H3W:Victory_and_Loss_Conditions].
- **Town/faction** (III: Castle, Rampart, Tower, Inferno, Necropolis, Dungeon, Stronghold, Fortress, plus Conflux from Armageddon's Blade [V3 H3W:Town]; V: Haven, Inferno, Necropolis, Dungeon, Sylvan, Academy, plus Fortress/dwarves in Hammers of Fate and Stronghold in Tribes of the East [V3 Wikipedia V]; VII: Haven, Academy, Stronghold, Necropolis, Sylvan, Dungeon, plus Fortress via Trial by Fire [V3 Wikipedia VII]). Each faction has seven creature tiers and its own buildings and heroes.
- **Difficulty** (III): Easy 80% / Normal 100% / Hard 130% / Expert 160% / Impossible 200%. It scales *starting gold* for the player (about 30000 / 20000 / 15000 / 10000 / 0) and gives the AI more, plus AI behaviour limits (hero caps, build frequency, Grail search) and, from Expert, daily AI resource bonuses (+25% / +50% in the first resource column) [V3 H3W:Difficulty_level; resource-column icons were stripped in the raw text, so the exact per-resource split is inferred].
- **Starting hero/bonus and opponents** (human/AI count, teams, handicap): [M] for III, `?` for V/VII.
#### 2. The loop
1. **Clock**: one *day* per turn; 7 days = a *week*; months follow; every player (or AI) takes a full day's moves on the adventure map in turn, ended with the hourglass "End Turn" button [V3 H3W:Tutorial; VII gamepressure world-map page shows the end-turn button with the day of the week].
2. **Your day**: pick a hero (Next Hero button cycles those with movement left); give a destination, shown as a path (green = reachable today, red = beyond today's movement points); the hero walks, picking up and flagging things; at a town you build, recruit, trade armies, hire heroes [V3 H3W:Tutorial].
3. **Between turns (automated)**: rival players/AI move; at day start gold arrives from Halls (500 / 1000 / 2000 / 4000 per day for Village / Town / City / Capitol) and mines/silos give resources; **on the first day of each week creature dwellings repopulate** and week/month events (e.g. plague halves town dwellings) apply [V3 H3W:Halls, Growth, Creature_dwelling]. Neutral towns may gain garrison troops each week (80% chance if fortified) [V3 H3W:Town].
4. **Interrupt = battle**: a hero walking into monsters, an enemy hero or town switches to the combat screen (a hex grid of 11 rows by 15 columns; creatures act by speed; each round a stack can move, attack/shoot, defend (+20% defense), wait; the hero casts one spell per round; morale -3 to +3 can give extra actions or freeze a stack; tactics phase if you out-skill the enemy) and returns to the map afterwards [V3 H3W:Combat, Morale]. Only heroes may retreat or surrender, and a defender in a town cannot [V3].
5. **V** keeps the day/week structure and says unit generation "happens weekly", depending on town buildings, captured map buildings and astrology weeks [V3 gamebanshee 2005-11-10 Q&A]; its battles use an initiative queue (units "take turns based on initiative", [S3 search snippet]). **VII** has seven resources (gold, wood, ore, Dragonblood crystals, Starsilver, Dragonsteel, Shadowsteel), map "influence zones", and a hero who either attacks or casts each turn, not both [V3 gamepressure world-map and battles pages].

#### 4. First hour (the official III tutorial map, 2 weeks; steps paraphrased from the manual text) [V3 H3W:Tutorial]
1. New Game -> Tutorial. Day 1: send the hero into the town; Hall screen: build a Fort.
2. Buy a spell book in the Mage Guild (a Knight starts without one); buy troops with Maximum; move them into the hero's army.
3. Walk to the Sawmill and Ore Pit to flag them (wood and ore are the building basics); take the chest and choose XP -> level 2, pick a skill; press the hourglass.
4. Day 2: build a Griffin Tower, recruit, visit a Fountain of Youth (morale/luck sites); end turn.
5. Day 3: build a Blacksmith; flag a map Griffin Tower (adds to your town's weekly growth); first fight (Gnolls) to learn hexes, speed order, ranged units; collect the artifact.
6. Day 4: upgrade the Guardhouse, hire a second hero at the Tavern, upgrade Pikemen, flag four mines; the "Move Hero" button resumes a long path.
7. Days 5-6: War Machine Factory (buy a Ballista), a stat-boosting site, then storm an enemy Inferno town (walls, catapult, spells each round); build its Birthing Pools (a horde building) and upgrade Town Hall to City Hall (doubles income).
8. Week 2, Day 1: dwellings repopulate; build a Tavern in the new town and hire a hero to ferry troops; sail a boat bought at a shipyard.
9. Days 2-4: beat a hero guarding an Obelisk (reveals the Grail puzzle), dig with D, carry the Grail to the Castle and build the Colossus: **that wins the tutorial** [V3].
#### 5. What changes later
Dwelling tiers 5-7 and upgrades; Citadel x1.5 and Castle x2 creature growth in fortified towns, horde buildings, Grail x1.5; Capitol 4000 gold/day [V3 H3W:Growth, Halls]. Heroes gain levels (XP threshold for level 30 is 496,963) [V3 Experience]. Siege warfare, the underworld level, Grail hunt via obelisk puzzles. AI at Hard+ builds every day and hunts the Grail earlier [V3 Difficulty_level]. V adds a skill wheel with a racial skill and "ultimate" level and 4 skill levels (Basic to Ultimate) [V3 Wikipedia V]; VII skill trees `?`.
#### 6. Failure and ending
Default win: conquer all enemy towns and defeat all enemy heroes. Default loss: no town for seven days, or last hero defeated with no town. Map-set extras: acquire an artifact, accumulate creatures or resources, upgrade a town, build the Grail structure, defeat a hero/monster, flag all dwellings or mines, survive N days; losses: lose a hero or town, time limit [V3 H3W:Victory_and_Loss_Conditions]. A hero who loses a battle is gone; the tutorial's advice is "load your Autosave" [V3 tutorial]. Recovery within a scenario is common via a second town and the Tavern [M].
#### 7. Consequential vs cosmetic choices
- **Consequential**: early economy: which mines/dwellings you flag first (they raise growth kingdom-wide) and Hall upgrades (income doubling each tier) [V3 H3W:Growth, Halls]. Spending the week's stock before the next growth day: unhired creatures persist in towns [V3].
- **Consequential**: stack merging and keeping heroes in towns/garrisons; war machines for sieges; which skill pair to take at level-up (two offered, right-click to read) [V3 tutorial].
- **Difficulty is a blunt lever, not a build choice**: Impossible starts you with zero resources while the AI keeps 10000 gold plus daily bonuses [V3 Difficulty_level]. Whether any faction or build is dominant: `?` (tier lists exist; I read none).
- **Cosmetic**: hero portrait/name, music themes, most Fountain-type sites after the first visit (effects do not stack until the next battle) [V3 tutorial].

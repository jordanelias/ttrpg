## 0. Focalization — what it determines

Focalization (SKILL.md, "The Master Principle") determines:
- **vocabulary** — a character hostile to authority calls officials "the inspectors"; one employed by authority calls them "colleagues";
- **salience** — a merchant notices prices; a fugitive notices exits;
- **hedging** — a grieving narrator hedges around the grief;
- **editorial stance** — "sinister" or "noble" is licensed when it belongs to a focalized perspective.

Each author focalizes distinctly: Tartt through obsessive first-person narrators whose aestheticism colours everything; Ishiguro through narrators whose restraint IS the focalization; Mistry by shifting between omniscient and free-indirect, picking up characters' attitudes mid-paragraph; Tolkien by elevating register for elevated speakers and plainening it for plain; Márquez by surfacing a village's collective voice; Lispector by collapsing narrator and character into one perceiving-and-questioning consciousness; Borges through scholarly apparatus that is itself a focalization (the encyclopedia-voice has attitudes); Ocampo through narrators who do not register the magnitude of what they describe.

## 1. Sentence Construction

- **Paratactic coordination [Tolkien].** Marked moments: heroic peaks, battle, ruin, chronicle-at-scale.
- **Hypotactic cascade [Tolkien].** Model: *The Hobbit*'s opening sentence — character, place and social context in one hypotactic arc.
- **Qualifying sentence [Ishiguro].** Qualifications signal emotional stakes: the more the narrator hedges, the more important the content.
- **Atmospheric sentence [Tartt].** Subordinate clauses serve immersion, not argument. The reader should feel the room before understanding its function.
- **Appositive cascade [Márquez].** Each appositive extends timeline or scope; the sentence becomes compressed history.
- **Elliptical stream of consciousness [Lispector].** The gaps between the fragments matter as much as the fragments.
- **Erudite metaphysical compression [Borges].** What takes a philosopher a chapter goes in a subordinate clause; the reader does the unpacking.
- **Flat declarative holding horror [Ocampo].** The narrator reports with the same investment given to domestic trivia.

## 2. Paragraph Architecture

- **Particular before general [Mistry/Lispector].** Not "the settlement was in decline" but "the market had contracted to six stalls."
- **Digression as revelation [Ishiguro].** "But I was speaking of the garden" returns to the surface, having exposed something unintended.

## 3. Time

- **Time as spiral [Márquez].** The second siege resembles the first.
- **"This instant-now" [Lispector].** "I want to grab hold of the *is* of the thing" (*Água Viva*). At low coherence this is not transcendence: it is the failure of temporal structuring, leaving only the raw now.
- **Eternal return / time as labyrinth [Borges].** "The Garden of Forking Paths": all possible outcomes exist simultaneously.

## 4. Voice and Stance

- **FID / direct thought / narrative report.** How the modes differ by author: Ishiguro sits closer to quoted monologue (Stevens narrates as if speaking, hedging in the rhythm of speech); Mistry picks up village idiom inside an omniscient frame; Lispector collapses narrator and character into a voice that resists assignment to any one mode; Borges's encyclopedia voice is closer to psycho-narration applied to a fictional consciousness. Name the mode deployed (`anti-patterns-skeleton.md` VI.3).
- **Narrator who does not register [Ocampo].** The incuriosity is the horror.
- **Encoded vernacular speech [all, context-dependent].** Name what the prose is actually doing in its own social context; do not appropriate the coded speech of a specific real cultural tradition.

## 5. Emotion and Interiority

- **Body as record [Mistry].** The prose names scars, posture and calluses without interpreting them.

## 7. Specificity

- **Charged objects [Ocampo].** Dolls, mirrors, photographs.

## 11. Structural Enactment — three sources of silence

The Wittgenstein constraint (`anti-patterns-skeleton.md` I.6) applies whether the content exceeds the prose because coherence has failed or because TS-perceived content resists articulation, but the silence has a different character in each case:

- **At the limit of human language (any tier).** Threads are the conditions of possibility for experience, thought and language; language cannot describe its own conditions from outside. The silence is the boundary where the tool meets what constitutes the tool — not a poetic gesture.
- **At low coherence.** The calibration that made language human is itself what is failing. The silence at Coherence 1 is that of an instrument becoming a different instrument.
- **At high TS with high coherence.** The language remains fully calibrated; the content lies beyond its range. The instrument works; the object is out of reach.

Two modes the skeleton table compresses: Lispector's failure is generative — "the unsayable can only be given to me through the failure of my language"; the construction fails, and the failure is the achievement. Stevens does not say "I could not determine my feelings"; he determines them wrongly, and the reader detects the wrongness (the fix for I.6 meta-inarticulation).

Mass Battle, Tick by Tick

One battle, followed from the data the season hands over to the grade the battle returns. The numbers in steps 4 to 7 are a real run of run_battle, not an illustration. Where the engine does something this page has not explained, it says so.

Example: A 400 heavy infantry, 200 archers, 100 light cavalry, advance B 400 spearmen, 200 archers, defend morale start 5 each · seed 3 · built with build_unit
Step 1 · the edge

The season hands over data; the battle runs on its own

The season loop supplies the data, content and context as one record: who stands on each side, each side’s army record, the terrain row from the territory and its fortification, and each side’s morale from stance and loyalty. It runs no part of the fight and the battle writes nothing to the world.

The module takes that record, builds one Unit per side, fights it and returns survivor shares and a degree. It never reads the world and keeps nothing between calls. The season loop then writes the new state from what comes back.

Which battle entry runs is the module’s own matter. The season edge calls resolve_field, which fights one engagement of at most 18 ticks. The module also has run_multi_turn_battle and run_multi_unit_battle; the workbench and the bat.py battery exercise those directly. This page follows the single-engagement path.

A side with an army record fights as that order of battle. If neither side has one, each fights as a single line sized by its headcount; if only one does, the other fights as one line of light infantry.

Code: modules/mass_battle/massbattle.py (resolve_field, _run_and_grade); host side engine/season/seam/wrappers/mass_battle.py. A lost field’s consequence, “casualties only, decrease in morale, and a grudge token”, is quoted from the massbattle.py docstring (ED-IN-0279 clause b) and written by the season’s _eff_march.

Step 2 · the field

Deployment: two lines, three empty rows apart

Side A advances toward lower row numbers and side B toward higher. Each army is placed by a fixed rule: melee in the front line, centred; ranged behind it; mounted on the flanks.

  • Points, not squares. Each cell is a node with a float position and its own facing (_node_pos, cell_facing_vec). The lattice supplies the formation’s footprint offsets and the integer binning used for contact identities; it does not place troops.
  • Scale. 100 troops is one Size; a node holds at most 200 (CELL_CAP). 400 spearmen are two nodes.
  • Gap. The front faces start 3 rows apart (_APPROACH_ROWS), the smallest gap measured to let every melee type meet inside one 18-tick fight.
  • Reach. Archers fire at the nearest enemy subunit 2 to 8 cells away (VOLLEY_MIN_RANGE, VOLLEY_MAX_RANGE). A’s archers (row 29) are 6 rows from B’s spearmen (row 23), so both sides fire at tick 1.
  • Stance. advance sets A’s melee and horse to aggressive; defend sets B’s to hold. Archers stay balanced in both.

The placement rule and the three strategies are marked ASSUMPTION in orderofbattle.py, for Jordan to correct.

Step 3 · inside run_battle

What happens in every tick

Each tick follows the same order. Two stages are built to be fair to both sides:

  • Movement is simultaneous. Every subunit’s target position is read before anyone moves, so A is not steering toward B’s already-moved position (a code comment cites a first-argument bias of about 10% before this fix).
  • Damage is simultaneous. Melee and volley losses for both sides are computed, then subtracted together. Size is recalculated only afterward.

The loop stops early the moment either Unit has routed. If neither does, the battle ends at tick 18 and the grade decides.

Code: run_battle, volley_phase, resolve_engagements_cascading, phase_boundary in modules/mass_battle/orchestration.py. The rout check at stage 9 runs every tick; the casualty-based break check runs only in stage 10.

Step 4 · the battle itself

Scrub through the example, tick by tick

Tick 4

Each square is one node at its logged float position, turned to its facing; the number is troops alive in it. Tick 0 is the deployment. The tick-end snapshots come from walkthrough.py’s recorder. The loop exits at the top of tick 16 because A has routed.

Step 5 · ranged fire

Volley arithmetic, tick 1

Archers fire before anyone moves. Each ranged subunit picks the nearest enemy subunit 2 to 8 cells away and rolls its Power as a d10 pool. B’s archers, firing at A:

poolPower 3, minus 1 for Discipline 32 dice
net successesd10 faces: 1 → −1, 2–6 → 0, 7–9 → +1, 10 → +23
armour, square lawminus target DR, × 0.25 × shooter Size1.0
densitya dense target takes more from area fire× 2.0

troops lost× 3 (VOLLEY_LETHALITY_SCALE)6

The trace shows A’s side losing exactly 6.0 at tick 1, which is this chain. A’s archers rolled net 2 and B lost 3.0.

A volley needs a target 2 to 8 cells away, so archers stop firing at anything that has closed inside 2 cells. Ranged units pulled into melee take a penalty (RANGED_MELEE_SIGMA).

The volley roller hands its dice to the same roll_pool melee uses, so both fire at TN 7 (Jordan, 2026-08-25: “TN7 always”). A volley floors at 0 net; a melee pool never drops below one die.

Step 6 · melee

One exchange, tick 4: first contact

For every pair of subunits in contact, each side rolls a pool, adds a modifier, and compares nets. The two rolls are compared against each other, so a side can fail outright even with a decent roll.

poolsA heavy infantry vs B spearmen3 vs 19
netsd10 pool plus the modifier shift1.86 / 9.26
degreeA: 1.86 − 9.26 · B: 9.26 − max(1, 1.86)Fail / Overwhelming
damage to A12 × contact strength × (1 + P − DR)19.6 troops
damage to BA failed: damage value 00

The modifier shift folds in flanking arcs, charge shock, morale, fatigue, encirclement and intent. Casualties scale with the enemy’s strength in contact, capped by frontage (the Lanchester linear law, K_LINEAR = 12). Surrounded bodies take more: ×1.5 from two sides, ×2.0 from three.

Not traced: why A’s pool is 3 against 19, and why it falls to 1 from tick 7. The trace records pools, not their derivation (subunit_combat_pool, pair_pool_contribution). [GAP: pool derivation not read]

Step 7 · attrition and rout

Losses accumulate; the break is drawn from a band

B’s losses are almost all volleys. A fails every exchange from tick 4 and bleeds steadily: 42% of its 700 troops are gone by tick 15.

A subunit breaks one of three ways:

  • Morale at zero. At each phase boundary, a subunit with under 50% of its strength left loses 1 morale, and under 25% loses another; exhaustion costs more. The loss is scaled by 0.7.
  • Too few left. Fewer than 80 troops (SUBUNIT_ROUT_FLOOR).
  • A drawn break-point. Each subunit draws one casualty fraction between 15% and 30% (skewed later for disciplined, high-morale troops) and breaks at the next phase boundary after crossing it. MB_STOCHASTIC_ROUT is on by default.

The whole Unit routs when its general is gone, its troop-weighted morale reaches zero, or the broken subunits held 60% of its starting strength (ROUT_CASCADE_FRAC).

Step 7b · the rout in the example

Where A’s heavy infantry broke

At the tick-6 boundary the heavy infantry had lost 18.3% and held. By tick 12 it had lost 47.8% and was routed. So its drawn break-point lay between 18.3% and 30%. That is inferred from two observations; the draw itself is not in the trace.

The whole Unit routed at tick 15. Its morale read 5 at tick 14 and 2.32 at tick 15, so the morale path was not the trigger; by elimination the broken share reached 60%. [CONFIDENCE: medium · which subunit broke last is not visible in the trace]

From tick 12 the heavy infantry’s pool is 0: a routed subunit deals no damage (its net is forced to 0).

Step 8 · grading and terrain

What the battle returns

The engine produces survivor shares, not a winner’s margin. _run_and_grade turns them into the degree the season receives, with the attacker as side A:

DegreeCondition
OverwhelmingA wins, keeps ≥ 75%, B left with ≤ 25%
SuccessA wins: A has not routed, and B routed or A’s share is higher
PartialA not routed, no win, A’s share ≥ 50%
Failureanything else, including any A rout

The example: A routed, so Failure, with A at 57.8% and B at 96.5% of starting troops. These bands are the older adapter’s own, not the canonical margin ladder in step 6, and the code says so; reconciling them is open lane work.

Terrain is a single row chosen from the territory and its fortification, and it changes the Units before tick 1:

RowEffect
WallsDefender DR +3
UphillAttacker −1D offence; defender +1D defence
River crossingAttacker −1D offence (its speed-tier drop is inert)
Broken forestFast becomes Standard (inert: run_battle never reads speed)
Narrow passIdentified, applies nothing

Walls outrank a river crossing. Flat ground applies nothing.

Limits of this page

What was and was not checked

  • Constructed matchup. The armies were built directly with build_unit and fought with run_battle, the engine path resolve_field uses. No season world was involved, so nothing here says how often a realm fields these armies.
  • Rerun it. python systems/mass_battle/sim/walkthrough.py --seed 3 reproduces the numbers in steps 4 to 7.
  • One seed shown. Seeds 1 to 6 gave B, A, B, draw, draw, A. The matchup is close; seed 3 was chosen because it shows a rout.
  • Not traced: pool derivation (step 6), the last subunit to break, and the multi-unit path (run_multi_unit_battle: morale cascade, freed attackers, pursuit, feigned retreat), which this page does not follow. The example gave no roles, so A’s horse sat at row 25, column 23 from tick 3. With the Flanker role the same seed sends it wide and back; walkthrough.py --role Flanker shows it.
  • What is logged. The engine’s own trace emits five event kinds (tick, volley, melee, casualty, positions). Facing, wheel, collision caps, orders and morale changes are not events; the page shows them as end-of-tick snapshots taken by the harness’s recorder. The octagon’s inputs were not traced end to end.
  • Reference only. Per the repo’s rule, this page explains the code; it is not a source of any value. The engine’s calibration constants carry CALIBRATED-DEBT tags in config.py.
